# Results

**1 200 cases/family · base seed `20260902` · exhaustive over the powerset for `2 ≤ n ≤ 7` ·
vacuous `(T,Π)` pairings excluded.**

# 1. The correction, first

The previous experiment reported that Zero fails to be element-wise **in both directions**. **The
measurement was right; the framing hid a dependency.**

`P9_balance` — a *cancelling* contract, net `(#positive − #negative)` — **was added by the executor**
for `KR-ZERO-ALGEBRA` precisely so that adversarial case J would be reachable. It was declared at the
time. **What was not then checked is whether it is the sole source of the phenomenon. It is.**

| | with all 9 contracts | **with `P9_balance` excluded** |
|---|---|---|
| cases | 826 | 746 |
| minimal Zero subset sizes | `{1: 1021, 2: 19}` | **`{1: 798}`** |
| **emergent minimal subsets** | 19 | **0** |
| intersection-closure failures | 2 | **0** |
| matroid circuit-exchange failures | 2 | **0** |
| downward-closure failures | 40 | **26** |
| **`Z` not generated by minimal** | 286 | **267** |
| ↳ of which case-I direction | 286 | **267** |
| ↳ of which case-J direction | **0** | **0** |

> `[EXP]` **Case I is a general property of the tested space.**
> `[EXP]` **Case J is a property of CANCELLING CONTRACTS, not of `Zero` as such.**
>
> **This does not make case J an artefact.** A balance-preserving contract is a legitimate
> preservation contract, and *under one*, emergent eliminability is real. **It makes case J
> conditional**, and the condition is now named.

# 3. `G2`–`G3` — minimal elements, and what they fail to generate

**1 040 minimal Zero subsets: 1 021 singletons, 19 of size 2.** Maximal Zero subsets range up to
size 6.

> `[EXP]` **`Z` is NOT generated upward by its minimal elements — 286 / 826, and the failure is
> entirely in the case-I direction** ("a superset of a Zero set that is not Zero"). The converse
> direction never fails, as it cannot: every `S ∈ Z` contains a minimal element of `Z` by
> construction.

**Consequence, and it is the practically important one:**

> **The hypergraph of minimal Zero subsets does not determine `Z`.** There is therefore **no compact
> generator-based encoding** of eliminability in the tested space — one cannot compute a set of
> "eliminable cores" and close upward.

# 4. `G4` — the matroid test

Minimal Zero subsets were tested against the **circuit-exchange axiom (C3)**: if `C₁ ≠ C₂` are
circuits and `e ∈ C₁ ∩ C₂`, then `(C₁ ∪ C₂) − e` contains a circuit.

**311 hold · 1 fails · 531 skipped (fewer than 2 circuits).**

> **REFUTED by a single decisive counterexample — and it is under the cancelling contract.**
> Without `P9_balance`, **circuit exchange holds in every tested case.**
>
> `[EXP]` **Matroid structure is available for non-cancelling contracts and is destroyed by
> cancellation.** That is a sharper statement than either "it is a matroid" or "it is not".

# 5. `G5` — pairwise determination, and the `FR-001` connection

**4 543 subsets of size 3–4 · 456 distinct signatures · 7 ambiguous.**

> `[NEG]` **Group Zero is NOT determined by the Zero-status of the singletons and pairs it
> contains.** Two subsets with identical singleton-and-pair signatures can differ in group Zero.

**This is an independent instance of the shape `FR-001` froze** — *pairwise distinguishability cannot
carry family-level complexity* — arising in a different domain, from a different construction.

> **It does not extend `FR-001`, and must not be cited as doing so.** `FR-001` is about
> distinguishability; this is about eliminability. **What is notable is that the same obstruction
> shape appears twice independently** — recorded as an observation, `[EXP]`, not a generalization.

# 6. `G7`–`G8` — overlap and classification

**Overlapping Zero subsets `A, B` with `A ∩ B ≠ ∅`:**

| operation | in `Z` | not in `Z` | failure rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| intersection | 17 450 | **30** | **0.17 %** |
| difference | 14 699 | 255 | 1.7 % |
| symmetric difference | 16 543 | 937 | 5.4 % |
| union | 15 814 | **1 666** | **9.5 %** |

**`Z` is closed under none of them** — but the rates order the operations, and **union is the worst**,
which is case I again.

**Classification of minimal Zero subsets:**

```
redundancy  (singleton eliminable)      1 065
cancellation (NO part eliminable)          15
mixed (some parts eliminable)               0     ← note
```

> `[EXP]` **The dichotomy is clean: a minimal Zero subset is either a singleton (redundancy) or has
> NO eliminable part at all (pure cancellation). Nothing in between was found.**

# 8. `G9` — which regime survives?

| candidate regime | verdict | conditional on cancellation? |
|---|---|---|
| simplicial complex / independence system | **REFUTED** | no — robust |
| filter (upward-closed) | **REFUTED** | no — robust, and the dominant failure |
| union-closed family | **REFUTED** | no |
| **closure system / lattice** | **REFUTED** | **YES — holds without cancellation** |
| **matroid** | **REFUTED** | **YES — holds without cancellation** |
| **hypergraph generated by minimal edges** | **REFUTED** | **no — robust** |
| pairwise-determined family | **REFUTED** | no |

> ## The answer to the commissioned question
>
> **Under non-cancelling contracts, `Z` is intersection-closed and matroid-consistent in every tested
> case — but it is NOT generated by its minimal elements.** The single robust obstruction is:
>
> ```
> a superset of an eliminable set need not be eliminable
> ```
>
> **Adding a cancelling contract destroys the intersection and matroid structure as well.**
>
> **`[OPEN]` — what this is NOT.** No regime is concluded to be *the* correct framework. **Rewriting
> theory is not concluded to be it either**, per the commission. What has been established is which
> standard regimes are *excluded*, and under what condition.
