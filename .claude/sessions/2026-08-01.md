### Governance vocabulary formalized — and the root cause named

| Category | Produces |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | an approved architectural **decision** |
| **Planning** | an approved **work package** *(approval **and opening**)* |
| **Execution** | an authorized **engineering activity** |
| **Delivery** | an accepted **implementation baseline** *(acceptance and **closure**)* |

**Each category is one distinct business event leaving one distinct durable artifact** — that is now the test, and **a label that produces nothing durable is not a governance category.**

> #### Why R-52 and R-60 were both mis-filed
>
> Delivery previously read *"acceptance **and progression** of bounded work packages."* **"Progression" silently covered opening**, so *opening a work package* went to Delivery **twice** before it was caught.

>
> **Opening creates INTENT. Acceptance creates COMPLETION.**

**Terminology is stable — no further evolution without operational evidence of a real deficiency.**

### Governance categories re-derived from LIFECYCLE TRANSITIONS

**The correction that matters: the organizing idea is the business transition, not the artifact.** The artifact exists **because** the transition happened — never the reverse. So the model now reads:

```
Lifecycle transition  ->  Governance category  ->  Durable artifact
     (the event)            (who owns it)          (the evidence)
```

| Category | Transition it owns | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Architecture** | a design question is **decided or interpreted** | approved architectural decision |
| **Planning** | a work package is **opened** (or its plan approved) | approved work package |
| **Execution** | engineering is **authorized to begin** | authorized engineering activity |
| **Delivery** | completed work is **accepted and closed** | accepted implementation baseline |

**Invariant: one category = one lifecycle transition = one durable artifact.** If a category cannot demonstrate all three, **it is incorrectly modelled**.

**Why this is more resilient than my previous version:** I had made *Produces* the organizing column, which works until two transitions happen to leave similar artifacts. **Deriving from the event is stable because the events are what actually differ** — *opening* creates **intent**, *acceptance* creates **completion**, and no artifact naming can blur that.

**The root cause is recorded as model evolution, not human error:** two rulings were filed consistently under a definition that admitted two events into one category. **The rulings followed the process; the definition was wrong.**

**Change bar recorded:** this vocabulary changes again **only on operational evidence that it cannot classify repeated behaviour — never for style.**

### Authority made explicit — the governance meta-model is complete

```
Lifecycle transition -> Governance category -> Governance authority -> Durable artifact
    (what happened)       (who governs it)      (who exercised it)     (what remains)
```

**Categories do not act. Authorities act.** A category says *whose kind of decision this is*; only an authority can exercise it. Without that step the model reads as though a category could decide something — **and no category can.**

**The invariant strengthens to four elements**, with a sharper failure mode: **a transition with no named authority did not actually occur — it was only described.**

**One thing the addition exposed:** *category does not determine authority.* **Planning Governance alone spans two** — EP-01 plan approval is the **Decision Authority's**, opening a work package is the **ARB's**. That is precisely why authority must be **named per transition**, never inferred from the category.

> #### ⚠️ A naming hazard, flagged before it costs anything
>
> **"Lifecycle transition"** (*opened · authorized · accepted · decided* — what happened to the work) and **"transition type"** (*Approval · Ratification · Authorization · Acceptance* — what kind of authority act) are **different, independent axes with confusingly similar names.**
>
> They must both stay: Architecture Governance takes **Approval** for a new ADR and **Ratification** for confirming an existing one's reading. **Recorded now because a pair of near-identical names in a shared definition is exactly the shape that mis-filed R-52 and R-60.**
