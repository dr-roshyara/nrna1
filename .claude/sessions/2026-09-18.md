## Decisions

- **Signal-type reliability, as calibrated by the review pass's spot-checks**:
  POSSIBLY-RELATION / UNKNOWN-CANDIDATE-GROUP / EXACT-STRING-REUSE trusted at face
  value (explicit agent-stated evidence); SHARED-NOTATION and STRING-SIMILARITY
  trusted case-by-case (generic short codes like `C-1`/`T1..T8` are noise; longer
  domain-specific notations and deliberate enumerations like `kos-law-01..11` are
  real); CO-OCCURRENCE treated only as a thematic-proximity index, never as a
  relatedness claim on its own.
- **The mechanical clustering cannot, and should not be expected to, catch narrative/
  behavioral patterns.** The review pass's cross-reference table found the Gita-lane-
  vs-disciplined-lane split (the corpus's single most-repeated structural finding
  across B0048-B0062) and the governance-ratification-unreliability finding (B0062)
  both fall entirely outside what label-similarity clustering can detect — these
  remain standing cautions to carry forward directly from `09-ORCHESTRATOR-FLAGS.md`
  into Phase 3, not something P2b's object families should be expected to encode.
- **Three specific object families are flagged for manual (not mechanical) 
  construction in P2b**, since disjoint vocabulary across batches defeated the
  string/notation-based clustering: (1) the competing minimal-kernel-size figures
  (14/B0043, 13-or-8/B0057, 49-incompatible-enumerations/B0062); (2) the fragmented
  Zero/Śūnya naming family (36 labels, ~10 disconnected sub-groups); (3) the
  v1.0→v1.2 impossibility-theorem lineage (only weakly traced via CO-OCCURRENCE).
- **Phase 2b (object family write-ups, ~2,497 per-label `.md` files) is explicitly
  NOT started this session.** Its scale is comparable to the entire 70-batch Phase 1
  effort. Recorded as a checkpoint in `.claude/CONTEXT.md` requiring an explicit
  scope/approach decision (exhaustive vs. prioritized vs. batched-like-P1) before
  launching, rather than assuming "continue" extends automatically to a task this
  large without being shown the shape of it first.
