Elevating them would create **second homes for frozen rules** — violating the project's own *"the ubiquitous language lives once."* Their correct relationship to the contract is **pointer, not policy**.

- **Exactly ONE clause earns elevation: R-2** (no consumer imports another context's **Infrastructure**, including its hydrators). Deptrac enforces it; **no ADR states it** — ADR-T16 speaks only of Domain/aggregate types. And WP-4 came within one design decision of violating it.
- **Two clauses deliberately held BACK from elevation:** **R-3** (hydrator is producer-side) is **contingent on the current relay design** — elevating it would freeze today's mechanism as law; **R-5** ("primitives only") would **over-constrain**: its strategic content is R-1 restated, and a literal reading makes legitimate structured payloads (Evidence metadata; ADR-T22's array) violations.

- **Strategic weakness recorded, not elevated:** the contract says how a crossing *works*, never how one is *retired* — v1-retirement in WP-1 was a slice decision under ADR-T5, not a stated rule. Deprecation is real future work.
- **Proposed ARB decision (7 items):** elevate R-2 only, ideally as a **succession-style annotation to ADR-T16** extending its scope from Domain types to any cross-context import (so the rule still lives once) · convert six clauses to pointers · mark R-3/R-5 tactical with reasons · add the scope statement + note Governance's open integration-style question · record retirement + the optional fitness test without ruling · **WP-4 may proceed.**
