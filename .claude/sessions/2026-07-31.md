- **Published-language review (unmodified):** no producer leakage · no consumer information embedded · still minimal (three facts, pinned by test) · context-neutral. **Observation, not a defect:** no current consumer needs `routedTo` — it is **producer-justified, not consumer-driven**, which is the correct reason for a published fact to exist.
- **Consumer responsibility map produced** (ownership, not classes): 8 of 10 responsibilities already exist — PM · local `ChallengeRef` · store · **both** duplicate-handling seats (inbox dedupe **and** PM idempotency) · `fromConsumed` · transport/ordering/audit continuity. Two are WP-4's.
- **THE PREREQUISITE (G-2) — the one finding that is not merely unwritten code:** PB-005's **F-1 ruling** binds — *the Domain never names messaging outcomes*; business conditions translate to inbox markers at one Application boundary. Contestation satisfies it (`ChallengeReactionOutcomeTranslator` + markers). **Adjudication has no equivalent**, and its PM entry points are `void` idempotent no-ops returning nothing a translator could read. WP-4 must decide that seam **before** writing the handler — because a PM taught messaging vocabulary cannot be un-taught without rework. No new ADR, no redesign: the rule and the precedent both exist.

**Contract:** `docs/architecture/Cross_Context_Integration_Contract.md` — marked **DESCRIPTIVE** (derived from implementation, not invented); elevating any clause to normative status is a separate decision it does not take.

- **Phase 2 — nine invariants discovered with evidence (R-1..R-9)**, incl. the two that matter most: **R-1** no consumer imports another context's `\Domain` (exhaustive 4×3 context-pair scan: **zero**) and **R-2** no consumer imports another context's **Infrastructure either** — `grep "Hydrator"` in consumer Application layers: **zero**. **R-3:** the hydrator is **producer-side** — `OutboxEventProcessor:141` is its *only* production caller. R-1 is **machine-enforced**: Deptrac treats cross-context as violation-by-omission, fail mode, 0 violations. The contract is already executable architecture.
- **Phase 3 — boundary object lifecycle** drawn end-to-end, showing precisely where each representation lives and that **only the payload + `InboxMessage` cross**.
- **Phase 4 — dependency verification: ZERO violations, zero architectural debt** across all three chains.
- **Phase 5 — THE AUDIT'S MOST VALUABLE CATCH.** The proposed contract answer *"Who reconstructs? Consumer (hydrator → local VOs)"* is **wrong on the mechanism**. Consumers reconstruct **directly from payload primitives** (`DeterminationId::fromString($this->stringField($message->payload, …))`) and **never touch a hydrator**. Had WP-4 followed the proposed answer, Adjudication would have imported `Contestation\Infrastructure\Outbox\ChallengeRoutedHydrator` — the codebase's **first cross-context Infrastructure dependency**, failing Deptrac. The audit prevented exactly the class of error it exists to prevent.

- **Phase 6 — the contract**, answering all eight governing questions; the sharpest clause: *what may never cross* now explicitly includes **the producer's Infrastructure, including hydrators**.
- **Consequence: WP-4 introduces no new architectural concept** — one `InboxHandler` + its registration, the fourth instance of an established, machine-enforced pattern.
- **One observation recorded, not recommended:** a dedicated fitness test for R-2/R-3 would make the *reason* legible, though Deptrac already covers the risk.

## Completed — Cross-Context Integration Contract STRATEGIC VALIDATION (recommendation only, no code)

**Report:** `engineering/verification/reports/2026-07-31-cross-context-contract-strategic-validation.md`. Verdict: **PARTIALLY NORMATIVE — with an unexpected partition.**

- **THE FINDING THAT REFRAMED THE COMMISSION: most of R-1..R-9 are ALREADY normative.** R-1/R-4/R-9 = **ADR-T16** · R-6 = **ADR-MP-06** + PB-006's closure ruling · R-7 = PB-006's permanent *Registration ≠ Delivery* principle · R-8 = **ADR-T3/T4**. Elevating them would create **second homes for frozen rules** — violating the project's own *"the ubiquitous language lives once."* Their correct relationship to the contract is **pointer, not policy**.
- **R-1's strategic grounding is far deeper than convention.** The ratified baseline states *"Zero Partnership/Shared Kernel anywhere is not a stylistic preference — it is a consequence of the domain's accountability requirement… constitutional trust requires every trust-bearing crossing to be attributable: who owed what to whom. Jointly-owned models blur precisely that attribution."* So R-1 follows from **constitutional attributability**, not from the current code — and Shared Kernel is not a counter-example but a **pre-agreed ARB gate** with a recorded falsifiability condition.
- **Exactly ONE clause earns elevation: R-2** (no consumer imports another context's **Infrastructure**, including its hydrators). Deptrac enforces it; **no ADR states it** — ADR-T16 speaks only of Domain/aggregate types. And WP-4 came within one design decision of violating it.
- **Two clauses deliberately held BACK from elevation:** **R-3** (hydrator is producer-side) is **contingent on the current relay design** — elevating it would freeze today's mechanism as law; **R-5** ("primitives only") would **over-constrain**: its strategic content is R-1 restated, and a literal reading makes legitimate structured payloads (Evidence metadata; ADR-T22's array) violations.
- **Counter-example analysis found exactly one genuine exception, and it lands on a clause already classified tactical** (R-5, external/third-party payload formats). ACL, Customer–Supplier, legacy strangler, long-running workflows, multi-producer, and version evolution all conform unchanged.
- **SECOND MATERIAL FINDING — the contract's SCOPE must be stated.** Applying it to future contexts: Evidence ✔ (R-5 strained) · Voting ✔ (anonymity reinforces it) · Appointment ✔ · **Governance ✘ — a scope limitation, not a violation.** Q-1 rules Adjudication consumes Governance's authority decision *"through a published contract"* whose **shape is explicitly undecided** and may be **request/response**. Every rule in the contract presumes an `InboxMessage`. **The contract governs event-carried asynchronous integration only** — left implicit, a future engineer would either force Governance into an event shape or read the contract as violated.
- **Strategic weakness recorded, not elevated:** the contract says how a crossing *works*, never how one is *retired* — v1-retirement in WP-1 was a slice decision under ADR-T5, not a stated rule. Deprecation is real future work.
- **Proposed ARB decision (7 items):** elevate R-2 only, ideally as a **succession-style annotation to ADR-T16** extending its scope from Domain types to any cross-context import (so the rule still lives once) · convert six clauses to pointers · mark R-3/R-5 tactical with reasons · add the scope statement + note Governance's open integration-style question · record retirement + the optional fitness test without ruling · **WP-4 may proceed.**
