## 1 · What is already settled for EVERY correction *(DERIVED — no ruling needed)*

* a correction to a published schedule **creates a new opportunity** for the affected phase; the previous is **superseded and permanently recorded** (`EM-VOC-005`);
* **no authorization, refusal or eligibility decision carries across** (`EM-VOT-005`);
* a correction **never itself advances the election** and **never bypasses a mandatory condition** (`EM-GOV-004`);
* it is **attributable, reasoned, auditable** (`EM-GOV-004`) and **recorded with the original preserved** (`EM-GOV-005`, `D-2`).

> **Consequence worth stating before the boundaries are debated: circumvention is ALREADY closed.** Every correction forces a **new** progression decision, so *"move the date and proceed on the old authorization"* is impossible today. **The boundary questions below are therefore about LEGITIMACY AND RELIANCE — what participants were promised — not about closing a loophole.**

## 2 · The boundaries

### B-1 · How far may a window move?
**OPEN.** Nothing in the record derives a limit. **Since circumvention is already closed (§1), the question is what participants may be expected to absorb** — a two-hour shift and a three-month shift are not the same promise broken.

### B-2 · May a window be extended?
**OPEN — and it must be decided together with `EM-OPEN-027`.** An "end tolerance" and an "extension" are the same act under two names. **For a voting window, extension means votes may be cast that the published window did not allow.**

### B-3 · May a window be shortened? *(new in this commission)*
**OPEN.** ⚠️ **Governance observes it is not the mirror of extension but the harsher case: shortening a window that is currently open REMOVES time that participants were promised and may have relied on.** Extension enlarges an opportunity; **shortening can disenfranchise someone who planned to act on the final day.** **Recorded as an observation, not a recommendation.**

### B-4 · May a window be moved backward?
**OPEN, with one candidate derivation offered for confirmation or rejection.** **A corrected schedule whose start precedes the moment of its own correction would be a commitment made after the time it commits to.** That sits badly with the determinate, non-retroactive requirement placed on publication (`EM-OPEN-023` constraint **C-2**) and with `EM-GOV-005`. **Governance does not rule it, because C-2 was framed for publication and a correction is not obviously the same act.**

### B-5 · What happens to dependent future phases?
**Substantially answered by the pending `EM-GOV-013`:** *reviewed and rescheduled **where their validity depends on the changed predecessor schedule***. ⚠️ **Conditional on adoption** — `EM-GOV-013` is pending **and** blocked on these very boundaries. **It is the one circular dependency in the set: B-5's answer sits inside the rule the boundaries unblock. Adopting `EM-GOV-013`'s dependent-phase clause independently would break the circle.**

### B-6 · May a completed phase's schedule be changed?
**OPEN.** A legitimately completed phase's window is **a recorded historical fact**, and `EM-GOV-005`/`D-2` forbid a later fact silently altering an earlier one. **Governance believes this likely settles it but registers it rather than deriving it**, because no ruling has addressed a *schedule change* to a completed phase.

### B-7 · May a schedule be corrected once votes have been cast?
**OPEN — the strongest case.** **Votes are irreversible facts, and under `EM-VOC-004` "voting started" is an outcome the opportunity has already reached.** Correcting the window of an opportunity that has produced votes changes the terms under which those votes were cast. ⚠️ **Sub-question that must not be lost: does it matter WHICH direction?** *(extending a voting window after some have voted · shortening it · moving it entirely)* — **three quite different acts.**

### B-8 · What happens to voting credentials already issued? *(carried forward — absent from the commission's list)*
**OPEN, and untouched since it was recorded.** ⚠️ **Governance flags a dependency: this is the only boundary whose answer may be constrained by rules outside this Manifesto** *(credential and entitlement rules)*. **It should be checked against them before it is ruled, not after.**

## 3 · What each boundary unblocks

**`EM-GOV-013`** needs **B-1…B-5** *(and B-5 circularly — see above)*. **`EM-OPEN-027`** *(end tolerance)* needs **B-2**. **`EM-OPEN-039`** *(cascade)* needs **B-5**. **`EM-OPEN-042`** *(recovery actions)* needs **B-1…B-3** to know what "reschedule" may mean.
