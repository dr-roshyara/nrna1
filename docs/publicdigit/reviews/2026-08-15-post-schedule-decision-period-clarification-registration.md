# Registration — `EM-OPEN-026`(a) Clarified: the "Post-Schedule Decision Period"

**Type:** Governance registration (Session 2) · **Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Basis:** PO/ARB clarification of `EM-OPEN-026`(a)

## 1 · Naming discipline adopted

> *"Do not call the post-start period 'tolerance' until its semantics are decided. Call it a **post-schedule decision period** provisionally."*

**Applied.** The proposed rules and open questions are restated in the neutral term. **"Tolerance" is recorded as the term the concept was first framed in, not erased** — the report of 2026-08-15 keeps its wording, and traceability runs through it.

**Governance's assessment of why this mattered, recorded because it is a reusable lesson:** *tolerance* carries **relaxation** in ordinary usage. Under reading B the very same period is a **restriction** — it is when the opportunity **dies**. **A name that answers half the question makes the other half hard to see.** The two-meanings finding was hard to reach precisely because the word had already picked a side.

## 2 · The gate, restated as the PO framed it

| | Reading | Tolerance-report equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Decision interval** — the Chief **may still submit** a request during a predefined period; evaluated against all mandatory rules; **the period authorizes nothing** | the **"grant"** reading |
| **B** | **Expiry interval** — the opportunity remains **unresolved** for a predefined period which does **not necessarily** grant a further right to request; at its end, a **governed terminal outcome** | the **"bound"** reading |
| **C** | **Differentiated** — say explicitly when such a period permits a further request and when it merely sets an expiry boundary | **separates two concepts the single word merged** |

**PO leaning registered as a leaning, not a decision: C.** Governance records the PO's stated reason — it avoids pretending *"the Chief may still request"* and *"the opportunity expires here"* are one concept.

**Governance adds one consequence of choosing B, which the framing makes sharper than before:** **if the period grants no additional right to request, where does the right to request after the published start come from?** The published window? The published instant only? **No adopted rule says.** Under A the period supplies the right; under B it does not, and the baseline must be stated. **B is not incomplete — it simply forces the prior question into the open.**

>
> *"Any post-schedule period must be explicitly governed; no technical grace period constitutes one"* is **`EM-GOV-006` — PROPOSED by Governance earlier today, and not adopted.** The instruction lists it among principles *"already established"*; **on the record it is not.**

Treating a passing reference as its adoption would be exactly the "observation silently becoming a rule" failure this programme exists to prevent.

## 4 · A question the PO's model raises — `EM-OPEN-031`

The model shows **scheduled start reached → protocol records evaluation**, and the principle *"if 10:00 arrives and a condition fails, the system records that fact."*

**If nothing is triggered, nothing is recorded.** Recording an evaluation at the scheduled time therefore implies **a clock-driven act that writes to the protocol**. **This does not violate `EM-VOT-004`** — it progresses nothing, and *"failure does not silently become success"* is preserved. **But it must be adopted deliberately rather than arrive incidentally.**

**Governance flags its own earlier proposal as the weaker candidate**, rather than defending it: the tolerance report proposed that *"scheduled time reached"* is **not** material. On reflection the distinction is finer — **an evaluation that was *performed* is an act, and is plausibly material even when its answer is *cannot proceed*; a clock merely *passing* a time is not.** The PO's principle points toward recording the evaluation. **Registered as `EM-OPEN-031`; not decided.**
