> ## **`SCB-9` is much smaller than it looks. One genuinely open question remains, plus one that belongs elsewhere.**

## 2 · The one open question, opened up

**Participants were promised 10.09–12.09. The window passed. What may now be promised instead, and what may not?** Three parts, none answered by any adopted rule:

**(a) Must a corrected elapsed window leave participants enough time to act?** A window elapsed on the 12th, corrected on the 13th to run *on the 13th*, is technically a valid new opportunity — **and gives an electorate no notice at all.** **Nothing in the record requires notice.** *(`EM-GOV-007` requires the schedule to be **visible**; visibility is not notice, and `EM-OPEN-036` — informed vs visible — is still open.)*

**(b) Is there a limit on how long AFTER elapse a correction may be made?** A window that elapsed **six months ago** and a window that elapsed **yesterday** are the same act under the current rules. **Governance observes only that `EM-GOV-014`'s pending recovery period would bound this in the halted case — and would not bound it at all where no halt was recorded.**

**(c) ⚠️ Does the REASON the window elapsed matter?** Two situations are currently indistinguishable in the rules and quite different in business terms:
* the window elapsed **because progression was halted** — a recorded evaluation, a recorded failure, a recorded reason (`EM-GOV-011`);
* the window elapsed **because nobody acted** — no evaluation, no record, nothing to point at.

**The first is a governed situation with a documented cause. The second is an omission.** **Nothing currently requires them to be treated differently — and nothing forbids it.** ⚠️ **Note the connection: `EM-OPEN-031` asks whether anything evaluates and records at a scheduled boundary with no request. If the answer is no, the second situation leaves NO protocol trace at all, and a correction would then be repairing something the record never witnessed.**

## 3 · What is binding on any answer *(DERIVED)*

* the corrected schedule **is a new opportunity** requiring a **new progression decision** — so **circumvention is already closed** (`EM-VOC-005`, `EM-VOT-005`);
* the original elapsed window **remains permanently recorded and is never re-pointed** (`EM-GOV-005`, `D-2`);
* the correction is **attributable and reasoned** (`EM-GOV-004`);
* it **never itself advances the election** (`EM-GOV-004`).

**So the answer to `SCB-9` cannot make an elapsed correction dangerous in the circumvention sense. It can only make it unfair** — which is why (a)–(c) are about **notice, staleness and cause**, not about authority.

## 4 · Governance's position

**None on the substance.** Governance narrowed `SCB-9` to what is genuinely undecided, returned sub-question 3 to `EM-OPEN-035` and sub-question 6 to `SCB-5` rather than answering either in the wrong place, and surfaced (c) — **the reason the window elapsed** — which none of the supplied lists contained.
