## 2 · The three questions

### Is *"intra-class call"* now semantically unambiguous?

**Partially, and the split matters.** For every **statically resolvable** form — `$this->m()`, `self::m()`, `static::m()`, `OwnClass::m()` as written, `parent::m()` excluded as out-of-frame, and the fully-qualified own-class spelling (`\Fq::b()` vs `\App\Fq::b()`, formerly `NEW-5`) — the answer is **yes**. It was settled by `D-1` (PO/ARB, 2026-08-16, applied, independently verified `PASS`) and, for the fully-qualified case specifically, by **Decision 13.3**, accepted into the Track-1 architecture and confirmed fixed by Track-1's independent verification.

For **dynamic own-behaviour member access** (`$this->$m()`, `call_user_func([$this,'m'])`, `$this->$p` — `V-3`) the answer is **no**. The pinned decision requires these be recorded as excluded (*"SEEN-AND-EXCLUDED"*); the accepted binding emits no fact for them at all (*"UNSEEN"*), and this is a decided contract requirement not yet met by any accepted architecture. Two competing proposals exist; neither is accepted, and the record says so explicitly rather than leaving it to be inferred.

### Is Stage-2 Python **blocked, authorized-but-not-done, done, or verified?**

**Done and verified — FAIL — for the original, regex-based differential experiment**, and now **superseded as a method** rather than merely paused. The Python collector was implemented from the contract, compared against 10/10 fixtures (twice, independently), and independently verified with a `FAIL — implementation defect` verdict: three confirmed divergences (heredoc, nowdoc, same-line attribute), all Python-side, reproduced from an independent probe. Breadth verification then found nine more divergences in the same collector, none corrected on record. Separately, and dated *before* the current commission (2026-08-18), the PO/ARB decided to proceed with **PHP as the sole implementation language for the current implementation**, placing Python and any other second binding **explicitly out of current implementation scope** and changing the *method* by which neutrality will be evidenced — from empirical cross-language comparison to an architectural claim resting on the L3–L5 stratification and declared expected evidence. **Stage-2 Python is therefore not merely unrepaired; pursuing its repair is not the currently authorized path at all**, absent a further governance act reopening language scope.

### Does anything on record already constitute a **neutrality verdict**, in whole or in part?

**In part, and only as a historical, scoped finding — not as a verdict on the work item as it stands today.** The original empirical experiment reached a scoped negative result for the specific regex-based PHP/Python pair (`FAIL`, later broadened from 3 to 12 confirmed divergences). That verdict is about *that pair*, not about the work item's current architecture. The current (Track-1) architecture has never been cross-language tested — no second binding exists, and Python is out of scope — so no empirical neutrality claim can be made about it. Its own independent verification confirms the architecture does not *semantically* depend on PHP (Gate 3 re-derived by execution in a PHP-absent runtime) but explicitly declines to call this conformance: *"Acceptance does NOT mean conformance has been established."* **Overall, for the work item as it stands: `NOT YET DETERMINABLE`** — and part of why it is not yet determinable is that what "neutral" is being asked to mean has itself changed (architectural claim vs. empirical comparison) without that change being reconciled against the commission's own still-listed deliverables (§3, Deliverable E).
