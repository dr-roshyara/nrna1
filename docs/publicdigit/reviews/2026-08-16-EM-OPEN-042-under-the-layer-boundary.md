## 0 · ⚠️ A refinement to my own earlier statement — because it changes what you decide next

**I reported that *"the inaction cell's missing terminal path is not a gap in `042` — it is `EM-OPEN-049`."* **That is right about the PROPORTIONATE remedy and wrong if read as *"`042` cannot answer Cell ② at all."***

> **Ending an ELECTION is not an officer sanction. It is an election-level consequence, squarely inside Election Governance's own domain.**
> **So the boundary does NOT forbid terminating a stalled election. What it forbids is the officer-directed remedy.**
>
> ## **THE CONSEQUENCE: `042` CAN ANSWER CELL ② — IT SIMPLY CANNOT ANSWER IT PROPORTIONATELY.**

## 1 · What Cell ② actually reduces to, once officer accountability is out of reach

**Halt is `C-1` (recovery available) · the responsible actor does not act · Election Governance may not touch the officer. Three answers are available, and ALL THREE stay inside the boundary:**

| | Answer | Cost, stated honestly |
|---|---|---|
| **(i)** | **The election remains halted indefinitely** | ⚠️ **Indefinite elections exist. Candidates and voters are left permanently unresolved, and the record is carried forever.** *"No indefinite elections"* — the stated advantage of Option A — is given up. |
| **(ii)** | **The election reaches a terminal outcome on expiry, regardless of whether recovery remains available** | ⚠️ **It terminates, and it does NOT discipline anyone — but the cost falls on candidates and voters rather than on the officer whose omission caused it.** |
| **(iii)** | **The election remains halted, and the stall is RECORDED and NOTIFIED to the organisation** | ⚠️ **Informing is not empowering: it confers no authority. If the organisation has no competent authority (`049` unresolved), notification changes nothing operationally — it only makes the stall visible.** **Compatible with both (i) and (ii).** |

> ## **SO THE REAL DECISION IN CELL ② IS A CHOICE BETWEEN TWO COSTS: AN ELECTION THAT NEVER ENDS, OR AN ELECTION ENDED BY SOMEONE ELSE'S OMISSION.**
> **Both are legitimate business answers. Neither requires inventing any authority.** **Governance expresses no preference — but records that the choice should be made KNOWINGLY, because the currently recommended rule makes it BY DEFAULT: it selects (i) silently, simply by never firing.**

## 2 · The condition axis is unaffected and remains answerable now

**The recommended rule — *cancel only where no further governed recovery action is available* — stands correct for `C-2`/`C-3`, with the period understood as a CONVERSION WINDOW rather than a deadline.** **Nothing in the layer boundary touches it.**

## 3 · What `EM-OPEN-042` must NOT do — restated as a constraint on drafting

⛔ **No removal trigger.** ⛔ **No escalation authority.** ⛔ **No duty imposed on any organisational body.** ⛔ **No inference that the Deputy assumes the Chief's recovery responsibility** — `EM-GOV-014` Part 1 assigns it to the Chief specifically, and `EM-GOV-008`'s Chief-or-Deputy publication authority is **operational only**.
⛔ **And no election-level cancellation wording until `EM-OPEN-110` settles the level ambiguity in *cancelled*.**

## 4 · What Governance recommends — structure only

1. **Answer the CONDITION axis now** — it is ready and unblocked.
2. **Make the Cell ② choice EXPLICITLY** — (i), (ii), or either plus (iii) — **rather than inheriting (i) as a side effect of a rule that never fires.**
3. **Record that the proportionate remedy is unavailable and why** — so a future `049` answer can supply it without re-opening `042`'s election-level rule.
