# `EM-OPEN-023` — Working Paper for the Business Discussion

**Type:** Governance working paper (Session 2) · **Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Purpose:** hold the `EM-OPEN-023` discussion in plain business language
**⛔ No recommendation. No option ranked. No architecture, no code, no lifecycle, no technical vocabulary.** Deliberately contains **no** reference to databases, fields, jobs, states or existing behaviour — the commission's own instruction, and the restraint the PO named as the strongest result of the earlier report.

## The question

> **What does it mean, from the election's business perspective, for a schedule to become officially published?**

## The ordinary-language anchor

In elections generally, *publication* means **the electorate has been told.** That is the meaning the word carries outside our system, and it is worth deciding **deliberately** whether it is our meaning too — rather than adopting or discarding it by accident. **If publication is an internal act of finalisation, we are using an ordinary word in an unusual way, and should say so explicitly.**

## Six questions that make the answer concrete

**These are not options. They are the questions whose answers, together, constitute the ruling.**

**Q1 · Is publication about telling people, or about deciding internally?** An announcement to the electorate, or an act of finalisation that may or may not be announced?

**Q2 · Published to whom?** *(Governance believes this is the least-examined question.)* Candidates typically learn a voting schedule long before voters do. Is a schedule published when **candidates** know it · when **voters** know it · when the **organisation** knows it · when it is **publicly** available? **These can be four different moments, potentially weeks apart, and each yields a different answer to "when did the voting opportunity begin?"**

**Q3 · Who publishes, and is it a deliberate act?** A named actor performing a recognisable act — or a consequence of something else being completed?

**Q4 · Must anything be true first?** May a schedule be published before the election is approved · before candidates are known · before voters are admitted? **If nothing is required, a schedule could be published for an election that cannot possibly run.**

**Q5 · Is publication a one-time fact or a status?** The domain already holds a PO ruling for **results**: *publication is an immutable fact; visibility is a separate control — hide/show, never "unpublish."* **Does a schedule work the same way?** *(Offered as an existing precedent to accept or reject, not as an answer.)*

**Q6 · Does publication oblige anything toward the electorate?** If a published schedule is later corrected, must those who were told be told again? **This is a duty question, not a mechanism question.**

## Six scenarios that force the answer into the open

**Each is a case where different answers to Q1–Q6 produce visibly different outcomes.**

1. **The silent change.** An organiser records 10:00–12:00 on 1 August and tells nobody. On 3 August they change it to 11:00–13:00. **Preparation, or a correction that supersedes an opportunity?**
2. **The gap.** The schedule is finalised internally on 1 August; voters are notified on 5 August. **Between the 1st and the 5th, is it published?**
3. **The asymmetric audience.** Candidates are told at nomination; voters are told a week later. The schedule changes in between. **Whose knowledge made it published?**
4. **The unannounced election.** A schedule is recorded and never announced. The scheduled time arrives. **Is there a voting opportunity at all — and can anything legitimately begin?**
5. **The re-announcement.** A published schedule is corrected. **Are those who were told entitled to be told again — and is an election legitimate if they are not?**
6. **The premature schedule.** A schedule is published for an election that is later not approved. **What was published, and what becomes of it?**

## What hangs on the answer *(already established — nothing new here)*

* **When a voting opportunity begins** — Governance has already established that this and publication are **one decision, not two**.
* **Which changes are corrections.** `EM-VOC-005`: a correction to a published schedule creates a **new** opportunity, superseded predecessor permanently recorded. **`D-1`: a change before publication is not a correction at all.** The publication moment is the line between routine preparation and supersession.
* **When the protocol history of an opportunity must begin** (`EM-GOV-005`).
* **When a post-schedule decision period could become binding** — `EM-OPEN-026`(d) is explicitly waiting on this.

## Constraints any answer must satisfy *(derived; they exclude no option)*

* **C-1** the moment must be **recordable as a material event**;
* **C-2** it must be **determinate and non-retroactive** — answerable at any time without knowing what happens later;
* **C-3** it must create **no significance threshold** — no "this edit doesn't really count", expressly rejected by the `EM-OPEN-022` ruling.

## Governance's position

**None on the substance.** Governance holds no preferred answer to Q1–Q6 and will not construct one. Its role here is to keep the question whole, to record the ruling exactly as given, and to say plainly if an answer leaves one of C-1…C-3 unsatisfied.
