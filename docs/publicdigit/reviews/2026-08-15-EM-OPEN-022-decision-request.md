# Decision Request — Does correcting a schedule create a new voting opportunity, or change the existing one?

**Prepared by Governance (Session 2) · 2026-08-15 · Business-language format · One decision · `EM-OPEN-022`**

**⛔ Decision request only. No implementation. No architecture. The interim safeguard below is already in force.**

## Purpose

Two rules you have adopted meet at a point neither one answers. This asks you to close it.

## Why it matters

You gave the Chief Election Officer the power to **correct a schedule** — including a window whose time has passed — because an election must be able to continue legitimately when the timing was wrong. You also ruled that **authorization belongs to one voting opportunity and never carries to another.**

So: when the officer corrects the schedule, **has a new opportunity come into being, or is it the same one with different times?**

Everything about what may be reused turns on that single word. A prior refusal, a prior authorization, a prior judgement that the conditions were met — do any of them still apply after the correction, or does the election start that decision afresh?

## What is already protected while you decide

**In force now, by your own instruction:** *no implementation may allow a previous authorization, refusal, or eligibility decision to become valid for a changed schedule unless Governance explicitly establishes that rule.* **So until you rule, a changed schedule carries nothing forward.** Nothing is at risk while this stays open — the safe reading is already the operating one.

## The two readings

### Reading A — a correction creates a **new** opportunity

The old opportunity ends (superseded); a new one begins with the corrected times.

- The officer **must decide again**. A previous refusal cannot be walked around by adjusting the calendar.
- Every correction produces a clean, separately recorded opportunity — which the protocol already requires to be distinguishable.
- **Cost:** more decisions. Even a small, obviously-benign correction — fixing a typo in the start time before anything has begun — would require a fresh authorization.

### Reading B — a correction **modifies** the existing opportunity

The opportunity persists; only its times change.

- Fewer repeated decisions; the officer's earlier work is not discarded for a trivial fix.
- **Cost, and it is the serious one:** unless explicitly fenced, an officer refused today could adjust the schedule and rely on an earlier decision — **reaching by two permitted steps what neither step permits.** Reading B would therefore need its own safeguard spelling out exactly which prior decisions survive a change and which do not.

## A third possibility, offered honestly

**The answer may not need to be uniform.** These are not obviously the same act:

- correcting a window that **has not yet opened** and about which no decision has been made;
- extending or shortening a window that is **currently open**;
- reviving a window that has **already expired unused**.

You may reasonably rule that some of these preserve the opportunity while others create a new one. **Governance raises this only so a uniform answer is chosen deliberately rather than by default** — it does not propose which lines to draw.

## Governance assessment *(clearly labelled — not a decision)*

The anti-circumvention clause you adopted — *the Chief must not bypass a refusal through administrative authority **or schedule manipulation*** — **points toward Reading A**, because Reading B is only safe if it is given precisely the fence that Reading A gets for free. **You may still prefer B with an explicit safeguard; that is a legitimate choice, and it must be explicit rather than assumed.**

## What this decision does NOT decide

The **expiry boundary** (when an unused opportunity ends) · **cancellation authority** · the **detailed extent** of rescheduling powers · **credential consequences** of a schedule change · whether correction is permitted **after votes are cast** · any **technical representation**. All remain separate Governance questions, unchanged.
