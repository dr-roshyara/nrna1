# 1 · One decision is actually open

## Should we keep or drop a small inconsistency we found in our own records?

**1 · What is the situation?**
We have a working rule that the *governance* role does not get its own tracked work-session — governance activity is recorded as an attribute of other entries instead. While checking something unrelated, we found **one old case, from 2026-08-14, where a governance work-session was created anyway.** It is inactive and belongs to a piece of work that has already moved on.

**2 · Why does it matter to the organization?**
Our records currently describe governance activity **in two different ways**. Nothing is broken, and no decision was affected. But if we are ever asked *"who performed this governance step, and when?"*, an inconsistent record makes that harder to answer confidently — and quietly weakens trust in the record generally.

**3 · What is affected?**
Auditability, and the reliability of our own bookkeeping. **No product behaviour, no election, no customer-facing capability is affected.** The practical impact today is close to zero.

**4 · What decision is required from you?**
Whether this observation should stay on the open-findings list, or be struck from it as out of scope. **You noted last time that this call is yours; it has not yet been made.**

**5 · What are the realistic options?**

| | Option | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Keep it registered, commission nothing** | It stays visible on the list as a known inconsistency. No work is scheduled. Costs nothing |
| **B** | **Strike it** | We accept it as not worth tracking and remove it. The observation is lost |
| **C** | **Commission a small piece of work** | Someone looks at whether the two ways of recording governance activity should be reconciled |

**6 · What does Governance recommend, and why?**
**Option A — keep it registered, commission nothing.**
It is a single historical case on inactive work, so there is nothing urgent to repair. Keeping it costs nothing. And it is genuinely useful evidence if you ever take up the broader question of *how we identify who did what* — **striking it would throw away a real data point to tidy a list.** Option C is disproportionate to one historical entry.

**7 · What happens if we do nothing?**
The inconsistency stays in the records, visible and labelled. Nothing degrades. A future reader may briefly wonder why one entry looks different from the rest — which is precisely what the register exists to answer.

**8 · What would change operationally?**
**Option A: nothing changes.** Option B: one line disappears from the list. Option C: someone would need to be commissioned, which is not currently justified.
