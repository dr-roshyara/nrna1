## 2 · ⚠️ One constraint binds every possible answer

> ## **The Election Committee must not be appointable by the Election Chief.**

**This is DERIVED from the PO's own ruling-out of Chief + Deputy.** That reading was rejected because Chief and Deputy are **operational partners** in the election they would then be accepting. **If the Chief appoints the Committee instead, Model A reproduces exactly the defect that ruled out Chief + Deputy** — the process is certified by people the conducting authority chose. **The dependency moves one step and survives.**

**Governance states this as a constraint, not a recommendation: any answer that leaves appointment with the Chief makes Model A self-certification by another route.**

## 3 · ⚠️ The appointing authority may itself be undefined

`EM-GOV-026` has the Committee constituted during Election Appointment; **`EM-GOV-027` establishes that the applicant does not become Chief** — but **the record does not define which organisational authority performs appointments.**

**This runs into frozen ground:** organisation-side governance is out of scope or **FROZEN** (`EM-OPEN-014`/`015`/`016`, Full Membership) — **the same wall `EM-OPEN-049` hit when organisational replacement of the Chief was proposed and then struck from the act.**

⛔ **So the question is not only *who appoints the Committee* but **whether the appointing authority exists in this record at all**.** *(Recorded, not resolved — Governance will not define an organisational body.)*

## 4 · ⚠️ A conflict-of-interest question nobody has asked

**Can one person hold two acceptance roles at once?**

**In Model C the Committee and the candidate representatives both participate.** **Nothing prevents the same person being a Committee member AND a candidate's chosen representative** — in which case **that person counts twice in a combined decision**, once institutionally and once by representation weight.

**Related and equally unaddressed:** may a Committee member be **a candidate** · **a voter in the election** · **the Deputy** · a person with a stated interest in the outcome? **`EM-OPEN-061` covers representative eligibility and nothing covers Committee eligibility — yet in Model C the two eligibility rules must interlock, or double-counting is possible by construction.**

## 5 · The decision — genuinely open

**(a)** who appoints the members *(subject to §2 and §3)* · **(b)** how many · **(c)** eligibility · **(d)** how independence from the Chief is **secured**, not merely asserted · **(e)** who may nominate · **(f)** when membership becomes binding *(candidate answer: on legitimate completion of Election Appointment — offered, not adopted)* · **(g)** what authority the Committee holds at the gates, **beyond casting an acceptance position** · **(h)** who may replace a member, and on what grounds.

⚠️ **On (g), one boundary is already fixed:** `EM-GOV-022` — **no individual participant may prevent progression**; the aggregate outcome is determined by the predetermined threshold. **Whatever authority the Committee is given, it cannot include an individual veto.**
