In our PublicDigit Membership architecture, Annul is a correction operation that allows the system to declare that a previously recorded Membership fact should no longer be considered live. It is not the same as deleting a database record, ending a membership, or removing a member.

The distinction matters because Membership is designed around an append-only history of facts: we correct the interpretation of history rather than silently erase it.

## 1. What is Annul?

Imagine a person is admitted as a member, and later the system discovers that the admission decision was recorded incorrectly.

1\. Admit

The system records an admission fact for a person in an organisation.

2\. Annul

An authorised correction records that the admission fact is no longer live. The original fact remains in the history.

3\. Resolve Membership state

The system evaluates the remaining live facts and applicable invariants to determine the resulting Membership state. It must not invent implicit consequences.

For example, suppose a person was admitted on 1 March, but the admission was recorded by mistake. Annul allows the system to correct the fact's status without pretending that the original record never existed.

## 2. Annul versus Void versus End

These are three different domain operations.

| Operation | Meaning                                                                                | Example                                                       |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `End`     | Records the end of a Membership period.                                                | A member leaves the organisation on 30 September.             |
| `Annul`   | Corrects the liveness of a targeted fact, subject to the applicable rules.             | An erroneous admission decision must no longer count as live. |
| `Void`    | Also changes the liveness of a targeted fact, under the approved correction semantics. | A previously recorded fact is declared no longer live.        |

Annul and Void are separate operations in the correction model, even though both change fact liveness. Their precise differences must follow the approved contract; they should not be assigned additional business meanings merely because their names sound different.

Neither Annul nor Void automatically creates a Membership-period end or another correction fact. That is the important architectural decision confirmed by D-COR-5 Option A.

## 3. What new capability does Membership gain?

Reliable correction of historical decisions

Membership can represent that a previously recorded fact is no longer valid for current resolution without physically deleting the historical record.

Historical reconstruction

With the appropriate recorded timestamps and resolution rules, the system can distinguish what was known at an earlier point from what is known now. This supports audit and investigation.

Correct handling of re-entry

A person can have a later Membership period without erasing an earlier one. Corrections must respect the separate periods and their invariants.

Explicit refusals instead of silent corruption

The correction operation can reject invalid requests with defined outcomes, including `TargetNotFound`, `TargetNotLive`, and `DecidedAtRequired` where applicable.

## 4. A more difficult example: re-entry

Suppose a member has two separate periods:

- Period 1: January–June.
- Period 2: October onwards, after re-entry.

If someone annuls an `End` fact from the first period, the system must not automatically terminate, merge, or otherwise rewrite the second period. Under the approved D-COR-5 rule, Annul changes the target fact's liveness only. The resulting state must still satisfy the Membership invariants.

This is precisely why correction semantics need explicit tests: a seemingly simple correction can otherwise have unintended consequences for later history.

## 5. What does C-1 deliver—and what does it not?

The current C-1 slice, `PBDIGIT-268`, is intended to implement Annul and Void in the existing Membership kernel, using a test-first approach.

| C-1 delivers                                               | C-1 does not deliver                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Defined Annul/Void behaviour and refusal cases             | The complete correction framework                   |
| Preservation of the append-only fact history               | Physical deletion of facts                          |
| Tests for valid transitions and invariant preservation     | Automatic generation of correction consequences     |
| Behaviour consistent with the approved correction contract | Resolution of the open CQ-9 legal/business question |
| A basis for later correction capabilities                  | Production cutover or live-write enablement         |

An important qualification: the implementation has not started according to the latest status you shared. These are the intended capabilities, not yet verified implementation results.

## 6. The larger DDD perspective

Annul is not merely a technical method. It represents a domain capability: correcting the interpretation of a previously recorded Membership decision while preserving the evidence of what was recorded.

That gives Membership a foundation for stronger auditability, historical reconstruction and controlled correction. But it does not itself decide every legal consequence of a correction. Questions such as whether an appeal after re-entry changes a person's legal standing remain separate business/legal decisions.

The next practical milestone is to implement C-1 against the approved contract, demonstrate the behaviour through tests, and only then assess which additional Membership stories are needed for the broader correction lifecycle.
