# Part 5 — Major Architectural Decisions & Decision Log (ADR Summary)

---

# 5.1 Purpose

This section summarizes the major architectural decisions that define the programme.

It is **not** intended to replace individual ADRs.

Instead, it provides future AI assistants with the architectural reasoning necessary to understand:

- why the current architecture exists,
- what alternatives were considered,
- what has already been rejected,
- which decisions are considered stable,
- which areas remain intentionally open.

Every future architectural proposal should first verify whether an equivalent decision has already been made.

---

# 5.2 Architectural Decision Philosophy

The programme follows a strict ADR philosophy.

An ADR exists to:

- record exercised authority,
- explain architectural rationale,
- preserve historical context,
- prevent repeated debate,
- guide future evolution.

ADRs do **not** create architecture.

Architecture exists only after appropriate authority has exercised the decision.

---

# 5.3 Evidence-Based Architecture

One of the programme's strongest architectural decisions is that architecture evolves only from sufficient operational evidence.

Therefore:

- observations remain observations,
- patterns remain candidates,
- engineering standards remain proposals,
- reusable capabilities enter KnowledgeOS only after repeated validation.

This decision influences every subsequent architectural decision.

---

# 5.4 PublicDigit as the Primary Product

Decision

PublicDigit is the primary engineering programme.

KnowledgeOS is **not** developed independently.

PKS is **not** the primary product.

---

### Rationale

Real engineering work provides higher-quality evidence than speculative platform design.

---

### Consequence

Engineering effort prioritizes PublicDigit.

KnowledgeOS grows through extraction.

PKS records engineering experience.

---

# 5.5 KnowledgeOS Evolution Strategy

Decision

KnowledgeOS evolves incrementally through operational evidence.

---

### Rejected Alternative

Design the complete engineering platform before sufficient engineering experience exists.

---

### Reason for Rejection

Premature generalization produces abstractions unsupported by operational evidence.

---

### Consequence

KnowledgeOS capabilities are promoted gradually.

---

# 5.6 PKS as a First-Class Architectural Asset

Decision

Operational engineering knowledge is treated as a permanent architectural asset.

---

### Rationale

Engineering observations contain reusable architectural value.

Without preservation, engineering experience is repeatedly lost.

---

### Consequence

Verification reports,

engineering observations,

implementation lessons,

repository findings,

documentation observations

become structured PKS artifacts.

---

# 5.7 Governance Before Engineering

Decision

Engineering begins only after explicit authorization.

---

### Architectural Principle

Authority

↓

Recording

↓

Engineering

Engineering never begins because implementation appears obvious.

---

### Consequence

Engineering authorization remains explicit throughout the programme.

---

# 5.8 Architecture Does Not Approve Itself

Decision

Architects recommend.

The ARB decides.

Engineering implements.

---

### Rejected Alternative

Architectural reviewers implicitly approving their own recommendations.

---

### Reason

Governance authority and architectural expertise are intentionally separated.

---

# 5.9 Documentation Classification Model

Decision

Documentation identity is established by classification.

Placement is derived from classification.

---

### Governing Invariant

Classification precedes placement.

Placement derives exclusively from classification.

Placement never determines classification.

---

### Consequence

Repository organization remains deterministic.

Documentation movement never changes architectural meaning.

---

# 5.10 Documentation Roots

Decision

Documentation is organized into three canonical domains.

```
docs/publicdigit/

docs/knowledgeos/

docs/pks/
```

---

### Rationale

Business documentation,

engineering methodology,

operational evidence

are separate architectural concerns.

---

### Consequence

Future documentation is classified before placement.

Internal organization remains owned by each domain.

---

# 5.11 Declarative Placement Registry

Decision

Documentation placement is resolved through configuration rather than hard-coded logic.

---

### Rejected Alternative

Embedding placement logic inside scripts.

---

### Consequence

Repository evolution becomes declarative.

Migration history becomes auditable.

---

# 5.12 Migration Registry

Decision

Repository migrations are recorded explicitly.

Migration history becomes engineering knowledge rather than implementation detail.

---

### Consequence

Repository evolution becomes repeatable.

Migration evidence becomes verifiable.

---

# 5.13 Confidence-Based Link Repair

Decision

Broken documentation links are repaired according to explicit confidence levels.

---

### Confidence Levels

- Exact match
- Git rename
- Documented migration
- Unique basename
- Ambiguous
- Missing

---

### Consequence

Deterministic repairs are automated.

Ambiguous repairs return to governance.

---

# 5.14 Repository Governance

Decision

Repository organization is considered architecture.

Repository evolution follows governance rather than convenience.

---

### Consequence

Folder organization,

documentation placement,

migration,

verification

all become governed architectural concerns.

---

# 5.15 Operational Evidence Feedback Loop

Decision

Engineering observations are preserved before becoming methodology.

---

### Lifecycle

Observation

↓

PKS

↓

Candidate Pattern

↓

Engineering Standard Candidate

↓

KnowledgeOS Capability

---

### Consequence

KnowledgeOS grows only through validated engineering experience.

---

# 5.16 Architectural Verification

Decision

Architectural verification is separate from implementation verification.

---

### Architectural Verification

Examples:

- dependency boundaries,
- capability ownership,
- collaborator verification,
- bounded-context integrity,
- ubiquitous language.

---

### Engineering Verification

Examples:

- tests,
- merge gates,
- repository validation,
- documentation validation.

---

### Consequence

Architecture remains independent of implementation mechanics.

---

# 5.17 Slice Governance

Decision

Engineering slices remain intentionally small.

Each slice has:

- objective,
- scope,
- boundary,
- acceptance criteria,
- authorization,
- acceptance ruling.

---

### Rejected Alternative

Large implementation batches.

---

### Consequence

Governance remains incremental.

Rollback remains manageable.

Architectural reasoning remains localized.

---

# 5.18 Evolution Through Refinement

Decision

Engineering refinements remain independent unless governance explicitly merges them.

---

### Example

A refinement discovered during implementation is not automatically absorbed into the current slice.

It receives independent authorization where appropriate.

---

### Consequence

Architectural history remains explicit.

Engineering work remains traceable.

---

# 5.19 Deterministic Engineering

Decision

Deterministic engineering work should be automated.

---

### Examples

- documentation placement,
- repository migration,
- link repair,
- validation,
- documentation integrity.

---

### Consequence

Human governance focuses on semantic decisions.

Automation handles mechanical work.

---

# 5.20 Semantic Ambiguity

Decision

Semantic ambiguity is never resolved automatically.

---

### Examples

- multiple valid document destinations,
- competing architectural interpretations,
- ownership uncertainty,
- stewardship questions.

---

### Consequence

Engineering stops.

Governance decides.

---

# 5.21 Deliberately Rejected Approaches

Throughout the programme several approaches have been consciously rejected.

## Premature Generalization

Rejected because operational evidence did not yet exist.

---

## Architecture by Convenience

Rejected because convenience should never redefine architectural boundaries.

---

## Folder-Based Classification

Rejected because placement cannot define identity.

---

## Implicit Authority

Rejected because architecture requires explicit governance.

---

## Framework-Driven Design

Rejected because business models should drive implementation.

---

## Mixing Business and Engineering Domains

Rejected because PublicDigit,

KnowledgeOS,

and PKS have distinct responsibilities.

---

## Speculative KnowledgeOS Development

Rejected because reusable engineering capabilities must emerge from operational experience.

---

# 5.22 Stable Architectural Decisions

The following decisions are considered stable unless future governance explicitly amends them.

- PublicDigit is the primary product.
- PKS records operational evidence.
- KnowledgeOS evolves from evidence.
- DDD is the primary architectural methodology.
- Governance precedes implementation.
- Architecture does not approve itself.
- Classification precedes placement.
- Repository organization is architectural.
- Operational evidence is a first-class architectural asset.
- Deterministic work should be automated.
- Semantic ambiguity returns to governance.
- Engineering methodology evolves incrementally.

These decisions collectively define the architectural baseline from which future work continues.
