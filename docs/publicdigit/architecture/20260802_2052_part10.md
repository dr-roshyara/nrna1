# Part 10 — Glossary, Ubiquitous Language & Canonical Terminology

---

# 10.1 Purpose

This glossary defines the canonical terminology used throughout the programme.

It serves three purposes:

1. Preserve the programme's ubiquitous language.
2. Ensure future AI assistants interpret architectural concepts consistently.
3. Prevent architectural drift caused by inconsistent terminology.

Unless superseded by an approved ADR or Engineering Standard, the definitions in this section are considered canonical.

---

# 10.2 Programme Vocabulary

## PublicDigit

The primary software product being engineered.

PublicDigit is a constitutional governance platform whose current implementation focuses on secure, auditable election management.

It is the primary source of operational engineering experience.

---

## KnowledgeOS

The reusable engineering platform.

KnowledgeOS contains engineering methodology rather than business functionality.

It evolves incrementally through validated operational evidence.

---

## PKS (Product Knowledge System)

The structured repository of operational engineering knowledge.

PKS captures:

- implementation observations,
- verification findings,
- engineering lessons,
- architectural discoveries,
- reusable engineering knowledge.

PKS records reality.

KnowledgeOS extracts methodology.

---

## Operational Evidence

Structured observations produced during real engineering work.

Operational evidence includes:

- implementation discoveries,
- architectural friction,
- governance findings,
- successful engineering patterns,
- repository observations,
- documentation observations.

Operational evidence is the primary input into KnowledgeOS evolution.

---

# 10.3 Governance Vocabulary

## Architecture Review Board (ARB)

The governing architectural authority.

The ARB exercises architectural authority by:

- approving ADRs,
- issuing rulings,
- authorizing implementation,
- accepting completed work,
- resolving architectural ambiguity.

---

## Principal Architect

The architectural steward.

Responsible for:

- architectural analysis,
- DDD guidance,
- architectural reviews,
- engineering recommendations,
- preserving architectural integrity.

The Principal Architect recommends.

The ARB decides.

---

## Decision Authority

The authority responsible for programme-level decisions outside architectural governance.

Examples include:

- prioritization,
- scheduling,
- execution planning.

---

## Engineering

The discipline responsible for implementation.

Engineering:

- writes code,
- writes tests,
- performs migrations,
- updates documentation,
- executes verification.

Engineering does not redefine approved architecture.

---

# 10.4 Architectural Vocabulary

## Domain

A business problem space with its own language, rules, and responsibilities.

---

## Bounded Context

An explicit boundary within which a ubiquitous language is consistent.

Each bounded context owns:

- terminology,
- business rules,
- capabilities,
- implementation responsibility.

---

## Capability

A business responsibility expressed independently of implementation.

Architecture reasons about capabilities before classes.

Capabilities remain stable even when implementations change.

---

## Contract

An architectural agreement describing collaboration between components or contexts.

Contracts should remain stable across implementation changes.

---

## Aggregate

A transactional consistency boundary within the domain model.

Aggregates protect business invariants.

---

## Entity

A business object possessing identity.

Identity remains stable throughout the entity's lifecycle.

---

## Value Object

An immutable business object without identity.

Equality is determined entirely by value.

Value Objects should encapsulate behaviour as well as data.

---

## Application Service

Coordinates a business use case.

Application Services orchestrate.

They do not own business policy that belongs inside the domain model.

---

## Domain Service

Encapsulates domain behaviour that does not naturally belong to a single aggregate or value object.

---

## Repository

An abstraction over persistence.

The domain depends upon repository interfaces rather than storage technology.

---

## Factory

Responsible for constructing business objects while enforcing construction invariants.

---

## Port

An architectural abstraction representing an external capability required by the domain.

The domain depends upon ports.

Infrastructure implements them.

---

## Adapter

An infrastructure implementation of a port.

Adapters isolate business behaviour from infrastructure concerns.

---

# 10.5 Engineering Vocabulary

## Work Package (WP)

A governable engineering objective.

A Work Package defines:

- objective,
- scope,
- acceptance,
- authorization,
- verification.

---

## Slice

A small engineering increment within a Work Package.

Each slice has:

- explicit scope,
- architectural boundary,
- acceptance criteria,
- independent authorization.

---

## RED

The first phase of Test-Driven Development.

RED demonstrates that:

- the requirement is expressed correctly,
- tests fail for the expected reason.

RED is evidence.

It is not implementation.

---

## GREEN

The implementation phase.

Engineering implements only enough behaviour to satisfy the approved scope.

---

## VERIFY

Engineering demonstrates:

- correctness,
- architectural consistency,
- repository health,
- successful verification.

---

## ACCEPT

A governance act confirming:

- scope completed,
- architecture preserved,
- verification satisfied.

Acceptance is exercised by authority.

It is never inferred.

---

## Merge Gate

The final engineering verification before completion.

Merge gates confirm engineering readiness.

They do not exercise architectural authority.

---

# 10.6 Documentation Vocabulary

## ADR (Architecture Decision Record)

A permanent record of an exercised architectural decision.

An ADR documents authority.

It does not create authority.

---

## Engineering Standard (ES)

A reusable engineering policy describing how engineering should be performed.

Engineering Standards define methodology.

They are not implementation documents.

---

## PKS Observation

A structured engineering observation recorded within the Product Knowledge System.

Observations remain observations until operational evidence justifies promotion.

---

## Verification Report

A document recording engineering or architectural verification.

Verification reports document evidence.

They do not authorize implementation.

---

## Developer Guide

Documentation explaining how engineering work should be performed.

Developer Guides support implementation.

They are not governance artifacts.

---

# 10.7 Documentation Governance Vocabulary

## Classification

The architectural identity assigned to a documentation artifact.

Classification includes:

- scope,
- steward,
- maturity,
- domain.

Classification determines placement.

---

## Placement

The repository location derived from classification.

Placement reflects classification.

Placement never defines classification.

---

## Steward

The role responsible for maintaining a documentation artifact.

Stewardship is independent of repository location.

---

## Maturity

The lifecycle stage of a documentation artifact.

Typical maturity stages include:

- draft,
- review,
- approved,
- adopted,
- deprecated,
- archived.

---

## Domain

The documentation domain to which an artifact belongs.

Current domains include:

- PublicDigit,
- KnowledgeOS,
- PKS.

---

# 10.8 KnowledgeOS Vocabulary

## Pattern

A reusable engineering solution identified through operational experience.

Patterns remain candidates until validated repeatedly.

---

## Candidate Pattern

A pattern with supporting operational evidence but not yet promoted into reusable methodology.

---

## Reusable Capability

An engineering capability promoted into KnowledgeOS after repeated operational validation.

---

## Knowledge Extraction

The process of promoting validated engineering knowledge from PKS into KnowledgeOS.

---

# 10.9 Repository Vocabulary

## Runtime State

Temporary AI execution context.

Examples include:

- session memory,
- planning,
- temporary notes,
- runtime automation.

Runtime state is not permanent engineering knowledge.

---

## Persistent Knowledge

Version-controlled engineering knowledge.

Examples include:

- ADRs,
- Engineering Standards,
- PKS,
- documentation,
- architecture.

Persistent knowledge survives individual AI sessions.

---

## Repository Migration

A governed change to repository organization.

Repository migration preserves architectural meaning while evolving repository structure.

---

## Repository Validation

Automated or manual verification confirming repository integrity.

Examples include:

- documentation validation,
- link validation,
- migration verification,
- repository consistency.

---

# 10.10 Canonical Engineering Principles

The following statements summarize the programme's ubiquitous language.

- Architecture drives implementation.
- Evidence precedes authority.
- Authority precedes recording.
- Recording precedes execution.
- Verification precedes acceptance.
- Acceptance precedes closure.
- Classification precedes placement.
- Placement derives exclusively from classification.
- Repository organization is architecture.
- Documentation is a first-class architectural asset.
- Operational evidence is a first-class architectural asset.
- KnowledgeOS evolves through operational evidence.
- PublicDigit remains the primary engineering programme.
- PKS preserves engineering knowledge.
- Deterministic engineering should be automated.
- Semantic ambiguity returns to governance.
- AI augments engineering; it does not replace governance.
- Generalize only after repeated operational evidence.
- The strongest architectural claim is the strongest claim supported by current evidence.

# 10.11 Closing Statement

This Architecture Knowledge Transfer (AKT) document captures the architectural foundation, engineering methodology, governance model, repository organization, operational philosophy, and ubiquitous language of the programme at the time of writing.

Future AI assistants should treat this document as the canonical onboarding reference.

Unless superseded by approved governance, the architectural principles, terminology, and engineering methodology described herein constitute the baseline from which future architectural reasoning and engineering work should continue.

The programme is intentionally evolutionary.

Architecture should continue to evolve—but only through disciplined governance, explicit authority, and operational evidence collected through real engineering work.
