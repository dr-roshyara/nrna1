## A. Business summary

- **The question is who may act when an organisation's own rules say nothing.**
- **PublicDigit has answered it in two different ways so far:**
  1. **"Nobody until designated."** The act waits until the organisation's competent body names who may do it.
     - Examples: D-58 newsletters, D-149 admission, D-150 moderation, D-154 structure.
     - **This never creates authority.**
  2. **"Someone in particular, by default."** PublicDigit names a body:
     - D-145: the Highest Committee;
     - D-53: the tribunal;
     - D-64: the committee;
     - D-115: the province committee.
     - **This creates authority by platform rule, which D-1a forbids.**
- **Separately, some defaults are not about authority at all.** They describe how the product behaves, e.g. who can **see** a forum and how names are shown. **These are harmless if the organisation can change them and they are labelled as platform defaults.**

## C. Principal decision brief (two questions, asked one at a time)

### Question 1: may PublicDigit ever assign authority by default?

| Option | Meaning | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Never (recommended)** | Where the rules are silent on **who** may act, the act **waits until a competent body designates someone** (kind C). **D-145 (2), D-53, D-64 and D-115 are amended:** <br>- **D-145:** the area has **no competent committee** until one is established or the organisation designates a body; acts needing it wait. <br>- **D-53:** moderation review goes to the body the rules or a designation name; **until then, a challenge is recorded and waits.** <br>- **D-64:** messaging stays off until a designated body switches it on. <br>- **D-115:** withdrawn as a default. <br>**Product defaults (kind D) stay, overridable and labelled** | **D-1a holds without exception**; parties see only authority their own rules created. Some actions wait for a designation, which the onboarding (D-148) can collect up front |
| (b) Platform fallbacks, labelled | Keep the four as **platform fallbacks**, applied only while the rules are silent and displaced by any written rule, each recorded as "platform fallback (D-nn)" (the consolidation review's proposed reading) | Faster operation; but **PublicDigit names competent bodies**, which D-1a forbids; **a contested act could be challenged as having no constitutional basis** |
| (c) Closed exceptions | List the four as exceptions to D-1a / D-1b | Same risk as (b), stated openly |

### Question 2: is D-161 a fixed product rule or an overridable default?

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) Overridable default (recommended)** | Applies while the organisation's rules are silent; **an organisation whose rules let lapsed members post (or bar suspended ones from reading) can say so**; labelled "platform default (D-161)" | consistent with D-153 and D-1b |
| (b) Fixed product rule | Every organisation on PublicDigit works this way | simpler, but a platform rule about members' rights |

## D. D-166: the operational boundary

**The promise:** technical operation is allowed by the joining grant; **human inspection of content needs a case grant.**

**Constraint:** a backup, dump, snapshot or restored copy **contains** content. "Not reading" must therefore hold for **people and copies**, not just for queries [AC].

| Area | Control (architect's recommendation) | Proof obligation [TU] |
|---|---|---|
| **Roles** | **two kinds of credentials:** <br>- automated **operator** roles (provisioning, migrations, backups, restores) run as **non-interactive jobs**; <br>- **no human has standing credentials** that can query an organisation's business tables. <br>**Interactive access** to an organisation database exists **only under a case grant**, issued for that case, time-limited, recorded and reported to the organisation (D-166) | an access-review test; role-permission tests |
| **Migrations** | run by the automated runner under the joining grant; **no ad-hoc SQL by a person** | runner audit log |
| **Backups** | **encrypted per organisation**; **restore only by the automated process into that organisation's own database** (or a fresh one for it); **opening a backup for inspection = content access → case grant** | restore-drill evidence; key-use log |
| **Troubleshooting** | technical metrics and **content-free logs** (ISO-10); anything needing content → case grant | log content tests |
| **Temporary copies and dumps** | none made by people; automated copies are encrypted and expire | inventory and expiry check |
| **Privileged support** | **case grant only**; every action recorded and reported | report delivery test |

**Honest limit [AC]:**
- whoever controls the server and keys can, technically, read data;
- **release 1's guarantee is procedural and auditable** (no standing human credentials, grants, records, reports), not cryptographic;
- **a cryptographic guarantee** (organisation-held keys) is a later option [TU];
- **the service terms must state the release-1 level truthfully.**

**[LQ] compelled disclosure:** unchanged and outside this design.

## H. Cross-cutting baseline invariant (from D-166)

**INV-ACCESS-1 (baseline, cross-cutting) [AD D-166]:** **PublicDigit's operation of the platform never confers access to an organisation's business content.**
- **Two distinct permissions exist**, both **given by the organisation**:
  - **(1) the service-operation grant** (at joining): technical operations only, never reading content;
  - **(2) a case-specific content grant**: for each inspection, recorded and reported to the organisation.
- **Neither can be replaced by:**
  - a central account binding;
  - a platform administrator role;
  - a support request;
  - possession of a backup.
- **It binds every context and every operational procedure** (provisioning, migration, backup and restore, troubleshooting, logging), not only the multi-database design's ISO-8. Its controls are §D.

## I. Named bodies under D-167 (IR-R1-02 M-1; recorder's interpretation note, no ruling)

**D-47** (election committee), **D-60** (the committee decides who sends newsletters), **D-63** (the tribunal's order), **D-71** (only the tribunal suspends): **each names a body whose competence must come from the organisation's own rules or a valid designation (D-1a, D-167). Where no such body exists or is designated, the act waits.**
- D-63 and D-71 are **restrictions** ("only by …"). Where no tribunal exists, **nobody** can read private messages or suspend a challenged decision; nothing is granted.
- D-60 is implemented as **the designating committee's decision** (D-58).
- **None of them makes PublicDigit name a body.**
