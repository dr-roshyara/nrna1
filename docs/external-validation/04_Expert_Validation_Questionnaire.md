# Expert Validation Questionnaire (EV-01 – EV-04)

## Purpose

This instrument gathers **independent expert diagnosis** of a constitutional design situation faced by a new, cross-border membership organisation that wants to run its own elections.

It is deliberately **neutral**, and it is deliberately **not a poll**. We are **not** asking you to choose between pre-defined options or to endorse a design the organisation already favours. We have not told you what its designers believe, and we have removed their internal vocabulary.

We want your **diagnosis**: what risks you see, what protections actually hold, what tends to fail, and what conditions you would consider necessary. We will do the work of mapping your diagnosis onto design options ourselves, afterward. **There is no preferred answer.** A response that says "this is not a real problem" is as valuable to us as any other.

> **Scope.** This questionnaire is for *human experts*. It is a different instrument from the comparative-literature research questions (`03_Research_Questions.md`), which are written for research tools. Those ask *"what does the literature say."* This asks *"what is your professional judgment."*
>
> **Please do not** treat any question as a request to pick a side. If you find yourself wanting to recommend a structure, describe the *reasoning and conditions* behind it rather than naming a winner.

---

## The Situation (read first — same for all panels)

A large diaspora community (hundreds of thousands of members living across dozens of countries) is building a system to run secure, anonymous, verifiable elections entirely online.

Its highest authority is a single sovereign body — call it the **Assembly**. Over time, the Assembly has come to hold most constitutional functions, directly or indirectly. It:

- sets the rules for what a valid election is,
- appoints the bodies that certify elections,
- appoints the body that interprets constitutional disputes,
- controls the process for amending the constitution.

The organisation has created an **Interpretation Committee** meant to be independent — able, in principle, to rule against the Assembly. But the Assembly appoints that committee, and the committee interprets the very provisions that define how independent it must be.

The organisation is **new**. It has no constitutional history, no established courts of its own, no free press scrutinising it, no rival parties, and limited institutional memory. Leadership rotates; members are scattered worldwide.

That is the whole situation. We are not proposing a fix. We want your diagnosis.

---

## How to record answers (every question)

For each question, please provide:

1. **Your diagnosis**, in your own words.
2. **Your confidence:** low / moderate / high.
3. **What would change your assessment.**

If at any point you would design something differently, **describe the design and the conditions that make it necessary** — but please do not reduce your answer to choosing between options we might have implied.

## EV-01 — Constitutional Law Scholars
*Sub-question this panel answers: **How is independence actually protected?***

**1.1** When an oversight or interpretation body's authority derives from the same body it is meant to check, by what mechanisms (if any) can it nonetheless be made genuinely independent? Which of those mechanisms do you regard as load-bearing, and which as decorative?

**1.2** Where a body interprets the provisions defining its *own* independence, what keeps that self-interpretation honest over time? When has that safeguard held, and when has it failed?

**1.3** Is "independence of origin" (where authority comes from) constitutionally distinct from "independence of operation" (freedom to act and rule once established)? Which matters more, and under what circumstances does the distinction become decisive?

**1.4** Reading the situation above, do you recognise it as a known constitutional problem, or as novel/unusual? Name it in your own terms.

---

## EV-02 — Election Governance Experts
*Sub-question this panel answers: **What actually fails in election oversight?***

**2.1** In your direct experience, what is the **first thing that fails** when the body running elections and the body overseeing them are too close? Describe the failure mechanism, not the principle.

**2.2** Which protections have you seen **actually prevent** that failure in practice (e.g. appointment rules, finality of rulings, removal thresholds), and which looked good on paper but did not hold?

**2.3** For an organisation with **no external enforcement** behind it — no state, no courts, no regulator — what changes about how oversight fails or holds?

**2.4** What is the single safeguard you would treat as non-negotiable for credible election oversight, and why that one?

---

## EV-03 — Diaspora & Transnational Governance Experts
*Sub-question this panel answers: **What actually breaks in transnational / diaspora organisations?***

**3.1** In organisations like this, what **actually goes wrong** with election oversight in practice — is the dominant failure structural design, or people, capacity, money, and attention?

**3.2** Have you seen oversight independence **erode slowly over years** while elections still appeared to run normally? If so, what allowed the erosion, and what (if anything) caught it?

**3.3** For a young, volunteer-run, geographically scattered organisation, does a more complex multi-body structure tend to be a **strength** or a **fragility** (more to staff, more to capture, more to neglect)? Give examples.

**3.4** What conditions, if present, make you trust such an organisation's elections — and what conditions make you distrust them regardless of the written rules?

---

## EV-04 — Institutional Design Experts
*Sub-question this panel answers: **How do organisations prevent the gradual concentration of power?***

**4.1** What design mechanisms have you seen **actually slow or reverse** the gradual concentration of power in one body over time — and which commonly-recommended mechanisms fail to?

**4.2** Distinguish **point-in-time independence** (independent today) from **durational independence** (independent over decades under pressure). What does it take to achieve the second, and is it achievable by design alone?

**4.3** Some independence arrangements work in mature institutions but may depend on surrounding maturity (precedent, scrutiny, professional culture) rather than on their own design. For an organisation **without** that maturity, can design substitute for it — and if not, what **preconditions** must be built first, and **in what order**?

**4.4** If an organisation cannot yet support a given safeguard, is it sound to use a stronger/temporary structural arrangement until defined preconditions exist, then transition? What would make such a transition trustworthy rather than a permanent excuse?

---

## Closing question (all panels)

**C.1** Is there a framing, a risk, or a design consideration we have **not** put in front of you that you think matters more than what we asked? If so, what is it?
