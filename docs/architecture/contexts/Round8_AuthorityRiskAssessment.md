## Section 1: Authority Explosion Risk Assessment

**Gate Question from Plan:** Are we discovering a real domain concept, or creating modeling inflation?

### Assessment

**Concern:** If every decision's owner is labeled as "having authority," we create 20+ authority types by definition alone. This would be concept proliferation, not discovery.

**Risk Level Assessment:**

**Probability of Inflation: MEDIUM**

**Rationale:**
- All 5 representative decisions show authority claims
- All 5 follow same lifecycle pattern (Claim → Origin → Exercise → Revocation)
- Pattern is uniform, not context-specific
- This uniformity could suggest:
  - **Scenario A (Low Risk):** Authority is a genuine cross-cutting concept (like Identity or Time)
  - **Scenario B (High Risk):** Authority is just "decision ownership" relabeled; uniformity means we're applying the same label everywhere

**Risk Indicators Present:**
- ✗ Every decision gets labeled as "authority"
- ✓ Authority claims have behavioral properties (not just labels)
- ✗ Authority acceptance mechanism still not well-understood (Acceptance stage absent from documentation)
- ✓ Authority requires documented origin (suggests real constraint, not labeling)

### Evidence Against Relabeling Hypothesis

**Observation 1: Behavioral Properties Identified in Phase 2**

Phase 2 discovered properties associated with authority:
- A2: Authority Requires Recognition (claimed ≠ legitimate)
- A3: Authority is Temporal (has lifecycle)
- A4: Authority is Challengeable (can be questioned)
- A5: Authority Requires Chain of Origin (traceable source)

**Candidate Interpretation:** If these properties are genuine constraints (not artifacts of analysis), they would distinguish Authority from simple "decision ownership."

**Confidence:** MEDIUM (properties documented in Phase 2, but not all independently confirmed in Step 4)

**Note:** This supports the "distinct concept" hypothesis, but does not eliminate the "relabeling" hypothesis.

**Evidence Consistent with Relabeling Hypothesis**

**Observation 1: Uniform Lifecycle Pattern in Sample**

The analyzed sample shows pattern: Claim → Origin → Exercise → Revocation in all five decisions.

**Candidate Interpretation A:** This uniformity suggests Authority is a genuine cross-cutting concept (like Identity or Time).

**Candidate Interpretation B:** This uniformity suggests Authority is a template applied to every decision (relabeling risk).

**Assessment:** Cannot distinguish between interpretations with current sample.

**Confidence:** LOW (pattern observed; meaning ambiguous)

**Observation 2: Acceptance Stage Not Documented in Sample**

All five representative decisions lack documented Acceptance stage:

- Membership approval: Acceptance not documented
- Election creation: Acceptance not documented
- Result certification: Acceptance not documented
- Membership reversal: Acceptance not documented
- Authority granting: Acceptance not documented

**Candidate Interpretation A:** Acceptance is implicit in all contexts (not documented in matrix).

**Candidate Interpretation B:** Acceptance occurs before decision point (outside matrix scope).

**Candidate Interpretation C:** Acceptance is not part of authority lifecycle in constitutional decisions.

**Candidate Interpretation D:** Decision Ownership Matrix structure does not capture acceptance data.

**Assessment:** Absence observed; meaning remains ambiguous.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM (absence is documented; interpretation uncertain)

### Authority Explosion Risk Assessment

**Observation:** Five representative decisions all exhibit authority-related behavioral patterns (Claim, Origin, Exercise, Challenge, Revocation stages).

**Candidate Explanation A:** Authority is a distinct domain concept with non-trivial behavioral properties.
- Consistent with: Observed properties A2-A5 from Phase 2 (Recognition, Temporal, Challengeable, Traceable)
- Confidence: MEDIUM (properties observed but not all independently confirmed in Step 4)

**Candidate Explanation B:** Authority is a relabeling of decision ownership applied uniformly.
- Consistent with: Uniform lifecycle pattern across all contexts; Acceptance stage universally absent
- Confidence: MEDIUM (pattern observed but ambiguous interpretation)

**Assessment:** Both explanations remain viable. Cannot distinguish based on Step 4 evidence alone.

**Risk Level:** MEDIUM (both explanations possible; explosion risk cannot be fully eliminated)

**Next Investigation:** Round 8 Synthesis and Step 5 (Verification Placement) may provide additional evidence to favor one explanation.

### Stage Frequency Assessment

**Consistently Present (6/8 stages):** Claim, Origin, Exercise, Verification, Challenge, Revocation

**Consistently Absent (1/8 stages):** Acceptance

**Conditionally Present (1/8 stages):** Delegation

### Model Viability Assessment

**Finding:** The 8-stage lifecycle model fits reasonably well, but one stage (Acceptance) is persistently absent.

**Implications:**

**Scenario A (Model Valid):**
- Acceptance is implicit in all contexts
- Sample happens to be too small to show Acceptance variation
- Expand to additional decisions to test

**Scenario B (Model Needs Revision):**
- Acceptance stage is not relevant to constitutional authority decisions
- Revise model to 7 stages (remove Acceptance)
- Alternative: Acceptance happens before decision point (outside scope of matrix)

**Scenario C (Modeling Inflation):**
- The 8-stage template is being forced onto decisions
- Some stages are artifacts of the framework, not real concept
- Evidence against: Stages don't appear arbitrary; they are directly observable

**Confidence in Model:** MEDIUM

**Recommendation for Synthesis:** Do not treat 8-stage model as established. Instead:
1. Test with additional decisions to establish whether Acceptance stage reappears
2. Allow decisions to legitimately omit stages
3. Consider whether 7-stage model (excluding Acceptance) is more parsimonious

## Section 3: Modeling Inflation Assessment

**Question:** Have we created unnecessary authority types or are we discovering a real concept?

### Authority Categories in Analyzed Sample

**Observation:** Five representative decisions map to five candidate authority categories:
1. Membership authority (approval decisions)
2. Election authority (election management)
3. Appeals authority (reversal decisions)
4. Governance authority (rule-setting)
5. (Possibly) Independent Certifier authority (marked PROVISIONAL)

**Sample Note:** These categories match the number of contexts analyzed (5 decisions / 5 contexts). This correspondence could indicate:
- Candidate A: Each context owns one authority domain (organizational structure reflected)
- Candidate B: We are mapping context structure onto authority labels (template application)

### Interpretation: Is This Inflation?

**Scenario A: Legitimate Differentiation**
- Each context claims authority over its domain
- 5 contexts = 5 authority types
- This is organizational structure reflected in authority structure, not inflation

**Scenario B: Artificial Distinction**
- We're labeling context ownership as "authority types"
- These are not distinct domain concepts; they're just role assignments
- Inflation risk: HIGH

**Assessment:** Cannot distinguish Scenario A from Scenario B with current data.

**Evidence for Scenario A:**
- Each authority type has different acceptance mechanisms (Governance → Membership rules differ from Appeals → fairness principle)
- Each authority type has different challenge paths (Appeals challenges Membership decisions; Membership doesn't challenge Governance)
- Each authority type traces to different origin (Governance = constitutional, Appeals = fairness)

**Evidence for Scenario B:**
- All authority types follow identical lifecycle (suggests template application)
- Authority type count matches context count (seems too convenient)
- No observation of "meta-authority" that manages authority types themselves

### Modeling Inflation Risk Assessment: MEDIUM

**Observation:** Five categories match five contexts exactly.

**Candidate Interpretation A:** Authority is context-family. Each context legitimately develops distinct authority variant. Correspondence is natural.

**Candidate Interpretation B:** Authority categories are artifacts of how we mapped decisions. Five categories appear because five contexts exist (template application).

**Confidence:** LOW (cannot distinguish between interpretations with current evidence)

**Note:** If H-C (Cross-Cutting Authority) is validated, five categories would be instances of one concept. If H-B (Authority Family) is validated, five categories would be legitimate variants. The choice between these architectural models will inform interpretation of category proliferation.
