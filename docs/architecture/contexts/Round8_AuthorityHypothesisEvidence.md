## H-B Hypothesis: Authority Family

**Definition:** Authority varies by context. Each context develops context-specific authority behaviors and rules. Authority stays context-local.

---

## Evidence FOR H-B

### H-B Evidence 1: Context-Specific Authority Rules

**Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md

**Observation:** Each context's decisions have different authority requirements.

- **Membership authority:** Requires eligibility verification, applicant evidence, governance rule compliance
- **Election authority:** Requires election configuration validation, voting window legitimacy, member list consistency
- **Appeals authority:** Requires case review, appeal grounds legitimacy, evidence of original unfairness
- **Governance authority:** Requires constitutional consistency, internal rule consistency

**Consistency with H-B:** Each context applies *different* verification and legitimacy criteria. This suggests authority is not unified; it is family-like (related but distinct types).

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-B Evidence 2: Authority Delegation Follows Hierarchy

**Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md, Round8_ContextRelationshipMap_v1.md

**Observation:** Authority delegation follows established hierarchy.

- Governance → Membership (delegation in defined governance rules)
- Governance → Election (delegation in defined governance rules)
- Governance → Appeals (delegation in defined governance rules)
- Membership does NOT delegate to Election
- Election does NOT delegate to Appeals
- Appeal does NOT delegate to Governance

**Consistency with H-B:** Authority flows through defined channels, not everywhere. Each context respects others' boundaries. This pattern is consistent with context-family where each owns their authority domain.

**Alternative explanation (H-C):** Authority is orthogonal; flow pattern is just how it happens to move through these particular contexts.

**Confidence: MEDIUM**

---

### H-B Evidence 3: Authority Exercise Is Context-Owned

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 3

**Observation:** Decision execution (Exercise stage) is always performed by the context that claims the authority.

- Membership exercises membership approval/denial
- Election exercises election creation/closure
- Appeals exercises reversal decisions
- Governance exercises rule definition

**Consistency with H-B:** Authority is not delegated to external executors. Each context controls the execution of its own authority. This suggests authority is tied to context identity.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-B Evidence 4: Authority Acceptance Varies by Context

**Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md

**Observation:** Different contexts have different acceptance requirements.

- **Membership:** Applicants accept by submitting application (implicit)
- **Election:** Voters accept by participating (implicit) or challenging (explicit)
- **Appeals:** Parties accept by acknowledging appeals process (implicit)
- **Governance:** Members accept by participating in governance (implicit)

**Consistency with H-B:** Each context defines its own legitimacy conditions. Acceptance is not universal; it is context-specific.

**Source data: MEDIUM confidence (some acceptance implicit, not documented)**

---

### H-B Evidence 5: Authority Revocation Is Context-Controlled

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 3

**Observation:** Authority revocation is exercised by the context that owns the authority (or Governance, which granted it).

- Membership can revoke (suspend, revoke membership)
- Election can revoke (cancel election)
- Appeals can revoke (overturn decision)
- Governance can revoke all

**Consistency with H-B:** Each context controls its own authority lifecycle. Revocation is not external or cross-cutting; it is domain-internal.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

## Evidence AGAINST H-B

### H-B Against Evidence 1: Appeals Crosses Context Boundaries

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 5

**Observation:** Appeals authority is exercised *over* other contexts' decisions.

- Appeals reverses Membership decisions (member approval/denial)
- Appeals reviews Election decisions (result certification)
- Appeals authority is not Membership authority or Election authority

**Inconsistency with H-B:** If Authority is family (context-local), how can Appeals authority reverse Membership authority? This suggests authority transcends context boundaries.

**Alternative explanation (H-B): Appeals owns a separate "correction decisions" domain.** But this requires Appeals to exist outside normal context structure.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-B Against Evidence 2: Governance Authority Affects All Contexts

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 5

**Observation:** Governance authority affects all other contexts.

- Governance defines Membership rules (affects Membership authority)
- Governance defines Election rules (affects Election authority)
- Governance defines Appeals rules (affects Appeals authority)

**Inconsistency with H-B:** If Authority is context-family, Governance authority should stay within Governance. Instead, it constrains all other contexts.

**Alternative explanation (H-B): Governance is constitutional authority that all contexts derive from.** But this suggests Governance is not part of the family; it is parent to the family.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-B Against Evidence 3: Imported Hypothesis — Cross-Contextual Recognition

**Source:** VerificationDiscoverySummary.md (Phase 2 Pattern A2)

**Imported Hypothesis:** Phase 2 identified Pattern A2: "Authority Requires Recognition." This hypothesis suggests authority requires *outside* recognition to be legitimate.

**Validation Status:** NOT independently observed in Step 4. Step 4 found:
- Acceptance stage absent from all five decision documentations
- No explicit documentation of "recognition" in Decision Ownership Matrix

**Inconsistency with H-B (IF hypothesis valid):** If authority requires cross-contextual recognition, then authority is not family-local.

**Confidence:** LOW (depends on Phase 2 pattern validity, not confirmed in Step 4)

**Note:** This evidence for H-B is conditional and should not be weighted equally with directly observed evidence.

---

### H-B Against Evidence 4: Authority Lifecycle Is Uniform

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 7

**Observation:** All five representative decisions follow the same lifecycle: Claim → Origin → Exercise → Verification → Challenge → Revocation.

**Inconsistency with H-B:** If Authority is context-family (different types), we would expect different lifecycle patterns by context. Instead, pattern is uniform across all contexts.

**Alternative explanation (H-B): Uniform lifecycle is because Decision Ownership Matrix imposes uniform structure.** But the matrix structure comes from the decisions themselves.

**Source data: HIGH confidence (from Section 3 analysis)**

---

### H-B Against Evidence 5: No Documented Context-Specific Acceptance

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 7

**Observation:** Acceptance stage (Legitimacy) is absent from all five decision documentations.

**Inconsistency with H-B:** If Authority is family with context-specific rules, we would expect Acceptance to be documented differently for each context. Instead, it is uniformly absent.

**Alternative explanation:** Acceptance is implicit in all contexts (not a documented stage). But this suggests uniform behavior, not family-specific behavior.

**Source data: HIGH confidence (absence documented)**

## H-C Hypothesis: Cross-Cutting Authority

**Definition:** Authority is orthogonal to context boundaries, like Identity or Time. It is present in all contexts, follows universal patterns, and is not subordinate to any one context.

---

## Evidence FOR H-C

### H-C Evidence 1: Authority Crosses All Context Boundaries

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 5

**Observation:** Authority is exercised from one context toward another:
- Governance → Membership
- Governance → Election
- Governance → Appeals
- Appeals → Membership
- Appeals → Election

**Consistency with H-C:** Authority does not respect context boundaries. It is orthogonal to domain structure. All contexts must accommodate authority from external sources.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Evidence 2: Consistent Lifecycle Pattern in Analyzed Sample

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 7

**Observation:** All five representative decisions follow identical lifecycle pattern: Claim → Origin → Exercise → Verification → Challenge → Revocation.

**Sample Size Caveat:** Five decisions analyzed out of ~20 in the full constitutional system. This is 25% sample. Pattern observed in sample; universality not confirmed.

**Consistency with H-C (IF pattern is universal):** Cross-cutting concepts (like Identity, Time, Trust) have consistent patterns everywhere. If this pattern holds across all decisions, it would support H-C.

**Confidence:** HIGH (within analyzed sample), but MEDIUM for universality claim (5 decisions insufficient to establish universal pattern)

**Note:** Recommend expanding analysis to additional decisions to test whether pattern universalizes.

---

### H-C Evidence 3: Authority Requires Universal Traceability

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 6

**Observation:** All authority claims trace to documented sources. No authority appears spontaneously.

**Consistency with H-C:** Cross-cutting concepts require universal rules (e.g., every identity must have an origin, every timestamp has a reference frame). Authority shows similar requirement: every authority claim must trace.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Evidence 4: Imported Hypothesis — Cross-Contextual Recognition

**Source:** VerificationDiscoverySummary.md (Phase 2 Pattern A2)

**Imported Hypothesis:** Phase 2 Pattern A2 suggests "Authority Requires Recognition." If valid, this would indicate authority requires acceptance across contexts.

**Validation Status:** NOT independently observed in Step 4. Step 4 found:
- Acceptance stage not documented in any of five decisions
- No explicit cross-contextual recognition documented

**Consistency with H-C (IF hypothesis valid):** If authority universally requires cross-context recognition, this would support H-C.

**Confidence:** LOW (depends on Phase 2 pattern, not confirmed in Step 4)

**Note:** This evidence is conditional on Phase 2 findings being valid.

---

### H-C Evidence 5: Imported Hypothesis — Temporal Boundaries

**Source:** VerificationDiscoverySummary.md (Phase 2 Pattern A3)

**Imported Hypothesis:** Phase 2 Pattern A3 suggests "Authority is Temporal" — has start, duration, expiration.

**Validation Status:** Partially confirmed in Step 4.
- Observed: Revocation documented in all five decisions (suggests expiration)
- Not observed: Explicit temporal boundaries (start time, duration) not documented in Decision Ownership Matrix

**Consistency with H-C (IF hypothesis valid):** If authority universally has temporal properties, this could support H-C.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM (temporality partially observed; full pattern from Phase 2 not independently validated)

**Note:** Phase 2 pattern more strongly confirmed than Recognition pattern, but still conditional.

## Evidence AGAINST H-C

### H-C Against Evidence 1: Authority Origins Are Context-Specific

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 3

**Observation:** Authority claims originate in different contexts:

- Membership authority originates in Governance rules
- Election authority originates in Governance rules
- Appeals authority originates in Governance rules + fairness principle
- Governance authority originates in constitutional definition

**Inconsistency with H-C:** Cross-cutting concepts have universal origin. Authority shows context-specific origins.

**Alternative explanation (H-C): Governance is the universal origin.** But Governance is a context, not cross-cutting.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Against Evidence 2: Authority Delegation Is Hierarchical, Not Cross-Cutting

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 5

**Observation:** Authority delegation follows hierarchy: Governance → others. It does not distribute laterally.

**Inconsistency with H-C:** Cross-cutting concepts (like Time, Identity) are uniformly available to all. Authority is concentrated in Governance, then flows downward.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Against Evidence 3: Authority Exercise Is Context-Owned

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 3

**Observation:** Each context executes its own authority decisions. No cross-context execution.

**Inconsistency with H-C:** Cross-cutting concepts are not executed; they are consulted. Authority is executed by specific contexts.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Against Evidence 4: Authority Does Not Appear in All Decisions Equally

**Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md

**Observation:** Some decisions have clear single authorities (Membership approval, Election creation). Some have ambiguous authorities (Certify Results marked PROVISIONAL).

**Inconsistency with H-C:** Cross-cutting concepts are uniformly present. Authority is patchy.

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

---

### H-C Against Evidence 5: Appeals Authority Is Exception, Not Rule

**Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md, Section 5

**Observation:** Appeals is the only context that exercises authority over other contexts. This is exceptional, not universal.

**Inconsistency with H-C:** If Authority is cross-cutting, all contexts should exercise authority over others. Instead, only Appeals does (and Governance, which is constitutional).

**Source data: HIGH confidence**

## Candidate Acceptance Mechanisms

**Question:** What conditions appear associated with authority being accepted or challenged?

### Observed Conditions (Step 4)

**Condition 1: Governance Rule Alignment**
- **Observation:** All five decisions reference Governance rules as authority source
- **Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md (all contexts cite "Governance rules" or "Governance defines")
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Interpretation (candidate):** Authority may be accepted if consistent with Governance

**Condition 2: Documented Origin**
- **Observation:** All authority claims have documented source (Governance rules, constitution, fairness principle)
- **Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md Section 6 (Authority Conservation Test)
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Interpretation (candidate):** Authority accepted if origin is documented and traceable

**Condition 3: Context-Specific Verification**
- **Observation:** All five decisions include verification stage before or alongside authority exercise
- **Source:** Round8_AuthorityFlowAnalysis.md Section 3
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Interpretation (candidate):** Authority accepted if context-specific verification occurs

**Condition 4: Challenge Pathway Availability**
- **Observation:** All five decisions include documented challenge/appeal pathways
- **Source:** Round8_DecisionOwnershipMatrix.md (all decisions have "Appeal Path: YES")
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Interpretation (candidate):** Authority accepted if challenges are possible

### Imported Hypothesis (Phase 2)

**Mechanism 5: Recognition (Not observed in Step 4)**
- **Source:** VerificationDiscoverySummary.md Pattern A2
- **Status:** Hypothesis from Phase 2; not independently confirmed in Step 4
- **Note:** Acceptance stage absent from all five decision documentations; recognition not documented

### Synthesis Observation

**Multiple conditions appear to support authority acceptance:**
1. Governance rule alignment
2. Documented origin
3. Context verification
4. Challenge availability

**Theory (not yet confirmed):** These conditions may combine to create authority acceptance. No single condition appears sufficient alone.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM (observable pattern, but Acceptance stage not documented; may be implicit)
