Discovery-Driven Strategic Domain Modeling for Constitutional Digital Governance:

Abstract

This paper presents a discovery-driven Domain-Driven Design (DDD) investigation conducted during the architecture of a constitutional digital governance platform. Beginning with an initial hypothesis centered on an Evidence Context, the investigation evolved through successive rounds of responsibility analysis, invariant discovery, decision ownership analysis, stress testing, and governance review. The study demonstrates how architectural stress testing repeatedly invalidated premature domain classifications and revealed deeper concepts including Verification, Legitimacy, Authority, and Recognition. The findings suggest that constitutional governance domains exhibit layered conceptual structures that cannot be adequately understood through traditional bounded-context identification alone. The paper proposes a discovery-driven methodology for strategic domain modeling in complex governance systems and discusses implications for digital elections, constitutional trust, and software architecture governance.
