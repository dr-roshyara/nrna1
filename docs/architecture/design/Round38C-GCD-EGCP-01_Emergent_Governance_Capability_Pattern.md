## Part 1 — Governance Recursion Point (GRP)

The phenomenon 38C called a "residual" is renamed for the capability phase. 38C was analysing threats; "residual risk" was correct there. GCD discovers capabilities; here the same thing is a **structural property of single-source constitutionalism**, not a threat.

**GRP-01 (emergent observation — scoped to NRNA evidence):**
> Every governance safeguard discovered so far **within the NRNA single-source constitutional architecture** contains one or more **Governance Recursion Points** — places where a stage of the governance lifecycle must be applied to *itself*. Within a single source these GRPs **cannot be eliminated**; they can only be **surrounded by safeguards and made observable.**
>
> *Evidence base: one architecture (NRNA), three safeguards (S-1, S-2, S-4). NOT generalized to "all single-source architectures" — that claim requires external research. Answer to "how many architectures analysed?" is currently: one.*

**GRP is a phenomenon *within* EGCP — not a parallel construct.** It is a structural property that may emerge **at any stage** of the lifecycle (in the three cases observed, at the Authority and Challenge stages). EGCP is the lifecycle; GRP is the point where a stage of that lifecycle must recurse on itself.

The three Pass-1 catalogs each surfaced one and the same GRP, wearing different clothes:

| Safeguard | The recursion | Capability that surrounds it |
|-----------|---------------|------------------------------|
| S-1 | Who entrenches the entrenchment? | GC-S1-06 Entrenchment Self-Protection |
| S-2 | Who appoints the appointers? | GC-S2-01 Appointment Distribution |
| S-4 | Who reviews the reviewer? | GC-S4-04 Independent Review Assembly |

These are **not three problems**; they are **one invariant** (the appointment / amendment / review faces of the Meta-CVI the 38C-15 ruling recorded as "managed, not eliminated"). GRP is the GCD-phase name for it.

## Part 2 — The emerging lifecycle (EGCP-01)

Each safeguard's capabilities decompose into the same five-stage grammar:

```
AUTHORITY  →  EXERCISE  →  OBSERVATION  →  CHALLENGE  →  CORRECTION
```

Mapping the three Pass-1 catalogs onto it:

| Stage | S-1 (Amendment) | S-2 (Appointment) | S-4 (Challenge) |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Authority** | GC-S1-01 Classification (what is protected) | GC-S2-01 Distribution, GC-S2-02 Cohort-Limiting | GC-S4-04 Independent Review Assembly |

## Part 3 — The deep structure (why the pattern and the GRP are the same thing)

Two observations fall out of the table, stated neutrally:

- **The Challenge (and Correction) stage appears *reusable across safeguard classes*.** S-1 and S-2 both route their Challenge and Correction through the *same* capabilities currently catalogued under S-4 (GC-S4-01/02/06). Recorded neutrally: **the Challenge stage appears reusable** — *not* "S-4 is special." (In DDD terms, much later, a reusable Challenge stage might become a *supporting* governance capability used by several safeguards. Not asserted now; recorded as an observation only.)
- **The GRP is the self-application point inside the lifecycle.** Whatever provides the Challenge stage cannot itself be challenged from outside a single source (who challenges the challenger?). That self-application point *is* the GRP.

So EGCP-01 and GRP-01 are not two patterns: **GRP is the point *inside* EGCP where a stage must recurse on itself.** The lifecycle closes for every safeguard *except* there. The safeguards' job is not to close it (impossible under single source) but to *surround and observe* it.

This also explains the recurring cross-couplings already logged:
- **"Detection → standing-to-act"** (GC-S1-07, GC-S2-06 → GC-S4-01/02) = the *Observation → Challenge* edge of the lifecycle, shared because S-4 owns the Challenge stage.
- **"Distribute the authority"** (GC-S1-05 ↔ GC-S2-01) = the *Authority* stage appearing in two safeguards.

## Part 4 — How EGCP-01 is used in GCD-04 / GCD-05

For S-3 (Jurisdiction) and S-5 (Finality), Pass 1 asks one extra question per safeguard:

> *How does this safeguard instantiate Authority → Exercise → Observation → Challenge → Correction, and where is its GRP?*

**Confirmation rule (tightened). EGCP-01 graduates from observation to confirmed meta-model only if ALL hold:**
1. S-3 instantiates the lifecycle, **AND**
2. S-5 instantiates the lifecycle, **AND**
3. **No safeguard requires an additional lifecycle stage.** (E.g., if S-5 introduces an *Appeal* stage between Challenge and Correction, EGCP **changes and must be revised** — it does not silently absorb the new stage.)

If any condition fails, that mismatch is a **first-class finding**: either different safeguard classes need different patterns, or the lifecycle has more/fewer than five stages. Either outcome is informative; the test is cheap.
