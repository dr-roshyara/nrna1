## Part 1 — Current Lean

**The lean is conditional, and it is weak.**

The program does **not** lean to "Family B is the answer." On the complete evidence chain (38C-14), the honest state is:

```text
Family A  = strongest THEORETICAL / structural guarantee
            (eliminates multi-root CERD and Meta-CVI unconditionally; 38C-14 Part 4)

Family B  = strongest COMPARATIVE precedent
            (all 8 comparative mechanisms support Functional Independence; F-08)

The ruling-blocking question is NOT "which is better in the abstract."
It is: Is NRNA sufficiently unusual — founding stage, no institutional
history, no judicial culture, Meta-CVI inherent, F-4 permanent — that the
comparative baseline (B) does NOT transfer, making A constitutionally
NECESSARY?
```

**Terminology precision (do not mischaracterize the families).** This is **not** "distributed risk (A) vs centralized risk (B)." Both families distribute constitutional functions; they differ in *how the trust anchor is distributed*:

```text
Family A = trust anchor distributed STRUCTURALLY (independent constitutional sources)
Family B = trust anchor distributed FUNCTIONALLY (one source; operational/interpretive independence)
```

Calling B "centralized" is inaccurate and will draw legitimate objection from constitutional scholars, since every mature constitutional court is single-source yet genuinely independent (F-08). The axis is *structural vs functional distribution of the anchor*, not *distributed vs centralized*.

**Stated as a falsifiable hypothesis:**

> *Family B (Functional Independence) is constitutionally sufficient for OQ-38B05-05 **if and only if** (i) the comparative constitutional baseline transfers to NRNA's institutional development stage, **and** (ii) the five minimum conditions for Functional-Independence sufficiency (38C-13A Part F) can be constitutionally designed within Family B — including FI-B-03 (PAN resistance) despite MA's 15 appointment-bearing functions.*
>
> *If either condition fails, Family A (Structural Independence) becomes constitutionally necessary — **either permanently, or until the missing preconditions are constitutionally established (phased model).***

**Reframing the pivot (per senior-architect challenge).** The question is *not* the binary "does Family B transfer?" — that smuggles in a yes/no when the honest answer may be "not yet." The deeper, correctly-framed question is:

```text
What minimum constitutional trust anchor / preconditions must exist
before Functional Independence (B) becomes trustworthy for NRNA —
and can those preconditions be sequenced over time?
```

This admits a **third hypothesis branch** that is neither pure A nor pure B:

> **Phased model:** Family A (or an A-equivalent structural anchor) during constitutional infancy → transition to Family B once defined institutional-maturity preconditions are constitutionally established. (Latent in 38C-14 Part 10, Q4 phased-ruling branch; never fully developed.)

So the hypothesis pivots on the **two existing ruling-blocking judgment calls** — *no new judgment call is created, and the stopping rule is preserved:*

- **JC-03 (transferability):** does the comparative baseline reach NRNA's institutional stage? This may resolve in three ways:
  1. **Transferable immediately** → Family B viable now.
  2. **Not transferable** → Family A necessary.
  3. **Transferable only after defined constitutional preconditions / trust-anchor conditions are established** → phased A→B.

  The third is a possible **outcome** of JC-03, not a separate question. It does **not** reopen 38C discovery.
- **JC-05 (designability):** can the five conditions — especially FI-B-03 PAN resistance — be built within Family B?

Both remain unresolved. That is precisely what external validation must inform. **The reframing — from "does Functional Independence transfer?" to "what must exist before Functional Independence becomes trustworthy for NRNA?" — changes how JC-03 is read, not how many judgment calls exist.**

NRNA has none. The comparative record proves Functional Independence *can* work; it does not prove it *will* work at NRNA's stage (38C-14 Part 7, transferability caveat).

## Part 3 — Evidence Supporting Family B

- **F-08:** All eight comparative mechanisms (Germany Art. 79(3), India Basic Structure, separation of powers, constitutional courts, protected-core models, non-merger doctrine, constitutional identity, Ackerman) produce evidence toward Functional Independence. None require Structural Independence as a baseline.
- **38C-14 Part 5 (strongest claim):** Constitutional independence has never meant independence of *source* — it has meant independence of *operation and judgment*. Requiring source-level independence imposes a higher standard than any comparative system has found necessary.
- **Proportionality:** Family A creates three AA-01-class pre-constitutional legitimacy questions (one per new sovereign); Family B leaves one (MA). A buys structural certainty at the cost of three new foundational questions.
- **F-09:** Severity ranking corrects 38C-13's over-penalization of A but also shows Meta-CVI is *partially manageable*, not automatically fatal.

## Part 4 — Evidence Supporting Family A

- **F-11:** The current NRNA design satisfies **none** of the five minimum conditions for Functional-Independence sufficiency. The gap is substantial, not marginal.
- **F-10 + 38C-13B:** Meta-CVI is inherent to Family B and **not eliminable** under Strategy B (only partially managed). Strategy C does not add independent Meta-CVI protection at Stages 3–4.
- **38C-14 Part 4:** Family A eliminates multi-root CERD *and* Meta-CVI unconditionally, and provides cross-root verification under three independent sovereigns.
- **Transferability caveat (JC-03):** No comparative mechanism operated without institutional history, legal tradition, and judicial culture — the exact resources NRNA lacks.
- **F-03 / F-4 / TM-39:** Independence Illusion is a permanent program-level FAIL risk regardless of strategy; a structurally grounded design reduces dependence on sustained interpretive integrity that F-4 puts at risk.

**The load-bearing condition (condition 5).** The five minimum conditions (38C-13A Part F) are not equal. Conditions 1–4 are governance engineering — appointment rules, tenure, finality, amendment thresholds, jurisdiction — all designable inside a single constitutional source. **Condition 5 is different in kind:** it requires that someone be able to say *"you are not independent enough"* from a vantage point the checked body cannot control, and inside a single-source architecture every possible challenger ultimately traces to the same sovereign.

The precise, honest formulation — the strongest argument against Family B — is therefore: under Family B there is **no *structurally* external vantage point.** (Deliberately *not* "no genuinely external vantage point" — constitutional courts, supreme courts, election commissions, and auditors-general are all created by the order they supervise and are nonetheless treated as independent. Family B rests on exactly that proposition: that *functional* independence can substitute for *structural* independence.)

Conditions 1–4 are not in doubt. **Condition 5 is where Family B must prove itself**, and whether the functional-for-structural substitution is sufficient for a founding-stage diaspora constitution with no institutional history is exactly what JC-02 (Meta-CVI fatal or manageable?) and JC-05 (is condition 5 designable within Family B?) leave open. This annotation flags which condition is load-bearing; it introduces no new judgment call.

## Part 6 — Falsification Criteria (PRE-REGISTERED — BINDING)

These are fixed now, before any external input is collected. 38C-14A and the 38C-15 ruling must report against each.

**External validation CONFIRMS the B-sufficiency hypothesis if:**
> A clear majority of independent experts, reasoning without the program's vocabulary, conclude that **functional independence + strong constitutional protection (entrenchment, appointment insulation, interpretive finality) is sufficient** for a founding-stage diaspora body — i.e., they treat the transferability concern (JC-03) as manageable through design.

**External validation FORCES Family A if:**
> A clear majority of independent experts conclude that **founding-stage organizations lacking mature external institutions (courts, free press, rival parties) require structural independence** for election oversight — i.e., they treat the comparative baseline as *not* transferring to NRNA's stage.

**External validation SUPPORTS A PHASED MODEL if:**
> A clear majority of independent experts conclude that **functional independence becomes safe only after specific preconditions exist** (institutional maturity, accumulated precedent, external scrutiny, capacity) — i.e., B is not wrong, but it is not yet safe, and a structural anchor is appropriate until those preconditions are constitutionally established.

**External validation DISSOLVES the question if:**
> A clear majority of independent experts conclude that **the risk is real but not constitutionally significant** — that ordinary procedural protection suffices and the program has over-modeled a theoretical concern.

**Disposition rules:**
- *Confirms B* → 38C-15 may rule Family B viable subject to the five conditions; transferability treated as a design task.
- *Forces A* → 38C-15 weights Family A as constitutionally necessary at the founding stage; comparative baseline treated as non-controlling for NRNA.
- *Phased* → 38C-15 considers an A→B path: structural anchor now, defined maturity preconditions, constitutional transition criteria. Requires the ARB to specify the preconditions and who certifies they are met.
- *Dissolves* → 38C-15 reconsiders whether OQ-38B05-05 requires a structural answer at all.
- *No clear majority / split* → the split itself is recorded as evidence that JC-01/JC-03 is a genuine constitutional value judgment the ARB must make without external cover.

**Note on inference.** External validators are **not** asked to vote A / B / phased / dissolve. They answer diagnostic questions only (see `04_Expert_Validation_Questionnaire.md`). The mapping from their diagnoses to these four outcomes is performed by the program in 38C-14A, against this pre-registered key — so the classification rule is fixed *before* the answers are seen.

**Anti-bias commitment:** A "dissolves" or "forces A" outcome is an equally valid and equally welcome result. The program commits to reporting disconfirming expert input at the same prominence as confirming input in 38C-14A.
