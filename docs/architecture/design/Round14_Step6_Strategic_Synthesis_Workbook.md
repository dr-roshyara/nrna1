## Candidate Structures and Their Origins

### Structure A: Linear Hierarchy

```
Governance
    ↓
Authority
    ↓
Verification
```

With Evidence, Trust, Consensus, Decision Lineage supporting.

**Origin of This Candidate:**

- Step 5 identified some directional relationships (Governance → Authority as candidate)
- Governance → Verification as strong relationship suggests possible hierarchy
- Authority-dependent-on-Governance and Verification-dependent-on-Authority patterns observed
- Step 1 decision flow shows sequential stages that could map to hierarchy
- Suggests: Is there a linear progression from rule-definition through authority-exercise to legitimacy-validation?

---

### Structure B: Verification-Centric

```
Governance → Verification
Authority emerges from successful verification
```

**Origin of This Candidate:**

- Step 5 shows Governance → Verification as strongest relationship
- Step 2A: Alternative explanations (Verification, Evidence, Trust) partially explain Authority
- Step 3: Both Verification and Evidence show high explanatory power
- Authority acceptance varies with verification outcomes (Step 2)
- Suggests: What if verification success is what creates authority rather than authority being independent?

---

### Structure C: Governance with Capabilities

```
Governance (Strategic Layer)
├── Authority (Unknown: Strategic or Capability)
├── Verification (Capability)
└── Supporting (Evidence, Trust, Consensus, Lineage)
```

**Origin of This Candidate:**

- Step 4 classifies concepts as different types (Strategic, Capability, Asset)
- Step 5 shows bidirectional/unclear relationships rather than hierarchy
- Multiple concepts at same level might be more accurate than linear
- Suggests: What if different concept types coexist rather than layer?

---

### Structure D: Governance Only (Strategic)

```
Governance (the only Strategic Concept)

Everything else = supporting mechanisms or operational implementations
```

**Origin of This Candidate:**

- Step 3 shows Governance as consistently foundational
- Step 5 shows all concepts reference Governance
- Step 4 classifies other concepts as Capability, Asset, Social Property (not Strategic)
- Could all other concepts be implementations rather than strategies?
- Suggests: What if Governance is the only strategic concept and everything else serves it?

---

### Structure E: No Coherent Structure Yet

Evidence does not yet cohere into recognizable strategic structure.

**Origin of This Candidate:**

- Step 4 shows concepts are incompatible types
- Step 5 shows relationships have low confidence
- Multiple contradictions exist in every other structure
- Unresolved questions outnumber resolved relationships
- Suggests: Is the evidence insufficient or misaligned?

## Unexplained Behavior Analysis (Core Findings)

These behaviors remain unexplained by all candidate structures. They represent where the domain's missing concepts likely exist.

### Critical Unexplained: Permission-to-Power Translation

**Observation:**

Governance defines permission. Authority exercises power. The mechanism by which permission becomes power is unclear.

**Current Explanations Insufficient:**

- Governance says "this role may decide" but doesn't explain why their decisions bind others
- Authority could explain this, but Authority itself is undefined
- Verification could explain it (verified decisions bind), but unverified authority also binds
- No candidate structure fully explains permission-to-power translation

**Implication:**

Missing concept: What transforms permission into binding power?

---

### Critical Unexplained: Authority Acceptance Mechanisms

**Observation:**

Some authority claims are accepted by others. Some are rejected. Same Governance scope, same Verification status, different outcomes.

**Current Explanations Insufficient:**

- Governance defines permissions (doesn't explain selective acceptance)
- Authority could explain it (claimed authority creates acceptance), but circular
- Verification explains legitimacy check (not acceptance)
- Trust could explain it (people accept trusted authorities), but Trust is unexamined

**Implication:**

Missing concept or mechanism: What creates acceptance of authority claims?

---

### Critical Unexplained: Trust Formation

**Observation:**

Trust relationships exist. Trust varies by actor pair, context, and history.

**Current Explanations Insufficient:**

- Governance doesn't create trust; it defines rules
- Verification could create trust (verified sources trusted more), but unverified trust exists
- No mechanism in candidate structures explains initial trust
- Trust appears to be independent phenomenon, not derivative

**Implication:**

Missing concept: How does initial trust form in systems?

---

### Critical Unexplained: Consensus Formation and Mechanics

**Observation:**

Some decisions require consensus. Consensus emerges from multiple actors. Consensus can override individual authority.

**Current Explanations Insufficient:**

- Governance defines when consensus is required, but not how it forms
- No structure explains consensus formation mechanism
- Consensus appears to operate independently of Authority or Governance hierarchy

**Implication:**

Missing concept or mechanism: What enables and sustains consensus?

---

### Critical Unexplained: Why Evidence Matters to Verification

**Observation:**

Verification requires Evidence. But the relationship between Evidence quality and Verification outcome is unclear.

**Current Explanations Insufficient:**

- Governance defines verification standards (doesn't explain Evidence role)
- Verification uses Evidence, but how and why?
- No structure addresses Evidence as strategic input vs. operational resource

**Implication:**

Missing concept: What is the decision logic that connects Evidence → Verification outcome?

---

### Additional Unexplained Behaviors

- **Governance-Trust Interaction** — How do rules interact with trust relationships?
- **Context-Specific Authority Variation** — Why does same role show different authority in different contexts?
- **Authority-Consensus Relationship** — Are these competing mechanisms or complementary?
- **Lineage Integration** — Is Lineage recording, emergence, or independent structure?

## Current Interpretation

Evidence supports:

- Governance is foundational and necessary
- Verification has independent explanatory power distinct from Governance
- Authority shows some independence but constraints from Governance
- Evidence, Trust, and Consensus are involved but not yet well-explained

No candidate structure explains all observed behaviors without introducing contradictions or requiring assumptions.

All structures handle some behaviors well while leaving others unexplained.

---

## What This Suggests

Rather than selecting among candidate structures, the evidence suggests:

**The domain contains concepts not yet identified.**

The unexplained behavior register (Permission-to-Power Translation, Authority Acceptance, Trust Formation, Consensus Mechanics, Evidence-Verification Logic) likely points to:

- Missing strategic concepts
- Incomplete understanding of existing concepts
- Relationships not yet discovered

These unexplained behaviors should become the focus of Step 7 investigation.
