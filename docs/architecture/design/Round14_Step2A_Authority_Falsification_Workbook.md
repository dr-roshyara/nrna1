# Round 14 Step 2A — Authority Falsification Workbook

**Strategic Design: Evidence-Based Model Stress Testing**

**Date:** 2026-06-05  
**Status:** Exploratory Analysis  
**Objective:** Investigate whether alternative concepts could explain behaviors currently attributed to Authority, without reaching premature conclusions

---

## Mission

Step 2 identified Authority as potentially a dependent specialization of Governance.

This workbook explores whether alternative concepts might explain Authority's candidate behaviors.

Goal: Document evidence FOR and AGAINST each alternative explanation.

**Important:** This is exploratory work. It asks questions, not answers them.

---

## Methodology

For each Authority behavior, this analysis:

1. **Identifies** the observed behavior
2. **Proposes** alternative explanation candidates
3. **Documents** evidence supporting each candidate
4. **Documents** evidence contradicting each candidate
5. **Assesses** confidence in each candidate
6. **Lists** open questions

**We do NOT conclude which explanation is correct.**

We document what we know and don't know about each candidate.

---

## Behavior 1: Authority Acceptance

**Observation:**

Some authority claims are accepted by other actors; some are rejected.

**Candidate Explanation A: Verification**

Authority claims succeed when verified as legitimate, fail when not.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Step 2 shows Governance requires verification
- Verification explicitly checks legitimacy
- Acceptance timing aligns with verification completion

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- "Acceptance" differs linguistically from "verification passes"
- Some accepted decisions later fail (acceptance ≠ permanent verification)
- Actors accept decisions before full verification (acceptance precedes verification)
- Rejected decisions may later be accepted (verification can change)

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- Can Verification explain why same authority claim is accepted in one context, rejected in another?
- Does Verification explain who decides verification standards?
- Can Verification explain gradual acceptance (partial acceptance over time)?

---

**Candidate Explanation B: Evidence Quality**

Authority claims succeed when sufficient evidence supports them.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Authority claims are backed by documentation
- Weaker evidence sometimes leads to rejection
- Evidence gaps trigger rejection

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- "Sufficient evidence" is not objectively measurable
- Same evidence level accepted in one context, rejected in another
- Evidence collection itself requires authority to validate what counts as evidence

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- How is "evidence quality" determined?
- Who decides what evidence is sufficient?
- Can Evidence concept stand alone, or does it depend on Authority determining sufficiency?

---

**Candidate Explanation C: Trust Relationships**

Authority claims succeed when trusted actors support them.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Trusted actors' decisions are more likely accepted
- Untrusted actors' decisions are more likely rejected
- Trust relationships appear to precede acceptance

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Some untrusted actors' claims are accepted if well-evidenced
- Some trusted actors' claims are rejected if poorly reasoned
- Trust can be context-specific while Authority appears broader

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- Is Trust independent from Authority, or does Authority create Trust?
- How is initial Trust established in new relationships?
- Can Trust explain why the same actor is trusted in one domain, untrusted in another?

---

**Current Assessment of Behavior 1:**

Multiple candidates could partially explain Acceptance.

No single candidate fully explains all observations.

**Likely:** Acceptance is multi-causal (Verification + Evidence + Trust together).

**Unknown:** Whether Authority is a fourth independent causal factor, or whether Authority is a redescription of combinations of the above.

## Behavior 2: Authority Context Specificity

**Observation:**

Same role with same formal authority produces different de facto authority in different contexts.

**Candidate Explanation A: Context-Specific Governance Rules**

Governance rules are context-sensitive. Same role has different permissions in different contexts.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Different contexts have different rule sets
- Governance explicitly defines context boundaries
- Different rules produce observed different authority patterns

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Some authority variation occurs where rules appear identical
- Same role, same rules, different effectiveness suggests non-rule explanation
- Rules do not explicitly prescribe all observed authority variations

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Open Questions:**

- Do Governance rules explicitly define all context variations, or is something else operating?
- Can Governance rules explain why the same rule produces different outcomes contextually?
- Are contexts themselves defined by Governance, or does something define contexts?

---

**Candidate Explanation B: Trust Variation by Context**

Same role trusted in one context, distrusted in another, independent of Governance rules.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Observed acceptance patterns vary by context
- Same role treated differently in Membership vs. Election contexts
- Trust relationships differ by context

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- No evidence that trust differs where rules are identical
- Trust patterns could follow from different Governance rules
- Trust alone does not explain all authority variations

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- Can Trust explain context specificity, or does Context explain Trust specificity?
- Is Trust stable within a context, or variable?
- How is context-specific trust established initially?

---

**Current Assessment of Behavior 2:**

Context specificity is best explained by context-specific Governance rules.

Possible secondary factor: context-specific Trust.

**Unknown:** Whether these explain all context variations, or whether Authority introduces independent context-specific logic.

## Behavior 3: Authority Delegation Chains

**Observation:**

Authority flows through delegation chains. Delegated authority inherits properties from original authority.

**Candidate Explanation A: Governance Permission Chains**

Governance permits delegation. Permissions flow through delegates. Delegated permissions inherit constraints from original permissions.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Governance explicitly permits delegation
- Delegated permissions do inherit constraints
- Permission chains align with observed delegation patterns

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- "Authority flows" differs linguistically from "permission passes"
- Delegated authority sometimes operates differently from original permission
- Delegation introduces new authority not explicitly in original permission

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Open Questions:**

- Does Governance fully define how permissions flow through delegates, or does something guide the flow?
- Can delegated authority exceed original authority scope?
- How are delegation limits determined?

---

**Candidate Explanation B: Trust Chains**

Delegation creates trust chains. Delegates trusted by originating authority; others transitively trust delegates.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Delegation appears to convey trust
- Acceptance patterns follow delegation chains
- Trust appears transitive through delegation

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Trust alone does not explain all delegation behavior
- Some delegated authority operates without visible trust relationships
- Formal delegation can create authority independent of trust

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- Is Trust independent from Governance in delegation, or derivative from it?
- How is trust validated in delegation chains?
- Can delegation create authority without trust?

---

**Current Assessment of Behavior 3:**

Delegation chains are explained by Governance permission flow.

Possible secondary factor: Trust delegation.

**Unknown:** Whether Authority adds independent explanation beyond Governance permissions flowing and Trust supporting.

## Behavior 4: Authority Recognition

**Observation:**

Some authority claims are "recognized" by others. Recognition differs from permission.

**Candidate Explanation A: Verification + Observable Execution**

Authority is recognized when a permission-holder is verified legitimate AND observed executing their decision.

Recognition = verified permission holder in visible action.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Recognition follows from verification
- Observable execution makes authority visible
- Combined, they explain when authority is "known" to others

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Recognition can precede observable execution
- Some executed decisions are not recognized (execution alone insufficient)
- Some recognized authorities never execute (authority exists before action)

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- What makes Verification + Execution result in Recognition in some cases but not others?
- Does Recognition require Consensus or just Verification + Execution?
- Can an authority be "recognized" without ever executing?

---

**Candidate Explanation B: Consensus Recognition**

Authority is recognized when relevant stakeholders agree it is legitimate.

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Recognition appears to require stakeholder agreement
- Unanimous recognition differs from contested recognition
- Recognition can change with stakeholder opinion

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Some authorities are recognized without explicit consensus
- Some consensus decisions are not about Authority
- Authority appears to exist independent of current stakeholder agreement

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM

**Open Questions:**

- Is Consensus required for Recognition, or is Verification + Execution sufficient?
- How is the "consensus group" determined?
- Can Governance define who must agree for Recognition?

---

**Current Assessment of Behavior 4:**

Recognition is multi-causal: likely Verification + Observable Execution + Consensus together.

**Unknown:** Whether Authority explains how these combine, or whether they operate independently.

## Behavior 5: Authority Permission vs. Authority Effectiveness

**Observation:**

Permission exists (Governance) but authority effectiveness varies. Same permission produces different authority in different situations.

**Candidate Explanation A: Permission Exists but Constraints Vary**

Not that authority is independent. Rather, Governance constraints operate differently in different contexts.

- Permission granted (Governance)
- Constraints vary by context (Governance contextual rules)
- Authority effectiveness = permission minus constraints in context

**Evidence Supporting:**

- Governance does vary constraints by context
- Different contexts do show different effective authority
- Constraints appear to explain the variation

**Evidence Contradicting:**

- Constraints alone do not explain all variation
- Same constraints sometimes produce different effective authority
- Something beyond constraints affects whether permission becomes effective

**Confidence in This Candidate:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Open Questions:**

- Can constraint variation fully explain all effective authority variation?
- What determines which constraints apply in a given situation?
- Are there authority variations not explainable by constraint variation?

---

**Current Assessment of Behavior 5:**

Permission-to-effectiveness gap is likely constraint variation + context factors.

**Unknown:** Whether this gap requires Authority as independent explanation.

## Critical Observation

**None of the candidate explanations eliminate Authority.**

But also: **None of them require Authority.**

This is precisely the right state for model stress testing.

The question remains open: **Can all observed behaviors be explained without introducing Authority as a strategic layer?**

That question requires deeper investigation, not premature closure.

## Open Architectural Questions

3. **Are Trust, Verification, Evidence, and Consensus themselves fully understood, or are they also candidates for decomposition?**

4. **Could the apparent "need for Authority" actually represent gaps in our understanding of Verification, Evidence, or Trust?**
