# Round 14 Step 2 — Governance ↔ Authority Analysis

**Strategic Design Exploration: Explanatory Power Testing**

**Date:** 2026-06-05  
**Status:** Step 2 Analysis  
**Objective:** Determine whether Authority possesses independent explanatory power or is derived from Governance

---

## Mission

Analyze the relationship between Governance and Authority to understand their explanatory power.

The Layered Model assumes Authority contributes distinct value beyond Governance.

This analysis tests whether Authority explains domain behaviors that Governance alone cannot explain.

---

## Primary Question

**What does Authority explain that Governance does not?**

**What does Governance explain that Authority does not?**

---

## Competing Hypotheses

### Hypothesis A: Authority is Independent

**Statement:**

Authority is a genuinely independent strategic concept explaining domain behaviors that Governance rules do not explain.

**What would support this:**

Authority demonstrates unique explanatory coverage in the Evidence Matrix
Removing Authority leaves specific behaviors unexplained
Authority operates according to its own logic independent of Governance rules

---

### Hypothesis B: Authority is Governance Applied

**Statement:**

Authority is not a separate concept but rather Governance rules being exercised in specific contexts.

**What would support this:**

Every authority claim is explained by referring back to Governance permissions
Authority patterns can be derived entirely from how Governance rules are applied
Removing Authority does not leave any behaviors unexplained that Governance does not also explain

---

## Observation 1: Authority Claims

**Observation:**

In analyzed constitutional decisions, authority is claimed by:
- Membership context (members vote on membership) 
- Election context (election officials conduct voting)
- Results context (certifiers declare results)
- Appeals context (appeals bodies override prior decisions)
- Governance context (rule-makers establish new governance structures)

Each claim references an authority source.

**Interpretation:**

Authority appears to be exercised in multiple contexts, each with different sources.

**Assumption:**

Authority sources are identifiable and traceable (confidence: HIGH).

**Alternative Explanations:**

Authority claims could be:
- Simply procedural steps in Governance (not a separate concept)
- Contextual expressions of role assignments
- Names for decision-making power without independent meaning

---

## Observation 2: Authority Scope

**Observation:**

Prior analysis identified that authority claims have boundaries:
- Membership bodies cannot make election rules
- Election officials cannot reverse results
- Results certifiers cannot change voting eligibility
- Appeals bodies cannot create new governance structures

**Interpretation:**

Authority appears to be constrained by rules about what each authority may do.

**Assumption:**

Authority scope is not arbitrary but follows from constitutional structure (confidence: HIGH).

**Alternative Explanations:**

Authority scope constraints could be:
- Implicit in Governance rules (Governance fully explains scope)
- Emergent from organizational structure (organization, not authority concept)
- Procedural limitations rather than authority limitations

---

## Observation 3: Authority Legitimacy

**Observation:**

In analyzed decisions, authority claims are treated as:
- Valid when they follow from established sources
- Invalid when they diverge from established sources
- Challengeable when legitimacy is questioned
- Subject to reversal when deemed illegitimate

**Interpretation:**

Authority requires legitimacy determination. Legitimacy is not automatic from holding a role.

**Assumption:**

Legitimacy validation occurs separately from role assignment (confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH).

**Alternative Explanations:**

Legitimacy could be:
- Simply part of Governance verification requirements (Governance explains it)
- A procedural formality with no independent meaning
- Verification applied to ensure Governance compliance

---

## Observation 4: Authority Delegation

**Observation:**

Prior analysis identified delegation patterns:
- Membership authority can be delegated to subcommittees
- Election authority can be delegated to regional officials
- Results authority can be delegated to independent certifiers
- Appeals authority can be delegated to higher bodies

Delegated authority is treated as derived authority: it inherits from the delegating authority.

**Interpretation:**

Authority can flow through delegation chains. The chain itself appears meaningful.

**Assumption:**

Authority delegation creates a distinct concept (derived authority) beyond role assignment (confidence: MEDIUM).

**Alternative Explanations:**

Delegation could be:
- Simply role assignment by Governance (not a separate concept)
- Procedural routing of decisions
- Governance permission to act in someone else's role

---

## Observation 5: Authority and Decision Binding

**Observation:**

In analyzed decisions, some decisions are treated as binding on other actors:
- Some decisions enforce themselves (others comply)
- Some decisions require enforcement mechanisms
- Some decisions are accepted without enforcement
- Some decisions are rejected despite authority claims

Not all decisions by those holding authority-like roles are treated as binding.

**Interpretation:**

Something determines which decisions become binding and which do not. Multiple concepts could explain this: Authority (the exerciser has power), Governance (rules require compliance), Verification (legitimacy determined), or Consensus (collective agreement).

**Assumption:**

Decision binding is not automatic from role assignment (confidence: HIGH).

The mechanism that creates binding power is not yet explained (confidence: MEDIUM).

**Alternative Explanations:**

Acceptance could be:
- Part of Governance verification (Governance explains acceptance)
- Organizational compliance mechanism
- Social rather than constitutional phenomenon

## Unexplained or Partially Explained Behaviors

Candidate areas where Authority and Governance together still leave questions unanswered:

### Authority Recognition

**Observation:**

Actors appear to distinguish between "claims of authority" and "recognized authority."

Some authority claims are accepted; others are rejected.

**Current Explanations:**

Governance: defines who may claim authority
Authority: explains why a specific claim is recognized (if independent)

**Remaining Uncertainty:**

What determines whether an authority claim is recognized? What creates the gap between claiming authority and being recognized as authoritative? Is this Authority operating, or is this Verification, or something else entirely?

---

### Authority Enforcement

**Observation:**

Some authority decisions create compliance; others require enforcement mechanisms; some are simply ignored.

Not all decisions by recognized authorities produce the same compliance patterns.

**Current Explanations:**

Governance: may define enforcement requirements
Authority: may explain compliance (if binding power is Authority's role)

**Remaining Uncertainty:**

What transforms an authority decision into enforcement-backed power? Why do some authority claims generate compliance without enforcement while others require it? Is this the difference between Authority and something else?

---

### Authority Transfer

**Observation:**

Authority can be delegated (decision passes to another actor). But can authority be transferred without delegation (e.g., authority passes from one actor to another without process)?

**Current Explanations:**

Governance: defines delegation process
Authority: explains how delegated authority operates (if independent)

**Remaining Uncertainty:**

Can authority exist independent of delegation/transfer process? Can an actor gain authority without explicit transfer? Does Authority explain voluntary transfer separate from Governance delegation rules?

---

### Authority Conflict

**Observation:**

In some cases, two apparently legitimate authorities disagree about the same decision.

**Current Explanations:**

Governance: may define conflict resolution mechanisms
Authority: may determine which authority prevails (if Authority has independent logic)

**Remaining Uncertainty:**

How are authority conflicts resolved? Does Governance fully determine the outcome, or does something about Authority determine it? Can a conflict between legitimate authorities remain unresolved?

## Explanatory Power Assessment

### Assessment A: What becomes unexplained if Governance is removed?

**Observed behaviors that would lose explanation:**

1. **Authority scope boundaries** — Without Governance rules defining scope limits, no source defines what authorities may do

2. **Legitimacy requirements** — Without Governance verification requirements, no standard exists for what makes authority legitimate

3. **Permission structure** — Without Governance definitions, no basis exists for what authorities are permitted to exercise

4. **Context independence** — Without Governance rules, no mechanism prevents authorities from exceeding their defined roles

**Current Interpretation:**

Governance provides essential structural definition that Authority alone does not provide. Authority assumes Governance exists.

**Confidence:** HIGH

---

### Assessment B: What becomes unexplained if Authority is removed?

**Observed behaviors that would lose explanation (if Authority is independent):**

1. **Permission-to-power gap** — Without Authority concept, what explains how Governance permission becomes actual exercised power? Governance says "this role may decide"; Authority must explain "this person in this role actually decides binding decisions"

2. **Context-specific variation** — Without Authority concept, how does the same Governance rule produce different authority behavior in different contexts? Same rule, different contexts, different patterns suggests something beyond rule application

3. **Delegation chain semantics** — Without Authority concept, does delegation differ meaningfully from permission reassignment? "Authority flows through delegation chains" suggests more than "permissions are passed along"

4. **Recognition asymmetry** — Without Authority concept, why are some authority claims recognized and others rejected? Governance defines legitimacy standards; Authority might explain why those standards produce acceptance in some cases and rejection in others

**Behaviors that might be explained by Governance + Verification alone:**

- Binding power (may emerge from Governance + Verification)
- Acceptance mechanisms (may emerge from Governance compliance)
- Authority conflict resolution (may follow Governance rules)
- Context variation (may follow from different Governance rules per context)

**Current Interpretation:**

Authority may provide independent explanatory value for permission-to-power translation and context-specific authority operation. However, many of Authority's candidate explanations could potentially be explained by Governance + Verification, making the separation uncertain.

**Key Finding:** Governance is necessary for Authority (Authority requires Governance permission/scope). Authority may not be necessary for Governance (Governance can exist as pure rule system). This asymmetry suggests Authority may be a specialization of Governance rather than a peer concept.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM (some Authority-specific behaviors are plausible; others remain explainable by Governance+Verification)

---

### Assessment C: What explanatory precision is lost if both are merged?

**Analysis:**

If merged into single concept:

1. **Rule definition vs. power exercise** — Governance explains "what rules exist"; Authority explains "who exercises power under those rules." Merging creates ambiguity about which is being discussed

2. **Permission vs. legitimacy** — Governance defines permission; Authority validates that permission is legitimate to exercise. Merging conflates permission with legitimacy

3. **Scope vs. context** — Governance defines scope (what authorities may generally do); Authority defines context-specific exercise (how they actually apply it). Merging loses distinction

4. **Role vs. power** — Governance assigns roles; Authority explains the actual power held. Merging makes it unclear whether discussing role or power

**Result:** Separation preserves explanatory precision that merging would lose.

## Synthesis: Does Authority Earn Its Place?

### Critical Asymmetry Finding

**Governance depends on Authority: NO**

Governance can exist as a complete rule system without explaining how those rules become actual power.

**Authority depends on Governance: YES**

Authority claims always remain constrained by Governance permissions and scope.

This asymmetry suggests:

```text
Governance
    = fundamental strategic layer

Authority  
    = dependent specialization layer
```

If this finding survives further analysis, it may mean Authority is not a peer concept to Governance but rather a lens on how Governance operates.

### Current Evidence Suggests

Authority may contribute some explanatory value, but with significant caveats:

- **Permission-to-power gap** may be explained by Authority, or by Governance + Verification + Trust
- **Context specificity** may be explained by Authority, or by different Governance rules in different contexts  
- **Delegation chains** may show Authority operating, or may simply show Governance permissions flowing
- **Authority acceptance** remains poorly explained by either concept alone

### Remaining Uncertainties

1. **Authority Independence** — Does Authority explain phenomena Governance+Verification cannot, or is Authority merely descriptive of how Governance operates?

2. **Verification Role** — Does Verification actually bridge Governance and Authority, making Authority redundant? Or does Authority remain distinct?

3. **Context Specificity** — Is context-specific variation Authority operating independently, or Governance rules being applied differently in different contexts?

4. **Specialization vs. Independence** — Is Authority a peer concept, or a specialized view of Governance in operation?

---

## Open Questions for Step 3

1. **Authority Universality** — Do all five decision types show authority patterns, or only some?

2. **Authority Independence** — Can any authority decision be made without reference to Governance rules?

3. **Authority Coherence** — Do authority patterns across contexts follow a consistent model, or are they context-specific?

4. **Authority Hierarchy** — Is authority layered (delegated authority is distinct from original authority), or flat?

## Competing Interpretations

### Interpretation A: Authority is Independent Layer

**Statement:**

Authority is genuinely independent from Governance, explaining how power is exercised while Governance explains what permissions exist.

**Supporting Evidence:**

- Authority acceptance requires explanation beyond permission
- Authority binding power is distinct from rule definition
- Authority context specificity suggests independent operation
- Delegation chains suggest Authority operates by its own logic

**Contradicting Evidence:**

- Every authority claim still requires Governance permission
- Authority scope is defined by Governance
- Authority legitimacy requires Governance verification
- Some behaviors may be explained entirely by Governance

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

---

### Interpretation B: Authority is Governance Applied

**Statement:**

Authority is not separate from Governance but represents Governance rules being exercised in specific contexts.

**Supporting Evidence:**

- Authority always constrained by Governance scope
- Authority always requires Governance permission
- Authority legitimacy determined by Governance verification
- All authority patterns could theoretically derive from Governance rules

**Contradicting Evidence:**

- Acceptance patterns suggest independent authority concept
- Binding power explanation seems to require Authority
- Context-specific variation suggests more than simple rule application
- Delegation chains suggest Authority operates independently

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

---

### Interpretation C: Authority is Partially Independent

**Statement:**

Authority explains some phenomena independently (acceptance, binding power, some context variations) while remaining dependent on Governance for scope, permission, and verification.

**Supporting Evidence:**

- Some evidence clearly supports Authority independence
- Other evidence shows Authority dependence on Governance
- Mixed results in Evidence Matrix suggest partial independence
- Both concepts needed for complete explanation

**Contradicting Evidence:**

- Unclear where Authority independence ends and Governance takes over
- Risk of double-counting if both concepts partially explain same behavior
- May represent incomplete analysis rather than genuine partial independence

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

---

All three interpretations remain consistent with current evidence. Additional analysis in Steps 3-5 will test these hypotheses further.

## Current Interpretation

Step 2 evidence reveals a critical asymmetry:

```text
Governance
    → can exist as rule system (permission, scope, legitimacy standards)
    → does NOT require Authority explanation

Authority
    → cannot exist without Governance permission/scope
    → explains HOW Governance operates in specific contexts
    → may explain permission-to-power translation
    → may be a specialization rather than a peer concept
```

Authority may contribute some explanatory value, but:
- Many "Authority" behaviors can be explained by Governance + Verification
- Authority's most distinct contribution (permission-to-power translation) remains partially unexplained
- The asymmetric dependency suggests Authority is hierarchically dependent on Governance

This is different from the H-C hypothesis (independent concepts).

It aligns more with a "specialization" model: Authority as the lens on how Governance creates actual power in specific contexts.
