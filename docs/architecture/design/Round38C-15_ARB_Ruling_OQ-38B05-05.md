## Definition adopted by this ruling
> **Functional Independence** is the constitutional property whereby an institution exercises its constitutionally assigned responsibilities free from improper influence, while continuing to derive legal authority from the same sovereign constitutional source.

## Standing of this ruling (nature of the decision)
This resolves OQ-38B05-05 as a **constitutional value judgment exercised under governance authority** — not an evidence-compelled proof.
> The comparative evidence *permits* Option B; it did not *compel* it. Alternative models remain constitutionally coherent but were not selected. ## 1. Question before the Board
Should the three trust roots — **Legitimacy** (who defines rules), **Authenticity** (who verifies evidence), **Temporal** (who records phases) — be **structurally separated** into independent constitutional sources, or may they **share a single sovereign source** protected by strong constitutional safeguards?

## 2. Alternatives Considered
| Opt | Model | Essence |
|-----|-------|---------|
| **A** | Structural Independence | trust roots as constitutionally distinct sovereign sources |
| **B** | Functional Independence | single sovereign source + permanent safeguards (per Definition) |
| **C** | Phased Model | functional now → structural later, gated on maturity milestones |

## 3. Holding (Decision)
**Option B — Functional Independence — is ADOPTED.** The three trust roots shall continue to trace constitutional authority to a single sovereign source; structural separation is **not required**. **Option A and Option C are deselected, not refuted** (A's reservation is retained — §9). **Permanent constitutional safeguards (§5) are adopted** as a condition of the holding.

## 4. Ratio Decidendi
1. **Sufficiency.** Functional independence adequately preserves constitutional autonomy while maintaining a single sovereign source; it avoids the complexity and contestability of multiple sovereign authorities and keeps all governance internal.
2. **Comparative basis (bounded claim).** Across the **eight mechanisms examined** during knowledge discovery (e.g. Germany's eternity clause, India's basic-structure doctrine, separation-of-powers, constitutional-court and election-commission models), functional independence is the **dominant pattern among the systems studied**; **no examined mechanism requires structural independence as a baseline.** *(This is evidence among systems examined — not a claimed universal or worldwide norm.)*
3. **Maturity as risk factor, not mandate.** NRNA is in **early constitutional development** and lacks the surrounding institutional ecology (judicial culture, independent press, rival parties, settled norms) that makes functional independence durable in mature orders. This **does not mandate structural separation**; it mandates **stronger permanent protections** substituting for the institutional maturity not yet present.
4. **Recorded residual.** Within a single sovereign source there is no structurally external vantage point; **Meta-CVI is managed, not eliminated.** Option B is adopted in explicit acceptance of this residual — the standing reason Option A's reservation is retained.

## 5. Constitutional Safeguards (permanent — not transitional, not maturity-gated)
**Constitutional-tier**
- **S-1 Amendment Protection.** Independence, jurisdiction, and interpretive-finality provisions protected at a threshold **exceeding all ordinary amendment procedures.** *(Exact threshold → Governance Capability Discovery; S-1 fixes the requirement, not the number.)*
- **S-5 Finality with Defined Exceptions.** In-jurisdiction rulings are constitutionally final; override requires amendment at the S-1 threshold. Retroactive invalidation on fundamental defect (OQ-38A05-02) remains reserved to the Constitutional Interpretation Chamber.

**Structural-tier**
- **S-2 Appointment Diversity.** No single Assembly cohort or election cycle may appoint a majority of any oversight body; terms staggered, non-renewable, longer than any Assembly term; removal only by supermajority on defined grounds.
- **S-3 Jurisdictional Protection.** Oversight mandate explicitly includes election oversight and trust-function evaluation; not narrowable by ordinary amendment.

**Procedural-tier**
- **S-4 Challenge Pathways + Recusal.** When an oversight body's own independence is challenged, adjudication routes through a distinct review mechanism whose members are **not participants in the challenged proceeding**; outside constitutional actors hold standing to initiate. *(A functional separation of role within the single source — not a separate sovereign.)*

> **Recorded residual risk.** These safeguards **reduce but do not eliminate** the residual risks identified in the 38C series (CERD, Meta-CVI, progressive lawful erosion). Functional Independence remains a **managed-risk**, not a risk-free, model.
