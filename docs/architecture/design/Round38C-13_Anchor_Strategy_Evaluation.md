## Part A — Scope and Analytical Frame

38C-12 identified three external constitutional anchor strategies as constitutional responses to CERD and CVI:

- **Strategy A — Structural Separation (Level 2b):** Three independent sovereign sources for the three trust roots
- **Strategy B — Source-Layer Constraints (Level 2a):** Tier 3 constitutional provision constraining MA's exercise of multi-root authority within Family-B
- **Strategy C — Constitutional Verification Checkpoint:** Procedural mechanism for evaluating whether Full CRC is being approached

This document evaluates each strategy against six required questions. The evaluation is symmetric — each strategy receives equal analytical depth. No strategy is ranked or recommended.

**Critical framing distinction (from 38C-12 and the architect's challenges):**

Two constitutional concepts must be kept separate throughout:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **External Reference** | A constitutionally grounded evaluation mechanism whose independence is not subject to the authority being evaluated |
| **Structural Independence** | A constitutional grounding that does not trace through the authority being evaluated |

A mechanism can provide External Reference without Structural Independence (functional independence within Family-B — Strategy B aim). A mechanism with Structural Independence automatically provides External Reference (Strategy A). A mechanism without either provides Procedural Oversight only (minimal Strategy C). These are genuinely distinct constitutional properties.

## Part B — Strategy A: Structural Separation (Level 2b)

### B.1 — Strategy Description

Three independent sovereign sources are established — one for each trust root (Legitimacy, Authenticity, Temporal). Each root's constitutional grounding does not trace through MA's trust function grants. MA's current role as source-of-source is redistributed or eliminated.

### B.2 — Q1: Does Strategy A Eliminate CERD?

**CERD formulation:** No constitutionally grounded authority exists that can evaluate MA's exercise of its three trust function grants from an independent constitutional vantage point.

Under Strategy A, MA no longer holds all three trust function grants. Each root's sovereign is constitutionally independent of the others. Cross-root evaluation is structurally possible: Legitimacy sovereign can evaluate Authenticity sovereign's constitutional legitimacy from a constitutionally independent vantage point.

**However:** Strategy A introduces three NEW single-root CERDs. Each independent sovereign faces the same structural question CERD poses for MA: can its own constitutional legitimacy be evaluated from an independent vantage point that doesn't trace through its own trust function grants? Under Level 2b, each sovereign's constitutional grounding is assumed to be independent — but that independence itself requires constitutional establishment (three new AA-01-class questions).

**Finding:** Strategy A eliminates MULTI-ROOT CERD (the condition that produces Full CRC from Scenarios A/B/C/D). It does NOT eliminate SINGLE-ROOT CERD for each new sovereign. The net CERD reduction is real but partial — multi-root convergence is structurally impossible; single-root self-referential validation remains a per-sovereign concern.

### B.3 — Q2: Does Strategy A Prevent CRC?

**Full CRC prevention:** Yes — constitutionally structural. Scenarios A/B/C/D from 38C-08 require multi-root convergence. Under Level 2b, each root has an independent source, making multi-root merger constitutionally unreachable through any single-sovereign path. ADR6-INV-01 CO-5 independence is at maximum.

**Per-sovereign CRC:** Possible. Each independent sovereign faces its own potential self-referential certification path — but this is a single-root problem, not a three-root convergence problem. The CO-5 certification outcome (requiring CO-2+CO-3+CO-4 independently satisfied) would still require cross-root coordination, making three-simultaneous-sovereign CRC structurally harder.

**TM-47 interaction:** The AC-31 ratchet (AW-05-07) operates within the Authenticity Root's independent governance. Under Level 2b, AC-31 compromise is contained within that sovereign — it does not automatically propagate to Legitimacy or Temporal roots. TM-47 risk is substantially reduced at the three-root level; a new TM-47 equivalent emerges within each root's governance.

**Finding:** Strategy A prevents Full CRC structurally. It redistributes single-root CRC risk across three sovereign governance domains rather than eliminating it.

### B.4 — Q3: Prevention vs. Detection?

Strategy A is **structural prevention** — the architecture makes Full CRC constitutionally unreachable, not harder to reach. This is the strongest form of constitutional protection.

However: each independent sovereign's governance creates NEW detection challenges — the three sovereigns must coordinate without creating a new convergence point (the coordination mechanism becomes a potential new CERD if it functions as a fourth sovereign authority). Strategy A prevents the known failure while creating constitutional design obligations for inter-sovereign coordination.

### B.5 — Q4: AA-01 Interaction

The original AA-01 question (MA's pre-constitutional legitimacy) becomes THREE separate AA-01-class questions — one for each independent sovereign's pre-constitutional legitimacy. The total pre-constitutional legitimacy burden increases:
- If any one sovereign's AA-01 equivalent is unresolvable, that root's constitutional grounding is challenged
- The other two roots remain independent — preventing full three-root collapse
- But two-root merger scenarios become constitutionally possible if one sovereign's legitimacy collapses

**Finding:** Strategy A DISTRIBUTES AA-01 exposure (three smaller questions instead of one large one) but INCREASES TOTAL AA-01 exposure surface. Whether distributed exposure is constitutionally preferable to concentrated exposure (current architecture) is an ARB determination.

### B.6 — Q5: OQ-38A05-02 Interaction

**OQ-38A05-02 PROTECTED:** Can TS-1 survive post-issuance discovery that AC-31 was false?

Under Level 2b, AC-31 is governed by the Authenticity sovereign, independent of Legitimacy and Temporal sovereigns. A false AC-31 discovered post-TS-1 is constitutionally isolated to the Authenticity root — it does not automatically implicate the Legitimacy or Temporal roots.

**Effect on OQ-38A05-02:** The question's scope may narrow — the finality-validity tension applies to AC-31's independent sovereign governance. But the constitutional question (can TS-1 survive?) remains open. Who adjudicates a cross-root finality challenge when roots are governed by independent sovereigns? The challenge procedure for cross-sovereign CO-5 challenges is constitutionally unspecified under Level 2b.

OQ-38A05-02 remains PROTECTED. Level 2b changes the institutional context of the question without answering it.

### B.7 — Q6: New Constitutional Authority Created?

**Major:** Three independent sovereign foundations require:
- Three constitutional charters (not one EC under MA)
- Three separate appointment chains with legitimacy independent of MA
- Three AA-01-class resolutions
- Inter-sovereign coordination mechanisms (potential new authority aggregate)
- Cross-sovereign challenge adjudication procedures (CIC equivalent for inter-sovereign disputes)

**New authority count:** Potentially 4+ new constitutional authority structures (three sovereign charters + inter-sovereign coordination body). This is the highest constitutional authority creation cost of the three strategies.

| OQ | Question | Constitutional significance |
|----|----------|---------------------------|
| A-OQ-01 | How are the three independent sovereigns constitutionally coordinated without creating a new CERD at the coordination layer? | HIGH — inter-sovereign coordination may replicate MA-level convergence in new form |
| A-OQ-02 | What are the three new AA-01-class questions and can they be resolved independently? | HIGH — unresolvable per-sovereign AA-01 undermines that root |
| A-OQ-03 | Does Level 2b remain within Family-B or require a new constitutional architecture family? | HIGH — affects compatibility with existing NRNA constitutional design |

| A-OQ-05 | Does the inter-sovereign coordination body itself require independence from all three sovereigns (creating a fourth CERD)? | MEDIUM — potential new single-root CERD |

## Part C — Strategy B: Source-Layer Constraints (Level 2a)

### C.1 — Strategy Description

MA remains the single source-of-source (Family-B preserved). A Tier 3 constitutional provision prohibits MA from exercising authority over all three trust root domains through a single constitutional act or through an act that produces simultaneous cross-domain effects on constitutional grounding. MA's functions are constitutionally partitioned — each root domain's governance is structurally constrained from MA-level simultaneous exercise.

### C.2 — Q1: Does Strategy B Eliminate CERD?

**Test:** Under Level 2a, does a constitutionally grounded authority exist that can evaluate MA's trust function grants from an independent vantage point?

The Tier 3 provision constrains HOW MA exercises its multi-root authority. CIC enforces the Tier 3 provision. But CIC's legitimacy traces to MA. The enforcement mechanism is constitutionally grounded in the same authority it constrains.

**But this framing may be too absolute.** The 38C-07 comparative evidence shows that eternity clauses (Germany Art.79(3)) are enforced by constitutional courts whose legitimacy derives from the constitution — yet they function as effective external reference anchors. Constitutional independence (functional separation) differs from structural independence (different legitimacy source). Strategy B aims to produce constitutional independence within Family-B.

The critical question: is CIC's enforcement of a Tier 3 provision constraining MA's multi-root authority genuinely functionally independent, or is it procedurally identical to MA self-constrained?

**Analysis:** CIC interprets; CAB adjudicates; the Tier 3 provision is protected from elimination by Tier 3 threshold (near-unanimity + 180-day deliberation + 90-day challenge + 2-year cooling). These procedural protections make the Tier 3 provision HIGH-COST to remove but not constitutionally unreachable by MA-controlled actors. CERD persists in structural form — but with elevated constitutional friction.

**Finding:** Strategy B CONSTRAINS CERD (reduces scenarios in which CERD produces Full CRC) but does NOT eliminate it. The structural condition (all constitutional evaluation authority traces to MA) remains. What changes is the cost of converting CERD from a latent condition to a realized failure. CERD reduction is substantial but not complete.

### C.3 — Q2: Does Strategy B Prevent CRC?

**Single-act Full CRC (Scenario D):** Yes — the Tier 3 provision directly prohibits simultaneous multi-root constitutional acts. ADR6-INV-01 CO-5 is protected from single-act Scenario D.

**Sequential-act paths:**
- Multiple individually-permitted acts that collectively produce convergence: NOT addressed by Strategy B unless the Tier 3 provision also covers cumulative effects
- TF-36C-04-03 exploitation (criteria ambiguity): Tier 3 provision constrains authority exercise, not criteria interpretation — this path remains open
- Progressive Tier 2 amendment sequence: Each amendment individually is Tier 2-level; the Tier 3 provision catches simultaneous effects, not progressive effects

**Finding:** Strategy B prevents the most direct Full CRC paths (single-act, simultaneous). Sequential-act and progressive paths remain constitutionally available depending on how precisely "simultaneous cross-domain effects" is defined in the Tier 3 provision. The precision requirement (ADR-30/TF-36C-04-03) is the critical constitutional drafting challenge.

### C.4 — Q3: Prevention vs. Detection?

Strategy B is **constitutional friction** — neither pure prevention nor pure detection. It raises the cost of reaching Full CRC rather than structurally preventing it. The Tier 3 threshold requirement creates a constitutional speed bump that makes convergence visible (near-unanimity requirement is itself a detection mechanism) but not unreachable.

An important property: the Tier 3 requirement makes convergence attempts constitutionally visible before they succeed. Near-unanimity (90%) requires a supermajority of constitutional actors to cooperate — this is a form of detection (the cooperation is observable) even if it is not structural prevention.

### C.5 — Q4: AA-01 Interaction

Strategy B preserves Family-B, so AA-01 remains a single question (MA's pre-constitutional legitimacy). The total AA-01 exposure is unchanged from current architecture. However:

The Tier 3 provision's near-unanimity threshold assumes that 90% of constitutional actors share the same quality of legitimacy as MA. If AA-01 is resolved negatively (MA's legitimacy challenged), the Tier 3 protection's legitimacy is also challenged — because the actors who must ratify the threshold all derive their appointment authority from MA.

**Finding:** Strategy B does not increase AA-01 exposure (same question, same concentration) but also does not reduce it. The AA-01 challenge affects Strategy B's Tier 3 protection proportionally.

### C.6 — Q5: OQ-38A05-02 Interaction

**OQ-38A05-02 PROTECTED.** Under Strategy B, MA's multi-root exercise constraints do not directly address TS-1 finality-validity tension. If AC-31 is discovered false post-TS-1, the Tier 3 provision constrains what MA can do about it (cannot simultaneously affect all roots), but it doesn't resolve the finality question for the already-issued TS-1.

The Tier 3 provision may provide indirect protection: it makes it harder for MA to constitutionally "correct" a false AC-31 in ways that contaminate the Legitimacy and Temporal roots — but this is a governance constraint on the RESPONSE to a false AC-31, not a resolution of whether TS-1 survives the discovery.

OQ-38A05-02 remains PROTECTED. Strategy B changes the governance context of the question without answering it.

### C.7 — Q6: New Constitutional Authority Created?

**Minimal compared to Strategy A:**
- One Tier 3 EC provision (constitutional non-merger constraint on MA multi-root exercise)
- Potential CIC charter update (enforcement mandate for the provision)
- Potential addition of constitutional precision requirements for "simultaneous cross-domain effects"

**New authority count:** Zero to one (CIC charter update, not new authority aggregate). This is the lowest constitutional authority creation cost of the three strategies.

### C.8 — Strategy B Open Questions

| OQ | Question | Constitutional significance |
|----|----------|---------------------------|
| B-OQ-01 | What constitutional definition of "simultaneous cross-domain effects" is precise enough to prevent TF-36C-04-03 exploitation while remaining practically applicable? | HIGH — precision is the critical constraint for Strategy B effectiveness |
| B-OQ-02 | Does CIC's enforcement of the Tier 3 provision constitute genuine constitutional independence or procedural self-constraint by MA-derived actors? | HIGH — determines whether Strategy B provides external reference or only friction |
| B-OQ-03 | Can sequential-act convergence (multiple individually permitted Tier 2 acts) be constitutionally addressed within Strategy B without expanding the Tier 3 provision scope to near-totality? | HIGH — determines Strategy B's protection against progressive convergence |
| B-OQ-04 | Does near-unanimity (90%) represent a constitutionally meaningful threshold for detecting convergence attempts, or does it set a lower bar than genuinely required? | MEDIUM — affects the constitutional value of Strategy B's friction mechanism |
| B-OQ-05 | What happens if CIC itself is subject to MA-derived capture before the Tier 3 provision is tested? (OBS-38B04-01 CIC dependency chain) | MEDIUM — CIC interpretive dependence on MA appointment authority |

## Part D — Strategy C: Constitutional Verification Checkpoint

### D.1 — Strategy Description

A constitutionally chartered mechanism specifically mandated to evaluate whether the trust root independence structure is being maintained — periodically verifying that no convergence threshold has been crossed or is being approached. The Checkpoint has constitutional standing to raise constitutional alerts when convergence is detected, potentially triggering CIC adjudication or higher-level constitutional review.

### D.2 — Q1: Does Strategy C Eliminate CERD?

**Core test:** Does the Checkpoint provide a constitutionally grounded external reference — an authority whose evaluation of MA's trust function grants is independent of those grants?

**If Checkpoint legitimacy traces to MA:** No. The Checkpoint is constitutionally grounded in MA's authority structure (appointed by MA, chartered in EC, operating under MA-derived constitutional provisions). Its evaluation of MA's multi-root exercise traces through the same authority it evaluates. CERD remains structurally. The Checkpoint provides PROCEDURAL OVERSIGHT without external constitutional reference — the architect's challenge is confirmed.

**If Checkpoint legitimacy is constitutionally independent of MA (Level 2b-equivalent):** Yes for the Checkpoint's verification domain. But this requires Strategy A-level constitutional independence — the Checkpoint becomes a fourth independent sovereign, with its own AA-01-class question. This makes Strategy C functionally a hybrid of Strategy A (for the Checkpoint's independence) and the current architecture (for the three roots).

**Finding:** Strategy C does NOT independently eliminate CERD. In its typical design (Checkpoint chartered within Family-B), it provides monitoring without external reference. To provide genuine external reference, Strategy C requires the constitutional independence provisions of either Strategy A (for the Checkpoint itself) or Strategy B (Tier 3 protection for the Checkpoint's operational independence). Strategy C is constitutionally DERIVATIVE — it requires A or B as its constitutional grounding.

### D.3 — Q2: Does Strategy C Prevent CRC?

**Without enforcement authority:** Detection-only. Strategy C identifies convergence but cannot constitutionally halt it without constitutional enforcement authority. Detection ≠ prevention. By the time the Checkpoint detects Full CRC (convergence complete), TM-47 ratchet (AW-05-07) may already have made it constitutionally final under TS-1.

**With enforcement authority (CIC trigger):** The Checkpoint detects and triggers CIC adjudication. CIC can issue interim constitutional orders (if so chartered) suspending convergence-producing acts pending adjudication. This provides DETECTION + SUSPENSION — stronger than pure detection, weaker than structural prevention.

**Critical timing problem:** The AW-05-07 ratchet operates automatically when AC-31 compromise occurs. If AC-31 compromise goes undetected until the Checkpoint's periodic evaluation, convergence may be constitutionally final before the Checkpoint can trigger CIC. Strategy C's effectiveness depends critically on whether the Checkpoint can detect convergence in real time (continuous monitoring) rather than periodically.

**Finding:** Strategy C prevents CRC CONDITIONALLY — only if (a) detection is timely (before constitutional finality), (b) enforcement authority is constitutionally sufficient (requires A or B as grounding), and (c) the automatic ratchet (AW-05-07) has not already crossed the finality threshold before detection.

### D.4 — Q3: Prevention vs. Detection?

Strategy C is **detection-primary, prevention-conditional.** In minimal form (monitoring only), it provides detection with no prevention. With enforcement triggers (CIC escalation), it provides detection + suspension. With strong enforcement (constitutional halt authority), it approaches prevention — but this requires the constitutional grounding of Strategy A or B.

A continuous-monitoring Checkpoint with real-time CIC escalation authority represents the strongest form of Strategy C. A periodic-review Checkpoint with report-only authority represents the weakest form. The constitutional design of the Checkpoint determines where on this spectrum it sits.

### D.5 — Q4: AA-01 Interaction

If the Checkpoint traces to MA (typical Family-B design): AA-01 exposure unchanged from current architecture.

If the Checkpoint has Strategy A-equivalent independence: A fourth AA-01-class question is introduced for the Checkpoint sovereign.

**Finding:** Strategy C's AA-01 interaction depends entirely on the constitutional design of the Checkpoint's independence grounding. In its standard form (Family-B), AA-01 exposure is unchanged. In its strong form (independent grounding), a fourth AA-01-class exposure is introduced.

### D.6 — Q5: OQ-38A05-02 Interaction

**OQ-38A05-02 PROTECTED.** The Checkpoint evaluates whether convergence is approaching — it evaluates the trust root structure, not the validity of already-issued certifications. If AC-31 false discovery occurs post-TS-1, the Checkpoint may detect it if the falsity is observable, but it cannot constitutionally reverse TS-1 (ADR6-INV-01: CO-5 void unless independently satisfied, but TS-1 finality is the constitutional boundary).

The Checkpoint might detect AC-31 false certification BEFORE TS-1 is issued (if the falsity emerges during the certification window). In this case, Strategy C provides a constitutional alert mechanism that could prevent TS-1 issuance when AC-31 integrity is compromised. But the post-TS-1 scenario remains unaddressed.

OQ-38A05-02 remains PROTECTED. Strategy C changes the pre-TS-1 detection environment without addressing the post-TS-1 question.

### D.7 — Q6: New Constitutional Authority Created?

**Depends on design:**
- Monitoring-only Checkpoint: CIC charter extension (moderate — not a new aggregate, but a new mandate)
- Checkpoint with suspension authority: New constitutional authority (enforcement power requires independent constitutional standing — potential 8th or 9th authority aggregate)
- Independently grounded Checkpoint: New sovereign-level authority (Level 2b equivalent — most significant)

**New authority count:** One (CIC extension) to one new aggregate (enforcement authority) to one new sovereign (independent grounding). Constitutional authority creation cost scales with the strength of constitutional independence provided.

### D.8 — Strategy C Open Questions

| OQ | Question | Constitutional significance |
|----|----------|---------------------------|
| C-OQ-01 | Does the Checkpoint provide genuine external reference (independent grounding) or procedural oversight (MA-traced legitimacy)? | HIGH — determines whether Strategy C eliminates CERD or merely monitors it |
| C-OQ-02 | Can the Checkpoint detect convergence before the AW-05-07 ratchet produces constitutionally final results? (timing criticality) | HIGH — determines whether detection translates to prevention |
| C-OQ-03 | What enforcement authority does the Checkpoint have? Detection only, suspension, or halt? | HIGH — determines constitutional prevention capacity |
| C-OQ-04 | Is Strategy C constitutionally independent of Strategies A and B, or is it always derivative of one of them? | HIGH — determines whether C is an independent option or a complementary mechanism |
| C-OQ-05 | What constitutional legitimacy chain does the Checkpoint's own existence and mandate trace to? | HIGH — determines whether the Checkpoint itself creates a new CERD |
| C-OQ-06 | Does a Checkpoint with CIC escalation authority combined with Strategy B (Tier 3 provision) provide a constitutionally coherent composite strategy? | MEDIUM — composite analysis not yet performed |

### E.3 — CVI Response Characterization

CVI (Constitutional Verification Incompleteness) names the general class of which CERD is NRNA's instance. How does each strategy respond to CVI?

| Strategy | CVI Response |
|----------|-------------|
| A | Restructures the domain of CVI — from "all three roots share one sovereign" to "each root has its own sovereign with its own CVI." CVI is not eliminated; it is distributed. |
| B | Provides constitutional friction within the CVI-affected domain — slows the path from CVI's structural condition to realized failure. CVI is managed, not eliminated. |
| C | Provides observational tools within the CVI-affected domain. CVI is monitored, not managed or eliminated. |

None of the three strategies eliminate CVI — consistent with 38C-12's finding that CVI may be a near-universal constitutional property managed through external anchors, not eliminated. The strategies differ in how they MANAGE CVI.

### E.5 — Composite Strategy Observation

38C-12's identification of Strategy C as a distinct option raised the possibility of composite strategies. One combination is architecturally notable:

**Strategy B + Strategy C (Composite):**
Tier 3 constraint on MA multi-root exercise (Strategy B) + Checkpoint with real-time CIC escalation (Strategy C with CIC enforcement trigger). This composite would provide:
- Constitutional friction against single-act convergence (Strategy B)
- Real-time detection and suspension of convergence attempts (Strategy C)
- Combined: sequential-act convergence attempts become visible (detection) and suspended pending CIC adjudication, while single-act convergence is constitutionally prohibited

Whether this composite adequately addresses the CERD structural condition or merely provides detection of CERD's progression is the critical evaluation question for any ARB consideration of composite strategies.

```
CVI → CERD → Partial CRC → [trigger] → CFM-4 → Full CRC → CFM-1+CFM-3 → Entrenched CERD
```

| Strategy | Intervention Point | Nature of Intervention |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|
