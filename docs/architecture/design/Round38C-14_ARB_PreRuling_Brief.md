## Part 4 — Strategy A: Strongest Constitutional Argument

**The structural independence argument for Level 2b:**

Trust root independence is CO-5's most fundamental constitutional requirement. ADR6-INV-01 is designated non-waivable. The constitutional invariant "CO-5 void unless CO-2 + CO-3 + CO-4 independently satisfied" cannot be satisfied by governance-layer differentiation alone — it requires that the sources from which CO-2, CO-3, and CO-4 draw their constitutional grounding are themselves constitutionally independent.

Strategy A is the only approach that:
1. Eliminates multi-root CERD completely — CO-5's independence requirement is satisfied structurally, not evaluated functionally
2. Eliminates Meta-CVI — the evaluating mechanism's independence is grounded externally, not self-referentially
3. Provides cross-root verification — under three independent sovereigns, any two roots provide external verification of the third's governance
4. Is constitutionally unconditional — independence does not depend on CIC's sustained interpretive integrity or PAN resistance over time

Per-sovereign CERDs (residual under Strategy A) are constitutionally distinct from multi-root CERD: they are bounded (one root's self-reference problem), cross-verifiable (the other two sovereigns provide external reference), and less severe than the problem being solved.

The three new AA-01-class questions Strategy A introduces are manageable because they arise in different constitutional domains — they do not produce the same convergence risk as a single AA-01 that grounds all three roots simultaneously.

**Strongest single claim:** No constitutional design that depends on the interpreted independence of CIC — which is both the evaluator of trust root independence and the interpreter of its own independence mandate — can provide the constitutional certainty that ADR6-INV-01's "independently satisfied" requirement demands over an indefinite constitutional time-horizon.

## Part 5 — Strategy B: Strongest Constitutional Argument

**The functional independence argument for Level 2a:**

Functional Independence is the constitutional norm in every major constitutional system studied. Germany's Federal Constitutional Court, India's Supreme Court, constitutional courts in every comparative jurisdiction — all are functionally independent institutions whose legitimacy traces to the same constitutional order they constrain. All are constitutionally recognized as providing genuine independence.

The constitutional design question is not "can an evaluating authority trace its legitimacy to the constitutional order it evaluates?" — the answer is universally yes. The constitutional design question is "can the evaluating authority's operations and judgments be protected from interference by the authority it evaluates?" — the answer is also yes, through Tier 3 protection, appointment independence, and jurisdictional completeness.

Strategy B requires that five minimum conditions be constitutionally designed (38C-13A Part F). If those conditions are satisfied:
1. CIC's trust root evaluation mandate is Tier 3 protected — MA cannot reduce it without near-unanimity threshold
2. CIC's judgments on trust function exercise are constitutionally final — MA cannot override
3. PAN-resistance mechanisms exist — appointment independence extends beyond MA's direct appointment cycle
4. CIC's jurisdiction explicitly covers MA's trust function exercise (not merely existing provisions)
5. Meta-CVI is partially managed through challenge standing (38C03-INV-02), CAB institutional insulation, and constitutional text specificity

The current NRNA design does not satisfy these conditions — but that is a design gap, not a constitutional impossibility. The gap is correctable.

**Strongest single claim:** Constitutional independence has never meant independence of constitutional source — it has always meant independence of constitutional operation and judgment. The comparative constitutional record is unanimous on this point. Requiring source-level independence imposes a higher standard than any comparative constitutional system has found constitutionally necessary.

## Part 6 — Strategy C: Strongest Constitutional Argument

*Note: Strategy C cannot be a primary strategy — it is derivative (F-04). The strongest argument for C is as a complement to Strategy B.*

**The constitutional verification checkpoint argument:**

Strategy C's constitutional contribution is not CERD elimination — it is constitutional transparency. CERD, once present, may progress toward Full CRC through governance convergence events. Strategy C creates constitutional tripwires that make CERD-proximity constitutionally visible before CRC becomes constitutionally entrenched.

The strongest claim for Strategy B+C over Strategy B alone: during Meta-CVI Stages 1–2 (before the independence interpretation has fully narrowed), Strategy C's detection layer remains constitutionally functional. If constitutional actors (S-2/S-3 per 38C03-INV-02) receive Checkpoint signals indicating CERD-proximity, they can trigger constitutional intervention before Stage 3 is reached. Strategy B alone has no mechanism that creates this pre-Stage 3 detection pressure.

**Strongest single claim:** Even if Strategy B alone cannot prevent Stage 4 Meta-CVI, Strategy B+C provides a constitutional window for intervention before Stage 3 that does not exist under Strategy B alone. Whether that window is constitutionally meaningful (versus constitutionally illusory because Stage 1–2 detection doesn't compel constitutionally effective response) is the ARB's judgment.

## Part 7 — Comparative Evidence Summary

**Eight mechanisms. One direction.**

| Mechanism | Type | Key Constitutional Lesson for NRNA |
|-----------|------|-------------------------------------|
| Germany Art.79(3) | Functional Independence + structural content protection | Eternity clause protects CONTENT (Tier 3 equivalent); enforcement is functionally independent court. Separation of CONTENT protection from ENFORCEMENT mechanism |
| India Basic Structure | Functional Independence (judicially created) | Supreme Court asserted independence doctrine against Parliament using constitutional authority derived from Parliament's own constitution. Interpretive finality of judgments = constitutionally sufficient independence |
| Separation of Powers | Functional Independence (shared democratic source) | Three branches from same constitutional order; genuine independence through operational domain boundaries and mutual non-subordination. Shared source ≠ absence of independence |
| Constitutional Courts | Functional Independence (source in constitution) | Universal model: constitutional court exists within constitutional order it interprets. Constitutional independence = operational independence + appointment insulation + interpretive finality |
| Protected Core Models | Functional Independence + structural content | Standard pattern: structural protection of SPECIFIC CONTENT (unamendable); functional independence of enforcement mechanism |
| Non-Merger Doctrine | Functional Independence | Merger constraints enforced by functionally independent courts from same constitutional order |
| Constitutional Identity | Mixed | Both independence types can coexist; structural content protection + functional enforcement is constitutionally coherent |
| Ackerman (Higher Lawmaking) | Functional Independence (conditional) | Independence achieved through heightened constitutional threshold — functional independence through procedural difficulty |

**Evidence direction:** All mechanisms support Functional Independence. The dominant pattern: structural protection of constitutional CONTENT + functional enforcement mechanism.

**The transferability caveat (architect's challenge, unresolved):** Every comparative mechanism operates within a mature constitutional system with institutional history, legal tradition, and judicial culture. NRNA has none of these. The comparative evidence proves Functional Independence CAN work. It does not prove it WILL work in NRNA's specific constitutional development stage.

- Position 2: Functional Independence sufficient — operational and interpretive independence (with five minimum conditions) is constitutionally adequate; comparative evidence supports this as the norm
- Position 3: Functional Independence sufficient in principle but Strategy B as currently designed is constitutionally inadequate; constitutional redesign required before ruling can apply

**JC-02:** Is Meta-CVI constitutionally fatal to Functional Independence in the NRNA context?
- Position A: Fatal — the self-referential interpretation structure of CIC creates an irreversible constitutional risk that partial management cannot adequately address; Structural Independence required
- Position B: Manageable — partial management (PAN resistance, challenge standing, constitutional text specificity) is constitutionally adequate; comparative evidence shows all constitutional courts operate with equivalent self-referential interpretation structures

**JC-03:** Is comparative constitutional evidence transferable to NRNA's institutional stage?
- Position A: Transferable — constitutional design principles are universal; institutional maturity can be built through appropriate constitutional design; the five minimum conditions address this
- Position B: Not transferable at current stage — constitutional courts in Germany and India emerged within centuries of legal tradition; NRNA begins with constitutional design and no history; Structural Independence may be required for the constitutional development phase

**JC-04:** Which risk profile is constitutionally preferable?
- Strategy A: per-sovereign CERD (bounded, cross-verifiable, present from instantiation, constitutionally less severe than multi-root CERD, eliminates Meta-CVI, creates three AA-01-class questions)
