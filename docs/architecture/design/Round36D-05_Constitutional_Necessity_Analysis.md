## Section 1 — Behavioral Integrity Model

**Governing question for this section:** Can a concentrated, perfectly behaving authority constitute constitutional legitimacy?

This section evaluates each of the five discovered D43 instances under a strict behavioral integrity assumption: the authority holder operates without error, without bias, without omission, and without any adverse intent. The question is whether this behavioral perfection — if it could be established — would produce constitutional legitimacy.

---

### 1.1 The Behavioral Integrity Assumption

For the purposes of this analysis, "behavioral integrity" is defined as the conjunction of:

- **BI-1: Correctness** — The authority makes no factual errors in the execution of its function
- **BI-2: Completeness** — The authority applies its function to all cases within its scope, without omission
- **BI-3: Consistency** — The authority applies the same standards to equivalent cases
- **BI-4: Transparency** — The authority's decisions are observable to those affected
- **BI-5: Non-malice** — The authority acts without adverse intent

This is a maximal assumption. In practice, no authority achieves BI-1 through BI-5 simultaneously across all decisions over time. The analysis tests whether even this maximal assumption produces constitutional legitimacy — not whether the assumption is realistic.

---

### 1.2 D43-Instance-ENROLLMENT: Concentrated + Perfectly Behaving

**Scenario:** A single enrollment authority, holding concentrated authority over who may and may not vote, operating under full behavioral integrity (BI-1 through BI-5).

**Test 1: Does perfect correctness (BI-1) produce constitutional legitimacy?**

If the authority includes all eligible voters and excludes all ineligible voters without error:
- "Eligible" is defined by a criterion. That criterion was set by a criteria authority (D43-instance-CRITERIA). If criteria authority is the same actor as enrollment authority, the actor both defines eligibility and enforces it. BI-1 (correctness) asserts correct application of the actor's own criteria — it does not assert that the criteria themselves are constitutionally grounded.
- Even if criteria authority is a distinct actor, perfect correctness (BI-1) cannot be established without an independent verification mechanism. The claim "I enrolled everyone eligible" is the actor's own assertion under BI-4 (transparency). For that assertion to have constitutional weight, it must be checkable. Without an independent challenge mechanism (L-3), the assertion is structurally unverifiable.

**36D-CF-05-01 (CANDIDATE):** Behavioral correctness in enrollment is structurally unverifiable without an independent challenge mechanism (L-3). Perfect correctness is a behavioral claim; its constitutional validity requires a constitutional mechanism to adjudicate it.

**Test 2: Does perfect completeness (BI-2) + transparency (BI-4) produce constitutional legitimacy?**

If all enrollment decisions are observable and no eligible voter is omitted:
- Observability (BI-4) is a precondition for challenge. But observability alone does not constitute a challenge mechanism. A voter who observes their exclusion must have a constitutional path to contest it.
- If L-3 (challenge mechanism) is UNDEFINED (as discovered in 36D-04 for enrollment), observation without recourse is constitutionally insufficient.

**36D-CF-05-02 (CANDIDATE):** Transparency (BI-4) is a necessary precondition for challenge, but it does not substitute for a challenge mechanism. A voter who can observe their exclusion but has no constitutional path to contest it has no constitutional protection.

**Test 3: Does behavioral integrity as a whole substitute for missing L-1 (legitimacy source)?**

If the enrollment authority behaves perfectly but has no constitutionally grounded source of authority:
- L-1 (legitimacy source) concerns the origin of the authority's mandate, not the quality of its execution. A medical procedure performed perfectly by an unauthorized person is constitutionally unauthorized regardless of its quality.
- In the constitutional context: an enrollment authority that exists by institutional gravity — because someone decided it should exist, or because it has always existed — without a constitutional grounding, has no constitutional mandate even if it behaves correctly.
- The absence of L-1 means the authority's decisions can be annulled by any actor claiming a competing authority, because no constitutional rule establishes the first authority's precedence.

**36D-CF-05-03 (CANDIDATE):** Behavioral integrity does not substitute for legitimacy source (L-1). An authority without a constitutionally grounded mandate executes without constitutional standing, regardless of behavioral quality.

**Enrollment Summary:** Concentrated enrollment authority + full behavioral integrity ≠ constitutional legitimacy. The missing elements (L-1, L-3, L-4, L-5 from 36D-04) are structural gaps — they concern the constitutional character of the authority relationship, not the quality of the authority's conduct.

---

### 1.3 D43-Instance-CRITERIA: Concentrated + Perfectly Behaving

**Scenario:** A single criteria authority, defining eligibility criteria consistently and transparently, applying them without error.

**Test 1: Does consistent, transparent criteria definition produce constitutional legitimacy?**

From 36D-04: L-1 for criteria carries the CANDIDATE characterization of "membership ratification." If the criteria authority is constitutionally grounded by membership ratification:
- The ratification grounding means the criteria are legitimate because the membership endorsed them.
- But "membership ratification" is a CANDIDATE characterization — not a discovered constitutional mechanism. The specific mechanism by which the membership ratifies criteria (voting threshold, periodic review, override authority) remains UNDEFINED.
- Perfect behavior in applying the ratified criteria does not resolve the gap in the ratification mechanism itself.

**Test 2: Does perfect consistency (BI-3) resolve the self-referential risk?**

From 36D-04: L-4 (revocation mechanism) for criteria is SELF-REF — the criteria authority's revocability appears to depend on the criteria authority itself. This is the TC3-NCQ-04 manifestation.

- If the criteria authority applies its own criteria perfectly and consistently, it is also applying whatever criteria it has set for its own revocability.
- Perfect consistency (BI-3) in a self-referential system means the self-referential loop is consistently maintained — it does not break the loop.
- An authority that consistently defines its own revocability conditions is constitutionally irremovable in practice, regardless of behavioral perfection.

**36D-CF-05-04 (CANDIDATE):** Self-referential revocability (SELF-REF in L-4) is not resolved by behavioral consistency. Perfect consistency in a self-referential system strengthens the loop — it does not break the constitutional recursion that TC3-NCQ-04 identifies.

**Criteria Summary:** Even under full behavioral integrity with CANDIDATE legitimacy grounding, the SELF-REF condition in L-4 represents a constitutional vulnerability that behavioral integrity cannot resolve. Who can revoke the criteria authority remains an open constitutional question. "The membership" is a CANDIDATE but the mechanism for the membership to exercise that revocation is UNDEFINED.

---

### 1.4 D43-Instance-AUDIT: Concentrated + Perfectly Behaving

**Scenario:** A single audit function, observing all election events correctly, independent of the election operator (IR-A), reporting all anomalies without omission.

**Test 1: Does perfect observation + operator independence produce constitutional legitimacy?**

From 36D-04: Gap A-3 is the undefined expected evidence set. The audit function observes "all events" — but what constitutes the complete set of events that should be observed?

- If the audit function both defines what should be observed and observes it perfectly, the observation is complete by self-definition. Behavioral integrity (BI-2: completeness) asserts that all events within scope are observed — but the scope is set by the observer.
- This is a constitutional gap that behavioral integrity cannot resolve: the audit function's judgment about what constitutes an observable event is itself unaudited.
- Applying OBS-36D-03-1: IR-H (independence from audited subject) — the expected evidence set cannot be defined by the audited subject. But if the audit function defines its own scope, the audited subject effectively influences scope by influencing the audit function's definition of completeness.

**36D-CF-05-05 (CANDIDATE):** An audit function that defines its own expected evidence set is constitutionally incomplete regardless of how accurately it observes within that self-defined scope. Perfect observation of a self-defined scope is not constitutional completeness.

**Test 2: Does operator independence (IR-A) fully satisfy the independence requirement?**

From 36D-03: IR-H requires independence from the audited subject. The audited subject is the election system, not only the election operator.

- If the audit function is independent of the election operator (IR-A) but its scope was defined by the criteria setter (who may not be independent of the election operator via IR-D), behavioral integrity does not close this constitutional gap.
- Applying OBS-36D-02-1: independence from the operator is an authority relationship question, not a bounded context partition question. The audit authority may span multiple contexts while still being constitutionally insufficient if the independence requirements are partially unmet.

**Audit Summary:** Concentrated audit function + full behavioral integrity within self-defined scope ≠ constitutional legitimacy. Gap A-3 (scope definition) and partial IR independence are structural gaps that perfect observation cannot resolve.

---

### 1.5 D43-Instance-GOVERNANCE-AUTHORIZATION: Concentrated + Perfectly Behaving

**Scenario:** A single governance authorization authority, processing all election phase transitions according to defined constitutional rules, without error.

**Test 1: Does perfect rule application produce constitutional legitimacy?**

From 36D-04: L-1 for governance authorization is CANDIDATE — the constitutional basis for governance authority is not yet discovered as CONFIRMED. L-2 (authority holder) is UNDEFINED.

- Applying OBS-36D-01-2 (governing): authorization of a governance transition ≠ execution of a governance transition. Even if the governance authorization authority applies transition rules perfectly, the constitutional question is who has authority to authorize the transition.
- If L-2 is UNDEFINED, the perfect execution of a governance transition by an unidentified authority holder has no constitutional standing. "Someone authorized this correctly" without identifying who had the constitutional power to authorize is a behavioral claim without constitutional grounding.

**Test 2: Does perfect execution (BI-1, BI-3) resolve the undefined succession (L-5)?**

- L-5 (succession mechanism) for governance authorization is UNDEFINED (from 36D-04, CF-04-05: succession universally undefined).
- If the governance authorization authority executes perfectly but succession is undefined, the authority's constitutional validity depends on the continued existence of a specific actor or arrangement.
- Perfect execution by the current authority holder does not establish who holds authority if the current holder becomes unavailable.

**36D-CF-05-06 (CANDIDATE):** Governance transitions executed without a constitutionally identified authority holder (L-2 UNDEFINED) have no constitutional standing regardless of their procedural correctness. Correct execution of an unauthorized transition is still an unauthorized transition.

**Governance Authorization Summary:** Concentrated governance authorization authority + full behavioral integrity ≠ constitutional legitimacy. Undefined L-2 (authority holder) means the authority to authorize transitions has no discovered constitutional basis.

---

### 1.6 D43-Instance-CERTIFICATION: Concentrated + Perfectly Behaving

**Scenario:** A single certification body, constitutionally appointed, independent of the election operator, certifying elections that meet all constitutional criteria without error.

**Test 1: Does legitimate appointment + perfect certification produce constitutional legitimacy?**

From 36D-04, CF-04-04: certification exhibits the most complete legitimacy gap — L-3 (challenge mechanism) and L-5 (succession mechanism) are UNDEFINED; L-4 (revocation) is also UNDEFINED.

From TF-36C-05-01 (carried from Round 36C-06): "Certifier legitimacy ≠ certification validity." This finding established that even a legitimate certifier can issue constitutionally problematic certifications.

- Perfect certification (BI-1 through BI-5) means the certifier correctly evaluates the election and issues a correct certification. But without L-3 (challenge mechanism), that certification cannot be constitutionally contested.
- An unchallenged certification is not necessarily a constitutionally valid certification — it is a certification that has not been challenged, which is an empirical observation about process, not a constitutional guarantee.
- The absence of L-4 and L-5 means the certification body's authority is both irremovable and constitutionally incomplete with respect to succession.

**36D-CF-05-07 (CANDIDATE):** A certification that cannot be constitutionally challenged (L-3 UNDEFINED) has the constitutional character of a final and unreviewable judgment. In constitutional systems governing elections, unreviewable judgments are constitutionally suspect regardless of their correctness, because correctness cannot be established without review.

**Certification Summary:** Concentrated certification body + full behavioral integrity ≠ constitutional legitimacy. The complete legitimacy gap in L-3/L-4/L-5 means certification correctness is constitutionally unverifiable and unchallengeably final.

---

### 1.7 Section 1 General Finding

Across all five D43 instances, behavioral integrity cannot substitute for:
- **L-1 (Legitimacy Source):** The origin of constitutional mandate is a structural property, not a behavioral one. Perfect execution does not create a mandate.
- **L-3 (Challenge Mechanism):** The ability to contest a decision is a constitutional precondition, not a remedy for errors. Even correct decisions require a constitutional mechanism by which they can be confirmed through challenge.
- **L-4 (Revocation Mechanism):** Revocability is a constitutional precondition for current legitimacy, not a consequence of behavioral failure. An irremovable authority that behaves perfectly is still constitutionally irremovable.
- **L-5 (Succession Mechanism):** Constitutional validity cannot depend on the continued availability of a specific actor. Perfect behavior does not establish succession.

**36D-CF-05-08 (CANDIDATE — MAJOR FINDING):** Behavioral integrity addresses the conduct of an authority holder. Constitutional legitimacy addresses the character of the authority relationship. These are categorically distinct. Behavioral claims cannot resolve structural constitutional gaps.

This constitutes primary evidence toward TC5-NCQ-04 that behavioral integrity cannot substitute for constitutional structure.

## Section 2 — Structural Distribution Model

**Governing question for this section:** Can structural distribution of authority substitute for missing legitimacy mechanisms? Is distribution always required, or is it function-dependent?

---

### 2.1 The Structural Distribution Assumption

For the purposes of this analysis, "structural distribution" is defined as the arrangement in which a constitutional function is executed by multiple independent actors, such that no single actor can execute or block the function unilaterally.

This analysis tests whether distribution per se resolves the constitutional gaps identified in Section 1.

---

### 2.2 Distribution Applied to Missing L-1 (Legitimacy Source)

**Scenario:** Distribute enrollment authority across three independent enrollment bodies, each without a constitutionally grounded mandate.

- Three enrollment bodies without L-1 = three instances of D43-instance-ENROLLMENT without constitutional grounding.
- Distribution multiplies the authority without grounding any instance of it.
- The constitutional question is not "how many actors hold authority?" but "from where does any actor derive constitutional authority?"

**36D-CF-05-09 (CANDIDATE):** Distribution without legitimacy grounding produces N constitutionally ungrounded authorities instead of one. Distribution does not substitute for L-1.

---

### 2.3 Distribution Applied to Missing L-3 (Challenge Mechanism)

**Scenario:** Distribute criteria authority across three criteria bodies. Each criteria body's decisions are still unchallenged (L-3 UNDEFINED across all three).

- If all three criteria bodies agree on a criteria decision, no challenge mechanism exists for any of the three.
- If the three bodies disagree, the disagreement itself has no constitutional resolution mechanism — it becomes a political dispute, not a constitutional process.
- Distribution actually introduces a NEW constitutional gap: the dispute resolution mechanism between distributed authority holders.

**36D-CF-05-10 (CANDIDATE):** Distribution without an independent challenge mechanism does not create constitutional legitimacy — it creates a new constitutional gap (inter-authority dispute resolution) in addition to the existing gap.

---

### 2.4 Distribution Applied to Missing L-4 (Revocation)

**Scenario:** Distribute certification authority across three certification bodies. Each is constitutionally appointed but none can be constitutionally revoked.

- Three irremovable certification bodies are collectively constitutionally irremovable.
- Distribution without revocability produces a constitutionally entrenched distributed authority — potentially more resistant to correction than a single irremovable authority, because removal of any one body does not change the certification outcome if the remaining two bodies persist.

**36D-CF-05-11 (CANDIDATE):** Distribution does not substitute for revocability. A distributed authority arrangement without revocation mechanisms is constitutionally more entrenched than a single authority without revocation, because removal of individual actors does not resolve the constitutional entrenchment.

---

### 2.5 The Independence Distinction

The analysis in Sections 2.2 through 2.4 shows that distribution does not mechanically substitute for L-1, L-3, or L-4. However, distribution has a distinct constitutional role that is not captured by the legitimacy dimension framework:

**The anti-concentration function of distribution:** Even when L-1 is constitutionally grounded and L-3/L-4/L-5 are defined, a single authority holding all constitutional functions simultaneously creates a self-referential risk. From Round 36C-05 and TC5-Q2: if all authority converges on a single actor, trustworthiness claims become self-referential — the single actor validates its own conduct without external constitutional reference.

This is distinct from the legitimacy dimension gaps. L-1 through L-5 address the constitutional character of individual authority relationships. The anti-concentration requirement addresses the structural relationship between authority holders across functions.

**36D-CF-05-12 (CANDIDATE):** Distribution serves a distinct constitutional function — preventing the convergence of all constitutional authority in a single actor — that is not captured by the legitimacy dimension framework (L-1 through L-5). This function is present even when all legitimacy dimensions are fully defined for each individual authority.

Applying OBS-36D-02-1: this is an authority relationship, not a bounded context partition. The anti-concentration requirement is a constitutional constraint on the structure of authority relationships, not a design recommendation about context partitioning.

---

### 2.6 Is Distribution Always Required?

From the analysis: distribution is not always required in the same form. The constitutional requirement appears to be **function-dependent**, with a structural threshold that cannot yet be fully characterized:

**Case A — Functions with full convergence risk (TC5-Q2):** Functions where a single actor simultaneously holds authority over the scope of the function, the execution of the function, and the validation of the function's correctness. For these functions, distribution or structural independence of at least one component (scope definition, execution, or validation) appears necessary.

**Case B — Functions with independent verification:** Functions where a single authority holds the primary execution but a structurally independent authority holds the verification mandate. Here, distribution of the primary authority may not be required — but independence of the verification function appears necessary.

**Case C — Functions with constitutional prohibition:** Functions where distribution is constitutionally prohibited (from 36D-02, Case 4 analysis). Here, the prohibition itself is constitutionally grounded — not every constitutional arrangement is a candidate for distribution.

**36D-CF-05-13 (CANDIDATE):** The constitutional requirement does not appear to be uniform distribution of all authority functions, but structural independence of at least the verification/challenge function from the primary authority. The specific form of structural arrangement (distribution, independent oversight, or other) is function-dependent and cannot be determined at the constitutional analysis level without further constitutional evidence.

---

### 2.7 Section 2 General Finding

Structural distribution alone does not substitute for the constitutional legitimacy dimensions (L-1 through L-5). Distribution that is not grounded in constitutional legitimacy is constitutionally equivalent to replication of an ungrounded authority. However, distribution serves a distinct anti-concentration constitutional function that is NOT captured by the L-1 through L-5 framework.

The emerging model: **Both** legitimacy dimensions (L-1 through L-5) AND anti-concentration structural arrangements appear necessary — but neither alone is constitutionally sufficient.

## Section 3 — Legitimacy Dependency Analysis

**Governing question for this section:** Which legitimacy dimensions (L-1 through L-5) have constitutional dependencies that require specific structural arrangements — and which can be satisfied independently of structure?

---

### 3.1 L-1 (Legitimacy Source) Dependencies

**What L-1 requires:** A constitutionally grounded source from which the authority derives its mandate.

**Does L-1 require distribution?**  
Not as discovered — a single constitutionally grounded legitimacy source can be constitutional. The requirement is that the source is:
- Independent of the authority it grounds (IR-G: self-independence — the authority cannot grant itself legitimacy)
- Constitutionally identifiable (not institutional gravity or historical accident)

**Independence requirement:** IR-G (self-independence) appears necessary for L-1. An authority that constitutionally grounds itself has a self-referential legitimacy source.

**36D-CF-05-14 (CANDIDATE):** L-1 (Legitimacy Source) appears to require structural independence from the authority being grounded (IR-G). It does not appear to require distribution of the primary authority, but it appears to require that the grounding mechanism is constitutionally separate from the authority it grounds.

Classification: L-1 structural independence = **Appears Necessary**

---

### 3.2 L-2 (Authority Holder) Dependencies

**What L-2 requires:** A constitutionally identified holder of the authority in question.

**Does L-2 require distribution?**  
Not as discovered — a single identified authority holder can be constitutional. The requirement is identification, not multiplicity.

**However:** If L-2 is UNDEFINED (as discovered for governance authorization in 36D-04), no structural arrangement can substitute for the identification. Distribution of an undefined authority produces undefined distributed authority.

**36D-CF-05-15 (CANDIDATE):** L-2 (Authority Holder) does not appear to require distribution, but appears to require constitutional identification. Distribution cannot substitute for undefined L-2 — distributing undefined authority produces undefined distributed authority.

Classification: L-2 constitutional identification = **Appears Necessary**; distribution = **Undetermined** (neither supported nor opposed by this analysis)

---

### 3.3 L-3 (Challenge Mechanism) Dependencies

**What L-3 requires:** A constitutional mechanism by which those affected by an authority decision can contest that decision.

**Does L-3 require structural independence?**  
The analysis supports this — and L-3 is the most constrained legitimacy dimension in this respect:
- The challenge mechanism must be independent of the challenged authority (otherwise the authority adjudicates challenges to itself)
- Applying IR-G: the challenge mechanism cannot be controlled by the authority being challenged
- Applying IR-H: the challenge function must be independent of the subject being audited/challenged

**Does L-3 require distribution of the primary authority?**  
Not as discovered — the primary authority can remain concentrated if and only if the challenge mechanism is structurally independent of that authority.

**36D-CF-05-16 (CANDIDATE):** L-3 (Challenge Mechanism) appears to require structural independence of the challenge function from the challenged authority. This independence requirement appears present regardless of whether the primary authority is concentrated or distributed. A concentrated authority with a structurally independent challenge mechanism may be constitutionally sufficient; a distributed authority with no independent challenge mechanism appears constitutionally insufficient.

Classification: L-3 independence of challenge mechanism = **Appears Necessary**

---

### 3.4 L-4 (Revocation Mechanism) Dependencies

**What L-4 requires:** A constitutional mechanism by which the authority holder can be removed from authority.

**Does L-4 require structural independence?**  
The analysis supports this — and the SELF-REF finding in 36D-04 (criteria authority) identifies exactly why:
- A revocation mechanism controlled by the authority being revoked is constitutionally inoperative
- The revocation mechanism must be held by an entity independent of the authority being revoked
- TC3-NCQ-04 (who governs the governors?) is structurally instantiated here: the revocation of an authority requires a higher authority or an independent authority, which itself requires constitutional grounding

**36D-CF-05-17 (CANDIDATE):** L-4 (Revocation Mechanism) appears to require structural independence of the revocation mechanism from the revoked authority (IR-G applies). SELF-REF in L-4 is a constitutional defect that behavioral integrity cannot resolve and that distribution alone cannot resolve if the distributed authorities are mutually revocable only by each other.

Classification: L-4 independence of revocation mechanism = **Appears Necessary**

---

### 3.5 L-5 (Succession Mechanism) Dependencies

**What L-5 requires:** A constitutional mechanism by which authority continues after the departure or incapacity of the current authority holder.

**Does L-5 require structural independence?**  
Partially — succession appears to require that:
- Succession criteria are pre-defined (not improvised at the moment of succession)
- The body that identifies or appoints a successor is constitutionally grounded
- Succession does not restart the L-1 through L-4 gaps (the successor inherits a constitutionally grounded authority, not a new ungrounded one)

**Does L-5 require distribution of the primary authority?**  
Not as discovered — but the constitutional validity of the successor depends on L-1 through L-4 being defined for the successor's authority, not merely the predecessor's.

**36D-CF-05-18 (CANDIDATE):** L-5 (Succession Mechanism) appears to require that succession criteria and the succession appointment body are constitutionally grounded (L-1 applied to succession). Constitutional succession cannot be improvised at the moment of succession without restarting the legitimacy grounding requirement from the beginning.

Classification: L-5 constitutional pre-definition = **Appears Necessary**; specific structural form = **Undetermined**

---

### 3.6 Cross-Cutting Legitimacy Finding

Reviewing L-1 through L-5 as a system:

The independence requirements for L-3 (challenge) and L-4 (revocation) imply that there must exist at least one constitutionally independent authority relationship distinct from each authority holder. This creates an apparent structural threshold:

**For any constitutionally grounded authority (A), what has been discovered:**
- A legitimacy source (L-1) that is independent of A (IR-G)
- An identified authority holder for A (L-2)
- A challenge mechanism (L-3) that is independent of A
- A revocation mechanism (L-4) that is independent of A
- A succession mechanism (L-5) that is constitutionally pre-defined

L-3 and L-4 are not properties of A — they are properties of the constitutional architecture surrounding A. The independence requirements for L-3 and L-4 imply that at least one constitutionally independent authority relationship must exist for each authority function.

**36D-CF-05-19 (CANDIDATE):** Constitutional legitimacy appears to require at least one constitutionally independent authority relationship for each authority function — so that challenge (L-3) and revocation (L-4) can be exercised without self-referential dependency. The minimum structural realization of this independent authority relationship remains unresolved. Whether this implies a separate entity, a separate constitutional mandate held by an existing entity, or some other constitutional arrangement has not yet been discovered.

## Section 4 — Constitutional Counterexamples

**Governing question for this section:** Can we identify constitutional arrangements where behavioral integrity clearly fails to produce constitutional legitimacy, even under ideal conditions?

---

### 4.1 Counterexample CE-01: The Perfectly Correct Unauthorized Enrollment

**Arrangement:** A historically established enrollment office (no constitutional mandate discoverable) has enrolled exactly the correct set of members for each of the past 20 elections without any known error.

**Behavioral Integrity Status:** BI-1 through BI-5 all satisfied — correctness, completeness, consistency, transparency, non-malice, all historically verified.

**Constitutional Question:** Is the enrollment for the upcoming election constitutionally grounded?

**Analysis:**
- The 20-year history of correct behavior does not retroactively establish a constitutional mandate.
- The office's decisions have no constitutional challenge mechanism — members who believe they have been incorrectly excluded cannot invoke a constitutional process.
- The history of correct behavior means the L-3 gap has not been triggered in practice — but its absence is a constitutional defect, not a resolved one.
- If a dispute arises in election 21, the absence of L-3 means the dispute has no constitutional resolution path.

**Finding:** Perfect behavioral history does not create constitutional legitimacy retroactively or prospectively. The constitutional character of the authority is established at the moment of authorization, not accumulated through behavioral track record.

---

### 4.2 Counterexample CE-02: The Perfectly Consistent Self-Referential Criteria

**Arrangement:** A criteria authority defines membership eligibility criteria and applies them with perfect consistency for 15 years. Its revocation mechanism is a supermajority vote of... the criteria authority itself (L-4 SELF-REF).

**Behavioral Integrity Status:** BI-3 (consistency) fully satisfied — the authority has never changed criteria capriciously and has always applied the same standards.

**Constitutional Question:** Is the criteria authority constitutionally revocable?

**Analysis:**
- Perfect consistency strengthens the SELF-REF loop: the authority consistently declines to revoke itself (because the criteria it applies to its own revocation are criteria it has set).
- Behavioral integrity confirms that the SELF-REF arrangement is working as designed — it does not indicate a failure.
- The constitutional defect is the structure, not the behavior.
- From TC3-NCQ-04: an authority that governs its own governors is a constitutional recursion. Perfect governance of self does not resolve the recursion — it perfects the recursion.

**36D-CF-05-20 (CANDIDATE):** A self-referential authority that behaves consistently is constitutionally equivalent to a self-referential authority that behaves inconsistently with respect to its constitutional defect — the SELF-REF structure is the defect, not the behavior within the structure.

---

### 4.3 Counterexample CE-03: The Perfectly Independent Unchallenged Certifier

**Arrangement:** A certification body holds structural independence from the election operator (IR-A), the funding body (IR-B), and political authority (IR-C). It certifies elections correctly under full behavioral integrity. No challenge mechanism exists for its certifications (L-3 UNDEFINED).

**Behavioral Integrity Status:** All BI dimensions satisfied.

**Constitutional Question:** Is the certification constitutionally valid?

**Analysis:**
- Independence (IR-A, IR-B, IR-C) is a structural property — correctly identified.
- Perfect behavior means the certifications are correct.
- But the certification is constitutionally final and unreviewable. In constitutional election law, unreviewable final judgments are generally constitutionally suspect because:
  - They concentrate ultimate authority in the certifier
  - They have no mechanism for error correction even in the event that evidence of error emerges after certification
  - They prevent any constitutional examination of the certification methodology

**36D-CF-05-21 (CANDIDATE):** Structural independence + behavioral integrity + correct certification ≠ constitutional validity of certification, if L-3 (challenge mechanism) is UNDEFINED. The certification may be correct, but its constitutional validity cannot be established without a mechanism through which it could be challenged.

This is TF-36C-05-01 reformulated in constitutional necessity terms: "Certifier legitimacy ≠ certification validity" now extends to "Correct certification ≠ constitutionally valid certification when L-3 is UNDEFINED."

---

### 4.4 Counterexample CE-04: The Perfectly Distributed Illegitimate Authority

**Scenario (testing distribution assumption from the opposite direction):** Three independent bodies collectively hold enrollment authority, each constitutionally independent of the others (full independence, no coordination mechanism). None has a constitutionally grounded legitimacy source (L-1 UNDEFINED for all three).

**Structural Distribution Status:** DISTRIBUTED authority with structural independence.

**Constitutional Question:** Is the enrollment constitutionally grounded?

**Analysis:**
- Three independent ungrounded authorities produce three instances of D43-instance-ENROLLMENT, each with L-1 UNDEFINED.
- The distribution produces a new constitutional gap: when the three bodies disagree about whether a person is eligible, the resolution mechanism is also constitutionally ungrounded.
- Perfect structural independence means there is no coordination mechanism — which means the dispute has no resolution path within the distributed arrangement itself.

**Finding:** Distribution without legitimacy grounding is constitutionally insufficient. This is the mirror image of CE-01: just as perfect behavioral history does not create legitimacy, perfect structural distribution does not create legitimacy.

**36D-CF-05-22 (CANDIDATE):** Neither behavioral integrity (CE-01/02/03) nor structural distribution (CE-04) independently produces constitutional legitimacy. Both appear necessary but insufficient conditions. Constitutional legitimacy appears to require the legitimacy dimension framework (L-1 through L-5) applied to constitutionally grounded authority holders.

## Section 5 — Trustworthiness Dependency Chain Re-Evaluation

**Governing question for this section:** What is the corroboration status of TC5-DI-03 after Round 36D analysis? What refinements does the evidence support?

---

### 5.1 TC5-DI-03 Original Formulation

> TC5-DI-03 (CANDIDATE PROGRAM-LEVEL): Ownership Assignment → Authority Distribution → Structural Independence → Constitutional Trustworthiness
>
> Three independent prerequisites: constitutional trustworthiness requires structural independence; structural independence requires authority distribution; authority distribution requires ownership assignment.

---

### 5.2 Evidence for Each Arrow

**Arrow 1: Ownership Assignment → Authority Distribution**

Round 36D analysis finding: Ownership assignment (DDD: identifying which bounded context owns which domain function) is a necessary precondition for evaluating authority distribution, but ownership assignment does not constitute authority distribution.

Applying OBS-36D-02-1: authority distribution is a constitutional property; ownership assignment is a design partition. The two are distinct. Assigning a domain function to an ownership boundary defines where the function is executed — it does not determine whether the constitutional authority is concentrated, distributed, independent, or prohibited.

Evidence: Partially supports TC5-DI-03 in that ownership assignment is a prerequisite — without knowing who owns what, constitutional authority cannot even be evaluated. But the arrow "ownership assignment → authority distribution" suggests that assigning ownership produces authority distribution, which is not supported.

**Refined Arrow 1:** Ownership Assignment is a prerequisite for evaluating Authority Distribution; it does not constitute Authority Distribution.

---

**Arrow 2: Authority Distribution → Structural Independence**

Round 36D analysis finding: Distribution without structural independence is "distribution theater" — multiple actors without constitutional independence from each other do not constitute distributed authority in the constitutional sense.

From Section 2.5 of this document: distribution serves the anti-concentration function. But distribution alone does not guarantee independence — actors can be distributed while still being controlled by a common authority, thereby collapsing the independence.

From 36D-CF-05-12: distribution serves a distinct constitutional anti-concentration function. But structural independence requires that each distributed actor satisfies its own IR requirements (IR-A through IR-H as relevant to the function).

Evidence: Partially supports TC5-DI-03 in that distribution that lacks structural independence fails to produce constitutional trustworthiness. But the arrow "authority distribution → structural independence" suggests that distributing authority automatically produces structural independence, which is not supported.

**Refined Arrow 2:** Authority Distribution is a necessary but not sufficient condition for Structural Independence. Structural Independence additionally requires that each authority holder satisfies the relevant IR requirements for that function.

---

**Arrow 3: Structural Independence → Constitutional Trustworthiness**

Round 36D analysis finding (this entire document): Structural independence appears necessary but not sufficient for constitutional trustworthiness. Constitutional trustworthiness additionally appears to require:
- Legitimacy grounding (L-1) — for each authority
- Challenge mechanism independence (L-3) — structurally separate from challenged authority
- Revocation mechanism independence (L-4) — structurally separate from revoked authority
- Succession pre-definition (L-5) — constitutionally grounded before succession occurs
- At least one constitutionally independent authority relationship per function (CF-05-19)

Evidence: Strongly supports the direction of TC5-DI-03 — structural independence appears necessary. But "structural independence → constitutional trustworthiness" without L-1/L-3/L-4/L-5 appears constitutionally insufficient.

**Refined Arrow 3:** Structural Independence + Legitimacy Grounding (L-1) + Independent Challenge Mechanism (L-3) + Independent Revocation Mechanism (L-4) + Constitutionally Pre-Defined Succession (L-5) appear together to constitute the conditions for Constitutional Trustworthiness.

## Section 6 — 36D-HYP-02 Evaluation

**36D-HYP-02 (from 36D-04 ARB Correction 3):** Constitutional interpretation may represent an additional D43 instance. Three candidate characterizations:

- **Characterization A:** Constitutional interpretation is a new D43 instance, distinct from Governance Authorization and Criteria Authority
- **Characterization B:** Constitutional interpretation is a specialization of Governance Authorization (authority over which transitions are legitimate = authority over how the constitution is applied in transition decisions)
- **Characterization C:** Constitutional interpretation is a specialization of Criteria Authority (what the constitution requires = a criteria about constitutional criteria)

---

### 6.1 Analysis of Characterizations

**Testing Characterization A (New D43 Instance):**

For constitutional interpretation to be a new D43 instance, it must exhibit:
- A distinct function not reducible to any discovered D43 instance
- Its own L-1 through L-5 legitimacy gaps
- A distinct authority holder gap

Constitutional interpretation: "Who authoritatively determines what the constitution requires in disputed cases?"

This function is distinct from:
- Governance Authorization: who authorizes phase transitions (application of known rules to specific decisions)
- Criteria Authority: who defines eligibility criteria (scope of membership)

The function of interpreting the constitution appears logically prior to both: the interpreter determines what the constitutional rule IS before governance authorization applies it or criteria authority implements it. This distinction provides evidence for Characterization A — interpretation is prior to application, which may distinguish it from a specialization of application.

However, "interpretation is logically prior" does not confirm that interpretation is a constitutionally distinct authority function. A single constitutional authority could hold both the interpretive mandate and the governance authorization mandate. Prior logical position does not necessarily constitute a separate D43 instance.

**Testing Characterization B (Specialization of Governance Authorization):**

If governance authorization = applying constitutional rules to phase transitions, and interpretation = determining what those rules require, then interpretation could be viewed as a precondition to governance authorization rather than a specialization.

A specialization would mean: governance authorization authority in one domain is exercised as interpretation authority in another context. But interpretation appears logically prior to governance authorization, not a context-specific variant of it.

Characterization B is WEAK — the evidence analyzed here suggests interpretation is prior to application rather than a context-variant of it. However, this is not a strong enough basis to rule Characterization B out entirely.

**Testing Characterization C (Specialization of Criteria Authority):**

Criteria authority defines who may vote. Constitutional interpretation defines what the constitution requires about all constitutional functions, including criteria authority.

If interpretation is a specialization of criteria, then criteria authority would include authority over constitutional requirements themselves — making criteria authority the supreme authority in the constitutional system.

This appears to invert the logical hierarchy: constitutional interpretation should be a constraint on criteria authority, not a specialization of it. Characterization C is WEAK for this reason.

---

### 6.2 36D-HYP-02 Finding

**36D-HYP-02 Assessment (CANDIDATE):** The analysis provides evidence that Characterization A is plausible — constitutional interpretation may be a distinct D43 instance on the basis that interpretive authority appears logically prior to and constitutionally separable from both governance authorization and criteria authority.

However, "plausible" does not mean "supported as a constitutional discovery." The analysis has not demonstrated that constitutional interpretation is definitively a separate constitutional function, only that:
- It appears logically prior to the two functions with which it might be identified
- Characterizations B and C have identified weaknesses

**36D-CF-05-24 (CANDIDATE — 36D-HYP-02 PARTIAL EVALUATION):** Characterization A (constitutional interpretation as new D43 instance) is plausible based on the logical priority of interpretive authority. Further discovery is required to confirm or reject this characterization. The authority holder gap for constitutional interpretation (who constitutionally grounds interpretive authority?) and the challenge mechanism gap (who can challenge an interpretation?) remain fully unresolved. These gaps cannot be characterized as discovered D43 structure until the function itself is confirmed as constitutionally distinct.

**Status:** 36D-HYP-02 → Characterization A PLAUSIBLE — further discovery required. Carry to Round 37.
