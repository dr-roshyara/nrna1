## Explanatory Coverage: Qualitative Assessment

**Question: How well does each concept explain observed domain behavior?**

### Governance

**Observed Explanatory Coverage:**

Current evidence suggests Governance explains:
- Rule definition and structure
- Authority scope boundaries
- Legitimacy standards definition
- Context boundaries
- Delegation permissions

**Observed Explanatory Gaps:**

Current evidence suggests Governance does NOT explain:
- How permission becomes power (Gap 1, Step 7)
- Why some authority claims are accepted and others rejected (Gap 2, Step 7)
- How trust relationships operate
- How consensus emerges

**Qualitative Assessment:** Strong explanatory coverage for rule-definition behaviors. Weak or absent coverage for acceptance/power/consensus behaviors.

**Evidence Source:** Steps 1, 2, 3, 5

---

### Authority

**Observed Explanatory Coverage:**

Current evidence suggests Authority may explain:
- Context-specific authority variation
- Decision binding patterns
- Authority recognition asymmetry

**Observed Explanatory Gaps:**

Current evidence suggests Authority does NOT fully explain:
- How unverified authority operates (contradicts verification-centric structures)
- Permission-to-power translation (Gap 1, Step 7)
- Why Authority acceptance is selective (Gap 2, Step 7)

**Qualitative Assessment:** Moderate explanatory coverage for context-specific behaviors. Significant gaps remain around legitimacy and acceptance.

**Evidence Source:** Steps 2, 4, 5, 6, 7

**Note:** Authority classification remains uncertain (Step 4). Its explanatory role may vary depending on what Authority actually is (Strategic Concept vs. Capability vs. Process).

## Contradiction Severity Assessment

**Question: What are the largest contradictions?**

From Steps 6 and 7:

### Tier 1 — Major Contradictions

**Unverified Authority Contradiction:**

Unverified authorities are observed to exist and operate (Step 2). Yet some candidate structures (Step 6, Structure B: Verification-Centric) assume Authority only emerges from successful Verification.

Current evidence shows this assumption contradicts observed domain behavior.

**Authority Classification vs. Role Uncertainty:**

Authority is classified as Unknown/possibly multiple types (Step 4). Yet candidate structures assume Authority is Strategic Concept. This mismatch remains unresolved.

---

### Tier 2 — Moderate Contradictions

**Governance-Verification Direct Relationship:**

Governance shows direct relationship to Verification (Step 5, strong). Yet linear hierarchies (Step 6, Structure A) assume Authority intervenes between them. This suggests either Authority's role is different than assumed, or hierarchical structure is incorrect.

**Permission-Power Gap:**

No concept fully explains how Governance permission becomes effective power (Gap 1, Step 7). All explanations are partial, suggesting either missing concept or incomplete understanding of existing concepts.

---

### Tier 3 — Minor Contradictions

**Trust and Consensus Independence:**

Whether Trust and Consensus are independent concepts or emergent remains unresolved. This does not fundamentally invalidate candidate structures but adds uncertainty.

---

## Current Interpretation

**What Current Evidence Suggests About Explanatory Coverage:**

- Governance appears to provide strong coverage for rule-definition and boundary-setting behaviors
- Verification appears to provide moderate coverage for legitimacy-checking behaviors
- Authority, Trust, and Consensus show weak or uncertain explanatory coverage
- Five major transition gaps appear to lack full explanation
- All concept relationships remain either partially understood or bidirectional/unclear

**What Evidence Does NOT Yet Show:**

- Which candidate strategic structure best explains the domain
- Whether current concepts are sufficient or if new concepts are needed
- Whether contradictions represent model failure or incomplete understanding
- Whether transitions require new concepts or reveal incomplete understanding of existing ones

**Overall Coherence Assessment:**

Current evidence provides coherent explanation for some domain phenomena (governance rules, verification, decision progression). Current evidence does not provide complete coherent explanation for other phenomena (authority acceptance, trust formation, consensus emergence).

The domain appears partially explained by current concepts.
