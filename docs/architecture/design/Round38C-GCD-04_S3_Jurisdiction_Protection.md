## 1. The constitutional property (S-3)

Per 38C-15 C.4: *The mandate of constitutional oversight bodies shall explicitly include election oversight and trust-function evaluation, and shall not be narrowed, reduced, or stripped by ordinary amendment.* S-3 is the **anti-jurisdiction-stripping** safeguard.

**Why S-3 may stress the model:** jurisdiction is **relational** in a way appointment and amendment are not. It carries concepts — *scope, delegation, reservation, competence, interpretation, boundary between bodies* — that concern *demarcating* authority among bodies, not a single authority acting. That is the suspected stress point.

## 2. Pass 1 — S-3 Governance Capability Catalog (purpose only)

### GC-S3-01 — Jurisdiction Definition Capability
**Purpose:** Establish the explicit mandate/scope of each oversight body (what it covers).

### GC-S3-02 — Scope Entrenchment Capability
**Purpose:** Protect a body's mandate/scope from being narrowed, reduced, or stripped by ordinary amendment. **↔ couples GC-S1-02** (tiered threshold); scope is one of the things S-1 entrenches.

### GC-S3-03 — Competence Determination Capability
**Purpose:** Determine, in ambiguous or contested cases, whether a matter falls within a given body's jurisdiction — including boundary disputes between bodies, delegation, and reservation of scope.
**⚠ STRESS POINT — candidate additional stage *or* a refinement of the Authority stage (see §4).** It answers *who has authority / where governance may occur* — it does not perform governance. Define / allocate / reserve / delegate competence may be **sub-capabilities of the Authority stage** rather than a new stage.

### GC-S3-04 — Jurisdiction Observability Capability
**Purpose:** Make scope and any change to it visible; detect stealth stripping or scope-creep over time.

### GC-S3-05 — Jurisdiction Challenge Capability
**Purpose:** Enable a challenge to a jurisdiction stripping or to a body's overreach. **Routes to GC-S4-01/02.**

### GC-S3-06 — Jurisdiction Restoration Capability
**Purpose:** Void an unconstitutional stripping and restore the mandate (the Correction for S-3).

## 4. The stress point — read three ways (NOT resolved here)

**Reading A — refinement of the Authority stage (EGCP holds; leading reading).** Competence determination answers *who has authority / where governance may occur* — it does not perform governance. Define / allocate / reserve / delegate competence may all be **sub-capabilities inside the Authority stage**. On this reading EGCP survives intact and GC-S3-03 enriches Authority rather than adding a stage.

**Reading B — genuine additional stage (EGCP stressed).** Jurisdiction is *relational*; allocation/interpretation of competence is a continuous activity distinct from granting, exercising, or adjudicating. It would sit between Authority and Exercise:
  `Authority → [ALLOCATION / COMPETENCE-INTERPRETATION] → Exercise → Observation → Challenge → Correction`
On this reading EGCP would need revision.

**Reading C — S-3 operates at a different level (bounds EGCP's scope).** S-3 may govern *boundaries between safeguards*, not the lifecycle *of* one safeguard — in which case EGCP (a single-safeguard lifecycle) neither breaks nor fully applies; its *scope* is simply bounded to entity-internal safeguards.

> **Recorded observation:** *S-3 appears to govern **inter-safeguard boundary integrity** rather than the internal lifecycle of a single safeguard.* It governs **interactions, not actions.** That is a stronger, more interesting finding than immediately inventing a lifecycle stage — and it is why all three readings remain open.

**Disposition (per frozen-lens discipline):** genuinely undetermined. GCD-04 **does not resolve it** and **does not edit EGCP-01 (frozen).** It records GC-S3-03 as a **candidate additional stage *or* Authority-stage refinement**, and flags S-3 **PARTIAL / STRESSED.** Per EGCP-01's tightened confirmation rule (condition 3), **EGCP-01 graduation remains UNDETERMINED pending S-5** — *not failed*; this is the falsification protocol **succeeding**. The question "does any safeguard require an extra stage?" is now live, to be settled only after GCD-05.

This is the "richer result": the most informative outcome was never "everything fit." It is *"most safeguards share one lifecycle; jurisdiction may be a distinct class — or a distinct level."*
