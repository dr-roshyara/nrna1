# Round 14 Step 4 — Concept Classification Workbook

**Strategic Design: Concept Type Exploration**

**Date:** 2026-06-05  
**Status:** Classification Exploration  
**Objective:** Map possible classification categories for candidate concepts without determining which classification is correct

---

## Mission

Step 3 discovered that concepts may not be peers:

```text
Governance
Authority
Verification
Evidence
Trust
Consensus
Decision Lineage

may belong to different architectural categories.
```

Step 4 explores what types these concepts might be.

Goal: Document possible categories with supporting and contradicting evidence.

**Important:** This is exploration, not classification. We preserve uncertainty, not resolve it.

---

## Candidate Categories

```text
Strategic Concept — Shapes domain structure
Capability — Enables domain function
Process — Sequence of steps/events
Asset — Material/information used by domain
Social Property — Relationship between actors
Temporal Structure — Pattern through time
Unknown — No category fits yet
```

---

## Investigation Method

For each concept:

1. List possible categories
2. Document evidence supporting each
3. Document evidence contradicting each
4. Note confidence level
5. Preserve competing interpretations

No verdicts. No final classifications.

## Concept 1: Governance

**Possible Categories:**

- Strategic Concept
- Temporal Structure
- Unknown

**Evidence for Strategic Concept:**

- Governance sets rules (acts on domain)
- Other concepts reference Governance
- Governance defines structural boundaries
- Governance creates permissible structures

**Evidence Against Strategic Concept:**

- Governance might only exist because community recognizes it
- Alternative: Governance is emergent from social agreement, not independent
- Alternative: Governance is procedural codification of behavior patterns

**Evidence for Temporal Structure:**

- Governance rules persist over time
- Governance can change through deliberate process
- Governance has history

**Evidence Against Temporal Structure:**

- Temporal structure alone doesn't explain rule-setting
- Time doesn't explain why rules constrain

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

**Current Interpretation:**

Governance may be Strategic Concept. Alternative interpretations possible.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Governance independent, or emergent from community recognition?
- Can Governance exist without actors to implement it?
- Is Governance purely Strategic, or does it have Temporal aspects?

## Concept 2: Authority

**Possible Categories:**

- Strategic Concept (if independent)
- Capability (if enabler)
- Process (if applied Governance)
- Social Property (if recognition-dependent)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Strategic Concept:**

- Authority could act independently on domain
- Authority could shape how decisions are made
- Authority could be autonomous from Governance

**Evidence Against Strategic Concept:**

- Authority depends on Governance permissions
- Authority may be just Governance applied in context
- Authority may be defined by Governance scope

**Evidence for Capability:**

- Authority could enable role-holders to make decisions
- Authority provides mechanism for power
- Authority serves other domain functions

**Evidence Against Capability:**

- Capabilities serve something; Authority appears autonomous
- Authority is not described as enabling others

**Evidence for Process:**

- Authority could be procedural (how Governance rules are applied)
- Authority could be operational flow
- Authority exercise follows steps

**Evidence Against Process:**

- Authority is described as explanatory concept, not procedural detail
- Authority appears to have strategic role

**Evidence for Social Property:**

- Authority is only effective when recognized by others
- Authority depends on actor acceptance
- Authority is relationship-dependent

**Evidence Against Social Property:**

- Authority involves acting, not just relationships
- Authority makes binding decisions (more than characterizing relationships)

**Confidence:** LOW

**Current Interpretation:**

Authority may belong to one of four categories. Evidence split among them. Cannot determine yet.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Authority independent from Governance?
- Is Authority effectiveness determined by others' recognition?
- Does Authority act, or does it enable others to act?
- Could Authority be fully explained by combination of Governance + Verification + Trust?

## Concept 3: Verification

**Possible Categories:**

- Capability
- Process
- Strategic Concept (unlikely)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Capability:**

- Verification enables legitimate decisions to proceed
- Verification provides legitimacy-checking mechanism
- Other concepts depend on Verification
- Verification requires Governance (what to verify) and Evidence (what to verify against)

**Evidence Against Capability:**

- Capabilities typically serve something; Verification appears independent
- Verification could be Strategic layer

**Evidence for Process:**

- Verification follows procedural steps
- Verification has sequence (check, validate, determine)
- Verification resolves in outcomes

**Evidence Against Process:**

- Process alone doesn't explain what makes decisions legitimate
- Verification appears to be about evaluation, not just procedure

**Evidence for Strategic Concept:**

- Verification could be independent layer that other concepts depend on
- Verification explains legitimacy determination

**Evidence Against Strategic Concept:**

- Verification depends on Governance (standards to verify against)
- Verification depends on Evidence (material to verify)
- Verification may be Governance's legitimacy-checking mechanism, not independent

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

**Current Interpretation:**

Verification may be Capability or Process. Whether it's Strategic remains uncertain.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Verification independent, or is it Governance's validation mechanism?
- Can Verification exist without Governance defining what to verify?
- Is Verification a single concept, or multiple sub-concepts (validation, verification, legitimacy-determination)?

## Concept 4: Evidence

**Possible Categories:**

- Asset
- Temporal Structure
- Process (unlikely)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Asset:**

- Evidence is material/information
- Evidence is acted upon (evaluated, referenced)
- Evidence persists (documentable)
- Other concepts use Evidence
- Evidence is managed/collected

**Evidence Against Asset:**

- Assets are typically passive; Evidence is interpreted
- Evidence role is more active than typical asset

**Evidence for Temporal Structure:**

- Evidence is created over time
- Evidence endures
- Evidence history affects decisions
- Evidence accumulates through time

**Evidence Against Temporal Structure:**

- Temporal structure alone doesn't explain evidence as justification
- Asset category captures evidence nature better

**Evidence for Process:**

- Evidence collection follows procedures
- Evidence evaluation follows steps

**Evidence Against Process:**

- Evidence is material/outcome, not procedural flow
- Process generates evidence; evidence is not itself a process

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Current Interpretation:**

Evidence may be Asset, with secondary Temporal aspects. This seems the strongest fit.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Evidence purely an Asset, or does it have active role?
- Does evidence evaluation belong to separate concept?
- Is evidence quality/sufficiency an independent concept?

## Concept 5: Trust

**Possible Categories:**

- Social Property
- Emergent Property (not independent)
- Capability (relationship-enabling)
- Temporal Structure (develops over time)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Social Property:**

- Trust is relationship between actors
- Trust is not independent thing; it's quality of relationships
- Trust varies by actor pair and context
- Trust influences behavior through social dynamics

**Evidence Against Social Property:**

- Trust may have independent effects beyond relationships
- Trust could be strategic in enabling domain function

**Evidence for Emergent Property:**

- Trust may emerge from Verification + Evidence
- People trust verified, evidence-backed claims
- Trust might not be independent from verification mechanisms

**Evidence Against Emergent Property:**

- Trust could be foundational social phenomenon
- Trust may precede verification relationships

**Evidence for Capability:**

- Trust could be domain's capability to form relationships
- Trust enables cooperation and decision-making

**Evidence Against Capability:**

- Capability suggests domain does something; Trust is something between actors
- Trust is not a capability the domain exercises; it's characteristic of relationships

**Evidence for Temporal Structure:**

- Trust develops over time
- Trust relationships change
- Trust history matters

**Evidence Against Temporal Structure:**

- Temporal aspect is secondary to relational aspect
- Trust is more about relationships than temporal progression

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM

**Current Interpretation:**

Trust may be Social Property. Whether it's independent or emergent from Verification + Evidence remains unresolved.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Trust independent, or emergent from Verification + Evidence?
- Is Trust a fundamental social phenomenon, or derivative?
- Is Trust strategic, or tactical/operational?
- Can Trust be created deliberately, or only emerge through time?

## Concept 6: Consensus

**Possible Categories:**

- Process
- Strategic Concept (if independent decision layer)
- Social Property (collective agreement)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Process:**

- Consensus has procedural steps
- Consensus has sequence/flow
- Consensus resolves in outcomes
- Consensus follows patterns

**Evidence Against Process:**

- Consensus could be independent decision mechanism
- Consensus might not be procedural; might be outcome

**Evidence for Strategic Concept:**

- Consensus could be independent decision layer
- Consensus determines what's legitimate
- Consensus could be alternative to Authority
- Different contexts might require different consensus rules

**Evidence Against Strategic Concept:**

- Governance might fully define consensus requirements
- Consensus might be just "applying Governance rules with multiple actors"
- Consensus may be procedural implementation, not strategic concept

**Evidence for Social Property:**

- Consensus is about collective actor agreement
- Consensus reflects shared relationships

**Evidence Against Social Property:**

- Consensus produces outcomes (more than characterizing relationships)
- Social Property alone doesn't explain decision-making role

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM

**Current Interpretation:**

Consensus may be Process or Strategic Concept. Whether it's independent from Governance remains uncertain.

**Open Questions:**

- Is Consensus a Strategic layer, or procedural (Governance rules executed with multiple actors)?
- Could Governance fully define consensus requirements?
- Is Consensus about decision-making power, or just about agreement?

## Concept 7: Decision Lineage

**Possible Categories:**

- Temporal Structure
- Asset (historical record)
- Process (decision flow recording)
- Unknown

**Evidence for Temporal Structure:**

- Lineage documents how things evolve through time
- Lineage traces decision progression
- Lineage shows temporal patterns (8-stage sequence)
- Lineage persists (history endures)

**Evidence Against Temporal Structure:**

- Temporal structure alone doesn't explain decision stages
- Asset category (historical record) also describes it

**Evidence for Asset:**

- Lineage is information/documentation
- Lineage is acted upon (consulted for reversals)
- Lineage persists
- Lineage is managed/maintained

**Evidence Against Asset:**

- Assets are passive; Lineage has structure/pattern
- Temporal structure better captures flow aspect

**Evidence for Process:**

- Lineage documents procedural flow
- Lineage records steps

**Evidence Against Process:**

- Process generates flow; Lineage records what happened
- Lineage is outcome, not procedure

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

**Current Interpretation:**

Lineage may be Temporal Structure, with Asset aspects. Whether the 8-stage pattern is inherent to Lineage or to decision-making process remains uncertain.

**Open Questions:**

- Is the 8-stage pattern a property of Lineage, or of decision-making itself?
- Is Lineage truly independent, or just "recording of how other concepts operate over time"?
- Could Lineage be emergent from other concepts operating sequentially?

## Observed Patterns

### Pattern 1: Unequal Classification Confidence

**Observation:**

Governance and Evidence show evidence for fewer category options.

Authority, Trust, Consensus show evidence for multiple category options.

Decision Lineage and Verification show medium-level confidence.

**Current Interpretation:**

Unequal classification confidence may indicate that some concepts are fundamentally different types than others.

**Alternative Interpretation:**

Classification framework may be imprecise, causing apparently different concepts to fit multiple categories equally.

---

### Pattern 2: Multiple Categories Per Concept

**Observation:**

Most concepts show plausible evidence for multiple category assignments.

Authority fits Strategic Concept, Capability, Process, and Social Property.

Trust fits Social Property, Emergent, Capability, and Temporal Structure.

**Current Interpretation:**

Multiple categories per concept may indicate concepts are fundamentally ambiguous about their architectural type.

**Alternative Interpretation:**

Concepts may belong to multiple categories simultaneously (e.g., both Capability and Process).

---

### Pattern 3: Governance's Own Category Uncertain

**Observation:**

Multiple concepts reference Governance.

Governance itself shows evidence for Strategic Concept and Temporal Structure.

**Current Interpretation:**

Governance's ambiguous classification is significant because other concepts depend on it.

**Alternative Interpretation:**

Governance classification may clarify once other relationships are understood.
