## Part B — Structural Independence

### B.1 — Formal Definition

**Structural Independence:** The property of a constitutional evaluating authority whose constitutional legitimacy does not trace through any function grant of the authority being evaluated.

In NRNA terms: a mechanism is Structurally Independent of MA's trust function grants if:
1. Its constitutional existence is grounded in a source that is not derived from MA's constitutional authority
2. MA's exercise of its trust function grants cannot constitutionally affect the mechanism's legitimacy, mandate, or jurisdiction
3. No modification of MA's trust function grants (even at Tier 3) can constitutionally alter the mechanism's grounding

**Structural Independence is a property of GROUNDING, not of OPERATION.** An authority may operate independently (functional independence) while remaining structurally dependent — if its existence traces to the authority it evaluates.

### B.2 — Required Properties

For an evaluation mechanism to be Structurally Independent of MA in the NRNA context, it must satisfy ALL three:

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **SI-1 (Source Distinctness)** | The evaluating mechanism's constitutional source does not trace through MA's three trust function grants (Legitimacy, Authenticity, Temporal) |
| **SI-2 (Amendment Immunity)** | MA's exercise of any of its 15 constitutional functions cannot constitutionally reduce or eliminate the evaluating mechanism's jurisdiction over MA's trust function exercise |
| **SI-3 (Standing Independence)** | The evaluating mechanism has constitutional standing to rule against MA's exercise of trust function grants without requiring MA's consent, ratification, or non-objection |

### B.3 — Failure Modes

**SI-Failure-1 — Source Contamination:** The evaluating mechanism's constitutional source traces through any MA function grant. Once contaminated, even operationally independent behavior cannot produce constitutional external reference — the SOURCE is the constitutional problem, not the OPERATION.

**SI-Failure-2 — Amendment Vulnerability:** If the evaluated authority (MA) can constitutionally reduce the evaluating mechanism's jurisdiction — even at Tier 3 thresholds — then structural independence is conditional on the evaluated authority's non-exercise of that amendment capacity. True structural independence is unconditional.

**SI-Failure-3 — Standing Derivation:** If the evaluating mechanism's constitutional standing to rule against MA derives from MA's own charter or provisions, then MA's constitutional power can eliminate that standing through provision modification (even without direct elimination of the mechanism).

### B.4 — Relationship to CERD

Structural Independence is the most direct constitutional remedy for CERD. CERD states: no constitutionally grounded authority can evaluate MA's trust function grants from an independent vantage point.

A Structurally Independent mechanism, by definition, satisfies the CERD requirement: its constitutional grounding is independent, its mandate covers MA's trust function exercise, and its standing is not derivable from MA.

Under Strategy A (Level 2b), Structural Independence is achieved at the trust root level — each root's sovereign is structurally independent of the others. The multi-root CERD is eliminated. However (addressing the architect's challenge from the assessment):

**Per-sovereign CERDs under Strategy A require separate evaluation.** Each new sovereign has its own self-reference problem — can its trust function grants be evaluated from an independent vantage point? But this is constitutionally different from multi-root CERD:

Under multi-root CERD: all three roots simultaneously lose external reference capacity — CO-5 certification becomes constitutionally self-referential because all three CO-5 preconditions (CO-2/CO-3/CO-4) are evaluated using standards controlled by the same source.

Under per-sovereign CERDs: each root's OWN governance has a self-reference problem, but CO-5 draws from three independently grounded roots. Cross-root verification remains constitutionally available — the Authenticity sovereign can evaluate whether the Legitimacy sovereign's governance is constitutionally maintained, and vice versa. The CO-5 independence requirement (ADR6-INV-01) is structurally preserved because the three CO-5 preconditions draw from constitutionally independent sources.

**Finding:** Per-sovereign CERDs are a lesser constitutional problem than multi-root CERD. Multi-root CERD directly defeats CO-5 constitutionality. Per-sovereign CERDs create single-root self-reference risks that are constrained by cross-root independence. The original 38C-13 treatment of per-sovereign CERDs as equivalent to multi-root CERD was overstated — they are constitutionally distinct.

## Part C — Functional Independence

### C.1 — Formal Definition

**Functional Independence:** The property of a constitutional evaluating authority whose operations and constitutional judgments are protected from interference by the authority being evaluated, even when the evaluating authority's constitutional legitimacy traces to the same ultimate source.

In NRNA terms: a mechanism is Functionally Independent of MA's trust function exercise if:
1. Its judgments on MA's trust function exercise cannot be overridden, set aside, or nullified by MA
2. Its operational mandate cannot be reduced without constitutional processes that provide genuine (not procedural) obstacles
3. Its appointment procedures provide meaningful protection against ongoing control by the authority it evaluates

**Functional Independence is a property of OPERATION and PROTECTION, not of SOURCE.** An authority may be functionally independent while remaining structurally dependent — if the source traces to the evaluated authority but the operations are constitutionally protected.

### C.2 — Required Properties

For an evaluation mechanism to be Functionally Independent of MA in the NRNA context, it must satisfy ALL of:

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **FI-1 (Non-Subordination)** | MA cannot override, nullify, or direct the evaluating mechanism's constitutional judgments on trust function exercise |
| **FI-2 (Constitutional Entrenchment)** | The evaluating mechanism's independence mandate is constitutionally protected at a threshold that provides genuine (not merely procedural) protection against elimination |
| **FI-3 (Appointment Insulation)** | The evaluating mechanism's appointment procedures do not give MA ongoing influence over the judgment-makers within the evaluation's relevant domain |
| **FI-4 (Jurisdictional Completeness)** | The evaluating mechanism's jurisdiction explicitly and unambiguously covers MA's exercise of trust function grants (not merely existing provisions) |
| **FI-5 (Interpretive Finality)** | Within the evaluating mechanism's jurisdiction, its constitutional interpretations are final — not subject to reversal by MA or MA-derived actors |

### C.3 — Failure Modes

**FI-Failure-1 — Progressive Appointment Normalization (PAN):** The most dangerous failure mode specific to Family-B. MA controls appointment processes (OBS-38B04-02: 15 MA functions including 6 appointment functions). Over time, constitutionally valid appointments fill the evaluating mechanism with MA-aligned actors. These actors progressively narrow the mechanism's interpretation of its own independence mandate — each individual interpretation is constitutionally defensible; the cumulative effect is functional capture without any formal independence violation.

PAN is constitutionally distinctive because:
- It is CONSTITUTIONALLY UNDETECTABLE at any single point (each appointment and each interpretation is individually valid)
- It is SELF-REINFORCING (a captured evaluation mechanism validates the governance structure that captured it — this is CRC applied to the independence mechanism itself)
- It is IRREVERSIBLE once entrenched (the captured mechanism interprets the constitutional protections that protect the mechanism — circular interpretation favoring its own continued capture)

This failure mode reveals that functional independence in Family-B has its own CVI problem: the independence mechanism can itself become subject to Constitutional Verification Incompleteness — the evaluation mechanism cannot verify its own independence from outside itself once captured.

**FI-Failure-2 — Interpretive Constriction:** Even without PAN, a constitutionally faithful evaluation mechanism may progressively constrain its own jurisdiction through conservative interpretation. If the mechanism interprets its mandate narrowly (e.g., "we evaluate existing EC provisions, not MA's exercise of authority beyond them"), CERD remains because the trust function grant evaluation is excluded from the jurisdiction in practice.

**FI-Failure-3 — Constitutional Amendment Asymmetry:** If the authority that benefits from reducing functional independence holds an asymmetric constitutional advantage in the Tier 3 amendment process (e.g., MA's 15 functions include appointment of key actors in the Tier 3 ratification process), then the "near-unanimity" threshold provides less protection than it appears. The threshold is nominally high; the effective threshold may be lower.

**FI-Failure-4 — Sequential-Act Erosion:** Even without reaching the Tier 3 amendment threshold, MA can progressively reduce the evaluating mechanism's practical effectiveness through individually Tier 2-permitted acts (reducing funding, narrowing scope of reviewed matters, introducing procedural requirements that constrain timing of evaluations). Each act is individually minor; the cumulative effect is functional equivalence to eliminating the mechanism.

### C.4 — Relationship to CERD

Functional Independence CONSTRAINS CERD rather than eliminating it. This distinction is architecturally significant:

- Under Structural Independence: the evaluating mechanism sits OUTSIDE the CERD-affected domain — it is constitutionally grounded independently of MA's trust function grants
- Under Functional Independence: the evaluating mechanism sits WITHIN the CERD-affected domain — it traces to MA's authority — but is constitutionally protected within that domain

The constitutional question for CERD purposes: is protection WITHIN a domain constitutionally equivalent to grounding OUTSIDE a domain for the purpose of external constitutional reference?

This is the central constitutional judgment in 38C13-OQ-01. The doctrine analysis in Part D provides evidence; the ARB determination is made in Part G.

## Part D — Comparative Evidence Mapping (38C-07)

### D.1 — Evidence from Each Mechanism

| Mechanism | Independence Type | Evidence for NRNA |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| **Germany Art.79(3)** | Functional Independence with structural protection | The Federal Constitutional Court enforces Art.79(3). The Court's legitimacy traces to the Basic Law it protects. Art.79(3) is structurally protected (unamendable by any process) even though the enforcement mechanism (Court) is functionally independent rather than structurally independent. **Evidence: Functional independence WITH constitutional entrenchment (Tier 3 equivalent) is constitutionally sufficient for the German model.** |
| **India Basic Structure** | Functional Independence (judicially created) | The Supreme Court derives its legitimacy from the Constitution of India, which Parliament originally created. The Court's basic structure doctrine is self-asserted — not granted by Parliament. The Court claims jurisdiction against Parliament using constitutional authority derived from the same constitutional order Parliament created. **Evidence: Functional independence can be constitutionally sufficient even when legitimacy traces to the authority being constrained, PROVIDED the evaluating mechanism has constitutional finality of its judgments.** |
| **Separation of Powers** | Functional Independence (shared source) | All three branches derive legitimacy from the constitutional order (ultimately from democratic sovereignty). They are NOT structurally independent of each other. Yet separation of powers is universally recognized as constituting genuine constitutional independence among branches. **Evidence: Functional independence with operational domain protection and mutual non-subordination constitutes constitutionally recognized independence even when legitimacy traces to a shared source.** |
| **Constitutional Courts** | Functional Independence (source in constitution) | Constitutional courts derive their existence from the constitutions they interpret. Their independence from the legislature and executive is functional (protected by constitutional provisions) not structural (independent source). **Evidence: The constitutional court model — functional independence — is the DOMINANT constitutional approach to independent evaluation of constitutional legitimacy. Structural independence is not the constitutional norm.** |
| **Protected Core Models** | Functional Independence + Structural Content | Protected core models (eternity clauses etc.) protect specific constitutional CONTENT structurally (content cannot be amended) while maintaining functionally independent enforcement. The content is structurally protected; the enforcement mechanism is functionally independent. **Evidence: The standard constitutional pattern separates CONTENT protection (structural) from ENFORCEMENT mechanism (functional). This is directly applicable to NRNA — Tier 3 provisions protect content; CIC provides functional enforcement.** |
| **Non-Merger Doctrine** | Functional Independence | Non-merger doctrine is enforced by courts that trace their legitimacy to the constitutional order that creates the merger risk. Enforcement is functionally independent; source is common. **Evidence: Non-merger constraints are constitutionally enforced through functional independence — not structural independence.** |
| **Constitutional Identity** | Mixed | Constitutional identity doctrine operates through courts (functional independence) but some versions also involve non-waivable constitutional commitments (structural content protection). **Evidence: Both independence types can coexist; structural content protection + functional enforcement is constitutionally coherent.** |
| **Ackerman (Higher Lawmaking)** | Functional Independence (conditional) | Ackerman's model relies on democratic mobilization as the external reference point — functionally independent of ordinary legislative process through the requirement of cross-institutional constitutional moments. Not a structural independence model. **Evidence: Conditional on — functional independence can be achieved through heightened threshold requirements that make functional capture constitutionally difficult.** |

### D.2 — Evidence Summary by Independence Type

**Evidence supporting Structural Independence as required:**
- None of the eight comparative mechanisms require structural independence as a baseline
- No mechanism examined in 38C-07 is based on genuinely independent constitutional source (all trace to the same constitutional order or democratic source)

**Evidence supporting Functional Independence as sufficient:**
- ALL eight mechanisms provide evidence that functional independence (with constitutional entrenchment and operational protection) is the constitutional norm for evaluating constitutional legitimacy
- The dominant pattern is: functionally independent enforcement mechanism + structurally protected content (eternity clauses, basic structure, etc.)
- Constitutional courts — the most direct analogue to CIC — universally operate on functional independence

**Critical observation from the evidence:** The comparative constitutional evidence does NOT support structural independence as the constitutional baseline for independence. Every major comparative mechanism operates on functional independence, often combined with structural content protection (eternity clauses, basic structure). This is architecturally significant for the 38C13-OQ-01 question.

## Part E — Family-B Analysis

### E.1 — Can Family-B Satisfy Functional Independence?

**Theoretical answer:** Yes — under specific constitutional conditions.

The comparative evidence (Part D) shows that functional independence with constitutional entrenchment and operational protection is constitutionally recognized as genuine independence. Family-B (single sovereign MA) can theoretically provide functional independence through:
- Tier 3 protection of CIC's independence mandate from MA modification
- Appointment independence provisions for CIC members (staggered terms, supermajority removal, etc.)
- Explicit jurisdictional mandate covering MA's trust function exercise
- Constitutional finality of CIC interpretations on trust function legitimacy

**The three challenges specific to Family-B:**

**Challenge 1 — PAN (Progressive Appointment Normalization):**
MA controls 6 of 7 appointment functions (OBS-38B04-02). Even with constitutionally constrained appointment procedures, MA's appointment influence over CIC means that over sufficient time, functionally valid appointments can produce CIC compositions that narrow CIC's interpretation of its own independence mandate. This is the STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT on functional independence in Family-B — not that functional independence is impossible, but that it is vulnerable to time-horizon capture that no single constitutional provision fully prevents.

**Challenge 2 — CIC Interpretive Dependency (OBS-38B04-01):**
CIC interprets the Tier 3 provisions that protect CIC's own independence. This creates a meta-level CVI: if CIC is captured, it interprets its own independence protections narrowly. This circular structure is recognized in 38B-07 (OBS-38B04-01 — CIC interpretive dependence) but its constitutional implications for OQ-38B05-05 were not fully resolved. Structural independence eliminates this meta-level CVI; functional independence is subject to it.

**Challenge 3 — Amendment Asymmetry:**
MA's 15 functions include appointment of actors in the Tier 3 ratification process (indirectly). If MA-aligned actors hold sufficient constitutional positions, the "90% near-unanimity" Tier 3 threshold may be achievable by MA-aligned actors seeking to reduce CIC's independence mandate. The effective threshold may be lower than the nominal 90%.

### E.2 — Conditions Under Which Family-B Can Satisfy Functional Independence

Family-B theoretically satisfies functional independence if — and only if — ALL of:

| Condition | Description | Current NRNA status |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **FI-B-01** | CIC's independence mandate is Tier 3 protected, explicitly including jurisdiction over MA's trust function exercise | NOT YET ESTABLISHED — CIC exists (38B-01); trust function evaluation mandate not explicitly specified |
| **FI-B-02** | CIC appointment procedures include constitutional PAN-resistance mechanisms (staggered terms > one election cycle; supermajority removal; term limits; appointment by multiple actors none of whom is MA) | NOT YET ESTABLISHED — 38B-04 specifies MA appointment; PAN-resistance not designed |
| **FI-B-03** | CIC's interpretations of its own independence mandate are constitutionally reviewable by a mechanism other than CIC itself (meta-review) | NOT ESTABLISHED — no meta-review mechanism for CIC self-interpretation |
| **FI-B-04** | Sequential-act erosion of CIC's effectiveness is constitutionally prohibited (not just single-act prohibition) | NOT ESTABLISHED — current Tier 3 provision prevents single-act simultaneous multi-root effects; sequential-act erosion not addressed |
| **FI-B-05** | The Tier 3 threshold for reducing CIC's independence mandate excludes actors whose appointment traces to MA's appointment functions | NOT ESTABLISHED — Tier 3 ratification includes MA-derived actors |

### E.3 — The Meta-CVI Problem Unique to Family-B

Under structural independence, the evaluating mechanism's CVI problem is contained within its own sovereign domain — the other two sovereigns provide cross-verification. Under functional independence in Family-B, the evaluating mechanism (CIC) faces its own CVI problem with NO cross-verification: CIC interprets its own independence mandate, and there is no constitutionally independent authority to evaluate whether CIC's interpretation of its independence mandate is constitutionally sound.

This Meta-CVI is the principal constitutional distinction between structural and functional independence:
- Structural independence eliminates Meta-CVI (the evaluating mechanism's grounding is constitutionally independent)
- Functional independence is SUBJECT TO Meta-CVI (the evaluating mechanism's interpretation of its own protection is self-referential)

Whether Meta-CVI is constitutionally fatal to functional independence, or constitutionally manageable (as CVI generally is manageable through external anchors), is an ARB judgment — identified in Part G.

## Part F — Sufficiency Test

### F.1 — Minimum Conditions for Functional Independence to Be Constitutionally Sufficient

Drawing from the comparative evidence (Part D) and the Family-B analysis (Part E), the following minimum conditions emerge as constitutionally required before functional independence can be considered sufficient for the external reference purpose that CRC/CERD identifies:

**Minimum Condition 1 — Constitutional Entrenchment at Appropriate Tier:**
The independence mandate must be protected at a constitutional tier that provides genuine (not merely procedural) protection. The German model and India Basic Structure both provide what in NRNA terms would be at least Tier 3 protection. Tier 1 or Tier 2 protection provides insufficient constitutional friction to constitute genuine functional independence against the authority being evaluated.

*NRNA implication:* CIC's trust root evaluation mandate must be Tier 3 protected — not merely specified in the EC as a Tier 1 or Tier 2 provision.

**Minimum Condition 2 — Non-Subordination in the Evaluated Domain:**
The evaluating mechanism's judgments on the specific subject matter for which independence is claimed (trust function exercise) must be constitutionally final — not subject to override, reversal, or nullification by the evaluated authority. Operational independence in other domains is insufficient; non-subordination must apply specifically in the trust function evaluation domain.

*NRNA implication:* CIC's rulings on MA's trust function exercise must be constitutionally final within CIC's mandate — not subject to MA ratification or veto.

**Minimum Condition 3 — PAN Resistance Mechanism:**
For functional independence to be constitutionally durable (not merely point-in-time), appointment procedures must provide genuine protection against Progressive Appointment Normalization. This requires at minimum:
- Appointment terms exceeding any single MA tenure
- Appointment by processes not solely controlled by MA
- Removal procedures requiring constitutional supermajority independent of MA appointment functions

*NRNA implication:* 38B-04's current MA-appointment model for CIC is constitutionally insufficient for trust function evaluation purposes without PAN-resistance provisions.

**Minimum Condition 4 — Jurisdictional Explicitness:**
The functional independence must be jurisdictionally explicit — the evaluating mechanism's mandate must unambiguously cover MA's exercise of trust function grants (not merely interpretation of existing provisions). Jurisdictional gap risks (FI-Failure-2 — interpretive constriction) must be constitutionally excluded.

*NRNA implication:* CIC's charter must explicitly state jurisdiction over "MA's exercise of its three trust function grants" — not merely "interpretation of existing EC provisions concerning trust roots."

**Minimum Condition 5 — Meta-CVI Acknowledgment and Partial Management:**
Since functional independence in Family-B is subject to Meta-CVI (self-referential interpretation of own independence mandate), the constitutional design must explicitly address this. Minimum management approaches:
- Explicit constitutional text designating which class of CIC interpretations on its own mandate requires heightened scrutiny
- CAB (constitutionally distinct from CIC per 38B01-INV-01) having jurisdiction over CIC's self-interpretation in the trust function domain
- Constitutional standing for S-2/S-3 actors to challenge CIC's interpretation of its own independence mandate (per 38C03-INV-02)

*NRNA implication:* Meta-CVI cannot be fully eliminated within Family-B, but constitutional standing to challenge CIC's self-interpretation provides partial management. This must be constitutionally explicit.

### F.2 — Conditions That Are Desirable But Not Minimum

The following conditions would strengthen functional independence but cannot be designated "minimum" based on available evidence:

- Fixed terms for CIC members (desirable but not proven constitutionally required)
- Independent funding source for CIC not subject to MA budget control (desirable; no comparative evidence makes this minimum)
- Public reporting requirements for CIC's trust function evaluation activities (desirable for constitutional transparency; not minimum)

### F.3 — The Gap Between Current NRNA Design and the Sufficiency Test

Comparing the five minimum conditions against current NRNA constitutional design:

| Condition | Current Status | Gap |
|-----------|---------------|-----|
| FI-B-01 (Tier 3 entrenchment) | CIC exists (38B-01); trust function mandate not Tier 3 specified | SUBSTANTIAL |
| FI-B-02 (Non-subordination in evaluated domain) | CIC interpretive; CAB adjudicative; trust function ruling finality not specified | MODERATE |
| FI-B-03 (PAN resistance) | MA appointment predominates (38B-04); no PAN resistance provisions | SUBSTANTIAL |
| FI-B-04 (Jurisdictional explicitness) | CIC mandate: interpretation of existing provisions; trust function exercise evaluation not explicit | SUBSTANTIAL |
| FI-B-05 (Meta-CVI management) | 38C03-INV-02 provides challenge standing; insufficient for Meta-CVI | MODERATE |

**Finding:** The current NRNA constitutional design does NOT yet satisfy the minimum conditions for functional independence to be constitutionally sufficient. This gap applies to BOTH OQ-38B05-05's assessment of whether Strategy B is currently adequate AND any evaluation of Strategy B's potential adequacy with additional constitutional design.

## Part G — Remaining ARB Judgment Calls

The following questions require ARB constitutional judgment. The doctrine analysis in this document cannot answer them — they are appropriately constitutional governance decisions.

### G.1 — Primary Judgment Call (Ruling-Blocking)

**ARB-JC-01:** Is functional independence — when combined with the five minimum conditions in Part F — constitutionally sufficient for the external constitutional reference purpose identified by CERD/CVI? Or does the Meta-CVI problem (the evaluating mechanism's self-referential interpretation of its own independence mandate) constitute a constitutional defect that only structural independence can address?

**What the doctrine analysis contributes:** Comparative evidence (Part D) strongly supports functional independence as constitutionally recognized across all major constitutional systems studied. The Meta-CVI problem exists in Family-B specifically. Whether Meta-CVI is constitutionally fatal (requiring structural independence) or constitutionally manageable (addressable through partial management mechanisms in F.1-Condition-5) is not determinable from the available evidence — it is a constitutional design judgment.

**Constitutional positions available to the ARB:**
- Position 1: Functional independence with five minimum conditions is constitutionally sufficient. Strategy B (if designed to satisfy the conditions) meets OQ-38B05-05's requirement.
- Position 2: Meta-CVI constitutes a constitutional defect that makes functional independence insufficient within Family-B. Structural independence (Strategy A) is required for OQ-38B05-05.
- Position 3: Functional independence with five minimum conditions is constitutionally sufficient in principle, but the current gap between NRNA's design and those conditions means that neither Strategy A nor Strategy B AS CURRENTLY DESIGNED satisfies OQ-38B05-05. Constitutional design work is required before a ruling can be outcome-determinative.

### G.2 — Secondary Judgment Calls

**ARB-JC-02:** Does the PAN failure mode (Progressive Appointment Normalization) constitute a constitutional defect in Family-B functional independence that cannot be adequately addressed through constitutional design within Family-B? Or can PAN-resistance mechanisms (Condition 3 of Part F) constitutionally adequate?

**ARB-JC-03:** Is the composite Strategy B+C subject to CIC common-mode failure? Both Strategy B and Strategy C depend on CIC for enforcement. If CIC undergoes PAN-capture, both mechanisms fail simultaneously. Does this common mode failure change the constitutional assessment of B+C relative to B alone?

**ARB-JC-04:** For the Strategy A per-sovereign CERD assessment: the analysis in Part B.4 identified that per-sovereign CERDs are constitutionally less significant than multi-root CERD (because CO-5 draws from three independently grounded roots, enabling cross-root verification). Does the ARB accept this characterization, or does it hold that any sovereign-level CERD constitutes the same class of constitutional problem regardless of multi-root vs. single-root scope?

**ARB-JC-05:** Given that the comparative evidence unanimously supports functional independence as the constitutional norm (Part D.2), and given that the evidence does NOT support structural independence as constitutionally required in comparative constitutional practice, is there NRNA-specific evidence that overcomes this comparative baseline and requires structural independence specifically for NRNA's trust root evaluation?
