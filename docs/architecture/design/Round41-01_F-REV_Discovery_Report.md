## D1 — Capability Architecture (Phases 1–2)

**Boundary.** F-REV = the capability that **resolves disputes about whether power was exercised legitimately and issues binding determinations.** Sub-capabilities: *Interpretation* (what the rules mean) · *Adjudication* (resolve a dispute) · *Appeal* (re-examine a determination) · *Finality* (terminate the dispute) · *Enforcement-by-acceptance* (the ruling takes effect).

**Boundary tests (hostile — falsify prior seams):**
- *vs F-AUTH:* appointing reviewers is F-AUTH; **F-REV reviews the validity of appointments** → these two are in a **recursion**, not a clean seam (RQ-REC-01).
- *vs F-PROC:* running the vote is F-PROC; ruling on whether it was correct is F-REV.
- *vs F-THR:* **F-THR's "Decision/Escalation/Adjudication" sub-capability (Round40-02/03 D4) was already F-REV.** F-THR escalates *into* F-REV; F-REV is the **terminus** of F-THR's detection→correction loop.

**Observation (O-REV-01).** F-REV is **less a sibling family than the constitutional *terminus*** every other family escalates into. *Interpretation:* the "flat family taxonomy" is wrong — F-REV is an *authority layer*, F-THR is *cross-cutting*, F-AUTH/F-PROC are *substrate*. *Prediction-impact:* supports RQ-THR-01 (F-THR cross-cutting) and challenges the peer-family model. *Alternative:* F-REV is a peer family that merely *also* serves as terminus — kept open.

**Constitutional purpose (Phase 2).** Review is **required by constitutional theory, not implementation convenience.** Without a finality mechanism, no dispute ever closes; with infinite contestability, **no decision is ever legitimate.** Therefore: **finality is constitutive of legitimacy** — and finality is what F-REV produces (grounded in S-2 final rulings, S-5 standing). Properties: *protected* = legitimacy, the other families' outputs; *consumed* = independence (F-AUTH), evidence (F-PROC/F-THR); *emergent* = finality, binding authority, legitimacy.

## D3 — Authority Architecture (Phase 5 — central)

Investigated independently. Findings on whether the authority *kinds* are same / overlapping / independent:

| Authority kind | Present in NRNA? | Notes |
|----------------|------------------|-------|
| Institutional | yes | the body exists and is mandated (S-1) |
| Procedural | yes | rules confer process authority |
| Constitutional | yes but **self-referential** | the constitution authorizes the body that interprets the constitution (recursion) |
| Decisional | yes | the power to issue a binding ruling (S-2) |
| **Legal (state-backed coercion)** | **ABSENT** | a voluntary diaspora association has no state to enforce rulings |

**Finding (F-REV-AUTHORITY).** Because **legal/coercive authority is absent**, NRNA's review authority is ultimately **consent-based (moral/political), not coercive.** Rulings are obeyed because members accept them. *Interpretation:* **legitimacy is not conferred once; it is continuously renewed by consent.** Capture = either withdrawing the consent base or **manufacturing false consent** (information capture, F-THR). *Evidence:* the enforcement gap (O-REV-02) + the absence of an external sovereign (Round40-04 D5). *Confidence:* Medium-High (structural, 1 family). *Population:* founding-stage voluntary association. *Alternative explanation considered:* authority could rest on reputational lock-in rather than live consent — but reputational lock-in is itself a form of accumulated consent → not simpler, not preferred.

## D4 — Independence Architecture (Phase 6 — highest priority; bears on SI-01 / RQ-SEM-01)

Re-investigated **without reusing** the Semantic Inventory's framing (used only as evidence). Result: institutional, operational, and decisional independence are **three distinct concepts in a strict dependency order**, plus a discovered fourth.

```
Decisional independence  (can rule free of influence)
        ⟹ requires ⟹
Operational independence (can act/detect without interference)
        ⟹ requires ⟹
Institutional independence (insulated source/appointment — S-3)
```

- You **can** have institutional independence *without* decisional independence (independently appointed yet politically pressured).
- You **cannot** have decisional independence *without* operational *without* institutional. → **strict implication chain: Decisional ⟹ Operational ⟹ Institutional.**
- **Discovered fourth — Perceived independence** (sociological): independence *as members see it*. A body actually independent but *perceived* as captured **loses legitimacy anyway** (because authority is consent-based, D3). Perceived independence is **distinct and load-bearing**, not reducible to the other three.

**Finding (F-REV-INDEP).** "Independence" is **four distinct concepts** — three in a dependency chain (Decisional⟹Operational⟹Institutional) plus an orthogonal **Perceived** independence. They are **not** facets of one term and **not** a flat spectrum. *This is the proposed resolution of SI-01* (recorded as a delta, D13; not frozen — the Ontology round formalizes). *Confidence:* Medium-High. *Alternative considered:* "one concept, four contexts" — rejected, because the failure modes differ (institutional-capture ≠ perception-capture).

## D5 — Legitimacy Architecture (Phase 7 — hardest; bears on SI-02)

Discovered **structurally**, not defined philosophically.

| Question | Discovery |
|----------|-----------|
| What **creates** legitimacy? | consent + correct process + independence (all 4) + finality + transparency |
| What **destroys** it? | capture; **perceived** capture; broken finality (infinite appeal); opacity; unremedied irreversible harm |
| What **maintains** it? | continuous consent; consistent rulings (precedent); transparency |
| What **restores** it? | acknowledged error + correction + accountability — but only **partially** |
| Partial recovery? | **Yes** — correct the future; past harm stands (= Contained-Only, Round40-03 D5) |
| Legitimacy without review? | **No** — without finality, disputes never close; nothing is legitimate |
| Review without legitimacy? | **Yes** — a captured body "reviews" but confers none → review is **necessary, not sufficient** |

**Finding (F-REV-LEGITIMACY).** Legitimacy is a **composite emergent property = {consent, independence(×4), finality, transparency, correctability}**, **continuously renewed (not conferred once)**, and **asymmetric**: slow to build, fast to lose, only **partially** recoverable. *Proposed SI-02 delta (not frozen).* *Confidence:* Medium. *Open question (O-REV-Q1):* is "consent" itself decomposable (active vs tacit vs manufactured)? Recorded, not answered.

## D6 — Failure Architecture (Phase 8 — assume failure)

| Failure | Mechanism | Severity |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Review without enforceability** | correct ruling ignored by the controlling body; no coercive backstop (O-REV-02) | **acute, founding-stage dominant** |
| **Infinite appeal** | no finality → no legitimacy | high |
| **Premature/locked finality** | S-2 + S-1 entrench a *captured* ruling (RQ-FAIL-03 confirmed) | high |
| Captured review | decisional independence fails | high |
| Perceived capture | actual independence intact, perception lost → consent withdrawn | high (consent-based) |
| Review blocked by **anonymity** | cannot re-derive votes to adjudicate a ballot dispute | structural (Contained-Only) |
| Constitutional self-interpretation deadlock | body interprets own mandate; no external arbiter | structural (Meta-CVI) |
| Review after irreversible damage | coerced/altered election cannot be un-run | partial-recovery only |

**Finding (F-REV-FAIL).** The two **knife-edges**: (1) **finality vs contestability** — too little finality destroys legitimacy (infinite appeal), too much locks in capture (deadlock); S-2 sits exactly on this edge. (2) **enforceability** — without coercion, a correct ruling depends on acceptance; **review-without-enforceability is the acute founding-stage failure.** *Both confirm RQ-FAIL-01/03.*

## D7 — Recursion Architecture (Phase 9 — second priority; the deepest result)

Every discovered recursion and **where it terminates:**

| Recursion | RQ | Terminates? | By what |
|-----------|----|-----|---------|
| Review of review (appeal of appeal) | — | yes | **fiat** — S-2 declares a final ruling |
| Appointment ↔ review of appointment | RQ-REC-01 | only at the **founding appointment** | **axiom** — the bootstrap appointment is treated as given |
| Evidence ↔ review of evidence (meta-audit) | RQ-REC-02 | yes | **axiom** — the immutable audit trail is trusted as ground truth |
| Oversight ↔ review (F-THR ↔ F-REV) | RQ-REC-03 | **no internal terminus** | only **consent** (Meta-CVI) |
| Constitutional self-interpretation | — | **no internal terminus** | **consent** (members accept; in extremis, re-found) |

**Finding (F-REV-RECURSION) — the central theoretical result.** Governance recursions terminate in exactly **three ways**: **(a) fiat** (the constitution *declares* a stop — S-2), **(b) axiom** (a ground is *trusted* without further review — the audit trail, the founding appointment), **(c) consent** (members *accept*; ultimately, re-found). **There is no internal logical/​proof-based terminus.** *Interpretation:* a constitution **without an external sovereign cannot terminate its own recursions by proof** — only by declaration, trust, or acceptance. Therefore the **final constitutional trust anchor resides in the constituent body (the members)** — exercised as consent and, in the limit, the power to re-found. **No organ can be the final anchor**, because every organ is inside a recursion that bottoms out in consent. *This is the answer to OQ-38B05-05 ("where is the final constitutional trust anchor allowed to reside?"): in the constituent, not in any organ.* *Confidence:* Medium-High (structural + consistent with 38C-15 Meta-CVI "managed not eliminated"). *Alternative considered:* an external anchor (state court, international body) — rejected by transferability (founding-stage voluntary diaspora association has none); the internal anchor is forced, and it is consent.

## D17 — Executive Scientific Summary

F-REV, approached as the **constitutional theory of legitimate authority**, produced the program's deepest result. Review is **constitutionally necessary** because **finality is constitutive of legitimacy** — without a terminus, nothing is ever legitimate. But the decisive discovery is about **authority itself**: in a founding-stage voluntary diaspora association with **no external sovereign and no coercive (legal) backstop**, review authority is **consent-based** — rulings hold because members *accept* them, so **legitimacy is continuously renewed, never conferred once**, and is **asymmetric** (slow to build, fast to lose, only partially recoverable). "Independence," re-discovered from scratch, is **four distinct concepts** — Decisional⟹Operational⟹Institutional (a strict dependency chain) plus an orthogonal **Perceived** independence that is load-bearing precisely *because* authority is consent-based. Legitimacy is an **emergent family-level composite** of {consent, the four independences, finality, transparency, correctability}. Most profoundly, **every governance recursion terminates only by fiat, axiom, or consent — never by proof** — so the **final constitutional trust anchor resides in the constituent body (the members), not in any organ**, answering the program's deepest open question (OQ-38B05-05): a self-governing constitution without an external sovereign anchors trust in *consent*, with the power to re-found as the ultimate terminus. The acute founding-stage failure is **review-without-enforceability** (a correct ruling the controlling body ignores), and the two knife-edges are **finality-vs-contestability** and **enforceability**.
