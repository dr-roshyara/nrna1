### B.14 — CD-08: 38B05-INV-01 Three Amendment Tiers

**Ruling:** AMBIGUITY CONFIRMED — CIC ESCALATION REQUIRED; ARB TIER DETERMINATION REQUIRED SEPARATELY

**Status:** AMBIGUOUS — NOT FINALLY CLASSIFIED

**C-11 Directional Finding:** C-11 (Misclassification Impact Test) directs toward Principle. The constitutional cost of False Form classification (losing meta-constitutional protection for the amendment process itself, enabling Tier 3 reclassification via Tier 2 procedure) dominates the cost of False Principle classification. This directional finding is noted. It does not constitute a ruling.

**Why Ambiguity Persists:**

The three-tier amendment structure exhibits both constitutional and architectural properties. The existence of tiered protection appears constitutionally required (Principle candidate). The specific threshold values (90%/near-unanimity, 180-day deliberation, etc.) are architectural (Form candidate). Separability was found in 38C-05, producing CD-08-P (Principle component) and the threshold values as its Form component. However, the CD-08 separability finding requires CIC resolution of Q1 (whether three-tier structure is itself constitutionally required versus one form of differential protection) before the Principle component can be confirmed.

**CD-08 Tier Routing — Governing the CIC/ARB Boundary (Revised per 38C-05B R3 and Reviewer Guidance):**

The tier question for CD-08 splits into two distinct sub-questions that must not be conflated:

*Sub-Question (a) — CIC Interpretation:*
"What constitutional protection does the three-tier amendment structure implicitly claim based on existing provisions?"
Specifically: does 38B05-INV-01, read in conjunction with its own Tier 3 Protected Core catalog, imply that the amendment process is itself Tier 3 protected? This is an interpretive question about what existing text means. It routes to CIC.

*Sub-Question (b) — ARB Governance Design:*
"What protection level should the three-tier amendment structure receive — should it be adopted as a Tier 3 or Tier 2 constitutional element?"
This is not purely interpretive. Existing text does not specify its own tier assignment. Choosing Tier 3 is a constitutional governance design decision. It routes to ARB. **This ruling does not make this determination.**

**CIC Escalation Status:** EscRule-01 TRIGGERED. Three questions route to CIC (see 38C-05B Updated Escalation Register): Q1 (Principle vs. differential protection form), Q2 (does existing provision specify reclassification procedure; if not, CD-08-GAP-01 flagged), Q3 (does three-tier structure require Tier 3 protection — CIC interprets what existing text implies). Q3 answer informs but does not replace ARB Sub-Question (b).

**ARB Determination Required:** After CIC Q1 and Q3, ARB must determine what tier the three-tier structure should receive. This is a constitutional governance decision, not an interpretation.

**Tier assignment for CD-08:** NOT made by this ruling. Cannot be made until CIC Q1/Q3 resolved and ARB makes Sub-Question (b) determination.

**Escalation Status:** CIC Q1, Q2, Q3 (HIGH priority). ARB Sub-Question (b) (HIGH priority, sequential after CIC Q1/Q3).

**Dependencies:** CD-08 classification depends on CIC Q1 determination. CD-08 tier depends on ARB Sub-Question (b). The Constitutional Self-Destruction analysis (OBS-38A06-SD1) depends on CD-08 tier assignment — if three-tier amendment structure is not Tier 3 protected, the active constitutional friction provided by 38B-05 loses its meta-constitutional character.

**Ruling:** AMBIGUITY CONFIRMED — ESCRULE-04 MANDATORY; OQ-38B05-05 ROUTES TO ARB

Whether structural separation is constitutionally required (Principle) or an architectural property that the current specification achieves by design (Form) is the question OQ-38B05-05 asks. Additionally, TR-05 reflexivity applies: any constitutional provision protecting trust root separation would use the trust roots as its own legitimacy grounding.

**CD-10 Cannot Be Classified By This Ruling:** OQ-38B05-05 is the MANDATORY PRIMARY REQUIREMENT for 38C. This ruling cannot resolve it. Classifying CD-10 without resolving OQ-38B05-05 would either implicitly answer the constitutional architecture question (if classified Principle) or implicitly decline to answer it (if classified Form). Neither is within the authority of this ruling.

**CIC Escalation Status:** EscRule-04 MANDATORY. Three CIC questions pending (Q1: is separation constitutionally required based on existing provisions?; Q2: if yes, at what tier?; Q3: single provision or emergent property?). These CIC questions ask what existing provisions mean. They do not substitute for the ARB constitutional architecture decision.

**OQ-38B05-05 Routes to ARB:** The question of whether trust root separation SHOULD become a constitutional principle is an ARB constitutional architecture decision. This is distinct from CIC's interpretive role. After CIC Q1 is answered (whether existing provisions mandate it) and ARB decides OQ-38B05-05 (whether it should be constitutionally required if not already mandated), the classification of CD-10 can proceed.

**CD-10-CONFORM-01 Placeholder:** Whether the current architecture satisfies the constitutional requirement (whatever CIC/ARB determine it to be) is an ARB conformity assessment that cannot begin until the requirement is defined. This placeholder remains deferred.

**Escalation Status:** CIC Q1/Q2/Q3 (CRITICAL priority). ARB OQ-38B05-05 constitutional architecture ruling (CRITICAL — MANDATORY PRIMARY REQUIREMENT).

**Dependencies:** The classification of CD-10 depends on OQ-38B05-05 ARB ruling and CIC Q1–Q3. The three trust root governance specifications (38B-01/02/03) and their deferred architectural candidates (DC-E01/DC-E02/DC-E03) all depend on CD-10's eventual classification.

## Part G — Readiness for OQ-38B05-05 Evaluation

*Note: This section characterizes the constitutional architecture landscape as it stands after this ruling. It does NOT evaluate or resolve OQ-38B05-05. It does NOT take a position on whether trust root separation should be constitutionally required.*

### G.1 — Evidence Now in the Record

The classification exercise, the ARB reviews, and this ruling have produced the following evidence relevant to OQ-38B05-05:

**Evidence for constitutional significance of trust root separation:**

1. *Three trust roots structurally differentiated (OBS-38B06-02):* The 38B specifications produce three distinct governance models (Legitimacy/Authenticity/Temporal), each with separate governance bodies, specifications, and constitutional grounding through MA. This structural differentiation exists in the current design.

2. *Differentiation is not constitutionally protected (OBS-38B06-02):* A Tier 2 coalition could collapse the Legitimacy Root (EC governance) and Authenticity Root (AC-31) without triggering Tier 3 protection. The structural differentiation is architecturally maintained, not constitutionally entrenched.

3. *C-11 Misclassification Impact Test result:* False Form classification of CD-10 would leave a Tier 2 coalition path to collapsing trust roots unaddressed. The constitutional cost asymmetry favors Principle classification.

4. *TM-39/F-4 Independence Illusion structural FAIL:* Dominant residual FAIL-class risk. Trust root collapse via coalition is the structural mechanism that F-4 describes. If trust root separation is not constitutionally protected, F-4's structural mitigation remains constitutionally unsupported.

5. *Three-root simultaneous failure (38A-05/06):* The analysis shows that EC + AC-31 + GovernanceState simultaneous compromise (C-F→F) produces a CO-5 constitutionally undetectable false result. This failure scenario depends on the roots not being constitutionally separated in a way that prevents coordinated compromise.

6. *TR-05 Reflexivity:* Any constitutional provision protecting trust root separation would use the trust roots as its own legitimacy grounding. This is not a reason to avoid the question — it is a constitutional design challenge that must be addressed when drafting the provision (if OQ-38B05-05 is ruled affirmative).

**Evidence against treating separation as already constitutionally required:**

1. *No existing EC provision mandates it:* CIC Q1 remains open. No existing constitutional text has been identified that explicitly requires trust root structural separation.

2. *MA anchors all three roots (OBS-38B06-03 / OBS-38B02-01):* All three trust roots trace to MA at the source-of-source level. MA's 15 constitutional functions include anchoring all three roots. MA is a sovereign concentration point. The roots are not fully independent from each other at the sovereign level — they share MA.

3. *OBS-38A06-SD1 friction limitation:* The three trust roots all depend on EC (Legitimacy Root) for their constitutional grounding. EC governs the other two roots' governance structures. If EC itself is captured (while maintaining formal validity), the three-root differentiation may become formal rather than functional.

### G.2 — What Remains Unknown

1. *Whether any existing constitutional provision mandates structural separation* — CIC Q1 is open. The program has not found an explicit provision. Whether implicit requirements exist is an interpretive question CIC must address.

2. *The minimum constitutional specification for trust root separation* — if OQ-38B05-05 is ruled affirmative, what specific constitutional text is required? How does TR-05 reflexivity factor into the drafting? These are EC Design questions that cannot begin until ARB rules.

3. *Whether structural separation is achievable without resolving AA-01* — AA-01 (Membership Assembly pre-constitutional legitimacy) underpins all three trust roots via MA. If MA is compromised at the pre-constitutional level, constitutional protection of trust root separation may be insufficient.

4. *How separation interacts with the MA tri-root dependency* — OBS-38B02-01 documents that MA now anchors all three trust roots. Constitutional separation of the roots from each other at the EC/specification level does not eliminate the MA sovereign concentration at the source-of-source level.

5. *Whether the three-root architecture satisfies any separation requirement that CIC/ARB defines* — CD-10-CONFORM-01 (ARB conformity assessment) cannot begin until the requirement is defined.

### G.3 — Constitutional Architecture Questions Remaining Open

1. **OQ-38B05-05 (MANDATORY PRIMARY REQUIREMENT):** Should trust root structural separation be constitutionally required, and if so, at what constitutional level?

2. **CD-10 CIC Q1:** Does any existing constitutional provision mandate structural separation?

3. **TR-05 Reflexivity Resolution:** How should a constitutional provision protecting trust root separation ground itself constitutionally without using the roots it protects?

4. **MA Tri-Root Dependency:** At what point does MA's anchor role over all three trust roots constitute a structural vulnerability that CD-10's Principle classification (if ruled affirmative) must address?

5. **OQ-38A05-02 Interaction:** If trust root separation is constitutionally required, how does OQ-38A05-02 interact when a finalized CO-5 is later discovered to involve AC-31 compromise? Does constitutional separation affect retroactive constitutional status? (Routes to CIC — not to be resolved here.)

### G.4 — Characterization of the Constitutional Architecture Landscape

The program has built a constitutionally grounded governance architecture over 38A and 38B. The three trust roots are structurally differentiated and separately specified. The constitutional challenge that OQ-38B05-05 poses is: the differentiation currently exists in the architecture — but is it constitutionally required to exist? If it is not constitutionally required, a valid constitutional process could eliminate it. If it is constitutionally required, the specification work in 38B becomes constitutionally entrenched at the appropriate tier.

The answer to OQ-38B05-05 is the most consequential single ruling the program has not yet made. After it is made, the constitutional architecture can begin to be evaluated for conformity (CD-10-CONFORM-01) and the EC extension process (38C03-CON-02) can begin for the Principles confirmed in this ruling.

*This section has described the landscape. It has not evaluated OQ-38B05-05. It has not taken a position. The ruling ends here.*
