# RATIONALE (r3, repair r1): one paragraph per gap, then the r1 repairs

**G1: R-A3 scope.** *Rule:* R-A3 applies only when three things hold: the section is a register row (`## R-<n>`); the span itself contains either a performative of one of the five listed lemmas (adopt, authorize, register, supersede, annotate), **or** a refusal, withdrawal or reversal of one of those five operations; and that act is a self-performance, not a report. It is never extended by analogy. Any other self-performance, positive or negative, is coded `act.state = NONE` with `act.basis = SELF_PERFORMED_OUTSIDE_RA3`. Examples are classify, recommend, apply, approve, retire, matrix verdicts, "Expressly NOT promoted", and "declines to resolve". The operation and the would-be state (`act.state_if_extended`) are recorded. Self-performance is defined in D3 by the text's own form (present-tense, headline or operative performative or disposition, with no reporting sign), without reference to R-A3. *Why:* R-A3 is the only r2 licence for treating text as its own act, and it was already flagged as building H-D's view into the observations. The schema should therefore apply it no further than its words, and **equally in both directions**. r0 let negative dispositions of any operation count as acts while positive ones were closed, so non-promotions were acts and promotions were not (review B1). The same gate now applies to both. A closed lemma list decides each case, so coders cannot disagree about what counts as "like adopting". This also matches the spirit of R-A1, under which acts are observed, not constructed from a text's normative character. The basis and `state_if_extended` tags put the convention in view, so an analysis can drop it or extend it (THEORY-CHOICES TC-1) without recoding. *Rejected:*
- Extending R-A3 to classify/recommend by analogy. This widens the flagged bias, and "analogous" is not operational.
- Deleting R-A3, i.e. coding all self-performance NONE (the other symmetric option the TASK offered). This would reverse r2's R-A3 first sentence outside the gap and is itself a theory choice. It stays available as TC-1 alternative 1b.
- Leaving negative dispositions unscoped (r0). This is asymmetric and was undisclosed.
- A separate basis value for self-performed negative dispositions. Polarity is already visible in `act.state` and `state_if_extended`. A new value would make the gate depend on polarity, which is the asymmetry being removed.
- Letting a section heading's "(adopted by …)" supply the act. That is the adopting row's act, and it belongs to that row's record.

**G2: Level of the governed act.** *Rule:* `about_operation` is set by literal attachment, i.e. the operation that the operator, verdict or status predicate grammatically governs. When one predicate governs coordinated verbs, they are listed together. An operation named only by a noun or option label supplies the target. In a span that carries a disposition verdict, content the span only *mentions* supplies `about_target` and never a separate norm. Mentioned content is a candidate behaviour's name, a proposed rule line, a quoted principle, or the evidence for the verdict. A disposition verdict must contain the meta-operation's verb ("PROMOTE", "NOT promoted") or an eligibility operator, and it is coded ELIGIBLE or INELIGIBLE about that meta-operation, unless an explicit operator outranks it (R-N3). A status label on the item ("Remains Candidate", "Stays in ADR-MP-06", a category letter) is a status predicate on the item, not a verdict. *Why:* the disagreement came from coders re-levelling the norm, up to *promote* or down to the behaviour. Re-levelling in either direction is an interpretation. Literal attachment is observable and gives one answer per clause. It is not tilted toward either level: "may begin RED" or "does not begin" attach to substantive work, and "PROMOTE" or "forbids promoting" attach to the meta-act, purely as the text has it. r0 listed "remains candidate" and "stays in …" as negative verdicts, which lifted literal status labels to *promote*. That is the re-levelling R-N1 forbids (review N2), so r1 removes it and lists the lifted reading as TC-2 alternative 2c. The mention/use distinction keeps a proposal's content from being coded as a norm in force. It is confined to verdict-bearing spans because that is where G2 arose. *Rejected:*
- Always coding the substantive behaviour (REQUIRED/STATUS_CONSTRAINT). This infers a deontic value no span states and builds in H-B's coupling.
- Always coding the meta-act, including via status labels. This builds in H-D's framing even where the text governs work or states a status.
- Mapping a recommendation to REQUIRED or AUTHORIZED. A recommendation neither obliges nor authorizes.
- Applying the mention/use rule to every span (r0). This drops norms cited as grounds or reported in logs (review N5) and reaches beyond G2. The choice is listed as TC-5.

The remaining question, whether adopting an instrument incorporates its mentioned behaviours as substantive norms, is left open as TC-2.

**G3: Priority, ACT_REFUSED vs ACT_OCCURRED.** *Rule:*
- A fixed ranking for competing candidates on one (operation, target): negative disposition (refused / withdrawn / reversed) > attempted > occurred. Self-performances outside R-A3's scope are removed first, whatever their polarity.
- Same-event candidates are merged. A generic deciding verb whose content is "not X" is coded as X, ACT_REFUSED. A listed lemma is never replaced by merging. Distinct events go to separate records.
- The refused operation X is what the refusal expression's complement names ("declines to resolve …" → *resolve*). It is never taken from the section or from what the refused item would have done.
- Refusal needs a proposal **stated in words** in the span or section, and a refusal expression that disposes of it. An inferred proposal does not count. An explicit refusal verb without a stated proposal is UNKNOWN. A bare negation without one is NONE, as R-A1 already requires. Continuative negations ("has not been", "remains un-"), contrastives ("rather than X") and reasons against an unchosen option are never refusals.
- On a single record with several candidates, the highest rank wins (rank 1 highest). Within a rank, the candidate stated first in the span wins, and the others are recorded as `SPLIT_REQUIRED`.

*Why:* the refusal is the more specific description of the same event. Ranking R-A3 first would turn every negative ruling into a positive norm-making act, which is the flagged convention's bias at its most visible. Requiring a stated proposal and an explicit disposition keeps refusals observable. Non-selection of an option, or mere non-occurrence, is not visible as an act. A proposal the coder infers is a coder judgement, and it was what left R01 open in r0 (review B2). Fixing X to the complement removes the remaining choice between "refused to resolve" and "refused to adopt R-81..R-85". R01 is now determinate: NONE / SELF_PERFORMED_OUTSIDE_RA3 (*resolve* is not listed), with `state_if_extended = UNKNOWN` (no proposal to resolve is stated). R-A5 decides only these competitions. It adds no condition to ACT_REVERSED or to reported ACT_OCCURRED, which keep their r2 meaning (review minimality item 3). *Rejected:*
- ACT_OCCURRED priority. This double-counts register acts and hides refusals.
- Treating every unchosen option in an option set as refused. This is an inference from non-selection, and the choice is already recorded on the chosen option's record.
- Splitting every such span into two records. This contradicts r2's "events resolving to one span are one record" for what is a single event.
- Allowing inferred proposals with a stated premise (r0). This reopens the coder judgement R-A2 is meant to close.
- Taking X from the item the refused act would have affected (e.g. *adopt* the prepared rulings). This reads the section into the refusal and is not what the span says.
- A substantive tie-break among same-rank candidates (e.g. the "more governance-relevant" operation). This is not observable. Span order is, and nothing is lost because the others are recorded.

**G4: PREPARED / unadopted rulings and ACT_ATTEMPTED.** *Rule:* ACT_ATTEMPTED means an actor initiated X and a **source statement** says the initiation did not by itself take effect. `attempt_kind` is PREPARED (marked prepared / not adopted / awaiting adoption) or FAILED (rejected or errored). ATTEMPTED outranks ACT_OCCURRED. These are not ATTEMPTED:
- conditional or provisoed acts (the condition goes on the NORM);
- proposals;
- planned acts.

A PREPARED ruling is both an attempt at its own operation (e.g. *authorize* WP-4C-1) and a proposal for a different operation, *adopt*, on the ruling itself. The two never share an (operation, target), so D4 and D6 do not conflict. A later adoption or hold goes in `act.later_disposition` and does not recode the act. The NORM still records the stated content (e.g. AUTHORIZED), with the PREPARED marker in `stated_by`, and does not code force. *Why:* effectiveness is decided only by the source's own marking, never by the coder's judgement of an actor's capability. Coder judgement would import H-D's "acts by actors holding the capability" into the coding. Separating content from effectiveness keeps the H-D claim that force depends on the making act's status testable rather than assumed (TC-3). *Rejected:*
- ACT_OCCURRED for prepared rulings. This ignores an explicit source statement of non-effect.
- NONE. This loses the fact that an instrument was issued.
- A new PREPARED state. This changes r2's state vocabulary when a defined ATTEMPTED plus a subtype suffices.
- Coding the norm as NONE or UNKNOWN when its act is PREPARED. This would infer a norm's absence from an act's status, the converse of R-A1.

**G5: REQUIRED vs STATUS_CONSTRAINT.** *Rule:* the value is read from the span. An obligation operator attached to an operation gives REQUIRED. A prohibition operator gives PROHIBITED, including a negated permission ("no self-issued act may adopt"). Without any operator, a status predicate gives STATUS_CONSTRAINT. Status predicates are:
- a standing label;
- a non-commencement statement, which is R-A1's own case "implementation does not begin";
- a listed performative participle, or its negation.

Status nouns such as "standard" or "permanent" stay STATUS_CONSTRAINT even when they imply conformity. Conditions being met or unmet are facts, not norms. The section may not supply the operator or the status predicate. R-A1's "PROHIBITED / a status constraint" is resolved to STATUS_CONSTRAINT. r1 lists only the operators this boundary needs (obligation and prohibition). The other deontic values keep their r2 meaning, and the broader prospective-indicative reading is left as TC-6. *Why:* the presence of a deontic operator is directly observable and splits the cases cleanly. Inferring obligation from a status ("a standard binds", so REQUIRED) is a coder judgement and was the source of the disagreement. Keeping the operator in the span honours the smallest-sufficient-span unit. The boundary is lexical and does not depend on whether the governed operation is work or norm-making, so it treats both hypotheses' material alike. *Rejected:*
- A functional test ("does it in effect oblige someone?"). This is not decidable from the span.
- Defaulting "does not / standard" to PROHIBITED/REQUIRED. This upgrades descriptions into operators.
- Using section text to supply the operator. This would make the deontic value depend on how much context a coder reads.
- A closed lexicon for every deontic value (r0's D7). G5 needed only the REQUIRED / PROHIBITED / STATUS_CONSTRAINT boundary. The full lexicon changed values outside the disagreement set and missed "no … may" (review minimality item 1).
- Treating every prospective indicative as a status predicate (r0's D8 (ii)). This turns descriptions and predictions into norms on work (review N4). It is listed as TC-6.

**G6: Shared spans and R-A3's second sentence.** *Rule:* records that share a verbatim span each code only their own act. The span's norm, selected by R-N3, is coded **identically on all of them** and cross-listed in `norm.shared_with`. R-A3's second sentence is mandatory whenever R-A3 applies, positively or negatively. The norm comes from the span's stated content. Where the only normative words are the performative itself ("X is adopted", "adopts R-81 through R-85", "ADR-T14 is not superseded"), it is STATUS_CONSTRAINT with `norm.basis = PERFORMATIVE_ONLY`. An authorizing performative is AUTHORIZED, with its governed operation. Every performative-token norm is tagged `performative_token = true`. When a span holds several norm clauses, one is chosen by class (operator > verdict > status predicate > performative only), then by span order. The rest are kept in `norm.other_clauses`. *Why:* coding the norm identically on all sharing records removes the choice that produced the R06/R07 disagreement. `shared_with` lets an analysis count it once or per record. r0's counting clause prescribed analysis, not coding, and is removed (review minimality item 6). Applying the second sentence uniformly follows r2 as written; the presence disagreement was a coder deviation. Tagging performative-token norms keeps r2's rule while making visible that such norms are definitionally paired with an act. That pairing would otherwise build the H-D view "norms change through acts" into the counts, so it is left as TC-4. This now includes bare authorizations (review N3). A tie rule is needed because r0 sent spans like "MAY BE PLANNED AND MUST NOT BE STARTED" to UNKNOWN, which contradicts R-A4: the span plainly decides. *Rejected:*
- Placing the norm on the record whose act it grounds. "Grounds" is often undecidable.
- Placing it on the lowest `record_id`. This is arbitrary and hides the norm from the other record.
- Coding NONE for performative-only rows. This contradicts r2's second sentence, and the choice remains available at analysis via `norm.basis` and `performative_token`.
- Making the norm component a list. This is a structural change beyond the six gaps. `other_clauses` is an auxiliary field: the primary component stays single-valued, and only the unchosen clauses are recorded.
- A substantive tie rule (e.g. "the most restrictive clause wins"). That is a theory of norm precedence. Span order is neutral, and the other clauses are kept.

## r1 repairs not covered above

- **D3 circularity (C1).** Self-performance is now defined by textual form alone. `SELF_PERFORMED` vs `SELF_PERFORMED_OUTSIDE_RA3` is then the R-A3 scope test applied to it. R-A3 (c) refers to self-performance, not to a basis value, so the definition no longer refers to itself. *Rejected:* keeping "SELF_PERFORMED = R-A3 (a)–(c) hold", which is circular.
- **Reproduced rulings (N6).** A register row that reproduces its own ruling verbatim, or names its issuer, is the instrument of that ruling, so its text is a self-performance. Naming the issuer is recorded in `act.attributed_to`, and the reported reading is computable (TC-1d). Only a named other ruling or instrument as the source makes the act REPORTED. *Why:* register rows always carry an issuer, so "an actor is named" cannot distinguish reports from rulings. "Another instrument is named" can. *Rejected:* treating every attribution as REPORTED. That would make most rulings reports and is a theory choice; it is kept as 1d.
- **R-A1 vs R-A2 (C4).** R-A1 now says "proposal or attempt", matching R-A2, which is the specific rule for refusals.
- **Section use (M2).** Only the two restrictions G1 and G5 need are kept: no performative or disposition from the section for R-A3, and no operator or status predicate from the section for the G5 boundary. Every other section use reverts to r2.
- **Not repaired here** (outside the TASK's list): D1's dependence on dataset heading format; TC-2 2b's missing join key; and an overlap that r1 noticed between D6 FAILED ("refused: …" by a mechanism) and rank-1 refusal. See CHANGELOG-r1.md.
