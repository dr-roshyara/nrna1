# RATIONALE (r3): one paragraph per gap

**G1: R-A3 scope.** *Rule:* R-A3 applies only when three things hold: the section is a register row (`## R-<n>`), the span itself contains a performative of one of the five listed lemmas (adopt, authorize, register, supersede, annotate), and that act is self-performed. It is never extended by analogy. Any other self-performance (classify, recommend, apply, approve, retire, matrix verdicts) is coded `act.state = NONE` with `act.basis = SELF_PERFORMED_OUTSIDE_RA3` and the operation recorded. Every act also carries `act.basis` (REPORTED / SELF_PERFORMED / …). *Why:* R-A3 is the only r2 licence for treating text as its own act, and it was already flagged as building H-D's view into the observations. The schema should therefore apply it no further than its words. A closed lemma list decides each case: coders cannot disagree about what counts as "like adopting". This also matches the spirit of R-A1, under which acts are observed, not constructed from a text's normative character. The basis tag puts the convention in view, so an analysis can drop it or extend it (THEORY-CHOICES TC-1) without recoding. *Rejected:*
- Extending R-A3 to classify/recommend by analogy. This widens the flagged bias, and "analogous" is not operational.
- Deleting R-A3. This would reverse an r2 rule outside the gap and is itself a theory choice; it stays available as TC-1 alternative 1b.
- Letting a section heading's "(adopted by …)" supply the act. That is the adopting row's act, and it belongs to that row's record.

**G2: Level of the governed act.** *Rule:* `about_operation` is set by literal attachment, i.e. the operation that the operator, verdict or status predicate grammatically governs. Content that the span only *mentions* (a candidate behaviour's name, a proposed rule line, quoted evidence) supplies `about_target` and never a separate norm. A classification or recommendation that assigns an outcome of a meta-operation under review is a *disposition verdict*, coded ELIGIBLE or INELIGIBLE about that meta-operation, unless an explicit operator on the same target outranks it (R-N3). *Why:* the disagreement came from coders re-levelling the norm, up to *promote* or down to the behaviour. Re-levelling in either direction is an interpretation. Literal attachment is observable and gives one answer per clause. It is not tilted toward either level: "may begin RED" or "does not begin" attach to substantive work, and "PROMOTE" or "forbids promoting" attach to the meta-act, purely as the text has it. The mention/use distinction keeps a proposal's content from being coded as a norm in force. *Rejected:*
- Always coding the substantive behaviour (REQUIRED/STATUS_CONSTRAINT). This infers a deontic value no span states and builds in H-B's coupling.
- Always coding the meta-act. This builds in H-D's framing even where the text governs work.
- Mapping a recommendation to REQUIRED or AUTHORIZED. A recommendation neither obliges nor authorizes.

The remaining question, whether adopting an instrument incorporates its mentioned behaviours as substantive norms, is left open as TC-2.

**G3: Priority, ACT_REFUSED vs ACT_OCCURRED.** *Rule:*
- A fixed first-match procedure: withdrawal/reversal > refusal > attempted > reported occurrence > R-A3 self-performance > other self-performance (NONE) > NONE.
- Same-event candidates are merged: a deciding act whose content is "not X" is coded as X, ACT_REFUSED. Distinct events go to separate records.
- Refusal needs an identified proposal (now defined) and a refusal expression that disposes of it. A bare negation counts only when the span is the decision on that proposal. Continuative negations ("has not been", "remains un-"), contrastives ("rather than X") and reasons against an unchosen option are never refusals. A refusal verb without an identified proposal is UNKNOWN. A negation without one is NONE, as R-A1 already requires.

*Why:* the refusal is the more specific description of the same event. Ranking R-A3 first would turn every negative ruling into a positive norm-making act, which is the flagged convention's bias at its most visible. Requiring an explicit disposition keeps refusals observable: non-selection of an option, or mere non-occurrence, is not visible as an act. *Rejected:*
- ACT_OCCURRED priority. This double-counts register acts and hides refusals.
- Treating every unchosen option in an option set as refused. This is an inference from non-selection, and the choice is already recorded on the chosen option's record.
- Splitting every such span into two records. This contradicts r2's "events resolving to one span are one record" for what is a single event.

**G4: PREPARED / unadopted rulings and ACT_ATTEMPTED.** *Rule:* ACT_ATTEMPTED means an actor initiated X and a **source statement** says the initiation did not by itself take effect. `attempt_kind` is PREPARED (marked prepared / not adopted / awaiting adoption) or FAILED (rejected or errored). ATTEMPTED outranks ACT_OCCURRED. These are not ATTEMPTED:
- conditional or provisoed acts (the condition goes on the NORM);
- proposals;
- planned acts.

A later adoption or hold goes in `act.later_disposition` and does not recode the act. The NORM still records the stated content (e.g. AUTHORIZED), with the PREPARED marker in `stated_by`, and does not code force. *Why:* effectiveness is decided only by the source's own marking, never by the coder's judgement of an actor's capability. Coder judgement would import H-D's "acts by actors holding the capability" into the coding. Separating content from effectiveness keeps the H-D claim that force depends on the making act's status testable rather than assumed (TC-3). *Rejected:*
- ACT_OCCURRED for prepared rulings. This ignores an explicit source statement of non-effect.
- NONE. This loses the fact that an instrument was issued.
- A new PREPARED state. This changes r2's state vocabulary when a defined ATTEMPTED plus a subtype suffices.
- Coding the norm as NONE or UNKNOWN when its act is PREPARED. This would infer a norm's absence from an act's status, the converse of R-A1.

**G5: REQUIRED vs STATUS_CONSTRAINT.** *Rule:* the value is read from the span through a closed operator lexicon. An obligation operator attached to an operation gives REQUIRED. A prohibition operator gives PROHIBITED. Without any operator, a status predicate gives STATUS_CONSTRAINT. Status predicates are a standing label, a prospective indicative ("does not begin"), or a listed performative participle. Status nouns such as "standard" or "permanent" stay STATUS_CONSTRAINT even when they imply conformity. Conditions being met or unmet are facts, not norms. Where several clauses share a target, the priority is OPERATOR > VERDICT > STATUS_PREDICATE > PERFORMATIVE_ONLY. The section may resolve referents but may not supply an operator. R-A1's "PROHIBITED / a status constraint" is resolved to STATUS_CONSTRAINT. *Why:* the presence of a deontic operator is directly observable and splits the cases cleanly. Inferring obligation from a status ("a standard binds", so REQUIRED) is a coder judgement and was the source of the disagreement. Keeping the operator in the span honours the smallest-sufficient-span unit. The boundary is lexical and does not depend on whether the governed operation is work or norm-making, so it treats both hypotheses' material alike. *Rejected:*
- A functional test ("does it in effect oblige someone?"). This is not decidable from the span.
- Defaulting "does not / standard" to PROHIBITED/REQUIRED. This upgrades descriptions into operators.
- Using section text to supply the operator. This would make the deontic value depend on how much context a coder reads.

**G6: Shared spans and R-A3's second sentence.** *Rule:* records that share a verbatim span each code only their own act. The span's norm, selected by R-N3, is coded **identically on all of them** and cross-listed in `norm.shared_with`, and it counts **once**. R-A3's second sentence is mandatory whenever R-A3 applies. The norm comes from the span's stated content. Where the only normative words are the performative itself ("X is adopted", "adopts R-81 through R-85"), it is STATUS_CONSTRAINT with `norm.basis = PERFORMATIVE_ONLY`. An authorizing performative is AUTHORIZED, with its governed operation. *Why:* coding the norm identically on all sharing records removes the choice that produced the R06/R07 disagreement, and counting it once prevents inflation. Applying the second sentence uniformly follows r2 as written; the presence disagreement was a coder deviation. Tagging performative-only norms keeps r2's rule while making visible that such norms are definitionally paired with an act. That pairing would otherwise build the H-D view "norms change through acts" into the counts, so it is left as TC-4. *Rejected:*
- Placing the norm on the record whose act it grounds. "Grounds" is often undecidable.
- Placing it on the lowest `record_id`. This is arbitrary and hides the norm from the other record.
- Coding NONE for performative-only rows. This contradicts r2's second sentence, and the choice remains available at analysis via `norm.basis`.
- Making the norm component a list. This is a structural change beyond the six gaps.
