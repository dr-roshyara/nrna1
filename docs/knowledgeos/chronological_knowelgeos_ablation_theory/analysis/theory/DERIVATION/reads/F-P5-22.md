- **[INT] What this batch contributes to the theory** (interpretation, ≤ 8):
  1. It moves the programme from "what is known" to "what is enough to act". Decision sufficiency, materiality and VoI make an unresolved distinction matter only relative to a declared inquiry and decision (F2642). Epistemic Containment then makes "resolution or demonstrable containment" (F2643 L3788–L3798) the candidate condition for acting under uncertainty.
  2. Zero keeps its role as a boundary-exposer. Every new evaluative layer (materiality, risk, governance) is attached as a derived projection *after* Zero ("Zero does not decide", F2643 L1922), not inside it.
  3. Each file repeats the same Kernel-reduction pattern for a new vocabulary (attention, risk, challenge, versioning, norms, normative closure). The conclusion is always that the inherited $\mathfrak K_{\min}=(ID,\mathcal R^\star,\mathsf{Sem})$ (reported from [REF-OUT], not originated here) survives, with new structure placed in L3/L4/L5 capabilities, regimes and contracts. The arguments are representability sketches, not proofs, and the PASS verdicts are self-declared.
  4. It adds an adversarial direction (F2644): the constructive and adversarial directions "meet at Assurance". The Support + Defeat + Stress triad and DeterminationReadiness [PROP] become candidate requirements for consequential determinations.
  5. It adds temporal decision integrity (F2645): replay versus retrospective reassessment, availability at decision time, non-erasure of revised decisions, and temporal leakage, which extends bitemporality (attributed to Step 419) to decisions and ML training.
  6. It opens a normative/governance strand (F2646–F2647) and draws its central boundary: analytical derivation and authoritative closure are distinct ($Cl^{ana}_N$ vs $Cl^{auth}_N$; $N\vdash n\not\Rightarrow Authorized(n)$). KnowledgeOS is positioned as "Epistemic Decision Support", never as the governance authority. Governance admissibility precedes decision optimization, and hard norms must not become MCDA weights.
  7. From F2645 on, the theory is driven by a concrete user case (Nexus / Cloud First). Many principles are formulated against it, so their generality is asserted, not shown. The user's own inputs are absent from the batch.
  8. Gate B (universal $Sat_\Gamma(K,r)$) stays HARD STOP throughout. Each step explicitly refuses to let RiskAcceptable, challenge-passed or $Sat^{gov}$ stand in for Sat, so the central open problem of the inherited kernel theory is unchanged by this batch.
