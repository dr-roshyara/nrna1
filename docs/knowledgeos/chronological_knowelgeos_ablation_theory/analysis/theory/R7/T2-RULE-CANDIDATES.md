**Observed vs unobserved coordinates (T-A rows).** O sees only record identities, recording times r and texts c (Def 1.11), and even these only partially (Obs O1: A07, F13, G01). By **Lem 1.12**, O does not depend on σ, f, Ref, con, d, Cap, k, or on 𝒜, E, W and their attributes, and every function of O inherits this. **A model invariant over any of these unobserved coordinates cannot be enforced or checked from records.** Column "coordinates / checkable" states this per row.

**Tension in the T-C criterion, and how it is resolved (for review).** The task's T-C criterion includes "any rule needing a concept that T1 marked X-n or open". Read literally, "open" would capture F1, F2, obj typing and the violation definition, because they read C6, C8, C9, C11, C12, C18, C20, which T1 marks "candidate with open question". The task's must-cover list places those rows in T-A. Resolution adopted here: T1's "open" on a C-row concerns **observability / identifiability**, not existence in the model. Such rows stay T-A, and the open question is carried in the "coordinates / checkable" column. "Open" in the T-C criterion is read as **a concept whose admissibility to the model is itself open or excluded**: X-rows (including X3, X12, which also carry OQ3, OQ4) and concepts R4 lacks (OQ10, roles). **This reading is an analyst interpretation [I] and is flagged for the reviewer.**

**[r1] The tier follows T1's verdict column.** Where a rule needs an X-concept, its tier is fixed by T1's *status* (verdict) for that X-row, not by the X-label alone:
- T1 verdict "NOT admissible" (as invariant, derivation or observation) → the rule is **T-C**;
- T1 verdict "admissible only as optional policy" (**X7, X8, X9, X23**) → the rule is **T-B** (R25–R28, R32, R33, R39), never T-C. The same X-concept used as an assertion or observation stays T-C (e.g. R45 for X23);
- T1 X3, X12 (NOT admissible, with an open question) → the rule is T-C; the question of need is T-D.

**[r1.2] X25 / R41 ground (analyst decision, disclosed here and in T1 r1.4; replaces r1.1's quantifier-strength ground).** Universality as such is **not** the criterion: R32 and R33 (universal B-conf) are universal and T-B. The ground is the **H11 / A7 conflict**: universal record completeness held as an invariant would license **inferring non-occurrence from absence** (an unreferenced act or work event would then not have occurred), which FROZEN H11 (MAY_NOT) and A7 (openness) forbid, and which is T1 X18 / R49 (T-C).
- **D-rec, B-recW and B-rec are EXISTENTIAL:** *some* record references *some* act (D-rec) or *some* work event (B-recW). They license no non-occurrence inference. Each is a **T-B optional policy** (R29, R35, R36) that encodes H-D and/or H-B and needs a human act. (The r1 wording "conditional, holding only for histories in H-D/H-B" stays withdrawn: adopting R29 as a policy asserts it unconditionally of the actual system.)
- **X25 and R-h are UNIVERSAL:** *every* act and *every* work event is referenced by some record. As an invariant this is **NOT admissible** (T1 X25; R41, T-C) on the H11 / A7 ground.
- **Disclosed neutrality asymmetry:** R-h is an H-B reading (R4 Table 9.4: B-rec read universally), so excluding it removes one H-B option with no matching H-D loss; the asymmetry is justified by the H11 / A7 conflict, not by a preference between H-D and H-B.
- **Human alternative (not adopted):** a T-B optional-policy form of R-h's universal clause, with the non-occurrence inference **not applied to the observed capture** (inside the model R-h entails that inference, so it cannot be "disallowed"; a policy can only forbid applying it to the incomplete record capture). Admitting it is a human decision. **[r1.5] Precision:** the A7 conflict is direct (R4 A7 admits items that no record references). The H11 conflict holds only **conditionally**: inferring non-occurrence from absence *in the corpus* also requires capture completeness, which O1, F11 and G01 deny.

**[r1.1] Proved lemmas tiered T-D (meta).** R66 (Cor 4.3) and R82 (Prop 4.1(i)/(ii), Lem 4.2), and likewise R67–R70, rest on **proved** R4 statements. They are tiered **T-D (meta)**, not T-A, because they are evidential requirements or statements about what an observation can separate, not constraints a domain model could adopt over its histories. R23 and R24 stay T-A (meta) because they state model-entailed properties that bound which T-A/T-B constraints are checkable or which records' mode is fixed. This choice leaves the counts unchanged.
