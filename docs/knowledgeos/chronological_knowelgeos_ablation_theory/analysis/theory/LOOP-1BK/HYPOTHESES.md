# Two competing hypotheses (frozen; stated neutrally)

**H-D (deontic / rule-making).** The governance record is primarily a record of **norm-making**: acts that create, adopt, hold, withdraw, supersede or annotate norms (permissions, prohibitions, requirements, eligibility, status constraints). On H-D:
- norms change **only** through such acts, by actors holding the capability to perform them;
- the **force** of a norm depends on the status of the act that made it (e.g. prepared vs adopted);
- the governed work itself is **outside what the record needs to contain**.

**H-B (broad governance and work).** The governance record is a record of a **coupled system of norms and governed work**. On H-B:
- norms change through norm-making acts **and** in response to work (execution evidence, completion, failure);
- work is expected to **conform** to the norms in force (execution begins only when permitted);
- work outcomes **feed back** into norm changes (acceptance after evidence, remediation after a failure);
- the record is expected to carry, or reference, both.

**Both** hypotheses accept that norm-making acts exist. They differ in whether governed work and its coupling to norms are part of the system the record documents.
