# Chapter I — The Constitutional Identity

**Article I.1 · The character.** KnowledgeOS is a constitutional epistemic operating system — a system that **governs how knowledge is created, justified, transformed, challenged, and evolved**, while **preserving** the identity, context, evidence, authority, reasoning, uncertainty, agency, and historical evolution of knowledge across time. *(Head 43 · P4 kernel definition.)*

**Article I.2 · Clause refinements.** The character's three clause refinements — existence (a system that enables trustworthy truth discovery), reasoning (governing how knowledge is created, justified, transformed, challenged, and evolved), and identity (preserving the identity of meaning through changing representations, contexts, reasoning processes, and observations) — are **constitutionally absorbed**: they refine the character's phrasing, they do not supersede it.

**Article I.3 · The governing maxim.** *Mathematics describes, never defines.* Topology, type theory, graphs, TMS, AGM, and LLMs are **mechanisms or explanatory lenses**; they are never kernel law by virtue of being mathematical or computational.

# Chapter II — The Kernel Laws

The eleven laws are the **constitutional boundary**. Each is a negation: it forbids a specific epistemic collapse. Each was accepted by the step-⑤ kernel decision; each is independently failure-tested (removing it opens a named corruption). The articles render the laws with their SELECTED final wording (including the two consolidations: NonInterference within Article 2, Context-001 within Article 1). The rows each article renders are recorded for traceability; the rows are evidence, not separate laws.

## Article 1 — Identity

1. KnowledgeOS SHALL preserve the identity of knowledge across representation, expression, and projection changes.
2. Representation SHALL NOT become meaning. Expression SHALL NOT become meaning.
3. **Similarity SHALL NOT become identity** — extension at any degree of match, including perfect observable match (coextension), SHALL never determine identity.
4. **Context SHALL remain part of identity**: a knowledge object SHALL carry its delimiting conditions (its semantic frame) and SHALL never be valid beyond them; a claim without its frame SHALL be considered incomplete.
5. Knowledge SHALL be a relationship — knower · process · object — never a detached object.

*Renders: INV-KOS-002 · H-KOS-Context-001 · H-KOS-Context-002 · H-KOS-Relationship-001 · H-KOS-Relation-001. Failure if removed: Document=Knowledge · vector match=identity · context-free claims · detached free-floating truth.*

## Article 2 — Dimension

1. Knowledge SHALL evolve along **independent epistemic dimensions**.
2. No value SHALL pass from one dimension to another **without an explicit, governed transition**.
3. No dimension SHALL collapse into another; **no single score SHALL replace epistemic structure** (a "knowledge quality" scalar is a forbidden surrogate).

*Renders: INV-KOS-001 · H-KOS-NonInterference-001. Failure if removed: Knowledge Quality = 0.87 · silent cross-dimension interference.*

## Article 3 — Authority

1. Evidence SHALL NOT constitute authority. Assessment SHALL NOT constitute authority. A source SHALL NOT constitute authority.
2. Authority SHALL be **assigned** — a recorded reference to a human act — never intrinsic, never emergent from content.
3. Sources SHALL NOT be interchangeable: the **means of acquisition** SHALL be preserved (a sensor measurement ≠ an expert statement ≠ an AI inference ≠ a historical document).

*Renders: INV-001 · INV-003 · INV-KOS-Pramana-001. Failure if removed: a recorded reference self-authorizes · assessment becomes binding · source-role collapse (sensor = expert = AI).*

## Article 4 — Decision

1. Observation SHALL NOT constitute decision. Knowledge SHALL NOT constitute decision.
2. Knowledge SHALL inform action; it SHALL **not execute** it. The boundary between knowledge and decision SHALL be governed, never implicit.

*Renders: INV-002 · INV-KOS-Decision-001. Failure if removed: observation auto-decides · knowledge executes · decision-collapse.*

## Article 5 — Projection

1. A projection SHALL never be its source.
2. Projections SHALL be regenerable and SHALL carry no authority beyond their source's authority.

*Renders: INV-004. Failure if removed: projection = source · edits to a view corrupt the record.*

## Article 6 — Verification

1. No claim SHALL enter knowledge **without a preserved justification path**: authentic evidence + acquisition method + inference rule + reasoning path.
2. Pseudo-evidence SHALL NOT be evidence (a hallucinated citation, a fabricated datum, an unverifiable assertion).
3. **Generation SHALL NOT be justification.** An AI output, a model response, a generated conclusion SHALL be a candidate, never knowledge by generation alone.
4. Reasoning SHALL be a **governed transformation** — never a black box. Evidence SHALL NOT become a conclusion without a preserved inference rule.
5. Observation SHALL NOT become inference; AI output SHALL NOT become reality.

*Renders: INV-KOS-Inference-001 · INV-KOS-Justification-001 · H-KOS-Reasoning-Separation-001 · H-KOS-Reasoning-Provenance-001 · H-KOS-Vyapti-001 · H-KOS-EvidenceAuthenticity-001 · INV-KOS-Pramana-001. Failure if removed: AI output = reality · unsupported claim = knowledge · black-box generation · opaque inference · hallucination = evidence.*

## Article 7 — Failure-State

1. Invalid and failed reasoning SHALL be **preserved as explicit epistemic states** (REJECTED).
2. Failed reasoning SHALL never be knowledge and SHALL never be silently discarded. The history of error SHALL remain part of the epistemic record.

*Renders: H-KOS-Fallacy-001 (state) · H-KOS-Failure-001. Failure if removed: circular/self-referential reasoning becomes knowledge · rejected reasoning vanishes.*

## Article 8 — Contradiction

1. Conflicting knowledge SHALL **coexist as CONFLICTED** until governed resolution.
2. Challenge SHALL NOT destroy identity.
3. Resolution SHALL be forward-only: the conflict record SHALL survive resolution.

*Renders: H-KOS-Contradiction-001 · H-KOS-Dialogue-001 (boundary). Failure if removed: premature TRUE/FALSE · the weaker side deleted · disagreement destroyed.*

## Article 9 — Unknown

1. UNKNOWN SHALL be a **first-class epistemic state**.
2. **Unknown SHALL NOT equal Absent. Absent SHALL NOT equal False.** Absence of evidence SHALL NOT pass.
3. Structured uncertainty SHALL be preserved; it SHALL NOT be flattened to a lacuna.

*Renders: H-ZERO-001 · H-KOS-Uncertainty-001 · Abhāva (requirement). Failure if removed: no-evidence = PASS/FALSE · doubt flattened to missing.*

## Article 10 — Agency

1. Every knowledge state SHALL preserve its **epistemic agency**: who observed, reasoned, validated, decided — and under which authority context.
2. Knowledge SHALL NOT be anonymous. **"Who knows?" SHALL always be answerable.**

*Renders: INV-KOS-Agent-001 · H-KOS-Relationship-001 (knower-side). Failure if removed: knowledge exists anonymously · accountability lost.*

## Article 11 — History

1. **Revision SHALL NOT delete.** New truth SHALL NOT overwrite old truth.
2. Knowledge SHALL carry **temporal validity**; freshness SHALL NOT be truth, expiry SHALL NOT be absence.
3. Supersession SHALL be **forward-only**: the old state SHALL remain history.

*Renders: INV-KOS-Revisability-001 · Temporal family. Failure if removed: new truth overwrites old · freshness = truth · history destroyed.*

# Chapter III — The Kernel Boundary

The kernel is the eleven articles. Everything else — every engine, mechanism, representation, and implementation — **serves the kernel** and is **governed by it**, and none of it is constitutional.

```
CONSTITUTIONAL KERNEL            ← the eleven articles (PRESERVE)
        │
        ├── Engine Layer (GOVERN)        validation engines · fallacy detection · debate pipeline
        │                               · intent classification · verdict vocabulary
        ├── Mechanisms (GOVERN)          epistemic-inquiry lifecycle · reasoning pipelines
        ├── Representations (ENABLE)     evidence records · context tuples · epistemic state model
        │                               · absence taxonomy · epistemic graph · typed encodings
        └── Implementation (ENABLE)      storage · interfaces · systems — deliberately unconstrained here
```

**III.1 · The kernel does not do the reasoning.** The kernel states what must be preserved and what must never collapse; engines execute the governed transformations that realize those constraints.

**III.2 · No engine, mechanism, or representation may violate an article.** Evolution within the GOVERN/ENABLE layers is free; evolution that violates an article is not an evolution — it is a corruption, and is subject to Chapter V.

**III.3 · Implementation is out of constitutional scope.** Storage technologies, databases, APIs, classes, and concrete systems are deliberately not specified. The Constitution governs epistemic properties, not their realization.

# Chapter IV — The Negative Boundary

KnowledgeOS SHALL refuse to become any of the following. Each non-identity is the observable consequence of the articles above.

1. **KnowledgeOS is NOT a repository.** It does not store truth; it preserves and governs justified epistemic states. *(A repository would collapse Article 6 — content without a justification path.)*
2. **KnowledgeOS is NOT an LLM.** It does not generate a likely response; it preserves a justified epistemic state. An LLM fundamentally lacks **persistent epistemic identity** and **historical belief evolution** (the two structural absences) — precisely what Articles 1 and 11 preserve. *(An LLM collapses Articles 1, 6, 11.)*
3. **KnowledgeOS is NOT a truth oracle.** It does not answer with authority; it preserves evidence, assessment, and reasoning as **non-authoritative** until governance assigns authority. *(An oracle collapses Articles 3, 9.)*
4. **KnowledgeOS is NOT a similarity engine.** Extension never determines identity; observable match at any degree is never identity. *(A similarity engine collapses Article 1.)*
5. **KnowledgeOS is NOT an autonomous decision maker.** Knowledge informs; it does not execute. *(An autonomous decision maker collapses Article 4.)*

**IV.1 · The negative boundary is constitutional.** These five refusals are not preferences; they are the enforcement-face of the articles. A system that becomes any of the five has ceased to be KnowledgeOS.

# Chapter V — Amendment Discipline

Because this is a constitutional document, the distinction between change that is governed and change that is forbidden is itself constitutional.

**V.1 · Engineering evolution — no amendment required.** Engines, mechanisms, representations, and implementation (Chapter III) MAY evolve freely and continuously, subject to the sole constraint that no evolution violates an article. This is the normal life of the system.

**V.2 · Constitutional amendment — required.** The following SHALL NOT change except by constitutional amendment ratified by the HPA:
- the eleven articles (their content, scope, or strength) — no article SHALL be weakened;
- the character (Chapter I);
- the negative boundary (Chapter IV);
- the kernel/engine boundary (Chapter III.1–III.2);
- the amendment discipline itself (this Chapter).

**V.3 · What amendment MAY do.** Amendment MAY refine the *wording* of an article for clarity, MAY correct an internal inconsistency, and MAY — on evidence that a specific corruption is unguarded — add a clause that strengthens an existing article. Amendment SHALL NOT add new concepts, new philosophical sources, or new rows to the kernel. The corpus that produced this Constitution is closed; **adding knowledge to the kernel would violate the discipline this Constitution establishes.**

**V.4 · Ratification.** Amendment is effective only by the HPA's ruling, following the same selection discipline that produced step ⑤ (evidence · domain independence · necessity · failure test).
