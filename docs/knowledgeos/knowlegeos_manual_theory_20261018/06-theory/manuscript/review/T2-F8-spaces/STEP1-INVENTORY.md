Notation: L = line number in the source. [I] = my own judgment. Ranking is by theoretical weight, with weight for F8 breaking ties.

Cross-source point that frames F8 [I]: none of the five sources asks "nested or adjacent?" directly. "Knowledge space" means at least four different things across them:
(a) a family of admissible states over a finite domain Q, the KST sense (S0651);
(b) an unbounded totality larger than any knower (S2221, and S0577 for Phase 2);
(c) a context or world that partitions claims (S0577 "Cognitive Worlds");
(d) an open-ended space of dimensions for inquiry (S2838).
The answer to F8 differs by sense. In sense (a), states are nested by ⊆ and domains can be restricted. In (b), local views sit inside one whole. In (c), worlds are adjacent partitions. In (d), the space is neither, because inquiry can go beyond the current representation.

S0577: KNOWLEDGE-CONCEPT-EXTRACTION-001
=====================================================================
1. "Knowledge Space" names two different concepts under one term. Phase 1 (`0200`) used it as a diagram label for Cognitive Worlds: provenance-homogeneous claim bundles that explain how contradictions can coexist through context separation. Phase 2 uses it for an unbounded, projected, participant-relative, time-indexed space. The source summarises this as "same term, different concept". L107, L144-152, L219-221.
2. Cognitive Worlds (K-5) partition the space into provenance-homogeneous bundles, so contradiction is treated as context separation, not corruption. This is the only explicit partition (adjacent) model of a knowledge space in the five sources. It was considered and then abandoned. L107, L147-149, L316. [I] It is the main corpus evidence for "adjacent".
3. The bound of the Knowledge Space is contested: "unbounded" (`135642`, `180730`) against "bounded-by-purpose" (`103542`). L228. [I] Bounded-by-purpose spaces imply a plurality of purpose-relative spaces, which bears directly on F8.
4. Knowledge's membership in the space is inconsistent: knowledge is a measure over it (`181038`) or a relation directed at it (`171008`). L227. Projection has three incompatible senses (CQRS read model, epistemic projection, measure over (Ω,F)), and the source says not to merge them. L240-246.
5. Knowledge Space is a hypothesis ("without turning them into formal architecture yet"), is not established as part of the Kernel, and its relation to the Kernel is unresolved. L226, L233-234, L350. It is absent from v1.1. L304.
6. Phase 2's distinctive move is relativisation: knowledge relative to a space, a participant and a time. The relatum moved from an internal list to an external space. L137-139, L358.
7. K-6: knowledge is a factive, participant-relative, time-dependent relation or state directed at a Knowledge Space. Factivity brings truth back in and conflicts with a system that has no truth access; this is unreconciled. L108, L207-209.
8. The most stable result is negative: knowledge is not stored content. This holds in both phases. L103, L141-142, L354.
9. K-2: knowledge is a justified or governed relationship. It is Phase 1 original thinking, not an import. Its set of relata is unresolved: no two documents list it the same way. L37-40, L104, L356.
10. K-11 (knowledge is primitive, Williamson) denies the whole definitional programme and is unreconciled with K-2 to K-9. L113-116, L361.
11. K-4 (pipeline), K-7 (capacity) and K-9 (measure) cannot support an identity-bearing aggregate. If any of them is true, the core domain has no object. L259-266, L303, L359.
12. Zero-lens result: four things survive across both phases: a knower, something known, a ground, and a record of their connection. L272-274.
13. Absence is distinguished from ignorance ("nothing exists" ≠ "we do not know"). Whether UNKNOWN is a state of an identified object is unresolved; `125708` argues for eight readings. L276-284.
14. Distinctions held: knowledge ≠ truth, ≠ belief, ≠ assertion. This is the strongest convergence between the corpus and the law. L201-213.
15. Time: "knowledge-at-a-time" appears only in Phase 2. Continuous change (K-8) conflicts with governed, forward-only transitions. "Does change create a new identity?" is never asked. L183-195.
16. Identity has four competing accounts: assigned, primitive, contextual/transformational (book-derived), relational (Phase 1). L161-170.
17. Questions never found in the corpus: can two projections differ without knowledge differing? Does absence from a projection mean anything? Does history belong to the object? L248-249, L322-324.
18. Method limit: every absence is keyword-plus-reading over 250 documents and is only a candidate absence. L326-327. Provenance slip: one root file is a byte-identical duplicate of a Phase 2 file, so location does not reliably mark phase. L54-56.
19. [I] Nesting appears only through the phrase "projection of an unbounded Knowledge Space" (K-7, L90): a bounded projection inside an unbounded whole. No relation between two spaces (inclusion, overlap, disjointness) is stated anywhere.

S0357: KERNEL EIGHT CAPACITIES K1-K8 AND THE MINIMUM KERNEL
=====================================================================
1. The Kernel is an epistemic state-transition kernel, much smaller than KnowledgeOS. Its question is: given a candidate meaning, its evidence, context, history and rules, which transition is admissible? L1-7, L42-46, L473.
2. K7, constitutional admissibility, is ranked the most important capacity. It asks whether a transition is admissible, not whether a statement is true. L194-210.
3. Capacity ceiling: the Kernel should be unable to interpret. Its output is "Provider P submitted X", never "X means". The worlds are layered in a chain: semantic world → candidate world → KnowledgeOS → knowledge state. L386-434. [I] This is a sequence of layers, not nesting of spaces.
4. K1, identity: same, different, unknown, candidate, conflict. Invariant: equal representations must never automatically mean equal identity. L54-70.
5. K4, context: the same representation can mean different things in different contexts. The Kernel preserves and evaluates context but does not infer it; context comes from an external provider. L115-133. [I] This is the closest the source comes to adjacent spaces (context-indexed meaning), but it treats context as an attribute, not a space.
6. K5, contradiction: A and ¬A with their evidence must coexist without destroying each other. The vocabulary (SUPPORTED, UNKNOWN, CONFLICTED, REJECTED, SUPERSEDED) is left open. L137-167. [I] This handles contradiction inside one state through status labels, not by separating worlds. That is a different mechanism from Cognitive Worlds (S0577).
7. K8, determinism: the same admissible input and the same prior state give the same result, which makes replay and audit possible. L236-250.
8. K2: evidence ≠ authority. L74-93. K3: revision is append-only and evolutionary, not destructive. L97-109. K6: temporal lifecycle states without erasing earlier states. L171-188.
9. Exclusions from the Kernel: semantic compiler, SNF, LLMs, NL parser, workflow engine, database, UI and API. All are providers or adapters. L254-344.
10. Minimum Kernel under the Reduction Test: a KnowledgeAggregate holding identity, meaning candidate, evidence, provenance, context, history, relations and epistemic state, followed by admissibility and a deterministic transition. Anything else must earn its way in. L438-465.
11. EKS and PKS must not be declared the Kernel by name. A capability mapping is required. L346-382, L489-522.
12. Two lists disagree: the box at L23-33 has nine items (including temporal state and admissibility); the K-list has eight; the minimum list at L443-452 adds "meaning candidate" and "relations". L22-33, L52, L443-452. [I] This is a source slip.
13. [I] The source has no knowledge space and no projection at all. The Kernel is defined without a space concept. For F8, this source is evidence that the Kernel theory can stand without settling F8.

S0651: How can an acquired knowledge state be represented mathematically? (Knowledge Space Theory)
=====================================================================
1. KST definitions. Q is a domain of items. A knowledge state is K ⊆ Q. A knowledge structure (Q,𝒦) contains ∅ and Q. A knowledge space is a structure that is closed under union. L7-19, L51-61, L690-708.
2. "Infinite Knowledge Space" has six possible senses: everything knowable, a universe of propositions, a space of epistemic states, KST's space, a possible-world space, and a domain-specific state space. The source says not to equate them. L69-84. [I] This is the most important item for F8. "Domain-specific state space" implies many spaces, each relative to its own Q, so the relation between them becomes a real question.
3. Four-way distinction: Knowledge ≠ Knowledge State ≠ Knowledge Space ≠ Knowledge Extraction. Also 𝒦 ≠ K_t^A ≠ P(K_t^A | O≤t). L33-41, L551-589, L860-872. The source asks the next research step to attack this distinction (L940).
4. H-KST-1: a Knowledge Space is the space of admissible epistemic states, not the set of everything knowable. L770-785. [I] In this reading a knowledge state is a member of the space, which answers the membership question S0577 left open (L227) for one sense only.
5. Domain and state are separate: a state is a subset of the domain, giving the pattern Domain → State projection. Mapping this onto the Kernel substrate is rejected as an invention of the analysed text. L130-177, L626-640.
6. Union closure plus the ⊆ order: states are nested inside each other, and learning runs along a maximal chain K0 → K1 → K2 → ... L480-510. [I] This is nesting of states inside one space, not nesting of spaces. KST's standard trace (𝒦 restricted to Q' ⊂ Q) would give nested spaces, but the source does not mention it.
7. Representation equivalences: knowledge spaces correspond one-to-one with entailments; Galois connections; the competency model represents any structure. So one phenomenon can have several equivalent representations. This supports "regimes" but does not justify putting it in the Kernel. L257-289, L367-383, L593-618.
8. Probability is structure over states, (Q,𝒦,p). It is not the state itself: Knowledge State ≠ Probability. Extraction can be probabilistic while the state is not. L387-431, L514-545, L789-800.
9. Response function r(R,K) models careless errors and lucky guesses. It meets Pritchard's luck question but answers a different question. L435-474.
10. Base and atoms: a very large state space (2^100) can have a compact generating set. This is a property of KST, not an argument for storing a base in the Kernel. L225-253. [I] It means 𝒦 is finite here, which conflicts with "unbounded Knowledge Space" in S0577 and S2221.
11. Surmise systems are AND/OR prerequisite structures. Prerequisite, correlation, entailment, causal dependency and inference dependency must not be conflated. L181-221.
12. Open question: when does an expert assertion become a structural rule rather than an epistemic fact? Provenance and authority semantics are needed. L291-321.
13. Open question: should the Kernel keep the generating model, or only the evidence from which the state can be rebuilt? L381.
14. Rejected claims: Kernel = Q; the state is a "perfect" projection (only a candidate, and it means "can solve"); KST is a complete framework. A list of what KST leaves unsolved follows. L622-682.
15. The KST time model covers learning only, not changing truth or domain state. L506-510.
16. Layered research model: historical substrate → observations → epistemic state → (knowledge space, probability) → regime → claim → understanding. The source says this is not architecture. L880-940. [I] The diagram puts KNOWLEDGE SPACE downstream of EPISTEMIC STATE, which reverses KST's order (a state is an element of the space). This is an internal inconsistency.
17. [I] The source never asks how two domains Q1 and Q2 relate (disjoint union or product, meaning adjacent; restriction or trace, meaning nested). This is a gap for F8.

S2221: Gītā Chapter 11, hypotheses for KnowledgeOS and the Kernel
=====================================================================
1. Central claim: the Kernel is a bounded knower's mechanism that processes a changing state and cannot contain the whole knowledge space. Kernel ≠ 𝒦. The Kernel observes a projection of 𝒦 from "within a particular epistemic position". L7, L43-77. [I] This is the clearest nesting statement: a bounded perspective inside a larger whole.
2. Different representations may be different projections of one larger whole, R1 = π1(𝒦) and R2 = π2(𝒦), not contradictions. Open question: when two states differ, are they contradictory, or different projections of a larger state? L526-574, H11-8 L873-876. [I] This is a third model for F8: overlapping views of one whole, neither strictly nested nor adjacent.
3. Observational equivalence: K1 ≠ K2 but πX(K1) = πX(K2), written K1 ≈X K2. Two observers may see different aspects of a larger whole. The source says this gives intuition, not proof. L578-606.
4. Bounded Context ≠ Whole Reality. A DDD bounded context is a local projection of a larger reality, and the Kernel must not confuse its represented world with the world itself. L699-729, H11-10 L885-890. [I] This is nesting (local inside whole) applied to DDD contexts. It does not say whether two bounded contexts are adjacent.
5. Scale question: does the Kernel operate at one scale or across several (Observation → Entity → Relation → State → System → Knowledge Space)? Labelled a research question, not a hierarchy. L654-695. [I] If adopted, this would be a nested chain of scales.
6. Unknown ≠ Non-existent. x ∉ O_t does not imply x ∉ 𝒦. The danger to block is "not observed → false". L172-230.
7. Possible third condition, "epistemically inaccessible": consistent but unprocessable, distinct from invalid and from unknown. Not claimed as a primitive. L401-437.
8. Research pipeline 𝒦 →Ω→ O_t →B→ D_t →δ→ K_{t+1}, with Buddhi (discrimination) as the control point. Not canonical. L488-522, L741-778, L905-933.
9. Observation ≠ Understanding ≠ Discrimination. More observation ≠ complete knowledge. Greater observability can expose more unknowns. L451-467, L612-648, H11-2, H11-5, H11-7.
10. Time: K_t ≠ K_{t+1}. Transitions may be irreversible (→ against ↔), so history matters. Event ≠ State. L234-397.
11. Kernel continuity ≠ state identity. The research question is what persists through a change of state. L268-302, H11-9.
12. Discipline: the Gītā corroborates and does not derive. The chain runs observation → interpretation → hypothesis → independent derivation → falsification → architecture. A list of things it does not establish follows (Ω = Sañjaya, 𝒦 = the universal form, and others). L79-89, L782-815.
13. Symbol type slip: 𝒦 is used as "Knowledge Space" (L48), as a superset of observations (O_t ⊆ 𝒦, L159), as something whose elements are items x (L196), and as a "larger state" (L572). In KST (S0651) 𝒦 is a family of states, so O_t ⊆ 𝒦 and x ∈ 𝒦 cannot both hold. L48, L159, L196, L553-572. [I] Source defect. It matters for F8 because "nested in 𝒦" changes meaning with the type.
14. Inconsistent formulas: L76 O_t = Ω(K_t, 𝒦, context); L132 K_t = f(𝒦, O_t, C_t); L145 Ω_C(𝒦) → O_t. Dependency runs both ways between O and K. [I] This is circular unless time-indexing is made explicit.
15. Relies on earlier steps whose content was not read here: Steps 285 and 287, the "ratified eight primitives" including Event and State, the five-axis Σ. L383, L564, L935. [I] Indirect evidence. It also conflicts with S0357's eight capacities, which are a different "eight".

S2838: Observation as an entry point into an unbounded dimensional space
=====================================================================
1. An observation is an entry point, not a point. D_t ⊆ D* (the discoverable dimension space), and KnowledgeOS cannot assume all relevant dimensions are already represented. L15-67.
2. Correction to the Principal's claim: do not say the dimensions are literally infinite. The dimension space is "open-ended relative to the current representation". L560-602. [I] This conflicts with "unbounded/infinite Knowledge Space" in S0577 and S2221, and agrees with S0651's six-sense warning.
3. Zoom-in(K_t, O, Q) → FocusedInquirySpace. It does not delete K_t (ZF1). A relevant dimension need not have been present in the initial representation (ZF3). L71-124, L747-765. [I] Important for F8: the inquiry space is neither a subspace of K_t (it reaches beyond it) nor adjacent to it (it is anchored in K_t). This counterexample defeats a clean nested/adjacent dichotomy.
4. Zoom-out = Integrate(Finding, F_t, K_t), not restoration. Earlier zoom-out experiments failed because they did not separate restoration from integration. L332-376, ZF6 L781-783.
5. Dimensions are not monotone: D_t ⊆ D_{t+1} is possible but not necessary, because dimensions can become irrelevant or superseded. L580-602. The notation at L589, "D_{t+1} ⊉ D_t necessarily", is ambiguous. It is meant as "not necessarily ⊇". [I] Source defect (notation).
6. Projection caution: do not call a Zoom-in a projection without π² = π or a defined semantic projection. Use "inquiry-relative view transformation". Discover the algebra rather than impose it. L695-727. [I] This conflicts with S2221's free use of π_i(𝒦) and S0577's "projection".
7. Graph traversal ≠ epistemic investigation. A knowledge graph is the representational substrate; Zoom-in explores over "that substrate and its discoverable extensions". L268-327.
8. Separation of roles: Zoom-in ≠ fact finding (Zoom-in → fact finding), Zoom-in ≠ determination (ZF5). The four roles answer four questions: where to look, what to do next (epistemic agency), what the evidence establishes, how the finding changes the state. L158-202, L506-556, L904-946.
9. Dimension selection policy d* = argmax E[adequacy gain | d] / Cost(d). It sits inside Zoom-in and does not define it. L465-502.
10. ZoomIn discovers and Zero tests (eliminability), both relative to (Q,C,Π,S). Marked [PROP]; the order is not fixed. L606-691.
11. "No Determination Without Alternative Space": the system must keep competing hypotheses (backup, runner, replication) before determining. L857-898.
12. Benchmark design: Nexus egress of 70GB/day, comparing restricted zoom (children only) against inquiry zoom. L795-853. [I] The whole model rests on one worked example.
13. Status: [PROP][OPEN]. Zoom-in is not a kernel operator. Open question: is it irreducible, or a composition of inquiry formation, dimension discovery, candidate generation, resource selection and traversal? L950-954.
14. [I] (Q,C,Π,S), Δ, ℛ and "Theory v1.2" are used without definition here, so they depend on other documents.

CONTRADICTIONS AND TENSIONS ACROSS SOURCES [I]

- Bound of the space: unbounded (S0577 L228; S2221 L7) against bounded-by-purpose (S0577 L228), against finite 𝒦 ⊆ 2^Q (S0651 L57, L229-235), against open-ended but not literally infinite (S2838 L568-575).
- Type of 𝒦: a family of states (S0651) against a container of items and observations (S2221 L159, L196).

- How contradiction coexists: worlds or partitions (S0577 Cognitive Worlds) against status labels in one state (S0357 K5) against different projections of one whole (S2221 L572).
- Use of "projection": used freely (S2221, S0577) against restricted to π² = π (S2838 L707-725).
- "Eight": the eight Kernel capacities (S0357) are not the "ratified eight primitives" (S2221 L383).

=====================================================================
STRUCTURAL OBSERVATIONS ON F8 [I]
=====================================================================
These are mine. They are not stated in any source.
- The sources contain material for at least three relations, not two:
  - nested: a bounded perspective or projection inside a larger whole (S2221; S0577 K-7);
  - adjacent: a partition into worlds or contexts (S0577 Cognitive Worlds; S0357 K4; S0651 "domain-specific state space");
  - overlapping views of one whole, or observationally equivalent states (S2221 L552-602).
- Zoom-in (S2838 ZF3) is a fourth case: anchored in K_t but extending beyond it.
- F8 is ill-posed until "knowledge space" is given one sense.
- In KST, nesting of states (⊆ inside one 𝒦) and nesting of spaces (restriction to a subdomain) are different things. The first is in the sources; the second is not.
