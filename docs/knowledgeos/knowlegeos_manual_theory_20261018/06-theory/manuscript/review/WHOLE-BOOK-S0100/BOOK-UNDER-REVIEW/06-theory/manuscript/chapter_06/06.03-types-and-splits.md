# 6.3 A context type and its instances; a boundary through a concept

## 6.3.1 In plain language

Two results from S0034 (the Engineering Knowledge Domain Model, 2026-08-02, a generated candidate) clarify where boundaries lie.
- **A product's knowledge space is a type, not a context.** Each product has its own, so what the platform owns is the *shape*; each product owns its *instance*.
- **"Evidence" is not one thing.** The rules for collecting it belong to the platform; the records collected belong to the product that produced them.

Both are cases where a concept looked like one boundary and turned out to be two.

## 6.3.2 Definitions

- **Context type (Src).** "PKS is a context TYPE, not a context" [S0034 L266]. The type is platform knowledge; each instance (PublicDigit's, a hospital's) is a product-side bounded context [S0034 L123–131]. `[CON-0086 · invalid]` (partly valid: the source's own later test weakens it to "an observation of one instance"; C3)
- **Boundary through a concept (Src).** "The boundary runs THROUGH the concept, not around it." [S0034 L111]. "Evidence" splits into an **evidence protocol** (the harvest question, the promotion ladder, the observation protocol: platform) and **evidence records** (reports, logs, rows: product) [S0034 L109–121]. "Split ownership is a boundary, not an ambiguity." [S0034 L118] `[CON-0087 · validated]` (as a distinction. *Limit:* canon's owner field assigns only the **records** to the producing product; that the protocol belongs to the platform is the source's own step.)

## 6.3.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. The type/instance split makes an open question well posed (Ded).**
- S0022 found that "one PKS per product" fails for configurable products: is a product deployed to fifty customers one knowledge space or fifty (Ch. 3 §3.7; CON-0043, CON-0042)?
- With the type/instance split, the question becomes precise: **what indexes the instances**, product or deployment? The type stays one platform concept either way.
- So the split does not answer the deployment question, but it moves it to the right place: the choice of **instance key**. That is a modelling decision, not an ontological puzzle.
- *Counterexample attempt for CON-0086, and its limit:* could a knowledge space be shared by two products, which would make an instance span products? The source's rule "one per product, never reusable" forbids it. But that answer cites the source's own rule, so the attempt **cannot fail**; it is not a test. The question stays open.
- **Nesting complicates the key.** By S0012, a deployment can itself be a knowledge space (Ch. 1 §1.11, RC-26; Ch. 3 §3.3), although a later source calls nesting still unevidenced (TODO T1-43). Instances may then contain instances, and the instance key may need to be hierarchical.

**C2. The evidence split explains where the platform's evidence lives (Ded; connects §6.1 C6).**
- By the split, the **rules** for evidence are the platform's, and the **records** are the product's.
- So the evidence that a platform method works lies, by design, in product records. The platform can only read it across the boundary. This is §6.1 C6's derived consequence, now stated by the corpus itself.
- The corpus's three or more homes for evidence (§6.1 C6) are then a placement failure for **records**. The protocol is correctly placed (ES-006, platform-side) [S0034 L230].
- *Counterexample attempt for CON-0087:* is there evidence that is neither protocol nor record, for example a pattern card (EPC)? A pattern is **derived** from records (Ch. 4 §4.1 C2), so it is a third kind: an inference over records, owned where the pattern is reusable. The split holds for protocol and records. Derived patterns need their own placement rule (open).

**C3. The source's own count of contexts did not survive its later test (Src).**
- The body counts "FOUR platform-side bounded contexts. Not eight." [S0034 L196].
- Its later annotations (2026-08-02) weaken two of them: "Method" from strong to medium, "Runtime" from strong to weak [S0034 L20–33]. They also weaken the **knowledge-space type** (D-7) to weak, "inferred from n=1", and require it to be restated as "an observation of one instance" [S0034 L33]. That bears directly on CON-0086.
- **The source's own ranking is not consistent.** The missing domain "scores 6 of 9 criteria, tying D-1 as the most strongly evidenced domain" [S0034 L16]; D-1 is credited with five criteria in its own section; and the method domain is called the "most strongly evidenced". The Strong/Medium/Weak scale used for all of these is never defined. They also add a missing domain, the knowledge platform, owned by a named individual [S0034 L12–18].
- **Int:** the count is not stable, and the admission rule has no sufficiency condition (§6.2 C1). The instability is what that missing condition predicts.

**C3a. What the source's domain analysis proposes (Src: proposals of a CANDIDATE document, partly weakened by its own later test).**
- **It proposes that method is a bounded context distinct from governance:** "Two constitutions · two ADR series · two baselines · two vocabularies." [S0034 L75]. The later test weakened this to medium, on a better ground: the method's own scope exclusion of governance discoveries [S0034 L27].
- **It proposes rejecting "workflow" as a domain:** it has no owner, no language and no consistency of its own. Its parts are responsibilities of governance and method under a third name [S0034 L88–97].
- **It classifies "capability" as a knowledge kind, not a context:** an earlier ruling (H-CAT-1) refused the parent abstraction as "not yet evidenced", and only the ARB may overturn it [S0034 L99–107].
- **Status:** none of the three is adopted; each is the source's classification, made with the admission test of §6.2. **Int:** it applies that test more strictly than its written form, the same pattern as in §6.2 C1.

**C4. Classification follows the model, not the files (Src).** "Classification follows the domain model." [S0034 L39]. An index keyed to today's file arrangement would freeze that arrangement. The source shows the effect: asking **who owns** the knowledge found product records misplaced inside the platform tree, which no vocabulary search could find [S0034 L234–238]. This is the meaning-first counterpart of "domain-free ≠ portable" (Ch. 8 §8.1).

**C5. Two reuse tests that are not the same (Ded; S0035).**
- S0035 states that the commission's litmus and the repository's placement test "are the same test" [S0035 L100]:
  - (A) "Would this responsibility still exist if PublicDigit disappeared and another product were created tomorrow?" [S0035 L10];
  - (B) "Could a different project adopt the document unchanged?" [S0035 L76].
- **They are not equivalent.** (A) asks whether a **responsibility** exists independently of the product. (B) asks whether an **artifact** can be used unchanged.
- A method with a product binding (§6.1) passes (A), since the responsibility exists in any product, and fails (B), since its text names the binding. So (B) ⇒ (A), but not the converse (given a mapping from documents to the responsibilities they carry).
- **What separates the two tests is more than the binding.** ES-005.3 sends a document to the project if it "Needs project context or evidence" [S0035 L76]. Under the lattice of §6.1 C3, (B) fails if the artifact is **bound or diluted with case law**. A third separator is ES-005.3's research clause, which places research on the project side whatever its content (S0031's OQ-K1). So the gap is the binding, the case law and the research clause.
- **The tests differ in kind as well:** (A) is **counterfactual** ("if PublicDigit disappeared") and cannot be run; (B) is **operational** and can be. That is a reason the corpus uses (B), and a reason it cannot stand in for (A).
- **Consequence:** (A) identifies **what** belongs to the platform. (B) identifies **which texts are already ready**. Using (B) as the boundary test would put every bound method on the product side, even though its method belongs to the platform. That is the misplacement S0028 and S0034 describe.
- `[CON-0088 · invalid]` (partly valid: non-equivalence holds; the gap was first stated as the binding alone; review C6)

**C6. Core and supporting are relative to a product (Ded; S0035).**
- Canon: "The Election System is the Core Domain; this platform is a Supporting Subdomain" [S0035 L30]. The same reference model also records that, were KnowledgeOS the product, engineering governance "would be its Core Domain — the inversion means platform rules (e.g. AIP-14) do not transfer unchanged" [S0035 L72].
- **Ded:** the same knowledge is supporting with respect to one product and core with respect to another. So "core" and "supporting" are a **relation** between a body of knowledge and a product, not properties of the knowledge.
- Rules attached to the classification (AIP-14's Product Primacy) are therefore indexed by the product they serve, and "do not transfer unchanged" when the product changes.
- *Limit:* the support is canon's own **conditional** ("would be"), so the inversion is counterfactual. The reference product is a different index from Ch. 0 §0.2's time horizon; the two must not be identified, and both may be needed.
- `[CON-0089 · validated]`

**C7. What a knowledge space is: five readings on one day (Src, conflicting).**
1. **A domain containing contexts:** "A domain, registered as such (O-19), containing bounded contexts — not itself a bounded context." [S0035 L144]. S0035 cites an earlier ruling (AD-1) for this, so it has more standing than the others.
2. **A context type** whose instances are bounded contexts (S0034's body; C1 above).
3. **A generated product artifact, one instance per product** (S0033), although S0033 itself concedes that generation is n=0.
4. **One space, authored, not generated** (S0032's hierarchy).
5. **A projection,** which S0034's later test calls falsified for the existing artifacts, since they carry an ARB endorsement [S0034 L31].
- **The readings differ in kind:** a domain **contains** contexts; a type **is instantiated by** them; an artifact is neither. Under the first, one product's space holds several contexts; under the second, each product's space **is** one context. **Open** (this bears on CON-0086 and CON-0042). The readings also differ in standing, from a cited ruling to a weakened candidate, and that standing should decide which is taken as the corpus's position.


## 6.3.4 Open questions

- What is the instance key of a knowledge space: product or deployment (C1)? And is a knowledge space a domain that contains contexts, or a context type (C7)?
- Where do derived patterns live (C2)?
- Are governance and method peers or nested (Ch. 1 §1.11; T1-50)?
