## 6.7.1 In plain language

The architecture diagrams of 2026-08-18 call themselves a proposal. Their three C4 views draw the KnowledgeOS / AI Engineering Platform as one box and draw no line between a kernel and its applications; they also disagree about what lies inside the box. The same capability, recording grants and handoffs, sits in different boxes in different views. The human authorizer's arrow points at a different box in each view, which may be an artefact of the notation. The arrows mix data flow, control and authority. In the views, verification never reaches acceptance, and the lifecycle has no verbs for refusing or withdrawing. The fourth diagram, a specification for a contract that was not yet implemented, does draw a line between a language-specific adapter and a neutral core, and it states a precise point: an output-only check can confirm the facts behind a result only where that result determines them.

## 6.7.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. The set declares itself a proposal (Src; an instance of §1.14.5 and P-8).**
- "They should be treated as an architecture proposal until the corresponding PO/ARB decisions are accepted into the authoritative model." [S0081 L3].
- **Int:** the set claims no standing for itself and names what would give it standing: acts accepted "into the authoritative model". That is §1.14.5's order, decision before enforcement, applied to a model. Which acts these are, whether any exist, and where "the authoritative model" is, the five files do not say.
- *Later evidence (window C16):* on 2026-08-19 a verifier found "**all four `.puml` files and both `README`s are untracked**" (S0090 L57; §8.9 C9). So at the time the set was not a governed artifact; S0089 classes such a diagram as evidence of non-registration, not of non-existence (S0089 L81).

**C2. The three C4 views draw no kernel/application line, and disagree on the system boundary (Src; Int for the reading; restated after review C15).**
- The three views put every capability inside one platform boundary, "KnowledgeOS / AI Engineering Platform" [S0083 L12; S0086 L8], or "KnowledgeOS Platform" [S0085 L7]. Inside sit a "Governance Engine" with the workflow acts "REGISTER, GRANT, HANDOFF, START, ACCEPT, COMPLETE, CANCEL" [S0086 L9], an "Architecture Workbench" and an "Implementation Workbench" [S0086 L12–13], and in the component view a "Drafting Studio" that "Produces bounded proposals and reports" [S0085 L14].
- **The boundary differs between views (Src):**
  - the repository is an external system in the context view [S0083 L13], and its store is drawn outside the platform in the component view [S0085 L19]; in the container view the estate, with "source and tests", is **inside** the boundary [S0086 L17];
  - the AI runtime is external in the context and component views [S0083 L14; S0085 L20]; in the container view there is none, and "Session Orchestration" has the technology "Agent runtime" [S0086 L15].
- The context view defines the system as a "Governed engineering operating system for evidence, architecture, implementation, verification and knowledge lifecycle" [S0083 L12]: an **engineering** platform, with domain functions inside its own definition.
- **Int (RESEARCH-PURPOSE, the test for every concept):** the views answer "what is the platform?", not "what is the kernel?". Under the purpose's stability test, the workbenches and the drafting studio are candidates for applications; the governance engine and knowledge engineering are candidates for the kernel. *Hyp:* that split is the book's reading; the views do not draw it.
- **A comparison with §0.2, restated:** the statement of 07-27 that "AI is a capability inside the system. It is not the system." (§0.2, DevConst L24) puts AI inside. Two views put the AI runtime outside; the container view puts an "Agent runtime" inside. *Int:* a capability that uses AI and an AI runtime are different things, and the views do not say which they mean, so no tension is shown.
- **The fourth diagram does draw a line (Int; see C7):** S0084 separates a language-specific "Language Binding / Extraction" from a "Language-neutral schema" and the levels above it [S0084 L7–8]. That is the shape of an adapter and a core, though not named kernel. So "no line is drawn" holds for the three C4 views, not for the set.

**C3. One capability, different homes in different views (Src).**
- Container view: the "Governance Engine" carries "GRANT, HANDOFF" [S0086 L9], and "Session Orchestration" carries "Assignment, grants, handoffs, mutation ownership" [S0086 L15]. Both write to the estate: the governance engine "Persists lifecycle records" [S0086 L30], and session orchestration "Reads / writes workflow state" [S0086 L35].
- Component view: one "Workflow Engine" carries "Grants, assignments, handoffs, mutation ownership and transitions" [S0085 L10], beside a separate "Session Manager" that "Registers and activates work sessions" [S0085 L9].
- **Policy also has two places** in the container view: the governance engine is "Workflow records / policy" [S0086 L9], and session orchestration "Loads platform policy" from the registry [S0086 L29].
- **Int (§6.6 C1, §9.6):** in the container view, grants and handoffs have two homes and two writers; in the component view they have one. The book's measured machinery, BC-7, keeps sessions, grants and transitions in one record with one fold (§6.5, §9.6). **It matches the component view in grants and handoffs, not in sessions,** which the component view separates (restated after review C15). A component view is meant to refine its container, not to move a capability between containers; S0085 also decomposes the whole platform rather than one container. Which view is authoritative the set does not say (C1: the set calls itself a proposal).

**C4. The human authorizer's arrow points at a different box in each view (Src; Int restated after review C15).**
- Context view: "PO / ARB" → "Governance Engineer", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0083 L16]. The Governance Engineer is a person with "Authority, lifecycle, grants, acceptance" [S0083 L6].
- Container view: "PO / ARB" → "Governance Engine", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0086 L20].
- Component view: "PO / ARB" → "Workflow Engine", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0085 L22].
- **Int (§1.13, §1.12), with two qualifications:** in the book's model, the human act happens outside the mechanism, and Governance registers a grant that points at it. Read literally, the two lower views draw the act as an input to a mechanism.
  - (a) The context view does not fit §1.13 cleanly either. Its Governance Engineer holds "Authority" and "acceptance" [S0083 L6]: a second holder of authority, not only a registrar.
  - (b) The change of target may be a notation artefact. At C4 levels 2 and 3 persons relate to containers and components; in the container view the Governance person is folded into "Engineering Roles" [S0086 L6], so the PO's arrow has to land on a box.
- *So:* the views are ambiguous about where an act happens and who holds authority. No contradiction with §1.13 is shown.

**C5. Testing the direction law needs a partition and dependency-identifiable relations (Ded, from the labels; restated after review C15).** `[CON-0147 · provisional]`
- The relation labels mix four kinds: data flow ("Persists state", "Reads evidence") [S0085 L32, L28]; control ("Bootstraps and constrains AI sessions") [S0083 L23]; authority ("Authorizes / accepts") [S0083 L16]; and use ("Consumes approved architecture") [S0086 L26].
- **Ded:** §6.6 C8's direction law is about **dependency**: applications depend on the knowledge system, never the reverse. Testing it on a view needs two things:
  1. a **partition** into kernel and applications, which the C4 views do not draw (C2);
  2. relations from which the dependencies can be identified.
- Without (1), the law cannot be tested at all. Without (2), mixed arrows block **confirmation** more than refutation: one arrow that is clearly a dependency ("Consumes") running from kernel to application would refute the law. For example, "Bootstraps and constrains AI sessions" (platform → AI) and "Executes assigned work under grants" (AI → platform) [S0083 L23–24] run both ways, but neither is declared a dependency.

**C6. "Supplies authoritative state": the homonym again (Src; a second instance of §6.6's homonym).**
- "Workflow Engine" → "Communication Capability": "Supplies authoritative state" [S0085 L26].
- **Int (§1.14, state ≠ authority):** "authoritative" here means the canonical source of a state, as in S0066's "establish engineering authority" (§6.6, S0066 L286–287). It does not mean standing conferred by an act. *Independence:* not shown either way. The first commit of S0066 and of this set is the same, but it adds 747 files, so it shows a batch upload, not a shared origin (corrected after review C15).
- **A second homonym:** "Governance Communication" is a person in the context view [S0083 L7] and a capability in the other two [S0085 L16; S0086 L10]. A role and a capability carry one name.

**C7. When an output-only check can confirm the facts behind it (Src; Ded; Ext for the metric's definition; restated after review C15).** `[CON-0146 · invalid as stated]` (restated form (a)–(c) below)
- The fact model's layers: source text → "L0-L2 extraction into language-neutral facts" → "L3 Fact Model" → "L4 Cohesion Semantics" → "L5 Metric", "LCOM4", "Derived cohesion metric" [S0084 L7–10]. The conformance evidence is "Node set + edge set + final metric" [S0084 L11]. Two constraints: "Qualifier kind is preserved, not inferred from final metric", and "Metric alone is insufficient" [S0084 L21–22].
- **Status of the source:** S0084 is a **specification**. The contract it draws, KOS-CONTRACT-NEUTRALITY-001, was "handed off having deliberately implemented nothing" by the day of the diagrams [S0079 L32]. So the diagram shows a design intention, not an observed mechanism.
- *Ext (definition, from the software-metrics literature; variants exist):* LCOM4 counts the connected components of a graph of a class's methods (and, in one variant, its fields).
- **Ded (identifiability).** Let f map an admissible fact graph G to a value f(G), and let A be the set of admissible graphs. For an expected value v, call F(v) = {G ∈ A : f(G) = v} its fibre.
  - (a) A check that compares only f(G) with v **rejects** soundly: if f(G) ≠ v, then G is not the expected graph. It **confirms** G exactly when F(v) has one element. For LCOM4 most fibres have many elements (two different connected graphs both give 1), but some are singletons (for a fixed node set, the edgeless graph is the only one with as many components as nodes).
  - (b) Comparing the intermediate levels, here the node and edge sets, is **sufficient** to confirm G. It is not necessary: any compared quantity that determines G on A would do.
  - (c) A property p of the inputs can be read off the output exactly when p is constant on every fibre. For example, LCOM4 = 1 implies that the graph is connected, while the qualifier kind is not constant on fibres. So it must be carried forward ("preserved") from the level where it is a fact.
- **Int:** this is **related to** §8.8 C2's rule (a count needs its method), which is about reproducing a count, not about identifying its inputs. It also states the layer each claim holds at, the point the map records as missing from the book (S0164). *Hyp (one specification):* that evidence for a derived claim in the kernel must carry the facts its result does not determine. The Ded part holds for any derivation.
- **Further L3 attributes:** the fact model includes "stable identity, method identity" and "determinability" [S0084 L8], so whether a value can be determined is marked at the level of facts. Conformance is checked against "Declared expectations" [S0084 L11]; who declares them is not stated.

**C8. In the views, verification never reaches acceptance (Src; for "how can it be trusted?"; added after review C15).**
- Component view: the review engine "Routes independent verification" to the verification engine [S0085 L30], which "Reads evidence / writes findings" to the knowledge manager [S0085 L31]. The review engine "preserves role separation" [S0085 L13]. Container view: verification "Challenges architecture artifacts" and "Challenges implementation" [S0086 L27–28], and "Reads evidence / writes reports" [S0086 L34].
- In neither view does verification or review have a relation to the governance or workflow engine, where acceptance is drawn [S0085 L22; S0086 L20]. Knowledge "Provides evidence / provenance" to governance [S0086 L24; S0085 L27]: it informs, and does not decide.
- **Int (the invariant-owner test, §9.6):** how a finding bears on an acceptance is not modelled. "Preserves role separation" names an invariant with no relation that enforces it. So the views give producer ≠ verifier (§1.14.3) a role but no mechanism.

**C9. A lifecycle with no verbs for refusing or withdrawing (Src; an extension of §5.7 C3; added after review C15).**
- The container view lists seven workflow acts: "REGISTER, GRANT, HANDOFF, START, ACCEPT, COMPLETE, CANCEL" [S0086 L9]. There is no REJECT, REVOKE, SUPERSEDE or RETRACT; ACCEPT has no negative; CANCEL is not a revocation of a grant.
- **Int (§5.7 C3):** this is a second instance of a lifecycle vocabulary without a needed ending, after the engine's missing "retire" and "un-cancel" (S0080 L46–48). An acceptance that cannot be refused within the vocabulary is met trivially by never deciding (§5.7 C1, the trivial-solution test). *Supersession* appears only once in the set, under "Knowledge Engineering" [S0086 L11].

## 6.7.4 What does not hold

*First version, refuted at review C15:*
- **"Every capability sits inside one boundary … Outside it are the repository and the AI runtime."** The container view puts the estate inside and has no external AI runtime (S0086 L15, L17). The boundary differs between views; restated in C2.
- **"No kernel/application line is drawn"** for the whole set. S0084 draws an adapter/core line; restated for the three C4 views.
- **A tension with §0.2.** Not shown: the views do not say which sense of "AI" they mean.
- **"Only the context view draws that: a person between the act and the record"** (C4). Its Governance Engineer also holds authority, and the change of target may be notation. Restated with (a) and (b).
- **"BC-7 … matches the component view"** (C3). Only in grants and handoffs.
- **"Can be neither confirmed nor refuted"** (C5, CON-0147 as stated). Mixed arrows block confirmation more than refutation; the main obstacle is the missing partition.
- **"Not independent of §6.6: S0066 and this set were first committed together"** (C6). The commit adds 747 files.
- **"Agreement of the final metric cannot show that the extracted facts are right"; "a check must compare the intermediate levels"; "a property of the inputs cannot be recovered from the output"** (C7, CON-0146 as stated). Each is too general: an output check confirms where the fibre is a singleton, intermediate levels are sufficient and not necessary, and a property constant on fibres can be read off. Restated as (a)–(c).
- **"§8.8 C2's rule … put as a theorem about derivations"** (C7). Related, not the same.
- **C7's kernel reading as resting on an observed mechanism.** S0084 specifies a contract that was not implemented (S0079 L32).
