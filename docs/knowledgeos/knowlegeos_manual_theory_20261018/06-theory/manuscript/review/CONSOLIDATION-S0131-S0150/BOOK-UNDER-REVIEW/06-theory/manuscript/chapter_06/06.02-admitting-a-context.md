# 6.2 Admitting a bounded context

## 6.2.1 In plain language

When is a part of a system its own bounded context, rather than a feature, a subsystem or a workflow inside one? The corpus's strategic-DDD protocol (Round47-OP, quoted in S0033, 2026-08-02) gives a closed list of acceptable reasons, nine in all. It explicitly rules out "it seems reasonable", "the ontology has this concept" and "the folder exists". The list is sound and domain-free. But it says only which reasons **may** count, not how much is **enough**, so two analysts applying it can, and on the same day did, reach opposite verdicts on the same candidates.

## 6.2.2 Definitions

- **Admission criteria for a bounded context (Src).** A candidate seam is "promoted to a bounded context only if" supported by one or more of nine criteria: semantic ownership · transactional consistency · lifecycle independence · invariants · ubiquitous-language divergence · team autonomy · deployment autonomy · integration characteristics · performance constraints. These are "the ONLY admissible justifications"; "No other justification is sufficient." [S0033 L29] `[CON-0085 · invalid]`
- **A guard against over-reading the domain (Src).** "Semantic truth does NOT automatically imply software structure" [S0033 L31]. A concept existing in the domain does not make it a software boundary.

## 6.2.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. The written rule is a necessary condition with no sufficiency condition (Ded).**
- The source's form is "promoted to a bounded context only if" supported by one or more of the nine [S0033 L29]. "Only if" makes the disjunction **necessary**: A(*c*) ⇒ *k₁*(*c*) ∨ … ∨ *k₉*(*c*). "No other justification is sufficient" rules out other reasons; it does not make any one criterion sufficient.
- So the rule says what a valid reason **must** be, and is silent on what is **enough**: how many criteria, and how much evidence for each.
- **The applications are consistent with the written rule,** not in conflict with it. The same document rejects "Bootstrap / Genesis" with "lifecycle independence only … one criterion, n=1" [S0033 L81], and also rejects Knowledge Validation and Operational Learning [S0033 L83, L86]. "Engineering Governance" is not admitted by the test: it is classed as already existing [S0033 L82]. A necessary condition permits all of these rejections.
- **What follows:** with no stated sufficiency condition, admission is **underdetermined**. *Rival for the evidence below:* the opposite verdicts may show within-rater inconsistency in one programme rather than a rule that cannot decide. S0034 downgraded its own verdicts the same day after a falsification test (D-3 "Strong → WEAK", D-7 "WEAK" [S0034 L20–33]). The two readings are not separated by this evidence. Any decision above the necessary condition rests on an unstated threshold, and is not reproducible from the rule.
- **The corpus shows the non-reproducibility directly (Src).** Applied on the same day to the same objects, the nine criteria gave opposite verdicts:
  - **Runtime:** a subsystem behind an anti-corruption layer, not a domain (S0033); a bounded context and generic subdomain, D-3 (S0034).
  - **Evidence and learning:** an "existing, non-functioning" mechanism, not a new domain [S0033 L131]; D-6a, a bounded context that has never executed (S0034).
  - **Governance:** a "Supporting Subdomain" [S0033 L82], and in the same document an internal subsystem, not a context (S0033's classification and map, L111, L176); a bounded context, D-1 (S0034).
  - **The central result:** S0033 and S0035 find no new strategic context; S0034 finds four platform-side contexts and a missing domain (§6.3 C3).
- `[CON-0085 · invalid]` (partly valid: correct as a list of admissible reasons, a necessary condition; incomplete as a decision rule).

**C2. Each criterion needs its own test (Int).**
- The source marks each criterion as "satisfied" or not, but gives no test for any of them.
- **Some criteria cannot vary in the corpus's setting.** "Team autonomy" and "deployment autonomy" presuppose several teams and deployments. The corpus records a single developer (S0029's CI/PR row) and no production deployment (Ch. 2 §2.8.7). Yet S0033 marks team autonomy as met for governance [S0033 L82].
- A criterion that cannot vary in a setting cannot discriminate there. This is the admission test of Ch. 2 §2.8.6, applied to boundaries: a criterion earns its place only if it actually varies.
- **The criteria are software-boundary criteria.** The protocol's own guard is "Semantic truth does NOT automatically imply software structure" [S0033 L31]. S0034 applies the same criteria to bodies of knowledge and sets of documents. For example, under "transactional consistency" it lists "ES-001..006 change as a set" [S0034 L54], and the void-overriding rule [S0034 L70], which is not what DDD means by the term. Whether the criteria transfer to knowledge boundaries at all is a separate question that the corpus does not ask.

**C2a. Two scope ambiguities around the same work (Src).**
- **The standing STOP on context maps.** S0035 refuses to draw a context map, under a standing STOP. The same day, S0033 draws a "Strategic Context Map" and S0034 a graph of domain relations. Either the STOP covers only one discovery, or two documents breach it. No source says which.
- **The classification of KnowledgeOS itself.** S0033 classes it as a "PRODUCT". S0032 and S0035 say that calling it the product contradicts AIP-14, and that it is a potential product only. S0031 accepts the vision "as destination, not evaluated". These are three standings for one name (compare Ch. 0 §0.2, CON-0027 and CON-0028).

**C3. Two classifications that must not be merged (Src).** "The two classifications are ORTHOGONAL and must not be collapsed" [S0033 L96]:
- **product ownership** (who owns the knowledge: stable);
- **architectural responsibility** (what the artifact is about: internal, changing).

"Promoting a subsystem responsibility to a repository root would encode an INTERNAL DESIGN DECISION as a REPOSITORY-WIDE OWNERSHIP BOUNDARY." [S0033 L116]. This agrees with §3.6 and Ch. 1 §1.11 (ownership).

**C4. Other points (repeats).** These are recorded in earlier sections, so the book is unchanged by them:
- the two extraction blockers, case-law dilution and coupling at the boundary (§6.1 C3, the lattice points (1,0) and (0,1)) [S0033 L156–167];
- bootstrapping as three responsibilities (§6.1 C5);
- the per-candidate events that would justify promotion (Ch. 5 §5.3) [S0033 L212–224].

## 6.2.4 Open questions

- What sufficiency condition admits a bounded context: how many criteria, with what evidence (C1)?
- Do software-boundary criteria apply to knowledge boundaries (C2)?
- What is the scope of the STOP on context maps (C2a)?
- How is each criterion tested (C2)?
- Is SD-1's rule part a platform rule (fork F10; OQ-S1 [S0033 L241])?
