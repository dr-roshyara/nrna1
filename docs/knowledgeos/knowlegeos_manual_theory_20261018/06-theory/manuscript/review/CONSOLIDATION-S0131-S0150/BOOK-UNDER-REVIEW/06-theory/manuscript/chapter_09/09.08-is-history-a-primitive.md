## 9.8.1 In plain language

"History" is on the list of the kernel's candidate primitives. This turn asks whether it is one. It separates two things the word covers: **state history**, "what standing did item c have at time t, as the record stood at time s?", and **act history**, "what happened, and who authorized it?". State history can be computed from the record if the record has some properties: every change to it is itself an entry, it carries two orders (when an act happened and when it was recorded), and the rules used to evaluate it are pinned. A truthful history needs more: every entry reports an act that really happened and was performed by someone entitled to it, and every act has an entry or a recorded absence. Those two conditions cannot be checked from inside the record. Whether this makes history a primitive depends on how the book's own "authority" and "policy" are read: as the book defines them, they already include parts of it. So "history" is a bundle of record properties, not an atom; whether it is a separate primitive, or part of authority and policy, is open.

## 9.8.2 Definitions

- **The record R:** a sequence of entries (e₁, e₂, …), each with an identity, a type and a payload, kept per record (the estate's `seq` is per record, §9.7). The book's acts, grants and transitions are entries (§1.13, §6.5).
- **Position** i; **recording time** τ(i), when entry i was written; **act time** t_x, when the act an entry reports happened (§1.14.1; §1.16.2).
- **Cut** R≤s: the entries recorded at or before s. Within one record it is a prefix only if τ does not decrease along positions. Across records it needs a rule: a common clock, or F26's options (§9.7 step 2).
- **The evaluator** CE(c, x, t; θ, R≤s): standing of c in scope x at t under parameters θ, against the cut R≤s (§9.7). **It is not yet defined:** §9.7.7 result 2 lists the missing coverage relation and actor–holder link, a recursive σ and a non-monotone υ. Every result below that uses CE is conditional on that repair.
- **State history:** Hist(c, x, t, s, u) = the standing of c in scope x at time t, as the record stood at s, under the parameters θ(u) in force at u. Fixing u = s gives "what did we decide then"; u = now gives "what would we decide now about then". Both are legitimate queries; S2782's two questions, what was true for the domain at a time [S2782 L775] and what KnowledgeOS believed or knew at that time [S2782 L779] (in meaning), are cases of the axes (t, s). Which u the kernel must answer is the contract-time question of §9.7 step 4 and F27.
- **Act history:** what acts happened, in which order, by whom, under which authority. S0129 records the two questions as "*"what is happening now?"* vs *"what happened, who authorized it, can we reconstruct it?"*" [S0129 L47].
- **Primitive (Rulebook §12b (a)):** a concept with a precise definition and an **independence model**, a model in which it alone fails while the other primitives hold. If no such model exists, it is derivable.

## 9.8.3 Properties of the record

- **H1′, every change to the record is an entry.** No entry is changed. An entry is removed only by a recorded act, with a recovery pointer or a recorded loss. Then R≤s is recoverable from R≤s′ for every s < s′, except for losses that are themselves on record. (This replaces the first version's "no entry is removed or changed", which merged recoverability with non-removal and excluded deletion required by retention or law; S2782 L1873–1905 makes deletion a matter of a retention contract, in meaning. S2782's own formula, H_{t+1} = H_t ∪ {e_{t+1}} [S2782 L868, in meaning], is a set union and drops order and duplicates; H1′ is stated over sequences.)
- **H2, two orders.** Each entry carries τ, and each act entry its t_x, kept apart; τ does not decrease along positions within a record.
- **H3, pinned evaluation inputs,** in two parts:
  - **H3a:** the policy values θ in force at each time are themselves entries (policy acts);
  - **H3b:** the definition of CE in force at each time is pinned and recoverable (§1.16.6). S2782: a different result of replaying under a later rule "does not necessarily mean the historical state was wrong." [S2782 L947]; it may mean that replay under version 4 differs from replay under version 3 [S2782 L952, in meaning].
- **H4, soundness:** every entry reports an act that occurred and was performed by an actor entitled to it. S0095: "A transcription records **a human act that was given**. No such act exists in this lane's record, and **Governance may not supply one.**" [S0095 L141].
- **H5, completeness or marked absence:** every act that changes standing has an entry, or its absence is itself recorded. S0095: the report exists and the record "contains no `START`" [S0095 L147]; after the disposition "that absence is now part of the governed record rather than a gap in it" [S0095 L149]. Its home is §1.16.8, "absence must be visible".

H1′–H3 are constraints on the record's form. **H4 and H5 are about the relation between the record and the world, and neither can be checked from inside the record** (§1.12: verifiable is not valid). *Refined at theory turn 6 (§9.15 R2, R5):*
- H4 has three parts, occurrence, attribution and entitlement. Entitlement of the named actor **is** decided inside (authorized(x)); occurrence and attribution are legitimacy assumptions.
- Under A1 recognized and C1 = constitute, H5 holds by definition for recognized standing. Its truthful reading, and M4, belong to A1-as-fact.

## 9.8.4 A conditional reduction of state history (Ded)

**Lemma D.** If H1′, H2 and H3 hold, F26 is settled for the cut across records, and CE is defined and deterministic for the chosen θ on every cut (met by CE* for every cut inside one well-formed record in D, which admits citations of earlier entries only, for the parameter values Θ_f, under C1 = constitute, §9.13.6; for a cut across records only if the merged cut is in D, a condition on F26 (c); not met for other values, nor for the present record, which is not in D), then

  Hist(c, x, t, s, u) = CE(c, x, t; θ(u), R≤s),

with θ(u) read from the record by H3a and the definition of CE from H3b.

**Proof.**
1. By H1′, R≤s exists as part of R, apart from losses on record.
2. By H2 and the cut rule, R≤s can be selected by recording time, and its acts ordered by act time with ties broken as F26 decides.
3. By H3, θ(u) and the definition in force at u are available.
4. CE is a function on these arguments (premise), so its value is determined. ∎

**What D is.** A conditional **reduction**, close to definitional: Hist is defined as standing as the record stood, and CE on the cut is that value by construction. Its content is the list of premises. It restates the form of S2782's replay theorem (Replay with versions, S2782 L3036–3054, in meaning), from the same conceptual line, not as confirmation. **It needs no stored result and no mechanism beyond H1′–H3.**

*Src (turn 7, corpus first done late).* The corpus states the fold, "The current state can then be derived by replay." [S0916 L606], and the limit that §9.15 places at the trust boundary: events tell "what the system recorded", not "what was true in the real world" [S0916 L625] ("EventHistory ≠ WorldHistory"; so H4 and H5 are about the world, §9.15). The corpus also has the logical/operational split that D formalises: state "could theoretically be reconstructed from" events [S0969 L585], "is not necessarily fundamental in the mathematical sense" [S0969 L597], "But operationally it is extremely valuable." [S0969 L599]. Same line of thought, not confirmation; D's premise list is the book's own.

**Three premises that D does not discharge:**
- **CE's repair** (TT2). Without it, Hist is undefined too; it does not become a primitive of its own.
- **The regress of H3a.** Finding θ(u) is itself a state-history query on policy items, under some θ′. D is well-founded only with a fixed meta-evaluator or root for policy items (§1.14.1, root acts). **Fork F31.**
- **Necessity rests on CON-0134, in its restated form.** A bitemporal store of computed standings would answer Hist with no act record. Only the rule that a derived value is never stored **as standing** (T-1b; CON-0134 as restated, CON-0141; §6.5 C4) rules that out. The absolute "never stored" is false of BC-7 and is not needed: the inference survives on T-1b. It then rests on a **design rule** (non-consultation by the fold), not on a mechanism fact about what is stored. So D shows sufficiency; the necessity of the record is that rule's.

**When current state suffices (S1609; Int).** S1609 turns the question into a test: the fold from histories to states "can be many-to-one" [S1609 L405], and "Two different histories can converge on the same state." [S1609 L415]. A state abstraction is adequate only if histories that give the same state still give the same state after every permitted operation: "**State abstraction is valid only if it is transformation-compatible.**" [S1609 L595] (a congruence condition). *Int:* the kernel needs H1′ exactly where some required query or operation does not factor through current state. Hist queries with s or u before now are of that kind wherever the fold is many-to-one on the items asked about. Act history is of that kind by definition.

## 9.8.5 Models: what fails, and what fails with it

Each model is checked against the other candidates **as the book defines them**, not only as listed: authority includes §1.2's checkability ("a claim's history is valid iff it starts in a non-authoritative state and every change is labelled by a recorded act", §1.2) and §1.16.2's time index ("valid at the time of the act"); policy includes §2.11 RM-3 ("Changing an authoritative rule requires explicit authorization", §2.11).

**M1: an overwriting act record (H1′ fails).**
- *Structure:* items with identities; claims with supersession links (I-7) and recording times, kept as objects; **act entries** (grants, revocations, standing changes) overwritten per item, keeping only the last.
- *What survives:* the content history of claims, through the I-7 chain and its times. *What is lost:* standing changes that leave no trace in the claim chain, such as a revocation or a retraction, and the act history.
- *What fails with it:* identity, I-7, evidence, relationships and A1 **as a fact about each change** (A1-as-fact, Ch. 1 §1.6) still hold. **Authority as §1.2 defines it fails:** a claim's history can no longer be checked. So M1 is an independence model for H1′ relative to A1-as-fact, **not** relative to the book's authority. Relative to A1-as-form it is not one either: overwritten act entries leave standing changes without their act labels, so A1-as-form fails with H1′ (§9.9.7 compares M-T2, which is the reverse).
- *The estate's cases:* commit `9c93e2f0` removed "**73 lines**" from an append-only session log, and the content "is recoverable at `e8b3842b`" [S0095 L38]: H1′ failed for the file and held at the level of git. And for the authority record itself, commit `de998173` committed the narrative documents "while **all 28 grants and 33 transitions remained outside git**" [S0129 L187]; "**The documents survived; the record they register against did not.**" [S0129 L187]. For the authority record, H1′ did not hold at the git level on 2026-08-19.

**M2: positions but no times (H2 fails).**
- *Structure:* an append-only record per item, dense positions, no τ and no t_x. This is the estate as measured: "Only 2 of 210 transitions carry any `date` field" (§9.7, input table, S0128). H1′ and H3 are stated over positions here.
- *What fails with it:* **authority's time index (§1.16.2)**: "valid at the time of the act" cannot be evaluated. So M2 is not an independence model for H2 relative to the book's authority either.
- *The two orders differ in the estate:* a START was transcribed at "seq 16", a position "later than the artifact it authorizes" [S0095 L218]; "A transcription that appeared at seq 15 would read as though the `START` had preceded the report. **It did not, and the record now says so.**" [S0095 L221]. *Int:* the record keeps the difference only in prose, and the START's act time itself is not recorded. M2 removes both orders together, so it shows that H2 as a whole is needed, not that τ and t_x are independent of each other.

**M3: an unpinned evaluator (H3 fails).**
- *H3a fails:* θ changes without a recorded policy act. **That breaks RM-3**: a change of an authoritative rule without authorization. So H3a is not independent of policy as the book defines it; given H1′ and RM-3, **H3a follows** (θ(u) is the latest policy acts in R≤u), up to the regress F31.
- *H3b fails:* the definition of CE is not pinned, so Hist is computed under today's definition. The other candidates hold. **H3b has an independence model.**

**M4: an empty or incomplete record (H5 fails).** H1′–H3 hold trivially for an empty record, and for a record missing an act (S0095's X-2). Hist is well defined and false. This is the trivial-solution test: H1′–H3 are must-never constraints, and H5 is the must-do.

**M5: an unattested author (H4 fails).** Entries are well formed, but their authors are "self-declared, not attestable" (S0129 L4, in meaning); "Recording ≠ Asserting", one of "the estate's existing principles" (S0129 L102, in meaning). H1′–H3 hold; whether an entry reports an act by an entitled actor is not decided by the record.

## 9.8.6 What follows (results, restated)

1. **State history decomposes** into H1′, H2, H3 and the evaluator (D), conditional on CE's repair, F26 and the regress F31. `[CON-0148 · invalid as stated]` (the restated form here is `[CON-0177 · provisional]`; see §9.8.10)
2. **Relative to the other candidates as the book defines them, history is not independent:** H1′ is part of authority's checkability (§1.2; M1), H2 part of authority's time index (§1.16.2; M2), and H3a follows from policy's RM-3 (M3). Only H3b has an independence model. *This dependence holds by definition:* the book defines authority's checkability and time index to include H1′ and H2, so the candidates overlap by construction, not by a finding about the record. **Relative to A1 read as a fact about each change**, H1′ and H2 are independent (M1, M2). So whether history is a separate primitive depends on how "authority" is split: valid against verifiable (§1.12). **For the Principal.**
3. **A truthful history needs H4 and H5,** which are not properties of the record's form and cannot be checked from inside it. They are the residual trust assumption of §1.12, applied to history.
4. **The record as a primitive** remains a *Hyp* without its own independence model: no model has been given in which "the record" alone fails while identity, order and authority hold. Two readings are open: the record is a primitive with axioms H1′, H2 (and H3b), or it is part of authority. `[CON-0149 · provisional]`
5. **The estate:** the session log's lines survived at the git level (S0095 L38); the authority record was outside git on 2026-08-19 (S0129 L187). Whether that has changed since is not shown.
6. **A history that is reconstructed is not history.** S0129 records the principle "**`History ≠ Reconstruction Guess`**" [S0129 L102], called new there and placed beside the estate's existing principles, in a section whose recommendations are "still `RECOMMENDATION`, not `DECIDED`" (S0129 L97, in meaning). S0095: restoring append-only history "is **itself a history edit**", and "**A recorded truncation with a recovery pointer is a truthful history. A silently reconstructed one is not.**" [S0095 L162]. And restored material is represented "as SUCCESSIVE ACTS, not as a blended text" (S0095 L166, in meaning). This is H1′ and H5 together: a loss is recorded, not repaired silently. *Int:* §5.7 C3's rule (recognized only when recorded), applied to history.
7. **Merging records is a separate conflict.** S0129's R-CONFLICT asks that a resolution preserve "sequence integrity", provenance and reconstruction ability, and not "delete one history" or "rewrite sequence" (S0129 L45, in meaning). It is a rule for merging records written by several writers, so it bears on H1′ for unions of records and on F26. It was not selected in S0129 (S0129 L158, in meaning). *(Updated at the C24 settlement, 2026-10-23, from S0130:)* **it is now adopted**: a governance evidence conflict "MUST NOT be resolved by: silently selecting one side · deleting historical evidence · rewriting sequence history", and a resolution "MUST preserve: provenance · sequence integrity · reconstruction capability" (S0130 L28–29, in meaning). The selection's basis act is not on the record (§8.17 C9). The adopted text has no seq-gap clause, unlike the proposed one (S0128 L161), and a dense sequence does not detect a dropped concurrent branch (§8.17 C5). It differs from conflict between claims (F24).

## 9.8.7 Boundary cases, and what a reviewer would need to challenge

- **If "the record" is defined to satisfy H1′,** M1 is not a model of the record, and the record is a primitive with axioms H2 and H3b. History is then still decomposed, not atomic.
- **If authority is read as A1 only** (a fact about each change), H1′ and H2 are independent of the six, and the record, or history, is a further primitive. **If authority includes verifiability** (§1.2, §1.16.2), they are parts of authority.
- **To refute result 2:** a model in which H1′ or H2 fails while authority as §1.2 and §1.16.2 define it still holds. **To refute D:** a model satisfying all its premises in which Hist is not determined.

## 9.8.8 Forks

- **F29, state history vs act history:** must the kernel answer act history ("what happened, who authorized it") as well as state history, or does act history belong to an application that reads the record?
- **F30, H1′ against an absolute append-only rule:** may a kernel record lose entries by a recorded act (retention, law), or must it never lose them? **Partial answer (the Principal, after theory turn 6):** removal is by **tombstone**.
  - The loss entry keeps the removed entry's identity, its kind and the fields citations rely on, and drops the content. The record stays in D, and answers resting on it are marked "rests on removed content" (§9.13.2).
  - So content may be lost by a recorded act, while structure may not.
  - Still open: whether a law may require dropping the identity too (P-loss), and how long a tombstone is kept.
- **F31, the regress of θ(u):** which meta-evaluator or root fixes the policy in force for policy items?

## 9.8.9 Open questions

- For the Principal: is verifiability part of "authority" in the list of primitives, or a separate property (result 2)?
- Should the list replace "history" by "the record", or record history under authority and policy (results 2, 4)?
- Which record must satisfy H1′: each governed file, the workflow record, or the repository's history (M1's cases)?
- H2 and F26: does the kernel require act times so that F26(c) is available, and how are act times made trustworthy (§1.12)?

## 9.8.10 What does not hold (first version, refuted at review TT3)

- **"So 'history' is not a primitive in the §12b sense"** (old result 3; CON-0148 as stated). Models in which history fails while other candidates hold would be independence models *for* history; the first version read them the other way. D shows a decomposition, not a derivation from the six (A, finding 1).
- **"H1, H2 and H3 are mutually independent, and independent of the six other candidates"** (old result 2; plain language). Under the book's definitions M1 breaks §1.2's authority, M2 its time index (§1.16.2), and M3 policy's RM-3 (A finding 2; B findings T1, T2).
- **H3 as one property, independent of H1 and H2.** The policy part follows from H1′ and RM-3, up to a regress; only the definition part is independent (A 3; B T2).
- **H1, "No entry is removed or changed".** It merged recoverability with non-removal and excluded deletion by retention or law; replaced by H1′ (A 5; B T12).
- **"The estate keeps H1 at the level of git, not of each file"** (old result 4). The authority record was outside git on 2026-08-19 (S0129 L187) (A 6; B T6).
- **"History, defined as Hist(c, x, t, s), is derivable from H1–H3 and the evaluator",** without the premises F26, the θ index and the regress, and with x missing on the right side; and "needs no stored field and no separate mechanism" (A 7; B T8, T9).
- **"If CE stays undefined … It would then have to be a primitive of its own, defined by its queries."** A non sequitur: without CE, Hist is undefined (B T10).
- **M1 as stated**, keeping I-7 and times while claiming the earlier cut is gone (A 12; B T3); **M2 as breaking H2 only** (A 8; B T13).
- **H1–H3 as the whole of history.** A truthful history needs the must-do H5 and the soundness H4 (A 4; B T4).
- **"Conflict persistence … a property of the state space, not of the record"** (old §9.8.6 item 5). R-CONFLICT is a rule for merging records, and it was not adopted at TT3 (A 11); it is adopted since S0130 (item 7).
- **"The record itself is not on the list of primitives"** stated as a finding in the plain language. It is a Hyp without an independence model (B T7).
