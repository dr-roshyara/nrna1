## 8.16.1 In plain language

When ownership passes from someone who refused the work, the new state can make it look as if the work was done. The record keeps "released" apart from "completed"; a reader of a summary may not. A field can have different owners for its meaning, its data, its rule and its authority. Storing a field does not make you its owner, and needing it does not either. A review that checked only completeness can be cited for completeness, not for correctness. And a decision's basis should be on the record, not only in someone's message.

## 8.16.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Releasing ownership is not completing the work (Src; for §9.16 R4a's transfer).** `[CON-0226 · validated]` *(validated at the C23 review, scoped to the engine's state semantics)*
- After the handoff from the lane that had refused, the fold shows that lane as handed off. The record keeps the two apart: "HANDED_OFF denotes OWNERSHIP RELEASED, not VERIFICATION PERFORMED." (S0121 L115, in meaning). The engine has a separate state for completion, and the lane "has never been COMPLETEd" (S0121 L113, in meaning).
- *"Owner" here is the mutation owner of a work item, a lane (W-8).* The risk lies in reading, not in the record: "the fold alone would mislead a later reader", because a lane that refused "now reads, in the state column, indistinguishably from lanes that finished their work" (S0121 L116, in meaning).
- *A premise of the source (W-10):* "indistinguishably from lanes that finished" holds only if finished lanes, too, are shown as handed off without completion. The engine has a completion state, so this is a premise about practice, unshown.
- The remedy is a clarification, not a transition: "rewriting or re-STARTing S5 would corrupt the history" (S0121 L118, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R4a; §9.13).** In the book's sketch a transfer moves a grant's owner and carries the last use. It records nothing about whether work was done. So a transfer must never be read as a use, or as the old owner's work being finished. CE* keeps them apart by kind: a transfer is not a use (`single_use_grants` excludes it), and no clause of Φ reads a transfer as an ending. What the book lacks is S0121's warning for the **reader**: a summary that shows only "current owner" can hide that the previous owner refused. *Adversarial check (before review):* is there a case where the record itself conflates the two? Not in D. The conflation is in a projection that drops the reason, which is a marks question: a mark "ownership moved; the earlier holder's work not completed" would carry it. Not proposed for the kernel; recorded.

**C2. An unreachability claim withdrawn as too strong (Src; corrects §8.15 C2; restated at review, W-7).** *(No concept: a correction.)*
- Governance had said that no new lane could be made reachable while the refusing lane held ownership. That claim is "WITHDRAWN — this was too strong." [S0121 L127]. The accurate statement was "not reachable without releasing S5's ownership" (S0121 L127, in meaning), and the release then happened at seq 29 (S0121 L100, in meaning).
- **Two facts the first version missed (W-7):**
  - S0121 calls the release legitimate, and handed off accurately means ownership released (S0121 L111, L118, in meaning). So there was no owner "that cannot honestly hand off". The misleading part is only a reader's projection (C1).
  - The seq-29 handoff was recorded by **Governance** (S0121 L100, in meaning). The engine's "from the current owner" names the source **lane**, not the actor who records the handoff.
- **What this changes in §8.15 C2.** That case is not an instance of S0071's rival rule ("only the current owner may hand off", by the owner as actor). Nor is it R4a's rule: the release was **a recorder's release, its basis not shown** (corrected at the consolidation S0101–S0130, A-12, I-14). It was recorded by Governance, no act is cited for it, and "legitimate" is Governance's word about its own transition. Under R4a it would be refused, since the recorder is neither the issuer nor an authority above. So **it is evidence for neither rule.** *(Senses of "owner" in the book, qualified: register owner (§1.10), maintainer (§1.11), mutation owner of a lane (S0121; §9.6 K4), owner application of a grant (R4a), the field roles of S0122 (§8.16 C3).)* *(The pointer in §8.15 C2 is amended.)*

**C3. Storing is not owning, and requiring is not owning; at least three ownership roles of a field are shown distinct (Src; restated at review, W-1, W-2, W-6, W-8).** `[CON-0230 · provisional]` `[CON-0227 · invalid as stated]` *(CON-0227's "four parts" is withdrawn.)*
- S0122 gives each field four owners: semantic, data, invariant and authority (S0122 L142, in meaning). Without the split, "C-10 stores it" reads as "C-10 owns it" (S0122 L144, in meaning). "NONE is a legal value, and an informative one" (S0122 L146, in meaning).
- Requiring is not owning. The knowledge version is required although its meaning is BC-1's. The objection "conflates ownership with requiredness" (S0122 L152, in meaning).
- **What the source's data shows (W-1).** On every field whose owners S0122 lists, the semantic owner and the invariant owner are the same:
  - BC-1 and BC-1 for the knowledge reference;
  - BC-7 and BC-7 for the session reference;
  - the external context for the correlation reference;
  - C-10 for the rest (S0122 L103–133, in meaning).

  So three roles are shown distinct (semantic, data, authority), and semantic versus invariant is **undecided**. The source's "four different answers for one field" (S0122 L142, in meaning) is false: the knowledge reference has three distinct values. *Source defect, listed separately.*
- **Sufficiency fails on the source's own field (W-2).** For the delivery timestamp, "the record is C-10's; the event is not" (S0122 L121, in meaning). That separates the owner of a fact from the owner of its record, which none of the four roles expresses. The table flattens it to "C-10 OWNED" (S0122 L94, in meaning). This is CON-0222's record-versus-world split appearing at field level.
- **No referent gap (W-6, corrected).** The referent of the required version is the **delivered** version, which exists if delivery occurred. What is unbuilt is BC-1's supersession or publication act (S0114 L59, in meaning), which bears on invalidation by supersession, not on populating the field. So the version can be populated wherever versions have identifiers. The open gap is that the superseded state has no trigger mechanism. *(Version 1 marked this pending as a CON-0220 gap; it is not one.)*
- **Int (Ch. 9), with the homonym qualified (W-8).** "Owner" has three senses in these sources and the book:
  - the **mutation owner of a work item**, which is a lane (S0121);
  - the **owner application of a grant** (R4a);
  - the **owners of a field** (S0122).

  The earlier line "R4a is already a split ownership" read S0122's "invariant owner" as the enforcer (the kernel). S0122 uses it for the context that defines the rule (BC-1 for the knowledge reference). That reading was an interpretation, and it is withdrawn.

**C4. The decided claim, in the authority's words; "world fact" is the producer's reading (Src; restated at review, W-3, W-4; the theory the skim of S0125 lost).** *(Extends CON-0222, restated.)*
- **The authority's text (S0125's verbatim block).** The decided claim boundary is "Governed knowledge delivery occurred and acknowledgement was recorded." (S0125 L18, in meaning). Its interpretation:
  - "governed knowledge delivery occurred" "refers to the governed event claim" [S0125 L21];
  - "acknowledgement was recorded" refers to "the recorded acknowledgement evidence" [S0125 L22].

  And: "The receipt records evidence associated with this claim boundary." [S0125 L24]. It "does NOT independently prove" the delivery event, among others (S0125 L26, in meaning).
- **The readings.** "World-fact" and "evidence toward a world-fact" are the producer's gloss (S0122 L38, L43, in meaning), repeated in Governance's rationale (S0125 L41–43, in meaning). The authority's wording is weaker: a **governed event claim**, with evidence **associated with** the boundary. Version 1 wrote the producer's gloss into the decision. That is the error type of C22's W-3 (authority versus registrar), repeated.
- **What is and is not settled about §8.15 C10 (W-4).**
  - Qualification 1 (the move to the record is partial) is answered **as the authority's intent**: the asymmetry is decided.
  - Qualification 2 (the acknowledgement element is met trivially by the receipt itself unless the acknowledgement is a separate record) is **untouched**, and open (§8.16.3).
- **Missing at first, added (coverage):** what kind of act a receipt's "claim" is. The decided text says the receipt "claims", while rule 1 says recording is not asserting and rule 7 says the receipt is not proof (S0122 L34, L69, L75, in meaning). Read in the authority's words, the receipt records evidence associated with a claim boundary; it does not itself assert. *Open fork (§8.16.3).*

**C5. Absence of a field concluded, not assumed; a field forbidden because it invites a forbidden inference (Src).** `[CON-0228 · provisional]`
- The actor field is "FORBIDDEN as a C-10-owned field" (S0122 L110, in meaning), with the rationale argued: absence "is not assumed to be required; it is concluded" (S0122 L115, in meaning).
- The reasons, in short:
  - an actor field invites the inference "identity binding established", which the decision lists as a non-claim;
  - it would import C-5's subject;
  - it could carry only a self-declaration;
  - the session association already carries what is needed (S0122 L111–114, in meaning).
- Drift is "blocked structurally, not by warning": no field asserts understanding, application or compliance (S0122 L188, in meaning).
- Conditional requirement: the invalidation reference is "REQUIRED when lifecycle_state = invalidated", since an unconditional requirement would demand it on every valid receipt (S0122 L132, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13 D), restated at review (W-5).** D's required fields are per kind, not per state. S0122's conditional field has a D analogue: the tombstone, which must cite a removal act only when removed. **But on the identity field the book does the opposite of S0122.** S0122 **forbids** the actor field so that it cannot invite the identity inference. The book **requires** the recorder (I15) and reads it in no clause of Φ, though one mark reads it since v9. Those are two strategies, structural absence and present-but-unread. S0122's argument ("presence invites the inference") applies to the book's own recorder field. *Version 1 called this an analogue; it is a tension, open (§8.16.3).* *Adversarial check:* does forbidding the field lose information the estate needs? The source says the need is real and routes it elsewhere, through C-5 attestation or as correlation content (S0122 L115, in meaning). So the claim is about where the information lives, not that it is unneeded.

**C6. A review is citable only for the questions it checked (Src; CON-0154's rule for reviews).** `[CON-0229 · provisional]`
- The governance review is "bounded". It checks completeness, provenance and decision-boundary preservation only, and "does not evaluate architectural correctness" (S0124 L7–9, in meaning). It "must NEVER be cited as independent verification of E2" (S0124 L9, in meaning).
- So its verdicts are three narrow ones: complete, traceable, preserved (S0124 L85–87, in meaning). It includes a provenance check of the proposal's claims "mechanically verified against the record, not accepted on assertion" (S0124 L45, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.10 C4, CON-0154; §8.15 C4).** CON-0154 asked "authoritative for which claim". S0124 applies it to a review: a review's result is evidence for the questions it checked and for nothing else. A completeness review that finds a model "tested, not asserted" (S0124 L43, in meaning) has checked that a test is present, not that the test is adequate. So §8.16 C3's adversarial point stands unanswered. The diverge/converge test shows that the four ownership parts are not one function, not that they are independent. S0124 does not reach it.
- **The pending point of C3 (b) stays open.** S0124 does not examine whether a receipt with a required version is populatable before BC-1's publication mechanism exists; its bound excludes it. *Open after this window* (§8.16.3).
- **A breach of the bound in the source itself (W-9).** S0124 states "No field classification is wrong" (S0124 L92, in meaning), while declaring that it assessed no field decision for correctness (S0124 L104, in meaning). So the bounded review cites beyond its bound in one line; CON-0229 applies to its own author.
- **Basis outside the record.** The resolution of the decided claim's asymmetry, and the seventh rule, came "from an act absent from the registered record" (S0124 L99, in meaning). The review counts this as the fourth occurrence of input "reaching a governed decision from outside the governed channel" and recommends registering it "so the artifact's basis is readable from the record alone" (S0124 L100, in meaning). *Int (§9.13 D):* D's rule that a citation must name an earlier entry present in the record is this recommendation for acts. *Labelled at review (W-9):* "a basis not on record is not a basis" is D's convention, not the source's reading. The sources call the case "a registration gap, not an authenticity one" (S0125 L12, in meaning).
- *Source defect (S0122, found by S0124):* "four of them are classified SUPPORTING", while the table has three (S0124 L92, in meaning). Not relied on.

## 8.16.3 Open questions

- Should a kernel summary that shows only the current owner carry a mark when the earlier holder's work was not completed (C1)?
- What kind of act is a receipt's "claim": an assertion, a boundary, or evidence associated with a claim boundary (C4)? Is the acknowledgement element met trivially by the receipt itself (§8.15 C10, qualification 2)?
- Does the book's required recorder field (I15) invite the identity inference that S0122 avoids by forbidding the field (C5)?
- Semantic owner versus invariant owner of a field: one role or two (C3)?
- *Coverage added at review (not yet theory):*

## 8.16.4 What does not hold

*Version 1, refuted at review C23:*
- **CON-0227's "four parts" of ownership** (C3). Semantic and invariant coincide on every listed field, and sufficiency fails on the delivery timestamp (W-1, W-2). Restated as CON-0230.
- **"Settles §8.15 C10's qualification", with "world fact" read into the decision** (C4). It is the producer's gloss, and the authority's wording is weaker; only qualification 1 is answered, as intent (W-3, W-4).
- **The required version as a referent gap** (C3 (b)). The referent exists; what is unbuilt is the supersession trigger (W-6).
- **The §8.15 C2 correction's residual** (C2). The release was legitimate and recorded by Governance, so the case is not evidence against the rival rule (W-7).
- **C5's "analogue"** (the recorder). It is an inverted strategy (W-5).
- **The S0125 skim line.** The skim lost theory (§3).
