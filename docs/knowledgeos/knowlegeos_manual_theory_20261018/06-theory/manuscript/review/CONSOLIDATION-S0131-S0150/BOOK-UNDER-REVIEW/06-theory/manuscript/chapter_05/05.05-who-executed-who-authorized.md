# 5.5 Who executed and who authorized: the record of a decision

## 5.5.1 In plain language

When an AI carries out a decision at a person's direction, the record says who ran the command, not who decided. The corpus meets this first as an accident in a log file, and then as a design rule: a record of authority must point at a human act, and it must never be the act itself.

## 5.5.2 Definitions (Src)

- **The actor provenance gap.** "the decision record's `actor` stamps the git user, but the decider was the AI at the user's direction — *when AI participates in decisions, the actor model needs to distinguish who-executed from who-authorized*" [S0044 L42]. `[CON-0109 · invalid as stated]` (valid core: executor ≠ authorizer, C1, `[CON-0165 · provisional]`)
- **A grant points at a human act.** "a grant registers a recorded human act by reference — the record never manufactures authority" [S0129 L147].
- **The chain of roles.** "Architecture proposal → Principal Architect recommendation → PO/ARB decision → Governance registration" [S0129 L94]; "Registration is not decision." [S0129 L19]
- **The chain of stages.** Knowledge → Determination → Decision → Authorization → Action [S2834 L906–916]: "An authorized decision can therefore be checked independently from the epistemic reasoning that produced it." [S2834 L918]

## 5.5.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. RD-6 is the first observed instance of Ch. 1's residual trust assumption (Src + Int).** `[CON-0165 · provisional]` (the valid core of CON-0109)
- **The law already exists.** Ch. 1 §1.12 names the "residual trust assumption": that the record "was written by the deciding person and not by an assistant on their behalf". §1.13.2 (point 4) finds the holder and "how the act arrived" mixed in one free-text field. §1.14.3 states recorder ≠ decider and producer ≠ verifier.
- **What S0044 adds** is not the law but its first observed instance [S0044 L42]. The record's one `actor` field holds the git user, a stamp of the account in whose name the command ran (Src). That the user's direction authorized the decision is an inference from "at the user's direction" (Int). The AI chose and executed (Src).
- **Two roles, as the source says:** "who-executed from who-authorized" [S0044 L42]. "Decide" is a homonym here. In the sense of choosing or computing an answer it belongs to execution or recommendation. In the sense of the act that confers standing it belongs to the authorizer, and under A1 (Ch. 1 §1.6, §1.14.3) only a person performs it.
- **What the record shows: neither the executor nor the authorization.** The `actor` field is a git stamp; it names neither who executed (the AI) nor an act of authorization. The attribution survives only in free text [S0044 L42]. So A1 cannot be shown from the record either: whether a person authorized is not recorded as an act. This agrees with C2: a record does not attest an act it does not reference.
- **The recorder** is a third role, already law-bound by §1.14.3.

**C2. A record cannot attest its recorder's own act as an outside act (Ded, with its premise; an instance of Ch. 1 §1.13.1).**
- S0129: "If one process both decides and registers, the `humanActRef` points at its own output." [S0129 L149]. Ch. 1 §1.13.1 already states that a grant points at an external human act.
- **Premise, stated:** the recorder is a non-human process, which holds no standing (§1.13.1; S0129's first ground [S0129 L14]). Without the premise there is a counterexample: a person who records their own decision attests authority.
- **Deduction:** a reference that resolves to the recorder's own output says nothing about an act outside the recorder. Given the premise, such a record attests no authority.
- **Two laws, kept apart:**
  - recorder ≠ decider: about self-reference;
  - producer ≠ verifier (S0129: "The producing process cannot later approve its own ADR" [S0129 L15]): about independence of judgement.

  Both are §1.14.3's.
- **Invariant owner:** the workflow engine refuses a grant whose `humanActRef` is **absent** [S0129 L147]. Nothing mechanical detects a reference that points at the recorder's own output. In S0129 that was prevented by the process's own restraint. So the mechanism guarantees presence; externality is held by discipline, not enforced (CON-0102).
- *Limit:* the process identity is "self-declared, **not attestable**" [S0129 L4]. This is S0014's "real as PROCESS, unevidenced as PEOPLE" (Ch. 1 §1.14.3).
- **Code-verified evidence (S0055):** four gaps in the workflow engine, upgraded from declared to code-verified: "dual-ACTIVE possible · second START transfers ownership unguarded · no CLOSE vocabulary · `recordedBy` an unvalidated free string on REGISTER/HANDOFF/START" [S0055 L75]. The last is the recorder-identity gap of this claim, observed in the mechanism: who recorded an act is a free string. These are invariants stated, not held (CON-0102).

**C3. An ambiguous act is registered at its weaker reading (Src; Int).**
- A message said "I would select Option B′" and then asked for the selection to be made. The registrar declines to choose: "choosing would be deciding by interpretation." It registers a position, "the weaker and safer of the two" [S0129 L34].
- The author later confirmed: "my previous message was a recommendation, not a PO/ARB decision" [S0129 L87].
- **Int:** under A1, standing comes only from an act. If whether an act occurred depends on reading its text, the reading that creates no standing is the one that cannot violate A1.
- **Its must-do half:** a registrar that never registers also satisfies this rule. So it needs an obligation: an act whose occurrence is in doubt is returned to its author for confirmation. S0129 did so [S0129 L72–73], and a blank field is not treated as a decision [S0129 L160].

**C4. Determination, decision and authorization are separate stages (Src; Int).**
- S2834 separates Determine(p) from Decide(a | p) (formulas, in meaning; S2834 L886–890) and decision from authorization [S2834 L906–918], and asks "Who authorized the action?" as a separate question [S2834 L1871].
- Determination is relative to a contract, not truth: the bridge Determine(p | C) ⇒ True(p) "can be explicitly represented. Otherwise it cannot be assumed." [S2834 L323–329]
- **Int, for Ch. 5:** a gap (§5.4) blocks a determination; a decision needs its own rules; an authorization is an act (Ch. 1). *Unverified:* this places §5.4's "Zero detects; Lord acts" boundary between the first and the others, and §5.4 is PROVISIONAL. S2834's separations are proposals, not results.

**C5. A duplicated decision is an invariant question, not a bug to patch (Src).**
- "A recommendation CAN currently be decided twice" [S0044 L43], after an interrupted session re-ran a command. Exactly-once or last-wins is "a domain decision", so: "do not patch the guard before the semantics are decided" [S0044 L43].
- **Int:** this is the invariant-owner question (who guarantees "one decision per recommendation"?) met in practice before it was posed. It also broke a count: "decided=2 · accepted=2 for ONE unique recommendation" [S0044 L43], a failure of the counting unit (CON-0070). The same act may also be a legitimate re-decision, which is an identity question (S0044's RD-4: definition, instance, revision).

**C6. An authorization check that ignores its subject cannot enforce a rule about subjects (Src; Ded; reviewed in C11/T3).** `[CON-0131 · validated]` (where the check is the only enforcement point, and the caller supplies the scope)
- **The source (S0058, 2026-08-17, one work item; see Ch. 8 §8.6 for lineage):** a probe of the workflow engine's `authorized` command returns true for the verification lane and also for "COMPLETED architecture lane" under the same verification scope [S0058 L82–83]. "**`authorized` never consults the session.** It answers *"does any AUTHORIZED grant carry exactly this scope string"* — not *"is this session authorized for this scope."* The `--session` argument is checked for existence and then **ignored**." [S0058 L86]. The finding was "carried there as a finding, decided nowhere" [S0058 L88].
- **Definition:** a check f(s, x) of subject s and scope x is **subject-blind** if f(s, x) = f(s′, x) for all existing subjects s, s′.
- **Ded (proof).** Take a rule that requires some subjects to be refused where others are allowed for the same scope. Producer ≠ verifier is such a rule: the producer of a correction is refused the verification grant of that correction, and a third process is allowed it. A subject-blind check gives both the same answer, so it cannot be the check that enforces that rule. ∎
- **Hidden premise:** the caller chooses the scope x. A check whose scope were bound to the subject elsewhere could still separate subjects.
- **Trivial-case test:** with exactly one subject, every check is subject-blind and nothing is lost. The defect appears only from two subjects onwards, which is the case of any role law.
- **Int:**
  - **an instance of Ch. 1 §1.14.1** ("operability is not authorization"), one step further: here the check that answers "authorized" does not ask about the actor at all;
  - **of CON-0102** (an invariant stated, not held): R-34's producer/verifier bar is stated in the grant, and the mechanism's check does not hold it. The bar is held by the processes' restraint, as in C2;
  - **of Ch. 8 §8.6 C3:** the record cannot show the verifier's independence, and here the engine's check cannot refuse its absence either.
- **Scope:** the proof covers any subject-blind check. That this engine is subject-blind rests on one probe in one source and was not re-run here. A later source of the same day re-measured it at source, but that was not done independently (Ch. 6 §6.5).
- **A competing reading (S0067, Trial T3):** the same behaviour is "boundary-conforming": the record context is not meant to evaluate policy at all. Both readings accept the proof; they differ on whose gap it is (Ch. 6 §6.5 C3).
