## 8.89.1 In plain language

1. **The first adviser concludes that the kernel is a "transformation boundary" where candidates become knowledge; the reply says that word swallows a whole architecture, and calls the kernel a "constitutional membrane" at which a candidate *may* become knowledge.** In the book a candidate becomes canon only by a recorded human act, whose effect the record constitutes and the kernel evaluates; the kernel itself transforms nothing. The reply's wording is the closer one. *(Restated at review: W-03)*
2. **The first adviser's central invariant is that identity, evidence, justification, state, confidence and history must stay coherent; the reply asks what must change atomically.** The book meets one part of it: the grade shown for a claim is derived from the record (and stated inputs) each time, so it cannot drift from the record; that is the reply's own "projection" option. The rest is open or absent in the book: identity against evidence is open, and the book has no concept of confidence or of justification. *(Restated at review: W-01, W-05)*
3. **The first adviser's removal test marks every domain capability "essential"; the reply asks for "the minimum responsibility required to prevent an illegitimate epistemic transition".** A kernel that refuses everything prevents every illegitimate transition, so this question needs what must be possible as well as what must be prevented. The reply's own derivation chain presupposes such a requirement without stating it. *(Restated at review: W-07, W-08)*
4. **The reply says the kernel cannot establish the truth of what it receives.** That agrees with the book: the kernel decides standing, not truth, and whether a recorded act really occurred stays an assumption about the world. *(Restated at review: W-09)*
5. **The reply lists distinctions the kernel must not collapse.** Several are already in the book: candidate and knowledge; absence and unknown; unknown and false; supersession and deletion; history and current state. Evidence and justification have no counterpart, because the book has no justification concept. *(Restated at review: W-10)*
