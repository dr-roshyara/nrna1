## 8.75.1 In plain language

1. **The PO/ARB commissioned a tool that, after a human says "Yes", sets up the next actor's lane automatically, including the record that a human started the work.** That is what the PO/ARB had already directed for the verifier, and what the verifier did by hand: one human decision, recorded once as the appointment and once as the START. Earlier processes refused to write such a START themselves. The commission forbids treating an AI message as a human act, and it fixes where identities come from; it does not say where the human's "Yes" is recorded, other than by the tool that acts on it.
2. **The commission's distinctions agree with the book.** It separates recommendation from appointment, appointment from activation, and activation from completion of the work. It asks the tool to "verify independence", which the record can only fail to refute.
3. **The commission and the refusals agree that a lane was missing.** The commission's answer is to let the tool record the lane and the human START once the human has decided. That moves the open question into the tool's interface for the human decision.
4. **A process refused to do by hand exactly what the tool would do, and was told it was "Governance Architecture", the tool's own name.** It refused because the appointment existed only in its prompt. Its standard is two records: first the PO/ARB's appointment, then the START that transcribes it. The open question is who witnesses the first record; that an act occurred is, in the book, an assumption about the world, not something the record can prove.
5. **The precedent it holds up met the standard with the beneficiary as the only witness.** For the verifier's lane, the appointment and the START were both written by the verifier. The refusing process had itself handled the same work item before, which it does not mention.
