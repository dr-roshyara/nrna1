## 8.97.1 In plain language

1. **The adviser says the research is done and the next step is to define the kernel's boundary, from nine responsibilities, by asking what must change together; the kernel is "not a generic" rules evaluator.** Within the book's record the method agrees. The book's kernel does evaluate fixed rules over one record, so the claim that it is not a rules evaluator holds only in the sense that its rules are specific and decided outside it. Of the nine responsibilities, the book has counterparts for some and none for others (justification, confidence, assigning an item's identifier).
2. **The prompt repeats a test the book has already recorded: a subagent reports its parent's session identifier, so the estate counts it as the same session and bars it.** This is the same recorder's probe again, not a new finding. A shared identifier is enough to refute independence; a different one would not be enough to establish it. *(Restated at review: W-01, W-09)*
3. **The candidate passes every listed bar and declares its independence undetermined, because it had looked at the work item and advised on it before being asked, and no policy says whether that counts.** The outcome agrees with the book's result that independence on the record can be refuted but never established. But the candidate expects a genuinely new session to return "PASS", which that result rules out: at most it is not refuted. Whether looking and advising count as participation stays the PO/ARB's question; the book records both readings of "participation" and does not settle it. *(Restated at review: W-02, W-05)*
4. **Both records say a process may declare its own identity but never choose its own role, which comes from "the commission".** That agrees with the book for identity. But "the commission" here is a mechanism's output, which names a different role; by the prompt's own rule that mismatch should stop the process, and neither record applies it. *(Restated at review: W-03)*
5. **A later notice corrects the prompt by adding to it, and a human continuation reopened the work item, after which the new binding mechanism refuses.** The first agrees with the book; the second answers a point the book had left open. "Verification ≠ adoption" agrees with the book. *(Restated at review: W-04, W-11)*
