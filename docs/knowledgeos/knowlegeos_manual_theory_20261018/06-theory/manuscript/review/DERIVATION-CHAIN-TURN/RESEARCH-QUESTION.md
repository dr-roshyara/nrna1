## 1. The question as the commission states it

> **From which object are the laws derived — primitive concepts, entry kinds and their fields, mechanisms, or Req — and does one law follow end to end from it?**

**Deliverable:**
1. **The unit of derivation**, chosen by stated criteria and argued against the other three; the homonym "primitive" split into named senses, each with its test.
2. **One law derived end to end** from that unit, with premises and a falsifier.
3. **Every law of §9.11 marked** *forced by Req* · *compatible with Req* · *independent of it*; each "independent" law classified as an application rule or a world assumption (the K/L split of §9.15 is the precedent).
4. What this changes for deliverable 1 (the primitives) and for primitive minimality (T9-03), **as consequences**.

**Limits:** no new primitive fixed; the primitive set not finalised; primitive minimality not claimed from mechanism minimality (T9-03 kept apart). If the laws do **not** derive from any candidate unit, that is a result, reported as such.

## 3. Criteria for the unit of derivation (committed now, before any argument)

A candidate unit qualifies only if it meets all four:
- **(K1) Defined.** The book defines it precisely and the definition is decided, not open.
- **(K2) Expressive.** The terms of the laws (§9.11) can be stated over it, or it says which cannot.
- **(K3) Behaviour-relevant.** Two systems that agree on it agree on what the laws are about; the unit is not inert in the sense of §9.21.3 (1).
- **(K4) Falsifiable.** A derivation from it can fail on an exhibited record or run.

**The four candidates, stated before any test (not preferences):**
- **(U-c) the primitive concepts** of RESEARCH-PURPOSE deliverable 1 (identity, lifecycle, authority, evidence, relationships, policy, history; BOOK-OUTLINE §1);
- **(U-k) the entry kinds and their fields,** with D's and W's clauses (§9.13.2; §9.18.4; §9.20);
- **(U-m) the mechanisms** (a pool over 𝒮, §9.19.4);
- **(U-r) Req** (§9.10; §9.19.3).

The answer may also be a combination, for example (U-k) for the state laws and (U-r) for the observer laws, if that is what the criteria show.

## 4. What would disprove each answer (committed now)

- **DC-1 (against the chosen unit).** A law of §9.11 whose terms cannot be stated over the chosen unit, and that is not marked as such.
- **DC-2 (against (U-c)).** If (U-c) is chosen, a candidate primitive whose definition is open (BOOK-OUTLINE §1 "~" or "—") but which is used as decided.
- **DC-3 (against (U-m)).** Two pools with the same step relation that give different laws. §9.21.3 (1) predicts none, so (U-m) would fail K3.
- **DC-4 (against the end-to-end law).** A record in D (or a run of C6) on which the derived law's conclusion fails while every stated premise holds, or a step that uses an open fork or an unstated premise.
- **DC-5 (against "forced by Req").** A system meeting Req (its references on every valid state, O1–O4 as EF) in which the law fails.
- **DC-6 (against "independent").** A law marked independent that follows from Req and the decided definitions.
- **DC-7 (against the application/world classification).** A law classed as a world assumption that the record could check, or as an application rule that a kernel reference definition reads.
- **DC-8 (the trivial case).** A derivation that also holds for an empty Req or an empty set of kinds says nothing about KnowledgeOS, unless it is labelled as such.

## 6. What a model may and may not do here

A finite model, if used, tests the **form** (for example, that a law holds on every record in a bounded family). It states its premises, bound and limits, and proves no general claim.
