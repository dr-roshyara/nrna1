## 8.35.1 In plain language

1. **"No knowledge state may ever be final"** holds in the book for items: every adoption can be revoked or annulled. The only final acts are roots, which are origins of authority, not items. Two things the sources ask for have no home in the book: replacing a statement by a newer one (supersession), and terminal states such as a final judgment.
2. **"When supporting knowledge changes, dependent decisions must be re-evaluated."** The book does this, under a rule the Principal has reserved, for **authority**: an adoption loses authority when a grant it cites ends. It is not yet done through delegated grants. It is deliberately not done for **evidence**: evidence changes no standing (a proposed clause). Dependence of one item on another, and of an action on knowledge, is not modelled.
3. **"A derived conclusion must not be treated as an observation."** For the kernel's own answers, the book agrees: they are derived and never recorded as facts. For **content**, the book does not yet tell an observation from an inference. It has the same gap the sources name.
4. **Contradiction has two senses.** Two conflicting adoptions of content both stay in force; the conflict is resolved where the knowledge is used, and it is reported only when both are rules. A conflict in the authority record itself gives "undetermined" with its reason. Both refuse circular support; the book's rule is stricter.
5. **The chat moved its position after the human stated a purpose.** It first put the reasoning engines outside KnowledgeOS, then inside it. Much of the move is in what the word "KnowledgeOS" names: the whole system or the kernel. Then one reply called the framework "architecturally complete … with total confidence", and the next one rejected that.
6. **The chat proposes a different central question.** It asks how a system moves closer to justified truth, or what corruptions it must make impossible. The book asks whether knowledge is valid, owned and trusted. The difference is recorded for the Principal.
7. **Lists marked "Established" still have no measurement behind them, and one text appears in the corpus twice.**

## 8.35.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Revision keeps history; for items nothing is final; replacement and terminal states have no home (Src; Ded against §9.13; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-01, W-08.)*
- **S0217 (B):** "H-KOS-Revisability-001: No knowledge state SHALL be irrevocably finalized. The kernel MUST provide a governed path for revision (retraction, replacement, or refinement) for every knowledge object" (L368–374).
- **S0217 (C)** weakens it: "is slightly too strong … Some domains have terminal states: historical fact after archival closure, legal judgment after final appeal, mathematical proof". It restates it as "preserve the possibility of epistemic revision while preserving historical identity and provenance of previous states", with "Old state remains historically true: 'I was believed at time T'" (L570–607).
- **Ded, against the book:**
  - **Items:** Q1's subject is an item k (§9.10 Q1). Every adoption can be revoked or annulled by a recorded act. For items, (B)'s "never final" **holds** in the book.
  - **Roots:** a root act cannot be annulled; a root is T by stipulation (§9.13 Θ_f ρ; model check 16). A root is an origin of authority (actor, scope, act time), not an item. It falsifies (B) only if a root counts as a "knowledge state", which (B) does not say.
  - **Replacement:** (B)'s "replacement" path is supersession, which D does not admit (§9.13 Θ_f σ; defect 5). A later adoption does not displace an earlier one (Θ_f, C6 row). *A gap in the book, not an agreement.*
  - **Terminal states:** (C)'s examples (a final appeal, a proof) have no counterpart. An item's standing stays revocable.
  - **History:** an annulment voids its target from the start but stays recorded, and a removal keeps every field D requires (§9.13.2). "I was believed at time T" corresponds to Q1 at an earlier cut (§9.10 Q1), with two qualifiers:
    - (a) Q1 answers current authority, "not the truth of k's content", and not belief;
    - (b) it is stable unless a recorded loss has since removed an entry, and a tombstone drops content (§9.13.2). (C)'s "preserving previous states" holds for identity and D's fields, not for content.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. Authority is coupled to its grants under a reserved rule; non-coupling to evidence rests on a proposed clause; item-to-item and knowledge-to-action dependency are not modelled (Src; Ded against §9.13, §9.18).** *(Restated at review: W-04.)*
- **The sources:** H-KOS-Decision-001: "When a knowledge state changes, its associated decisions SHALL be flagged for re-evaluation, ensuring that actions do not outlive their justification" (S0217 L358–364). Restated: "Changes to supporting knowledge SHALL trigger epistemic re-evaluation of dependent decisions" (L516–528).
- **Ded:**
  - **Authority:** under Θ_f's P, an adoption's authority lapses when any cited grant's period ends, or an effective ending of it comes after the adoption (§9.13 Θ_f P, conjunctive over the cited grants). CE* recomputes it at every cut. **P is reserved to the Principal**; its rival is "some basis that authorizes the adoption is intact". An ending of a parent grant does **not** reach acts under a child grant (defect 2, open).
  - **Evidence:** an evidence entry changes no standing value (§9.18 (c1)); no standing entry takes an evidence entry as a basis, a new clause of D_S, **proposed** at settlement (c2) *(adopted with the evidence kinds, T9-19; kept, with no guard on adoption, T9-20; 2026-10-31; consolidation S0253–S0277, C-06)*. Check 33 shows equal values on a sample (empirically). Promise (b) is decided.
  - **Decisions:** the sources' "decisions" are downstream actions. The book places acting agents outside KnowledgeOS (§10.2). Its coupling is grant → adoption, not knowledge → decision.
- **Int:** the sources' "supporting knowledge" has three readings: grants, which **authorise**; evidence, which **supports content**; and **premise items** (S0217 L668). The book models the first, keeps the second apart, and does not model the third: bases are grants or roots only (Θ_f χ). Under (b), a re-evaluation driven by evidence can only be a **display** beside standing (PL-14).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. The kernel's own answers are derived and never recorded; for content, the book does not tell observation from inference (Src; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-03.)*
- **The sources:**
  - "Derived conclusions SHALL remain distinguishable from authoritative knowledge" (S0216 L359–363);
  - "Observation ≠ Inference": "Model generated statement treated as observation — epistemic corruption", and "Derived knowledge SHALL preserve the reasoning path and premises" (S0217 L615–668);
  - "the kernel currently treats all evidence uniformly" (S0217 L381);
  - S0215's "Derived belief treated as core belief" forbidden (L301).
- **The book, on its own answers:** CE*'s answers are derived from the record and are never entries. A stored derived value has no standing (CON-0141, provisional). *Agreement.* T and F answers publish their support (the acts they rest on; §9.10 Q1); the *reasons* for T and F are not yet defined (§9.13 L166); the reasons for U are given (§9.13.3).
- **The book, on content:** an evidence entry has polarity and producer, but no observed/inferred field (§9.18); the class vocabulary is open (G7, §10.2). Derivation links between items are not admitted to D ("link", CON-0188). So S0217's "treats all evidence uniformly" is **true of the book**. *The book has the gap the sources name; it has not answered it.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Contradiction of content coexists and is resolved at use; contradiction in the authority record gives U; acyclicity; context as scope (Src; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-02.)*
- **S0215** gives the truth-maintenance task as keeping conclusions "not known to be contradictory" with "no belief is kept without a reason" (L40). - **The book:**
  - **(i-a) Content:** two conflicting adoptions both stay current: "an earlier adoption stays current unless revoked; the conflict itself is Q5's matter" (§9.13 Θ_f, C6 row). Contradictions coexist with a visible status and are resolved **at consumption** (I-4; §2.05). *(Narrowed at C43, W-07:)* Q5 reports conflicts only among rules written in L (§9.10 Q5), and no relation between items is admitted (CON-0188); a conflict between two non-rule items is neither recorded nor reported. The C6 row's "Q5's matter" covers rule items only (noted for the Ch. 9 rotation).
  - **(i-b) Authority:** a contradiction in the authority record (an annulment cycle, a tie) gives **U with its reason** (§9.15 G6; §9.13 R2).
  - In both senses, "without resolution" is a normal state, not a violation. For evidence, g0 is refutation-first, and "contested" is T9-17's open rival; S0237 and S0888 favour preserving conflict (§9.18). *(Since decided: T9-17 (b), 2026-10-29: "contested" is now the class, g1, §9.18.5.)*
  - **(ii) Acyclicity:** the book's D allows citations of **earlier entries only** (§9.13.2), which implies acyclicity. The Principal chose it over D_acyclic, the rival with the same guarantee. *Agreement; the book's rule is stricter.* This holds for the authority citation graph; a content justification graph is not in the record (C3).
  - **(iii) Context:** S0216 (A)'s "cognitive worlds": "Contradiction is not always corruption. Sometimes it is context separation" (L318–338). The book's form of context for standing is the **queried scope**: Q1 answers per scope x, through covers (§9.13 Θ_f ω). Two adoptions under different scopes do not conflict for either scope's question. *Limits:* a revocation's scope is not read (it revokes for every queried scope; whole-or-nothing; Θ_f), and S0215's worlds are provenance bundles, not scopes.
- **Not a new concept.**
