## 8.56.1 In plain language

1. **The word-order record proposes a "Semantic Normal Form": different sentences are parsed into one stored meaning structure, so "two different sentences → same knowledge object; two similar sentences → different objects".** Which item a sentence is about is then settled by a parser. Read as a proposal that a governed act then confirms, it agrees with the book only in form, since the book leaves open whether registering a sentence under an existing item is itself an act about sameness. Read as identity decided by the parser, or by the verification gate the record puts before the knowledge object, an engine settles identity with no act. Whether equal normal forms are a match of representations (which the Constitution forbids as a ground of identity) or of meaning (then fixed only by the parser's rule) is not said. "Expressions become views" agrees with the book only if the original expressions are kept as the source.
2. **The Character Definition grows by one section per record and restates many of the records' verdicts.** It says of itself "nothing adopted". Its Constitution line moves from "PROPOSED, pending HPA ratification", through a quoted ratification, "PROPOSED" again, and "the character's text stands PROPOSED pending ratification review", to "RATIFIED" from §43 on. The ratification is shown only in prose, as §8.43 found.
3. **The document states the kernel–engine split in so many words: "The Kernel does not *do* the reasoning."** That agrees with the book's kernel. Its canonical one-sentence character ("head 43"), which calls KnowledgeOS "a constitutional epistemic operating system", is the earlier synthesis's final definition word for word, already in the book's comparison; the new occurrence is the document's heading. Its "begins from Unknown" holds for evidence; for content the kernel holds no value; for authority, an item no act recognises is "not recognized", not unknown.
4. **One of its rules says "A conclusion without an explicit inference relation should remain a hypothesis".** It restates a rule the book read earlier, with three readings: a status computed from the warrant (which breaks the rule that only an act confers standing), a guard on adoption (decided against by the Principal), or a label of content (which agrees).
5. **Several points agree with the book:** a change of evidence is not a change of authority; revision is not deletion (with the tombstone's qualification); knowledge informs decision and action but does not act; agency is traced, not bound. "Unknown ≠ absent ≠ false" agrees for evidence; for authority the book's "not recognized" is closed-world.
