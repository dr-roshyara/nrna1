## 8.55.1 In plain language

1. **The protocol was tested on an invented scenario ("controlled, synthetic", "no migration state touched"), and the product owner then accepted the test: "the mechanism solved the intended coordination problem".** The acceptance was again registered by the protocol's own producer, with the producer bar disclosed. The registration says the result was "demonstrated on the DV-correction chain"; the test record says the scenario was artificial. And in the same window the test's own named next step skipped a stage: it sent the chain to the Governance review although the independent review had sent it first to Architecture for repairs; the Governance review found that gap and sent it back. Whether that is the handoff failing or the mechanism working (the gap was caught) is open. The test record also says the protocol was "integrated into agent templates", a statement of the producer's, without the Governance acceptance the gate named (§8.52 C4).
2. **The Governance bounded review does what the book asks of a reader of authority:** it "folds the record, not accepting from the prompt", declares that it decides and accepts nothing, and states that its identity as the grant's designee cannot be shown. It finds that a review document survives while the authority record does not; whether that file is outside the designated record depends on which carrier was designated.
3. **The first grammar record admits a fifth outside source "by the HPA's commission"; the second cites four new web sources and says "no new external source enters, no admission act", because it deepens a family already admitted.** Whether new material inside an admitted family needs an act depends on reading "source" as a family or as a text; the closure's words, the line's own earlier admission of a cited web source, and its "single-source" admissions all fit the second. The count "fifth" leaves out three outside sources entered without a counted admission (§8.54 C1). The classification is the recorder's.
4. **One record says "the kernel stores the semantic roots and rules, not the generated expressions"; elsewhere in the same record "none enter the kernel"; the next record says the grammar is "the internal language of the engine, never a kernel law".** The first record uses "kernel" in the narrow sense throughout, so the tension is inside it. If a root is an identity origin, as its own table says, it bears on item identity, which the book holds open. Both records cite context to Article 2, which is about dimensions; context is Article 1.4. A rule-based "conflict resolution" when composing meanings meets the Constitution's "conflicting knowledge coexists as CONFLICTED until governed resolution"; the book records no conflict between such items at all.
5. **Several points agree with the book:** a token is not its meaning; one word is not one meaning; a rule is applied with situation and judgment, which adds no exception to the kernel if the judgment is a recorded act. The "case grammar" of relations is a vocabulary for relations between items, which the book requires but has not admitted; the two records map the cases differently.
