# 3.10 Knowledge spaces: nested or adjacent? (fork F8)

*Written under Trial T2 (topic first, 2026-10-19) and revised after its independent review (`review/T2-F8-spaces/`).*
- *New sources:* S0577, S0357, S0651, S2221 and S2838, chosen by a fixed rule from the unread search hits.
- *Already-read sources used:* S0005, S0008, S0009, S0012, S0013, S0021, S0022, S0031, S0033, S0034 and S0035.
- *F8 is open.* The review **narrowed** it (C6); no verdict settled it. All new concepts are provisional unless marked otherwise.

## 3.10.1 In plain language

Fork F8 asks whether one knowledge space can sit **inside** another, or whether spaces only sit **side by side**. Two concrete cases:
- does the engineering knowledge platform (EKP) contain a product's knowledge space (PKS)?
- is a customer deployment a space within its product's space?

The question has three parts that must be fixed before it can be answered:
1. **which "knowledge space" is meant.** The phrase has several senses, and even the container sense has several readings.
2. **what "inside" means.** There are at least five criteria: membership, authority, type, instantiation and ownership. They can give different answers for the same pair. So can "side by side".
3. **whether the question is about one pair, or about the model.** F8 asks two things:
   - **F8-instance:** is *this* pair nested?
   - **F8-type:** may the model contain space-in-space relations at all, and with what properties?

## 3.10.2 Definitions

**Senses of "knowledge space" (Src).** `[CON-0098 · provisional]` *(replaces CON-0096, INVALID: its container sense was defined by a quotation that already contained F8's answer.)*
*Scope: the uses in the sources named above. Not shown to be exhaustive. S0651 lists six possible readings of the phrase "infinite Knowledge Space" (S0651 L71–82).*

| Sense | What it is | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **S0, coordinate space** | the space NATURE × REPRESENTATION × GOVERNANCE-STATUS (× AUTHORITY-SCOPE) in which every claim has a position; a **PKS can then be read (Int) as a region** of it, the claims whose canon scope is one product (GKC L10–14; PKSM L4, L25; Ch. 0 §0.3) | GKC, PKSM (reviewer-checked; outside the allowed list for this book, so attribution is U) |
| **S1, container** | a set of artifacts with a declared boundary, an owner and a governing constitution (S0008 L37, **without** its added clause "spaces NEST", which is S0008's claim about S1, C2) | S0008, S0012, S0013, S0021; the book's CON-0011 |
| **S2, epistemic space** | the space of possible knowledge that a knower observes only in part; observation is a view of it, O_t ⊆ 𝒦 (S2221 L159). **Its bound is disputed:** unbounded, bounded by purpose (S0577 L228), "open-ended relative to the current representation" (S2838 L568–575), or finite (S3) | S0577 §9, S2221 |
| **S3, KST knowledge space** | a family 𝒦 of knowledge states K ⊆ Q, closed under union, containing ∅ and Q: "a real mathematical definition" [S0651 L65] (S0651 L51–60) | S0651 |

- **S1 has several readings.** §6.3 C7 records five readings of a knowledge space on one day:
  1. a domain containing contexts;
  2. a context type;
  3. a generated artifact;
  4. an authored space;
  5. a projection.

  They differ in kind. The S1 definition above fits readings 1 and 4 at most. An F8-instance question must name its reading. S0035's reading 1 has the most standing, since it cites a ruling (§6.3 C7).
- **Polysemy, with a limit on the evidence.** S0577 shows that "Knowledge Space" changed meaning inside its own corpus ("same term, different concept" [S0577 L151]). That corpus is the `brainstorming/` folder only (S0577 L6–7). So S0577 supports polysemy in general, not specifically S1 ≠ S2. Its claim about the phrase's first appearance does not reach the 08-02 S1 sources.
- **S2 and S3 are partly one line.** S0651 proposes replacing "everything knowable" by "the space of admissible epistemic states" (H-KST-1, S0651 L770–785), and S2221 reuses its notation 𝒦 and K_t. But S2221 uses 𝒦 both as a set containing observations (L159) and as a set whose elements are items (L196). Part of the S2/S3 difference is therefore a notation slip, not two concepts.
- **In S1, `scope.include` does two jobs.** It is the space's **membership boundary**: a space is "defined by a declared boundary (`scope.include`), not by a folder" (S0016 L30). It is also the **coverage** of the knowledge graph's checker: the "13% coverage is a declared boundary", and extending it "would amend a declared scope" (S0020 L34–35). By Ch. 2 §2.8.1 these are two scope concepts, subject scope (what the space is about) and evaluation scope (what the checker reached), and an absence report must state the second. This book keeps them apart: **boundary(A)** for membership, **coverage(A)** for what a check examined.
- **F8 per sense (Int).** "Are spaces nested or adjacent?" has a different form in each sense:
  - **S0:** regions of one coordinate space nest by inclusion trivially; the question becomes whether a PKS's region is defined by canon scope alone;
  - **S1:** nesting by **membership** (boundary(A) ⊆ boundary(B)) or by **authority** per subject (the criteria below); the H-3 pairs are asked here (C1);
  - **S2:** a local view of a larger whole (C1); nesting is the relation between a view and what it views, not between two containers;
  - **S3:** restriction to a sub-domain Q′ ⊂ Q (C1).
- **"PKS" may be a homonym.** S0357 places "PKS" among supporting **systems** beside the kernel, together with EKS and the AI platform (S0357 L356–372). It never expands the acronym, so reading it as "product knowledge space" is not verified.

**Criteria for one space being inside another (Ded, a proposal of this book).** `[CON-0097 · provisional]`
*Scope: S1 spaces. "Adjacent" is defined for each criterion.*

| Criterion | *A* is inside *B* when | "Adjacent" then means | The case outside both |
|---|---|---|---|
| **membership** | every artifact of *A* is within *B*'s **declared** boundary, boundary(A) ⊆ boundary(B) (§3.3: a boundary is declared, not a folder); coverage plays no part | the boundaries are disjoint | **overlap**: they share some artifacts, and neither contains the other |
| **authority**, per subject | *A*'s constitution is subordinate to *B*'s **for a given subject**: policy, method or domain knowledge (Ch. 1 §1.11, three authority systems) | neither is subordinate for that subject | **partial subordination**: subordinate for one subject, not for another |
| **type** | *A* is an instance of the type that *B* holds (§6.3 C1) | not an instance | — |
| **instantiation** | *A* is the method of *B* applied to *A*'s binding: a PKS = M(β) plus evidence (§6.1) | no shared method | — |
| **ownership** | *A*'s owner answers to *B*'s owner | independent owners | — |

- **Membership and authority are logically independent (Ded).**
  - *Authority without membership:* a deployment's bindings can lie outside its product's declared scope while the product's rules govern them.
  - *Membership without authority:* one artifact identity can fall inside two declared scopes, and only one of them governs it.

## 3.10.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. The pairs posed by H-3 are S1 objects; S2 and S3 offer candidate formal models (Int; scope: the H-3 pairs).**
- The two pairs in the sources, EKP and PKS, and product and deployment, are containers in sense S1.
- **S2 and S3 are not irrelevant to F8.** They contain relations between wholes and parts that could serve as formal meanings of "inside":
  - **a local view in a larger whole:** a bounded context as "a local projection of a larger reality" (S2221 L699–729);
  - **a bounded projection of an unbounded space** (S0577 L90);
  - **several purpose-relative spaces**, if the space is bounded by purpose (S0577 L228);
  - **a "domain-specific state space"** (S0651 L82).

  In KST, restricting a space to a sub-domain Q′ ⊂ Q is a standard space-in-space relation. That is the reviewer's observation; the source does not state it.
- Within one S3 space, states are nested by inclusion, and a learning path is a chain K_0 → K_1 → … (S0651 L480–498). That is nesting of **states**, not of spaces.
- **A caution from S2 bears on evidence for adjacency (Src):** x ∉ O_t does not imply x ∉ 𝒦; "not observed" is not "does not exist" (S2221 L190–205). Not finding a containment edge is not evidence that there is none (§3.4).

**C3. The either/or holds only per pair and per criterion (Ded).** *Validated in review, in this scope: membership criterion, instance level, "adjacent" read as disjoint.*
- For a family of spaces, "nested" and "adjacent" do not exclude each other: some pairs can be nested and others disjoint.
- For a single pair under membership there are **three** cases: nested, disjoint or overlapping. The fork names only two.
- *Not established as a corpus fact:* that any two S1 spaces actually overlap. S0022's case depends on whether a deployment's declared boundary includes the product's base, which no source states.

**C4. Laminar families form forests (Ded; scope: membership containment).**
- A family is **laminar** if every pair is nested or disjoint. Every laminar family forms a forest under inclusion, because each member's supersets form a chain.
- **Overlap is necessary, not sufficient, for failing to be a forest.** A family fails to be a forest exactly when some member has two incomparable supersets.
- *Computed check:* *(No script or run record was kept; reproduced at the consolidation S0131–S0150 by reviewers A, B and the integration reviewer, 1,940 / 0 / 946. A script and its run are owed: B-35.)* over all 1,940 families of 1 to 4 non-empty subsets of a 4-element set, no laminar family fails to be a forest. 946 families are forests without being laminar. This is a finite check; the general statement is a standard set-theoretic fact.
- **Consequence for §3.8:** containment is a tree if the spaces are laminar. But membership containment on S1 spaces is only a **preorder**: two spaces with the same artifacts and different owners or constitutions contain each other. It is a partial order only if a space is identified with its artifact set, which S1's definition does not do.

**C5. Coexisting contradictions need not separate spaces (Src + Ded).**
- **Partition:** S0577 reports an early device, "Cognitive Worlds", "provenance-homogeneous claim bundles" [S0577 L107], which let contradictory claims coexist as separate contexts (S0577 L146–149). It was "Considered then abandoned" [S0577 L316].
- A partition is a **laminar** case: the blocks are disjoint and all lie in the whole. It is not a third relation beside nesting and adjacency.
- **Status labels:** S0357 lets A and ¬A, each with its evidence, coexist **inside one state**, marked by statuses such as CONFLICTED (S0357 L137–167).
- So contradiction does not force a split into spaces. Context-relative meaning (S0357 L115–133) is a further alternative.

**C6. EKP and PKS under the book's own results (Int, conditional; scope: the premises named).**
- **By membership: not nested,** *if* the EKP holds only transferable knowledge. Engineering knowledge is "rules, methods and decisions that transfer between products", while a PKS holds a product's bindings and "never contains reusable method" (I-7(RO)) (S0008 L23–24, as the book records it). So a PKS's bindings are not EKP artifacts.
- **By authority: not nested for the domain-knowledge subject,** because the product team holds that authority (Ch. 1 §1.11). Nesting **may** hold for the method subject. Whether method sits under policy is itself open (§1.11, T1-50).
- **By type: possibly nested**, if the platform holds the PKS type (§6.3 C1; the source's own later test weakens this to one observed instance).
- **By instantiation: related**, since the PKS applies platform method to a product binding (§6.1).
- **Product and deployment: open.** The question reduces to §6.3's instance key: is a space indexed by product or by deployment?
- **This narrows F8 for one pair and settles nothing.** Each conditional premise needs a reviewer's verdict.

**C7. "Projection" needs care (Src).**
- S2838 warns against calling every focused view a projection, since a projection is idempotent (π² = π). It proposes "inquiry-relative view transformation" [S2838 L715] until that property is shown (L705–725).
- S2221's O_t ⊆ 𝒦 (C1) uses "projection" without stating the property.
- S0577 records three senses of "projection" in the corpus (L240–246).

**C8. The Principal's later direction distinguishes three unbounded objects (Ext; role: gap-filler candidate; Hyp; PROVISIONAL).**
- *Scope, stated first:* EXT-029 (2026-09-27) is external direction, not evidence. It bears on the disputed bound of S2 (CON-0098, itself PROVISIONAL and not yet reviewed, so unverified) and does not settle it.
- **It separates "three different infinities" that "should not be conflated"** (EXT-029 L1230–1258):
  - the space of possible propositions;
  - the relational structure between them;
  - a space Ω of "possible complete interpretations/models of the world".

  Ω is a space of **world models**, not of knowledge states. So it is not S3, the family of admissible knowledge states (C1; H-KST-1, S0651 L770–785). *(Ded, from the definitions; step-8 correction.)*

  The bound dispute of S2 may be partly a conflation of a different pair, which the file names elsewhere: the **possible** vocabulary V_possible against the **relevant** one V_relevant, the denominator of coverage (EXT-029 L357–375). S2 would then be unbounded as V_possible and bounded by purpose as V_relevant (the two readings of CON-0098). *(Hyp.)*
- **It proposes that a finite knowledge state approximates an ideal one,** with stabilization tested as d(K_N, K_2N) → 0 (EXT-029 L1024–1055). For S1 containers this has no counterpart yet.
