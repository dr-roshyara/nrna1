# 3.7 Testing the model against other products

## In plain language

A model of knowledge built from one product may describe only that product. The corpus tests the relationship model of §3.6 by **holding it fixed** and applying it to three products: PublicDigit (actual), a hospital information system and an ERP system (both hypothetical). The aim is "NOT to prove the ontology correct" but to see what breaks [S0022 L10]. Most of the model held. Three things broke, and two of them were **hidden by PublicDigit itself**.

Source: S0022, a generated CANDIDATE submitted to the Decision Authority.

## The method `[CON-0047 · validated]` (Src)

"The ontology is HELD FIXED throughout. No concept added, removed or renamed during validation." [S0022 L5]. The model is projected into each domain, and what breaks is recorded [L9].

**Limit (Int):** the two hypothetical domains are the author's constructions. The method tests the model against what the author knows about hospitals and ERP systems. It is well defined, but its evidence is only as strong as those constructions. Counterexample attempt recorded in the register.

**For Ch. 8:** this is the first candidate method in the corpus for validating a theory of knowledge across products (CHAPTER-PLAN note).

## What held (Src, with the book's checks)

- **Design policy** held in all three: "an order names a prescriber", "an entry cites a source" [L42]; "the layer discovered last projected best" [L187]. Two of the three domains are hypothetical, and adopting the layer is still open (§3.6; CON-0034).
- **Knowledge does not execute** (I-11 as restated) held, "and my original wording would have FAILED here": a clinical protocol informs the generation of an order set but does not execute [L67] (CON-0039).
- The source counts "10 of 11 invariants survive three domains" [L69]. **Two of those are weak** (E-S0022 §10):
  - I-6 (the runtime never owns knowledge) held "vacuously" in two domains, because "no runtime concept appears at all" [L62];
  - I-7 (a PKS never contains reusable method) is counted as held, but the only **actual** product breaches it [S0021 L111].
- **The tallies do not agree.** The concept table gives 8 invariant, 1 specializes, 2 fail, 1 disappears. The summary line says "8 invariant · 2 specialize · 1 disappears · 1 relationship fails" [L52]. The verdict lists three concepts needing specialization [L161]. The projection also covers 12 concepts where §3.6 has 13 (vision is left out).

## PublicDigit masked two of the three failures (Src)

"It is that PublicDigit MASKED two of the three failures." [L166]
- **Deployment:** PublicDigit has multi-tenancy in its code (`organisation_id`), and the model treats it as one product. "The concept is present in the product and absent from the ontology." [L106]
- **External authority:** "election law is treated as product domain knowledge, not as external authority" [L115].

**Int:** a model validated against one product inherits that product's blind spots. The source's conclusion that cross-product projection is "justified by evidence, not by principle" [L191] follows from these two cases. The strength of that evidence is limited by the hypothetical domains (above).

## Inert and attested projections `[CON-0045 · invalid]`

The source proposes to split projection into **inert** (index, graph: cited by nothing) and **attested** ("signed, filed, authoritative") [L125].

**Int (why it is marked invalid here):** §3.5 defines a projection by its lifecycle: it "regenerates from its sources" (DP-2). A signed and filed financial statement no longer regenerates; in the source's own words, "a closed period is immutable" [L30]. So an "attested projection" may not be a projection at all. It may be a **derived artifact whose regeneration has ended**, which then gains standing by an attestation act. That reading keeps three things apart, as Ch. 1 §1.14.4 already does: projection (a lifecycle), derived provenance (an origin), authoritative standing (the result of an act). ## Composition, not transformation `[CON-0046 · invalid]`

The source finds four missing relations: **specializes**, **constrains** (external → internal), **instantiates** (product → deployment), **attests** (agent → projection) [L134–137]. It groups them: the existing relations describe "what things ARE MADE OF", the missing ones "what happens TO them" [L139]. "The ontology describes composition. It does not describe transformation." [L190]

**Why the grouping is marked invalid here** *(a stronger counter added at the consolidation S0131–S0150, B-34: `generates`, production, is in the source's own composition set, so the split between composition and transformation fails on the existing side too)*:
- "Specializes" (is-a) is, in ordinary ontology practice, a **structural** relation. *Background, not corpus evidence:* the W3C RDF Schema's `rdfs:subClassOf` is the standard example.
- "Constrains" (a regulation constrains a principle) is not something that happens to a thing over time.
- The source argues that one shared shape makes the gap "far more likely to be a real omission" [L142]. But the four relations share the shape partly **by definition**: they were grouped because none is one of the four structural verbs. TODO T3-17. *Link (SEQ-0010):* S0012 had called SPECIALIZES the one genuinely ontological gap (Ch. 1 TODO T1-41).

**What survives:** the **attests** relation is the same act Ch. 1 already requires for standing (§1.6; §1.14.4). Its absence from the relation vocabulary is consistent with the mechanical diagnosis that `authority` has no state machine [L79].
