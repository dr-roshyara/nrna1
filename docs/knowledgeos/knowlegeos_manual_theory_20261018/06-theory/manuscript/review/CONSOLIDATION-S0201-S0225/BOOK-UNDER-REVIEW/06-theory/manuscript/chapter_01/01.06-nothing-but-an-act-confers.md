## 1.6 Nothing but an act confers authority

**The idea.** GA collects a family of statements of the same form [GA L424]:
- relevance ≠ authority;
- activity ≠ authorization ("active ≠ authorized");
- ownership ≠ authority;
- existence ≠ permission.

GA lists this family as items to promote [GA L424]. Separately, it calls "the mechanism records authority; it never grants it" the strongest convergence in the repository [GA L294] (attribution corrected, audit R-08). It was derived twice: compiled into running code as a verdict that a mechanical check may not emit, and stated independently in a document that never read that code [GA L288–296]. **Src** (the code comment is quoted by GA; I have not opened `Verdict.php`).

**Development (Ded, given a stipulation).** These are not separate rules. They are **consequences of one stipulated definition** under the model of §1.2:

> **A1 (a stipulated definition of recognized standing).** *Scope:* the standing of claims and the permissions of actors, as the organisation recognizes them. The organisation recognizes a change of standing only if a recorded act effects it (§1.2). Relevance, activity, ownership, existence, detail, repetition and correctness are properties a claim can have *without any act occurring*. Therefore, **given A1, none of them can change recognized standing.**

**A1's readings, named.** Later sections use A1 in different senses; each use should name its reading:
- **A1-de-jure:** a norm the organisation adopts: standing *ought* to change only by recorded acts;
- **A1-recognized:** the stipulated definition above: a standing change is *recognized* only if a recorded act effects it (the reading this section states);
- **A1-as-form:** every standing change in the record is labelled by a recorded act (a property of the record's form; Ch. 9 §9.9.7);
- **A1-as-fact (A1-fact):** standing changes only by acts that occurred (a claim about what happened; Ch. 9 §9.9.7, §9.8 M1).

A1-recognized and A1-de-jure are not tested by any instance: they are adopted or not. A1-as-fact is an empirical claim, kept apart below. **Decided by the Principal (DB-1, 2026-10-20), as the brief recommended: "A norm (a stipulated definition of recognized standing), with named readings; the empirical claim kept apart as a hypothesis."** So "norm" means A1-recognized adopted by the organisation; A1-de-jure is its practice-facing obligation. What this means for the kernel's evaluator: Ch. 9 §9.12.

Each "X ≠ authority" is a consequence of A1 for one property X. *(Example from a later source, added R1-9:* "a similarity score is only a relevance signal. It is not permission, authority, applicability, or truth" [AG L53]. In retrieval terms, embedding similarity measures topical closeness, not whether a claim **applies** here, **binds**, or is **true**. So similarity ranking can never **replace** filtering by visibility, applicability and standing. Filtering must have happened **before the result is used**, whatever the order of computation.) The list is open-ended: further instances, such as "popularity ≠ authority" and "age ≠ authority", follow by definition, without new rules. A property that by its definition includes a recorded act, e.g. "ratified", is the act itself, not a counterexample.

**The empirical claim, kept apart (Hyp).** *Claim:* in the estate, standing changes in practice only through recorded acts. *Falsifier:* a claim that the organisation treats as standing (acts on it as canon) with no recorded act. *Rival:* **standing conferred by practice**; Ch. 1 §1.14.5-13's "governs by a proposed constitution" (S0015 L66) is a candidate instance. This rival is not refuted by "the counterexample is the act itself", which applies only to the stipulation.

**Consequence.** The "X ≠ authority" family needs no growing list of prohibitions. It does not rest on A1 alone: its derivations use A1 together with the companion grant premise ("permission exists only through a grant that references an act", §1.13), P-E and P-R (§1.14.5), INV-A3 (§1.14.5-6), the reason condition ι (§1.14.1) and alternative C1 ("records constitute authority", §1.14.1). Each derivation should name the premises it uses.

> **Refined (Chapter 2 §2.8.2; restated after audit L-05, L-06, E-03).**
> 1. **Domain.** The axiom is about **normative status**: either the *standing of a claim* or an *actor's permission to act*. "Relevance ≠ authority" is an instance for claims. "Active ≠ authorized", "existence ≠ permission" and "ownership ≠ authority" are instances for actors. They need the companion premise "permission exists only through a grant that references an act" (§1.13). Because the theorem follows from the axiom by definition, its "further instances" (popularity, age) are **consequences, not predictions**; they test nothing.
> 2. **Conditions on a valid act:** the act is recorded (strictly, the condition for the organisation to **recognize** the resulting standing change; the act's existence does not depend on its record, §1.13.1), **and** the subject scope of what it changes lies within the actor's **grant scope**, **and** the act falls within the grant's **validity period**. Authority = act × scope × time.
> 3. **Open:** grant scope and canon scope (Ch. 0 §0.3) live in different value spaces. Containment between them is undefined until a mapping exists.
> 4. **Standing:** the ARB calls the containment chain a "**candidate relationship … not an adopted universal law**" [SD L317]. The scope and time conditions are therefore **Ded**, not ruled.

**Evidence is necessary, not sufficient: two keys (batch C-1; revision RC-7).** The programme's own rule is "evidence EARNS; governance GRANTS; promotion requires BOTH" [Src: S0018 L105, the primary statement; S0002 L37 restates it in the same lineage, so the two are one line of support; E-S0002 Q8]. This section says no property except an act confers standing. The rule adds the converse condition for promotion: **an act of promotion without evidence is also not the programme's practice**. In business terms there are two keys: evidence shows the knowledge is worth trusting; an authorized person turns the authority key. Neither opens the door alone. The corpus aligns this with technology-readiness levels and GRADE, both of which "separate evidence accumulation from adoption decisions" [Src: S0007 L28]. **Src** for the rule; **Int** that it is a necessary condition on acts, where the source states it for promotion only. *Consolidation review (2026-10-19): the rule is VALIDATED for promotion along an evidence ladder; the general Int form is **INVALID**. Counterexamples: questions that "need only a human act" (§1.4), freezes by declaration [S0017 L55], and genesis, where no prior evidence exists [S0024 L159–162]. TRL and GRADE support separating the two keys, not requiring both.*

**Why the two keys cannot replace each other: granted vs earned (S0018; revision RC-37).** The Progression Model gives the reason, as a difference of **cause**:

| Kind of change | Moved by | Reversible by |
|---|---|---|
| governance-act progression `[CON-0019 · validated]` | a decision by a named role | a further act (supersession) |
| evidential progression `[CON-0020 · invalid]` `[CON-0163 · provisional]` (survives as "cannot be decreed"; re-analysis and staleness also lower it, §4.1) | accumulating evidence; "nobody can decree it" | not by decision; the source's "refutation only" rests on CON-0020 and is not kept (re-analysis and staleness also lower it, §4.1) |

"A GOVERNANCE-ACT progression can be granted. An EVIDENTIAL progression can only be earned." "No ARB ruling can make an observation Replicated. No amount of evidence can make a document frozen." [S0018 L61–68; E-S0018 Q8].

**Ded (corrected after review C3 F-03; RC-37a):** the two keys are **causally** distinct, not just procedurally. **No act can decree an evidential progression, and evidence alone cannot effect a governance-act progression** [S0018 L61–62, L68]. This is a ban on *decree*, not on *causing*: work that an act authorizes does produce evidence (the source's table row: "P-7 produces P-10", "work produces evidence" [S0018 L106]; two cells, not one sentence; corrected at the consolidation S0101–S0130, B R1-10), and evidence does produce *evidential* standing [L105]. The source's own example ("No amount of evidence can make a document `frozen`" [L68]) concerns a **status** value, not standing. So this section's axiom (only acts change standing) and its converse (promotion also needs evidence) cannot be collapsed into one rule. A single lifecycle would force one engine onto all four, "requiring either that evidence be grantable by decree, or that decisions wait on evidence that may never come" [S0018 L121]. The full taxonomy of change belongs to the planned Ch. 4.

**Canon is human-made; AI output tops out at candidate (S0013; revision RC-27).** The corpus's inventory of 2026-08-03 states the axiom as a classification rule: "Canonical" means "ADOPTED, FROZEN, RULED, or ENFORCED BY HUMANS", never "validated by this programme". The reason given: "engineering never accepts its own work" (R-34) [Src: S0013 L22; E-S0013 Q6, Q8].
- **Its consequence, observed:** all 12 canonical concerns are human-made, and every product of the AI discovery programme sits at Candidate or below. "That is the system working, not modesty" [Src: S0013 L22, L112].
- **Int:** this is this section's axiom applied to a whole body of work. No volume of AI-produced validation crosses into canon without a human act.
- The source also separates **knowledge states** from **decision outcomes**: "Rejected is an outcome, not a state; Open Question is unresolved work, not knowledge" [Src: S0013 L17] (evidence for TODO T0-07).

**The engineering canon keeps authority acts out of its decision catalogue by design.** The Engineering Decision Model indexes eight engineering decisions and deliberately excludes ADR-making, capability approval, authority acts and promotion rulings: "Promotion is a human governance act and is never automated"; "the AI evaluates and recommends — authority remains with governance" [Src: S0006 L36, quoting the model; E-S0006 Q10, Q11]. This is a statement of the pattern in §1.14.3 (AI recommends; humans decide), quoted from the engineering canon. *(Corrected RC-7a after independent review: S0006 dates only three of its quotes to 2026-07-11 (L21, L22, L88); these quotes are **undated** in the source, so no claim that they predate the August corpus is made.)* **Src.**
