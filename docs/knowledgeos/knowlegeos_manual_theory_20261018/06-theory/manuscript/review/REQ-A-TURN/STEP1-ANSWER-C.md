## 2. Relative to what is "a minimal kernel" well posed?

**Requirement side.** Every item below is a stipulation or a Principal act; none is evidence.
- (R1) The readings of valid, owned and trusted (§9.10.4, premises).
- (R2) Req = (Q, O), each item with a reference definition. Q1 on D/Θ_f under C1, Q2, Q3 (construction), Q5 exist. Q4 is conditional on g1.
- (R3) How O is read: EF from s₀, or enabledness.
- (R4) The invariant set I_S, I_T, I_R as F36 decided. It is part of Kernel′, not of Req, but minimality is relative to it.
- (R5) Req is the **system's** (§9.10 scope note), not the kernel's.

**Interface side.** "Where the system ends":
- (A1) inputs carry outside acts;
- (A2) W and E are excluded (§9.11 R2);
- (A3) replies.

**Interface side.** "How finely mechanisms are cut":
- (A4) the pool the interface offers (F16, T9-03);
- (A5) closed reading or guards (F17);
- (A6) where the kernel ends inside the system: the kernel's mechanisms K against application mechanisms M_A, tested by relocation under K3.

[I] A6 is indispensable. If M_A may contain any preserving mechanism, the kernel part K = ∅ is minimal at system level. The kernel is non-empty only through non-delegability. On the book's evidence plus the Principal's acts, the one forced element is the coordination point for single-use grants. It rests on K3, on prevent for single use (R3a), on L9 and on L10 (§9.16 R4c, R4d). That result is **normative-conditional**, not empirical.

[I] Observers do not enter mechanism minimality. Under C6 result 7(a), observer correctness is independent of M. So which questions sit in the kernel is decided only by the boundary questions, never by "minimal".

**Three minimalities that must not be merged:**
- Req-item minimality (§9.10.5 F-min);
- mechanism minimality over the pool (§9.1 C2/C6);
- primitive minimality (no primitive derivable from others; RESEARCH-PURPOSE).

§9.1 C5 shows the last two differ. [I] The first also differs from the second: §9.10.5 itself says Q1 = CE∘Q2.

**What the book supports, and at what strength.**
- **Ded, general, form only:**
  - existence by one check for S/T/O/R under the closed reading;
  - H only inside result 5's class;
  - no existence by one check with guards, for L, or for AE.
- **Computation, toy model:** minimal kernels depend on the pool. Re-run on a scratch copy of `kernel_model.py`, the minimal kernels are [recgrant] and [grant, record], and only [grant, record] without recgrant.
- **Conditional Ded:** the core Req list.
- **Principal decisions:** F22 (C + O).
- **Cannot be stated yet:**
  - which set is the minimal KnowledgeOS kernel (the pool, T9-03, F13 and the guard reading are all open);
  - existence or uniqueness in the guard reading, which (C + O)'s owner guard puts the kernel in;
  - Req's sufficiency for §9.5 C1's question.

**Uniqueness, a structure condition (Ded, elementary; [I]).** Take the closed reading, invariants in S, T, O, R or result-5 H, and U = the preserving pool. Then the kernels form an up-set in 2^U, and:
- (a) the minimal kernel is unique **iff** the intersection of all kernels is a kernel;
- (b) the necessary mechanisms (in every kernel) **are** that intersection;
- (c) a sufficient pool condition is **no substitutes**: every required target has exactly one minimal enabling set.

These are checked on 956 random small pools (claim (a) held in all 956; (c) gave uniqueness in all 644 no-substitute cases). This is form only.

**Corollary, against RESEARCH-PURPOSE's per-capability test.** With substitutes, the necessity test can find **nothing** necessary even though ∅ fails Req. In C4: record, grant and recgrant are each absent from some kernel. So "necessary and not delegable" is well posed as a kernel definition only under the no-substitute condition. That is the same condition that makes the minimal kernel unique. [I]

## 4. Trivial cases

- **∅ (empty mechanism set):** fails O1–O4 under EF, but meets all of Q1–Q5 given correct observers. So the must-do content lies entirely in O, and O is weak (§3 above).
- **Refuse-all:**
  - As a mechanism it fails EF.
  - As a guard it passes the enabledness reading and every P_S, P_T and P_R.
  - [I] ARCH-INV-1's guard half (law 4b: refuse only when a permitting record is absent) would exclude it only once a permitting record can exist. In a rootless system none can, so **O4 is what gives 4b force**.
- **Unchanged state:** meets every T and R invariant (stuttering). The assurance default (§9.4 C7), the empty contract (§5.4 C9) and the never-started session (§9.5 C1) are all instances of it. So each of the six deferring claims is a case of a must-never met by the unchanged state. Req as (Q, O) answers them only as reachability. Whether something must *happen* is L or E, outside C6's existence argument. So L179's "missing" object cannot be fully supplied by Req in its current form. [I]
