## 2. The research question: relative to what is "a minimal kernel" well posed?

**The requirement side, Req.** Req = (Q, O) is fixed by four things:
- (r1) the Principal's act for Q1 / R_s (09.06 §9.6.6a);
- (r2) the Int readings of valid, owned and trusted (09.10 §9.10.4), including the Hyp that the trust combination f is application policy;
- (r3) the chapter must-dos (Q5);
- (r4) the form of O: EF, or the open rival, enabledness.

Each Q item is only as strong as its reference definition:
- Q1 exists, on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute;
- Q3 is a construction that has not been modelled;
- Q4 is absent, or g1 if G7 maps it;
- Q5 exists.

**The interface side, A. Two separate parameters:**
- **(a1) Where the system ends.** This fixes four lines:
  - **kernel / applications.** Necessity is defined over "kernel plus applications" (09.06 L82). Placement is kernel-only. Under F22 = (C + O), applications record, while the kernel holds the contract and the owner guard.
  - **system / world.** Under the input-origin condition, acts are made outside the kernel. W and E assumptions are not expressible (09.11 §9.11.2).
  - **record / carrier.** Designation is not a property of entries (09.17 R1); the root designation sits under L11.
  - **mechanisms / observers.**
- **(a2) How finely mechanisms are cut.** The pool comes from A (09.01 C4–C5, F16, T9-03). Two further distinctions:
  - whether refusals are a mechanism's own partiality (⊥, closed reading) or **guards** in C6's sense;
  - whether O1 counts as one item or as one item per act kind.

## 3. Disproof: what would refute this answer

- **Insufficiency of Req (EF).** [D] Take a system with a root that accepts one act of each O kind and then refuses everything. It meets O1–O4 under EF, and it answers Q1–Q4 correctly on its own record. So §9.10's F-ins test, "a judgement undecidable", is **not** triggered by a useless system. Req (EF) plus decidable judgements is still met vacuously in this sense. My answer is refuted if some book item already rules this system out; I found none.
- **Redundancy.** As mechanisms, Q1 at a cut is CE over Q2 (09.10 L194), and Q3 is read off the same fixed point (09.13 R5). As requirements, they are independent only through separation of duties and the actor–holder link. My claim that Q adds nothing to mechanism minimality is refuted if the book adopts reading (b), observers on reachable states. Reading (b) is antitone and would bring Q into the minimisation.
- **Vacuity.** If O1 is one item, a system accepting only root acts plus one grant kind meets it. Every per-kind mapping in Task 1 (establishment, recusal, apply-held-act, declassification) is then unprotected. This is refuted only if O1 is split into one item per kind.
- **The boundary at which a result fails.** [D] §9.1 C2's "∅ is not a kernel" holds when O is checked over the **kernel's** mechanisms. §9.10 states Req of "the system the kernel serves" (L5). Under (C + O), applications' M_A can reach O's effects. Then a kernel with **no mechanisms** (contract + guards + observers) meets Req, and the ∅ result fails at that boundary. The minimisation must say whether M_A is fixed, free or counted.
- **The granularity at which a result fails.** The scratch check refutes "atomic cut ⇒ unique minimal kernel". C4's recgrant shows that one coarse mechanism can create a second minimal kernel.
