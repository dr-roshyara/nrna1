# Theory turn 1 (Req), review A (Opus): independent, adversarial

*2026-10-20. Reviewer A. Object: §9.10 (`chapter_09/09.10-required-capabilities.md`, commit `c0ab6daab`), CON-0188, EXT-038–042, and the edits to BOOK-OUTLINE, TARGET-TOC, the Ch. 9 index and T9-01 made in the same commit. Brief: `review/TURN1-REQ/BRIEF.md`. REPORT-B was not read. Labels: Src / Ded / Int / Hyp / Ext. Severity: Rulebook v1.3 R5 and REVIEWER-INSTRUCTIONS (critical / high / normal).*

**Overall verdict.** Not resolved. Every item has grounds, and no item is invented without any. But four of the five "resolved when" conditions fail:
1. Three operations (O2, O3, O4) are missing most of the six fields, and Q6 has only part of them.
2. Several grounds labels overstate their support.
3. The vacuity test (F-vac) is wrong for Q3 and was not run for Q4 or Q6.
4. Prior work for the (Q, O) arrangement exists and was not found.

Only the separation of internal guarantees from world assumptions mostly holds. Of the six steps in the derivation, step 1's Q1 ← O1 holds conditionally. Step 1's Q1 ← Q3, and steps 2 and 4, each lack a premise. Under R5 F1 those gaps are critical, because CON-0188 (PROVISIONAL) cites the derivation. The strongest single result of this review is the vacuity finding A-3. Apart from Q2's completeness and Q5's liveness, a log that records every input and answers U, "ungraded" or "deny" meets every stated observable consequence. So Req's content lies almost entirely in reference definitions that the book does not yet have: CE, the grading rule, the read rule and the identity scheme.
