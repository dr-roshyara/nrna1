## 8.96.1 In plain language

1. **The answer makes the "KnowledgeClaim" the aggregate root that owns the commitment, and says splitting it would make the duty unenforceable; the reply rejects this as not yet shown.** Within the book's record the reply is right: an authorized person's recorded act admits, and a duty is not a container.
2. **The answer gives identity as opaque, not derived from content, assigned at admission, and "not a relation".** As in the last window, that fits an item's governed identifier, not an entry's identity. "At admission" has two senses: if it means when an entry is accepted, the book has nothing to compare, since an entry names its item and the kernel assigns no item identifier; if it means when standing is given, it conflicts with the book, where an item has its identifier before adoption. *(Restated at review: W-01)*
3. **The answer lists ten invariants, all of them prohibitions.** Within the record some agree (one path for changes, held as an assumption; unique identifiers; no identity or confidence from mechanisms; failed candidates leave no trace; accepting an entry only if the record's rules are met); one conflicts (every admitted claim must have evidence); some have no counterpart (justification, confidence, a sufficiency test for standing). A kernel that refuses everything satisfies all ten. The last one, that failed candidates leave no trace, is exactly why the record cannot tell a refusal from no claim. *(Restated at review: W-01, W-04, W-08)*
4. **It weighs four boundaries and calls an event stream per identity the absolute minimum.** Within the record the book has one stream across all items, because order across items matters and the checks read across items; the boundaries the book has decided are per grant and per check, not per claim; evidence need not be contained for standing, since standing needs no evidence. *(Restated at review: W-03)*
5. **The reply asks whether evidence shared by many claims, and the "supports" link itself, can change on their own.** Within the record the book records the item end of that link: an evidence entry about one item, which can be withdrawn, restored or annulled without touching the item's standing; the evidence itself is outside. *(Restated at review: W-06)*
6. **The answer says the invariants are given from outside and the kernel only enforces them; the reply asks what an item with an identity and nothing else is.** Within the record the rules are decided outside the kernel (the book's reason is not the theorem). An identifier with nothing recorded about it cannot be shown at all: like a refused candidate, it cannot be told from no claim. An item with evidence entries and no adoption is a different case: an item without standing. *(Restated at review: W-02, W-09)*
