## 8.90.1 In plain language

1. **The first adviser says the kernel is where the domain makes and stands behind an "epistemic commitment"; the reply calls this its strongest formulation: the kernel owns the commitment, not the machinery.** Within the book's record there are two readings. For standing, the commitment is an authorized person's recorded act, which the kernel evaluates. For the grade of a claim, the kernel itself works out a class at each cut, but that is not a commitment that persists. What agrees is that the machinery stays outside. *(Restated at review: W-02)*
2. **The first adviser says admission creates a historical fact that cannot be undone, even if the claim is later retracted.** Within the record the book agrees: what persists is the entry's required fields; its content can be removed with a tombstone, and its effect can be annulled. *(Restated at review: W-06)*
3. **The first adviser's accountable object must keep identity, evidence, state, confidence, justification and history co-consistent.** As in §8.89: the book meets the grade part by deriving it from the record; the rest is open or absent.
4. **The reply puts it as "The Kernel removes illegitimate transitions into knowledge".** A kernel that refuses everything removes every illegitimate transition; what must be let through has to be stated as well, and even then a kernel that accepts each kind once and then refuses is not excluded. *(Restated at review: W-08)*
5. **The reply asks what must stay the same while a claim's state changes: its identity.** For a recorded entry the book answers: identity comes from its content and never changes with standing. For an item across versions the question is open. *(Restated at review: W-01)*

**C2. Admission as an irreversible historical fact (Src; Int, agreement within D under K1)** *(Restated at review: W-06)*.
- **S0366 (A):** "Admission is the **irreversible (in the domain sense) transition from mechanism-space to domain-space**. Irreversible does not mean immutable — epistemic states can change — but it means the candidate now *exists* in the domain's history. Even if later retracted, it remains in history as \"knowledge that was admitted and then retracted.\" Admission creates a **historical fact** that cannot be undone." (L45).
- **Int:** *Agreement within D, under K1:* every change is an entry, removed only by a recorded act, by tombstone (§9.15 G3); removal without trace is a K1 failure. *What persists is the entry's identity, kind and required fields, not its content* (a tombstone drops content, §9.18.7) *and not its effect:* an annulment voids its target from the start and can itself be annulled (§9.18.4), which (A)'s irreversible transition (L45) does not provide for. (A)'s "retracted" has no admitted kind in D (§8.88 C5, the withdrawal homonym).
- **Not a new concept.**

The book has no justification or confidence concept (§8.87 C4), so confidence boundedness (L166) has no counterpart. Of (A)'s six invariants at KnowledgeCreated (L161–167), identity uniqueness has its home in I-0 for assigned identifiers (§1.10) and in entry identity from content (§9.16 R2) (W, step 1).
