## 8.28.1 In plain language

1. **The kernel owns how things are done, not what they mean.** The estate proposes six tests for whether something belongs in the kernel. Two of them ask whether another domain would need the thing, and no source in this window answers that from evidence (I-22).
2. **Nobody in the estate can prove which AI process did something.** The record holds what a process says about itself (S0190 L25). The book's design assumes the kernel can mark entries it accepted, in a way that cannot be forged; no such mark is reported in the sources read (I-22).
3. **A design that exists only on paper is not the current system.** Neither is a test that only shows code exists.

## 8.28.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. The kernel owns the mechanism, not the domain meaning; six criteria for kernel membership (Src; evidence for BOOK-OUTLINE §3 and §6.6 C8).** *(No new concept.)*
- "The kernel owns the **mechanism**, not the domain meaning." (S0187 L221, in meaning). Example: authority mechanics belong to the kernel; "which architecture is correct" does not (S0187 L277–283, in meaning).
- Six criteria are given (S0187 L734–756, in meaning):
  1. would PKS need it?
  2. would a future knowledge domain need it?
  3. does it have its own invariant?
  4. does it have a stable lifecycle?
  5. can it exist without engineering-specific concepts?
  6. can EKS depend on it without the kernel depending on EKS?

  The dependency direction is kernel ← EKS, PKS (S0187 L776–792, in meaning).
- **Int.**
  - Criterion 6 is the book's direction law (§6.6 C8).
  - Criteria 3–5 are close to the boundary questions in BOOK-OUTLINE §3 ("necessary? must the kernel provide it? delegable?"). They are not the same questions, and no mapping is claimed.
  - Criteria 1 and 2 are counterfactual. No source in the window tests them against PKS evidence (S0191 L436–444, in meaning; step-1 inventory, S0187 item 3).
- *Revision within the line:* S0187 would grow the kernel "out of the existing EKS" (S0187 L671, in meaning). S0191 replaces this with a convergence study of EKS, PKS and the proposal, saying "we cannot yet conclude that all five belong in the kernel" [S0191 L354] and "we must not prematurely promote any of those into kernel boundaries" [S0191 L657]. The book's kernel is specified, not extracted (§8.26 C6), so neither method is the book's.
- **Two limits of the criteria (added at review, C35 W-03).**
  - Criterion 1, and S0191's test below, presuppose that EKS and PKS are two independent domains; criterion 2 names no PKS (I-09). S0186, which S0187 reads, speaks of "the system historically called **PKS / EKS**" (S0186 L14, in meaning), while S0189 leaves the exact PKS/EKS runtime boundary unknown (S0189 L1983, in meaning), which presupposes two (I-09). If they are generations of one system, what they share reflects lineage, not independence of domain, and S0191's test ("What do EKS and PKS already have in common…", L465–467, in meaning) loses its force. Routed to T0-03b.
  - Criteria applied element by element do not decide a sufficient kernel: a litmus test is not a placement (CON-0105, §9.2 C2).
- **The candidates themselves are not assessed here (C35 W-04, a reasoned exclusion).** They are S0187's "YES" table (L81–103), "GovernedWork" (L172–221) and its kernel definition (L685), and S0191's observation protocol, ChangeSet and rule port (L162–298). Assessing them means applying the three boundary questions, and that is a theory turn, which needs the Principal (T0-03; consolidation B-10). They are listed for T0-03. *Note:* the rule port rests on the "Rule" homonym (§8.28.3).
- *Closer homes for the criteria (the reviewer's reading, R):* criterion 3 → CON-0132 (an invariant owned or preserved); criteria 1, 2 and 5 → domain independence (CON-0075, §9.2 C2); criterion 6 → §6.6 C8; criterion 4 has none.

**C2. Nothing in the estate attests a process's identity; no gate reads it (Src; a second instance for §1.14.2-1; bearing on §9.17 (c′)).** *(No new concept.)*
- "Nothing in this estate can attest an AI process identity, so this line is a claim by the claimant" (S0190 L25, in meaning). The controls named are falsifiability and a reproducible measurement (S0190 L25, in meaning).
- Because identity "is evidential only and no gate reads it", the engine "would accept a START of `S5` and then report the lane `ACTIVE` and operable — while the evidence in it was false"; "The control here is not mechanical; it is this disclosure." (S0190 L103, in meaning).
- **Int (§1.14.2-1).** This is the S0097 L41 point a second time, in a different process: "no gate reads process identity". It shows, again, that the record can be unsound without challenging ARCH-INV-1.
- **Int (§9.17 (c′), marked for review).** The design sketch assumes that every entry the kernel accepts carries an attestation that cannot be forged. That attestation is of **acceptance by the kernel**, not of **who the caller is**: a different object. Strength (restated, C35 W-10): for **identity**, the estate reports none (S0190 L25; also S0186 L175, an earlier home, one line). For **acceptance**, none is reported in the sources read; that is a search result, not an established absence. *Restated (C35 W-02):* v20's owner check reads the **declared** moving application, so its "detected across merged applications" needs an attestation of the second kind, of who moved. S0190 is an instance of the same kind of premise, a declared identity: identity is evidential and no gate reads it. That the moving application is declared by the moving process is a step still to state (I-25). This is recorded for T9-11 as a premise to be shown, not a defect in the sketch.
