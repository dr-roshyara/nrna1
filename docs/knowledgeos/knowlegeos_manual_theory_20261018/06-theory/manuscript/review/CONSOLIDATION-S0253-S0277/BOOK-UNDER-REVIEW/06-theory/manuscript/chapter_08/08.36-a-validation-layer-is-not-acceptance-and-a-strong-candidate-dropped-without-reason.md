## 8.36.1 In plain language

1. **A check that passes does not accept anything.** In the book, standing comes only from a recorded act by an authorised actor. A "validation boundary" can at most be a gate that waits for such an act. The sources name no one who accepts.
2. **One source says reasoning must not contradict authority.** In the book as proposed, a refutation can be recorded as evidence, and it changes no standing. The source's own reason ("evidence is not authority") does not lead to its rule. The book does let authority decide *whose* refutation counts: only an authorised producer's.
3. **Circular support splits in two.** Support that cites itself in a circle cannot enter the record: an entry may cite only earlier entries. Circles of cancellation (acts that annul each other) can occur; the book does not refuse them, it reports "undetermined" and names the acts. That is what the sources ask for: detect and keep.
4. **"Doubt is a first-class state"** depends on what is in doubt. For authority, an item no act recognises is "not recognized" (F), not "undetermined". For evidence, the empty value is "not attested", which agrees with the sources. A conflict between two claims of content corresponds to "contested", which was then a rival the Principal had not adopted. *(Since decided: T9-17 (b), 2026-10-29: "contested" is now the class, g1, §9.18.5.)*
5. **"Who observed and who validated" is recorded in part**: the book records who adopted (validated) and, as proposed, who produced evidence. "Who interpreted" and "who reasoned" have no kind of act. Whether sources of different kinds (sensor, expert, AI) are classed differently is open.
6. **Again, one reply declared the work finished ("written in stone"), and the next reply took that back.** Later, two candidates that one source called "strong" were dropped or renamed without a word.

## 8.36.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A passing check is not acceptance; the sources leave acceptance without an owner (Src; Ded against A1 and ARCH-INV-1; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-02, W-03, W-04.)*
- **The sources:**
  - S0218 (C): "Reasoning Engine → KnowledgeOS validation boundary → Accepted epistemic state" (L807–815). The kernel "Does not: … decide" (L779–784).
  - S0220: "Knowledge Validation → Critical Reasoning Engine" (L169–175). Its architecture puts a "Validation Layer" (Tarka, contradiction check, fallacy detection) directly before "Knowledge State" (L419–431).
  - S0218 (A): "Knowledge → Assessment → Decision → Action" (L382–393).
- **Ded, against the book:**
  - Only an act confers standing (A1, §1.6). An automated check is not an act, so its pass confers none (§8.06). The mechanism records authority and never grants it (ARCH-INV-1, §1.14.2-1; a relayed ruling, one line of support).
  - Acceptance is a recorded act by an authorised actor, person or system (§10.4 R1; §8.34 C3).
  - That the actor is a person is the book's **norm** (a human act, §1.13.1), not a property D checks. The actor is an identity, and the actor–holder link is an input assumed correct (K3). An engine identity holding a covering grant could record an adoption, and CE* would give T. *"No engine does it" is a norm, not something the kernel enforces.*
- **Int:**
  - **"Validation" is a homonym here:** in S0218 it is an acceptance gate (L811); in S0220 it is critique by a "critical reasoning engine" (L169–175, L419–425).
  - The draft called the sources inconsistent with themselves. That is too strong: S0218's "validation boundary" can be read as a guard that refuses unless an accepting act is recorded. Such a guard reports an absence and judges no merit, so it is compatible with ARCH-INV-1 and with "does not decide". What the sources lack is an **owner** of acceptance: they name no actor.
- **Q1 and action:** S0218 (A) asks "Why was this action justified at that time?" (L397). Q1 at the action's cut answers whether the item had authority then. It gives neither the justification of the action nor the link from the action to the item; that dependency is not modelled (§8.35 C2).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. Reasoning that contradicts authority is recordable, as proposed; authority decides whose refutation counts (Src; Ded against §9.18; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-06.)*
- **S0219's TARKA-005:** "Reasoning must respect organizational authority boundaries (no 'dustarka' — futile reasoning contrary to authority)". It is graded "Strongly supported — aligns with Evidence ≠ Authority, Assessment ≠ Authority" (L249–257), with "Reasoning contrary to authority without governed transition" forbidden (L269) and "Kernel relevance: High" (L294). The same source warns that the Vedic constraint "may not generalize across domains" (L316).
- **Ded:**
  - **The non sequitur:** Evidence ≠ Authority bars evidence from *changing* standing. It does not bar evidence that *contradicts* an adopted item. The same justification is reused unchanged for TARKA-001 and TARKA-003 (L207, L231, L255).
  - **What the book holds, conditionally:** §9.18's evidence kinds are **proposed, not adopted**; today the record holds only acts of authority (G1). Under the proposal, a refutation is an evidence entry, shown in promise (b)'s display, and it changes no standing value (premise (c1); the clause (c2) is proposed). Marks are equal only on records in D_W, on a sample (check 33). *(Since then: the evidence kinds are adopted (T9-19) and (c2) is kept with no guard (T9-20), both 2026-10-31; annotated at the C56 settlement, W-08.)*
  - **Two narrow readings of TARKA-005 that the book holds:** (i) "without governed transition": authority changes only through a recorded act (revoke, annul, a later adoption); (ii) **only an authorised producer's refutation counts** towards "refuted" (T9-12 (c), decided); others are shown, not counted. So authority does constrain *whose* refutation counts, but not whether contradicting reasoning may be recorded.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Positive cycles are prevented; negative cycles occur and are detected; circularity in content is outside the record (Src; Ded against §9.13.2).** *(Restated at review: W-01.)*
- **The sources:**
  - S0219 lists circular reasoning (self-dependency, mutual dependency, circular dependency) and infinite regress (L88–93, L270).
  - S0220's H-KOS-Fallacy-001 says KnowledgeOS "SHALL detect and preserve invalid reasoning patterns", with "A proves B, B proves C, C proves A" (L356–378).
- **Ded:**
  - **Citations** must name earlier entries (§9.13.2), so no cycle of support through citations can enter the record. *Prevention, by the Principal's D decision (R3).*
  - **Targets** of annulments, endings and revocations are not restricted and may be later entries. Negative cycles therefore occur in D: a self-annulling act, a self-cancelling act (S0219's self-dependency), a mutual annulment, an annulment ring. Each gives **U with a reason naming the acts** (§9.13.2; R2). *Detection, and the record keeps them.* This is what H-KOS-Fallacy-001 asks for.
  - **Regress:** the record is finite. A citation chain ends at an entry that cites nothing, which is F unless it is a root; only T answers rest on roots (R4).
  - **Content:** circularity inside an entry's *content* (an evidence text that rests on the claim it supports) is not in the record. That is assessment work, for applications (§10.2).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Doubt and conflict on three axes (Src; Ded against §8.33 C1, §9.13.3).** *(Restated at review: W-05, W-07.)*
- **The sources:**
  - S0218 (A): "Knowledge begins from a neutral state … Unknown … 'I do not yet know'" (L218–244); and "The system records: CONFLICTED instead of forcing TRUE / FALSE prematurely" (L273–285).
  - S0219's TARKA-004 (structured doubt), "aligns with UNKNOWN as first-class state" (L237–245).
  - S0220's H-KOS-Uncertainty-001: "uncertainty as a structured epistemic state, not as missing data" (L330–332), with a lifecycle that starts at "Unknown" (L304–322).
- **Ded:**
  - **(i) Authority, U:** the book's U carries its cause (an undefined covers pair, a negative cycle, or a tie) and a reason (§9.13.3). That is a *structured* undetermined value, not missing data. *Agreement.*
  - **(ii) Content conflict** *(corrected before review after C42 W-02; narrowed at review, W-07)*: two conflicting adoptions of *content* both stay current (§9.13 Θ_f, C6 row; I-4); a conflict in the *authority* record gives U with its reason (§9.15 G6). Neither forces T or F. S0218's "CONFLICTED" (claim A and claim B, each with its evidence) is the content sense: coexistence **agrees**. But Q5 reports conflicts only among rules written in L (§9.10 Q5), and no relation between items is admitted (CON-0188). So a conflict between two non-rule claims is **neither recorded nor reported**: it has no home.
  - **(iii) The empty value, per axis** (§8.33 C1):
    - for **authority**, an item no act recognises is F, "refuted or not recognized", in the closed world of decision C1;
    - for **evidence**, it is "not attested", which agrees with the sources' "I do not yet know" (§8.33 C1);
    - for **content**, the kernel has no value.
  - **A four-valued lens:** the sources' Unknown + CONFLICTED is a status of *content* (neither / both). The book's U is a three-valued *authority* value with a cause, not "neither". The content-side counterparts are "not attested" (neither) and T9-17's rival "contested" (both); g0, refutation-first, collapses "both" into "refuted". *(Since decided: T9-17 (b), 2026-10-29: "contested" is now the class, g1, §9.18.5.)*
- **Not a new concept.**

## 8.36.4 Challenges recorded

- **C1, rival:** the "validation boundary" is a guard that refuses unless an accepting act is recorded.
  - *Answer (restated at review, W-03):* then it is compatible with the book (a guard reports absence; ARCH-INV-1), and the sources are not inconsistent. They still name no actor who accepts.
- **C2, rival:** "without governed transition" makes TARKA-005 harmless.
  - *Answer:* only for changing authority. A second reading the book holds: only an authorised producer's refutation counts (T9-12 (c)). The rule as written forbids the *reasoning*, and the grading rests on a principle that does not imply it.
- **C3, rival (added at review):** mutual annulment through a forward target is a circular dependence in the record.
  - *Answer:* yes; C3 now says so. It is detected (U with its reason), not prevented.
- **C4, rival:** an item that no act has touched is "unknown" in plain speech.
  - *Answer (restated at review, W-05):* for evidence the book's value is "not attested", which says that. For authority it is F by decision C1. The two axes must not be merged.
