## S0384 — `20260823-210001-topological-lens-boundaries-connectedness-and-failure-modes.md` (595 lines) · depth: FULL

1. **Correction of S0383 (L1, L227–253).** The topological lens was omitted and must not be folded into Escher. Escher is invariant-preserving transformation; Topology is invariant structural connectivity under continuous deformation. "They overlap, but they are not duplicates."
2. **Central principle (L583, L58, L219).** "Knowledge identity is not necessarily located in the form of a representation; it may be located in the invariant relational structure preserved across permissible transformations." The quantifiers are hedged ("not necessarily", "may", "sometimes").
3. **Accidental vs structural properties (L42–54).** Accidental properties change; structural properties persist and carry identity. Geometry properties are set against topology properties (L13–29).
4. **Boundary model (L68–111).** Inside, boundary, outside, mapped onto the KnowledgeCore Admission Boundary. The source claims this is "much deeper than simply saying bounded context" (L111).
5. **Ten-question boundary checklist (L446–459).** Covers inside, outside, what forms the boundary, what crosses, transformation at crossing, whether identity survives crossing, bypass, the boundary disappearing, two regions merging, one region splitting. It is applied to a chain of boundaries: Expression → Semantic interpretation → Candidate → Admission → KnowledgeCore → External consumers (L425–442).
6. **Failure taxonomy (L463–526).** Boundary leakage (bypass), false connection, broken connection, and boundary collapse ("very serious"). Identity-preserving transformation is the desired case.
7. **Topology and Zero (L156–188).** An absence can change the connectedness of the structure (removing B disconnects A from C), not only the inventory. This is offered as "stronger vocabulary for the Zero principle".
8. **Relational structure as the object (L192–221).** Identity may reside in the network of relations rather than in any node. This is presented as a bridge to semantic representation.
9. **Worked SNF example (L261–303).** Active, passive and nominal paraphrases share one SEE(ACTOR, OBJECT) structure. Reversing A→B gives a different object despite lexical similarity (L347–361). This restates S0383's discrimination rule (L363 cites the "Sanskrit/SNF work").
10. **Continuity over time (L367–417).** Structural continuity might separate revision from replacement, and supersession from identity destruction. Status: "candidate analytical mechanism, not … Kernel". [I] No criterion is given for when a structural break counts as discontinuity. "Permissible transformation" is used but never defined, so the principle in item 2 is open-ended.
11. **Revised lens stack (L534–575), a change from S0383.** Topology is added under TRANSFORMATION, SNF moves out into a new SEMANTIC IDENTITY tier, and a BOUNDARIES tier holds Topology + Zero. Unremarked: S0383's RESPONSIBILITY tier (Dharma/Leonardo) and THRESHOLD tier (Gaṇeśa) disappear, and DDD becomes the final ARCHITECTURE tier rather than a lens alongside the stack.
12. **Status caution (L589–595).** The author found no evidence of an admitted standalone Topological Lens in the 25+4 register; topology ideas are recorded under Escher. The lens is classified "strongly justified analytical lens, but not yet an independently admitted … research family". Rule cited: a lens-bearing file that confirms an existing family "should not silently become a new register row" (reported).
13. **Open [I].** The source treats boundary-crossing and identity under transformation as topological, but defines no topology (no open sets, no neighbourhood structure on knowledge objects). "Topological" is used as a metaphor and vocabulary, not as a mathematical model. Whether the lens adds anything beyond DDD plus Escher plus Zero is asserted, not shown.
