# 5.6 What an act covers: conclusions, conditions, and things not yet seen

*Window C10 (2026-10-19). Sources, in index order: S0052, S0053 (2026-08-16) and S0055 (2026-08-17, one sentence). **Status: reviewed in C10 (2026-10-19); settled in `review/C10-S0051-S0055/SETTLEMENT.md`.***

*Lineage:* S0052 (a decision summary) and S0053 (an approval registration) are written by the same registrar on one day, in one work item: one line of support. Each claim below rests on that one case unless stated.

*Existing homes (book search, lexical and by meaning):* Ch. 1 §1.12 (a record of an act is not the act); §1.13 (a grant is a recorded reference to an external human act); §1.14.3 (roles); §1.16.2 (authority is valid at the time of the act); Ch. 5 §5.1 (the decision package); Ch. 5 §5.5 (who executed, who authorized); **§1.17.2** (a valid act need not be well founded; found by the meaning search and missed before review); §1.16.6 (pinning); §1.16.8 (a stipulation is declared as such). This section refines them on the **object** of an act.

## 5.6.1 In plain language

An approval approves something. These sources are careful about what: the conclusion or also the reasoning, now or only after a repair, a document that exists or one still to be written, and which of several levels.

## 5.6.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Approving a conclusion is not approving its argument (Src; Int).** `[CON-0123 · provisional]`
- "**Approving the boundary on the current text would approve a correct conclusion on an unsound argument.**" [S0052 L16]. So the approval of that boundary was made conditional on a repaired justification.
- **Int (a refinement of §1.17.2):** §1.17.2 separates a valid act from a well-founded one. Here the object of an act may include its argument, and the sources choose to: the conclusion was approved "on the recorded interpretation, **not** the current text" [S0052 L66], so conclusion and argument were **joined** in the approval, not separated.
- **"Correct" is relative to a chosen premise:** the argument became sound by adding uniqueness, and §9.4 (F19) shows that premise was chosen, not forced; an aggregate would also make the outcome definite.

**C1a. The interpretive act (Src; a new fork).**
- The premise was supplied by the Human as an interpretation: "*I interpret the approved Assurance Model as requiring at most one established assessment per Assurance Claim at a time.*" [S0052 L81], framed as "not a new business rule and not a new candidate invariant" [S0051 L77].
- **Int:** "invariant" is a homonym here: a logical constraint the model needs, against a governed item with its own standing. An interpretation that adds a constraint the text does not state works like an amendment without being recorded as one. §1.16.8 asks that a stipulation be declared as such.
- **Open (fork F21):** does an interpretive act change what is in force, and must it then be recorded as an amendment?

**C2. A conditional approval needs a second act to land (Src).**
- The rows read "**APPROVED AFTER the F-1 repair**" [S0052 L66] and "**APPROVED AFTER F-2's full disposition**" [S0052 L71]. After the repair, a separate act: "*record Q-B1 and Q-B6 approved after repair.*" [S0052 L85]
- **Int (Ch. 1 A1; scope: conditions that need judgement):** where meeting the condition is a judgement (here: was the repair adequate?), taking it as met automatically would change standing without an act, so the second act is where the judgement is made. *Hyp:* for conditions a machine can decide, whether a second act is still needed is open.

**C3. An acceptance of a deliverable covers only a delivered, fixed version (Src; Hyp).** `[CON-0124 · invalid as stated]` (valid in the restricted form, `[CON-0168 · provisional]`)
- An acceptance was spoken before the verification had been delivered, and "the act **held until the deliverable existed**" [S0052 L87]; the Human directed "*Deliver verification first*".
- **Restricted claim (Hyp, one case):** an act that **accepts or approves a deliverable** covers the delivered version only, pinned (§1.16.6; S0053 lists the approved items by commit [S0053 L21–23]). Its must-do half: a held act is applied once its object exists, or returned to its author (compare §5.5 C3).
- **Not a law of all acts:** a grant permits future activity (Ch. 1 §1.13.1); Q-C1 approved a Stage-2 assignment that did not exist yet [S0052 L74]; a conditional approval covers a text still to be written (C2).
- The verifier's "must not be read as pre-approving any corrected text it has not seen" [S0055 L112] is the scope of a report, not an act; it is a parallel, not a second case.

**C4. The act states what it covers, and its levels are kept apart (Src).**
- The approval lists what is "**In force as the approved Stage-1 design**", item by item [S0053 L25–35], and what it does not do: "the three-level status, never collapsed" [S0053 L37]. The aggregate recommendation is approved; bounded-context placement is "**NOT YET CONFIRMED**"; the target architecture is "**NOT APPROVED**" [S0053 L41–43].
- **Int:** an enumerated scope makes the act checkable: one can ask of any item whether it is covered. *Hyp (one case):* "approved" without its level is read as approving the levels below it.
- **Limit:** an enumerated scope is only as checkable as the supersession accounting of the documents it lists. The approved composite includes rev 2, whose §7 is superseded only in a trailing note (§8.5 C8).

**C5. Separate verbs for decisions and for the work's lifecycle (Src).**
- "RECORD for events and decisions · START/COMPLETE for opening and formally closing a session" [S0052 L42]. Accepting a review and closing its session are two acts [S0052 L34–35].
- And the reviewer "correctly declined to write this summary itself — a producer recommending the disposition of its own findings would make the review its own advocate" [S0052 L4].
- *Hyp (one remark):* producer ≠ verifier (§1.14.3) extends to producer ≠ disposer of its own findings.

**C6. Three acts of 2026-08-17: a second case, and a tension (Src; Int; added in Trial T3; reviewed in C11/T3, tension VALIDATED).**
*Lineage:* S0061, S0064 and S0065 are registrations by Governance or by the producer, all on one day in one programme. They are a second work item beside S0052–S0053, in the same estate and under the same registration practice, so they are not independent of it.
- **A pinned object, again (C3):** "**The accepted content is the blob Verification #3 verified: `40026b12`.**" [S0061 L27]. This is a second case of the restricted form (CON-0168), in the same estate.
- **The tension:** after acceptance the document's banner is "annotated **ACCEPTED 2026-08-17** — a lifecycle-status annotation only … the verified content (`40026b12`) is otherwise untouched" [S0061 L55]. The same is done for the v2 deliverables [S0065 L40]. **Ded:** the accepted object is the blob. From the annotation on, the file no longer has those bytes. A reader who checks the file against the accepted hash finds a difference, and only the record tells them it is "lifecycle only". Covering a pinned version and annotating the same file are compatible only if the annotation is outside what the hash covers, or the pin names the version before the annotation. Here the pin does the second.
- **Two messages, one act:** "two messages, one act — the second refines the first; registered once" [S0061 L5]. **Int:** the registrar decides what counts as one act. That is an identity question for acts (compare §5.5 C5, a decision recorded twice).
- **A short act read through a prepared text (an instance of F21, C1a):** the act is "**"Accept KOS-ARCH-BASELINE-002 strategic model."**" [S0065 L7]. The registration reads it as signing the text the reviewer "left **prepared, unsigned**" [S0065 L9]. It registers as in force that ADR-AIP-01's table "is hereby amended" [S0065 L27], citing the deliverables' own banner. The act's six words state neither. **Int:** the scope of the act is filled in from the object's own terms and from a draft. That is an interpretive reading by the registrar, in the sense of F21. The draft itself says "a suggested text signs nothing" [S0064 L183]. This is the case C1a leaves open: whether an interpretation that adds content changes what is in force.
- **Acceptance without independent verification:** "the PO/ARB accepted without a separate independent verification of the v2 model — the acceptance rests on the Principal (human) review and the PO/ARB's own authority" [S0065 L20]. **Three facts, kept apart (PT, Principal testimony 2026-10-19 [PT-001], for the first two; the record for the third):**
  1. **Authorship:** an AI wrote the S0064 reviews.
  2. **Agreement:** the Principal agreed with them. That is a human act on an AI-authored review, not a review written or performed by a human.
  3. **Independent human verification:** none is shown, and the record says so ("without a separate independent verification").

  "The Principal (human) review" in S0065 is therefore half true. The agreement does not validate the review's claims; each keeps its own evidence status.
- **The PO/ARB act, assessed on the record only (PT-001 adds nothing to it):**
  - **who performed it:** the record names "PO/ARB" [S0065 L9]. Which person is not shown;
  - **whether it was a human act:** the record calls it "the delivered Human/PO/ARB act" [S0065 L3]. That is a recorded claim (Ch. 1 §1.12); an attestation is not shown;
  - **its scope:** the act's words are six [S0065 L7]. The further content registered as in force (the ADR-AIP-01 amendment, the "does not authorize" list) comes from the prepared draft and the deliverables' own terms (C6 above, F21). That the act itself stated it is not shown. S0065 is not edited. **Int (A1):** acceptance needs the act. Verification is evidence the act may require. Here it was offered as a routing choice and not taken. Producer ≠ verifier governs who may verify, and does not make verification a precondition of every acceptance.
- **Caveats dropped at acceptance:**
  - Governance recorded that verification #2 ran outside any governed lane, "so it is weighed at acceptance, not discovered afterwards" [S0058 L51]. The acceptance lists verification #2 as completed and does not weigh the caveat [S0061 L19];
  - the acceptance of the v2 model quotes the first-pass verdict "**VERIFIED WITH NOTES**" [S0065 L16], although the registration had declared that pass superseded [S0064 L5].
  **Int:** a caveat recorded for an act does not reach the act by itself. This is the failure case of Ch. 9 §9.5 (a recorded rule must reach the actor), here for a caveat. *Two cases, one estate, one day.*
- **A review of the wrong object:** a first review "was for the wrong artifact", and its author superseded it [S0064 L5]. **Int (§8.5 C1):** a review must name its object before its verdict is worth anything. Here the registration kept the first pass as history and recorded the correction "by its author's own declaration".

**C7. Points added after the C10 completeness test (Src; Int; provisional, not yet reviewed).** *Reported missing by the test reviewer (TEST-C10-planted-gaps, findings 4, 5, 12 and the coverage table); the book search found no home for these.*
- **The approved object is a composite (finding 4).** The Stage-1 approval covers rev 3, rev 2 and rev 1, "**as repaired** by R-1…R-8", with the decisions and the landed Q-B1/Q-B6 [S0053 L18–23]. It also records that note V-1, an imprecision in the repair's supersession accounting, "does not affect any closure and **no document was edited** in response" [S0053 L48]. **Int (restricts C3):** the approved text exists at no single commit. It is a stack whose boundary is drawn by supersession accounting, and that accounting was known to be imperfect when the act was taken. "Pinned" in C3 therefore means each part is pinned; the whole is defined by the accounting. *Home of the accounting defect:* §8.5 C8.
- **The order of acts in a conditional approval (finding 5).** Q-B1/Q-B6 "LANDED after the repair pass" [S0052 L85], and the independent verification of the repaired design was accepted afterwards [S0052 L87]. The repair itself had been reviewed only as "P-2 Class-A … not a fresh independent review" [S0051 L121]. **Int (refines C2):** the landing judgement rested on a producer-reviewed repair; the independent check came later; the final design approval came after both [S0053 L23]. So "a second act lands the condition" was in fact three acts in sequence. *Open:* what evidence must precede a landing act.
- **Accept, approve, establish: three words, three acts (finding 12).** The Human's acceptance of the review "is a decision-recording act only" [S0052 L50], distinct from approving the design. And an event was renamed from `AssessmentAccepted` to `AssessmentEstablished` [S0051 L66], so that the epistemic state is not read as a business acceptance. **Int:** C3's "accepts or approves" merges two of them. The book keeps them apart: acceptance of a deliverable (C3), approval of a design (C4), and establishment of an epistemic state (Ch. 9 §9.4).
- **What the act does not do, listed (coverage table).** The approval lists that INV-ATTR-4/5/6 "remain CANDIDATE / NOT ADOPTED … not adopted by this act", that there is no implementation, and that "**Stage 2 is not opened by this approval**" [S0053 L45–47]. **Int (extends C4):** an act's negative scope is part of what it covers. The Stage-2 line also shows a gate across work items: one work item's acceptance is a precondition of another's assignment (compare §5.6 C6, S0061 L51).

## 5.6.3 Open questions

- Should every act in the kernel's record carry an explicit object (what it covers) and level (C1, C4)?
- Is "the object must exist when the act is performed" a law of the kernel (C3)?
- **AI authors, a human agrees (Int, open; from PT-001):** is an agreement an act in the sense of A1 (Ch. 1 §1.6)? Can it replace a review, or does it only adopt one? In the terms of §1.12 (a record ≠ the act) and §1.14.3 (roles), the agreement is a decision by the Principal, the review is a production by an AI, and verification would be a third role nobody performed here. Which of these an acceptance needs is not decided.

## 5.6.4 What does not hold

- **CON-0124 as first stated:** "the object of an act must exist and be fixed when the act is performed". INVALID as a law of acts: grants permit future activity, and Q-C1 and conditional approvals cover objects not yet written (review C10, finding 2).
- **§5.6 C1's first reading,** that the sources treat conclusion and argument as separable in the approval. They joined them (review C10, finding 3).
- **"Whether that review was human is UNKNOWN"** (S0065's acceptance, first version; no concept ID). It had rested on S0164 L1119–1135 (review C11/T3, finding 10). Withdrawn at the consolidation settlement S0036–S0065 on the Principal's testimony PT-001: the S0064 reviews were written by AI, and the Principal agreed with them; these are two facts.
