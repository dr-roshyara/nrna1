## 5.7.1 In plain language

A piece of work is closed only when both the work itself and the record of it say so. Prose that calls something finished is not enough. A lane that produced nothing may not be marked complete. A supersession has to be recorded; it may not be inferred. A recommendation authorizes nothing. And once a model is accepted, it is reopened only on new evidence.

## 5.7.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Closure needs both the deliverable and the lifecycle evidence (Src; an extension of §6.6 C4).**
- The rule applied: "Close only work whose substantive deliverable and lifecycle evidence both justify closure; do not close merely because prose says it is finished." [S0079 L4]. And "no START or HANDOFF manufactured retroactively · no off-record execution converted into on-record execution" [S0079 L5].
- **A lane that did nothing is not completed:** completing the ineligible refinement lane "would assert it finished work it never did" [S0079 L57].
- **Int (§6.6 C4):** completion is a closure act on a record. It asserts that the work happened. A COMPLETE on a lane with no output would be a false record of an occurrence, and §1.12 already separates the record from the act it reports.
- **The drift that the rule corrects, a count only:** "work completed in prose but not closed in the record" accounted for 15 of the 19 open lanes [S0079 L74]. *Count only* (entry 46); the lanes are the whole portfolio of one day, not a sample.

**C2. A verification that ran off the record, a second instance (Src; an instance of §8.6 C3).**
- "**The Verification #3 lane was never STARTed on the record.** … The work ran off-record, and the closure therefore closes a lane the record never shows as active" [S0076 L57]. Nothing was back-dated, and "the acceptance stands on the report's substance" [S0076 L59].
- **Int:** this is the second verification in the estate accepted without a governed START, after verification #2 of BASELINE-001 (§8.6 C3, S0058 L49). The acceptance carries the defect as a stated qualification: "permanent qualifications on this chain's assurance, disclosed at the moment of acceptance rather than after it" [S0077 L41].
- **Ded (§1.12):** the record shows no START, so for the record the verification has no lifecycle. Its content is evidence; its standing as an assurance step rests on the acceptance act, which names the gap.

**C3. A supersession may not be inferred (Src; an extension of §8.5 C8 and I-7).**
- A stale verification lane is "Plausibly superseded …, but **that supersession has never been recorded**" [S0079 L62]. The ARB recommends "**SUPERSEDED/CANCEL unless evidence says it must run** — *"do not infer"* supersession" [S0080 L47].
- **Int (I-7):** supersession is a link, never an edit. **It also has to exist as a record.** A reader who infers a supersession from plausibility creates a link nobody made. This is §8.5 C8's rule, that a supersession must be named where the reader looks, taken one step back: it must first be recorded at all.
- **The vocabulary has gaps:** "`CANCEL` is terminal in this engine — there is no un-cancel edge" and "The engine has no "retire" transition" [S0080 L46–48]. *Int:* a lifecycle whose vocabulary lacks a needed ending forces the ending into a governance registration outside the record. §6.5 C9 records the same gap for endings that never ran.

**C4. Capability first, people and agents last (Src; for the boundary questions).**
- The ARB's guard: the governing question is "**What capabilities does a sustainable AI Engineering Platform require?**", evaluated in the order "capability → ownership → bounded context / stewardship → human role → agent → shared platform service" [S0080 L28–33]. It is registered "before commissioning, so the discovery cannot drift into org design" [S0080 L36].
- **Int (RESEARCH-PURPOSE, §9.3 C4):** this is the order the book's boundary questions assume. A capability is tested first, and who or what provides it comes after. Modelling an agent or role first would decide a boundary by staffing.
- **And a recommendation authorizes nothing:** "a recommendation is never an authorization" (ES-001.2), so the four dispositions the ARB recommends "are NOT executed by this registration" [S0080 L4].

**C5. Stability of an accepted model (Src; for the "stable" test).**
- "once a bounded-context model has been accepted, subsequent work should be driven by new evidence or explicit change pressure, not by endless architectural polishing" [S0080 L57].
- **Int (RESEARCH-PURPOSE, the stability test):** an accepted model changes only by an act on evidence. This is A1 for models: no change of standing without an act, and an act needs a reason that is new evidence or recorded pressure. *Hyp (one source):* that this is a general rule for kernel-level models.
- **Two messages, one acceptance, again:** a five-act instruction and a fuller formal act on the same day are "Registered once, as the governing text of this acceptance" [S0076 L43]. That is the second case of §5.6 C6 (S0061 L5).

**C6. A figure handed on as a check criterion (Src; an instance of §8.8 C2 and P-8).**
- The measurement-provenance issue is routed on, stated as "*a figure stated without its method cannot be reproduced, and a figure handed onward as a check criterion inherits authority it has not earned.*" [S0076 L28].
- **Int (P-8, §1.14.4):** being used as a criterion is an act-free property, so it confers no standing on the figure. This is §8.8 C2's hazard put as a rule about authority.
