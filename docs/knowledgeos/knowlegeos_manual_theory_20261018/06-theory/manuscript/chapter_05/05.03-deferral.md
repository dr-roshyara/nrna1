# 5.3 Deciding in advance: deferral and activation criteria

## 5.3.1 In plain language

Engineering often fails in one of two ways:
- building structure (a service, a layer, an abstraction) before there is evidence it is needed;
- arguing about every proposal from scratch.

The corpus's Deferred Architecture Register (S0029, 2026-08-04, a derived view) aims to avoid both by **deciding in advance**. Each postponed idea gets a written trigger, the engineering event that would justify building it. Until then, something smaller protects the need.

That is the register's **aim**. Its own log shows the practice falling short. Triggers were revised on the day of use, and most activations came from commissions and incidents, not from triggers.

## 5.3.2 Definitions

- **Deferral with an activation criterion (Src).** "A deferred concept has an explicit engineering activation criterion." [S0029 L9]. Each row has three parts [L20]:
  - the **current invariant**: what protects the need today;
  - the **activation criterion**: an engineering event;
  - the **evidence required**.

  Rows are to change "only when a criterion FIRES (an engineering event) or an authority re-rules a criterion — never by re-reasoning" [L10]. `[CON-0077 · validated]` (as a design)
- **Gate by risk (Src).** "every gate protects against a SPECIFIC failure mode, which is what justifies it later" [L14]. Six families are named, capped at "~5–6" [L14]. The verdict splits in two:
  - **the closed family list with its cap** `[CON-0078 · invalid]`: the register's actual gates include friction [L23], duration [L30] and demand [L66], which fit none of the six. The cap is a judgement. (The volume gate [L27] may fit "sufficient dataset", L14.)
  - **"a gate is justified by the failure mode it prevents"** stands as a design principle `[CON-0187 · provisional]`.
- **Three questions (Src).** "Can we build it?" · "Should we build it now?" ("deterministic ≠ demanded") · "Should it join the platform?" [L16]. The source calls them "ORTHOGONAL". `[CON-0079 · validated]` (orthogonality of the answers)
  - **Ded:** the answers are logically independent: every combination of yes and no is possible (demanded but not yet buildable; buildable but not demanded; either, and not reusable). What is ordered is the **asking**: one asks "now?" usefully only of what can be built. That is an order of work, not a dependence between the answers.
  - **Activation is a conjunction:** "A row activates only when the first two are yes; the third is a separate, later decision" [L16].
- **Pre-specified decision rule (Ded, the book's term).** A rule, fixed before the evidence arrives, that says which observation triggers which decision. (This is not a "stopping rule", which in sequential analysis decides when to stop sampling.)

## 5.3.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. A criterion fixed in advance is the decision-side form of a fixed counting class (Int: an analogy), and the register does not achieve it (Src).**
- **The analogy.** Ch. 4 §4.1 C3 showed that a count is meaningful only if the class, the membership test and the unit are fixed before the instances are classified. A pre-specified decision rule is the same safeguard for decisions. Written before the evidence arrives, it stops the evidence being reinterpreted to fit a wish to build. "Never by re-reasoning" [L10] states it.
- **The record falls short:**
  - gates were revised on the day of use ("OVER-APPLIED", L43, L59; a criterion "over-strict", L40);
  - rows were created at the moment of activation [L63–64];
  - the threshold policies point opposite ways: "no threshold invented before data" [L37], beside candidate values of ≥100 cycles and ≥50 outcomes set at "today: 1 cycle" [L29];
  - setting a threshold *from* baseline data is **calibration**, not pre-specification.
- **A related case (S0030):** the AI-workflow log names its measurements, seven cost signals [S0030 L14–24], before any entry exists. It sets **no** threshold or decision rule, so "This log EARNS the future orchestrator, or it doesn't." [S0030 L5] cannot fail. It is a measurement plan, not a decision rule. It also makes itself "the ONLY path" to that decision [S0030 L7], excluding outside evidence; this **might not be valid** (TODO T5-06).

**C2. The `init` dispute is about the scope of the claim (Ded).**
- **The dispute.** S0024 and S0025 judged `knowledgeos init` premature, as an automated **bootstrap of a new product** done once (Ch. 5 §5.2). Two days later S0029 judged the second-adopter gate "OVER-APPLIED" to "automation of deterministic work" and built `init` **for this repository** [L43, L59].
- **Resolution by scope.** The rule "n ≥ 2 before structure" (Ch. 1 §1.8, CON-0076) governs claims of **reuse across contexts**. Automating a procedure **in the context where it was performed** makes no such claim. Both sources can then be right:
  - S0029's local `init` claims nothing about reuse;
  - S0024 and S0025's objection stands for `init` as a bootstrap of other products.
- **The source agrees:** extending `init` to another repository is "the second adopter again" [L43], and reusable-platform extraction stays behind "THE second-adopter gate" [L44].
- **"Deterministic" is not "generic."** A procedure can be fully repeatable and fully bound to one product. "Idempotent on this repo" [L59] tests only the source repository. Which of its steps are generic is an inferred split of the kind Ch. 6 §6.1 C1 describes (a forward reference).
- **Not covered:** further components built on the same day at n=1 by commission, such as the observation runtime, `knowledgeos dev` and the provisioner slices [L60–65]. The scope argument covers them only if they too make no reuse claim.

**C3. Activation came from three sources, and none was an independent criterion (Src, counted here).**

| Row | Source line | Activated by |
|---|---|---|
| Track A (`init`) | L59 | a review's correction, "accepted" [L43]: reasoning, which L10 forbids, unless an authority accepted it (a permitted re-ruling, L10); who accepted it is not stated: **undetermined** |
| FileSaved / IDE trigger | L60 | "AUTHORITY re-ruling, not a criterion fire" |
| IDE-extension adapter | L61 | authority commission |
| Real-time adapter | L62 | "FIRST CRITERION-FIRED TRANSITION", but the criterion ("an IDE-extension adapter arriving") fired **because** of the commission in L61 |
| Session ownership | L63 | "INCIDENT-FIRED demand" (no row existed) |
| ClaudeCodeTrigger | L64 | incident + commission (no row existed) |
| Provisioner | L65 | "PARTIALLY ACTIVE"; no activating event stated |

- **Int:** in the register's first day:
  - **no** activation came from an independently fired criterion;
  - acts (commissions, re-rulings) and incidents did the activating.
- An incident is evidence **without** a pre-specified criterion. When it alone triggers building, it sits uneasily with the book's rule that evidence does not by itself effect an act (Ch. 1 §1.6). Unless an unrecorded act stands behind it, incidents are a third kind of activation.
- The register's body also disagrees with its own log: two rows still read STAGED [L37, L41] after the log marks them ACTIVE [L60, L62].

**C4. The register breaks its own rules (Src).**
- **The standing rule:** nothing new is built "unless a documented activation criterion below is actually met" [L12]. Two rows were activated without any row or criterion [L63–64].
- **The update rule:** rows change "never by re-reasoning" [L10]. The Track A correction is reasoning [L59]. Whether it breaks the rule is **undetermined**: an authority's acceptance would be a permitted re-ruling, and the acceptor is not named [L43].
- **The regeneration rule** was not applied: stale rows remain [L37, L41, L74].
- "nothing is minted here" [L9] sits beside new gate families and a cap.
- **Int:** whether commissions count as a lawful second path, beside criteria, is the same open question as Ch. 1 §1.13's "second lawful authorization channel" (D-2).

**C5. A view that states rules (Src, with Int).**
- The register calls itself a projection, "derived, non-authoritative, regenerable; on conflict the cited source wins" [L7].
- Yet it opens with a "CONSTITUTIONAL" purpose and a "Standing rule" [L3, L12], both attributed to a review of 2026-08-04.
- By §3.5 (a projection is cited as authority by nothing), these rules bind only through that review record, which has not been read.

**C6. Two different gates: precision and identification (Ded).**
- "analytics on n≈1 analyzes noise" [L28]. Learning is gated on candidate values of ≥100 cycles and ≥50 assessed outcomes [L29]. These are **precision** gates: enough data to estimate.
- They do not answer Ch. 4 §4.1 C4's problem, which is **identification**: without a baseline or comparison, a hundred uncontrolled cycles still cannot estimate a guidance effect.
- A sound gate needs both. The candidate values themselves are not derived from any power or stability argument.
