# 5.4 The gap operator: what a decision still lacks

*From research branch BRANCH-01 (2026-10-19), revised after its independent transition review (`review/BRANCH-01-zero-gap/REVIEW-REPORT.md`).*
- *Sources:* dated 2026-08-22 to 2026-08-28, far ahead in the reading order (S0208, S0721, S0896, S1296). They have been consulted but not yet read in order, and this section will be revisited when they are reached.
- *Status:* the concepts here are **provisional** (Amendment 32a).
- *Notation:* formulas without quotation marks are reproduced in meaning from LaTeX in the sources.

## 5.4.1 In plain language

Before a piece of work can go ahead, certain things must be known or done: the current version, a tested rollback, an approval. The corpus's late "Zero" concept is a way to say, for each of those things, **whether it is in place and, if not, in what way**:
- not known;
- known to have failed;
- conflicting;
- out of date;
- not yet done;
- forbidden.

These call for different responses. A single "no" loses that information, and so does a single number that forgets which requirement is in which state.

The word "Zero" has three meanings in the corpus; this section is about one of them.

## 5.4.2 Definitions

- **Three referents of "Zero" (Src).** The corpus's own synthesis keeps them apart: "Kinship acknowledged; identity denied; register enforced." [S1296 L232–233]. The three:
  - **Z1, neutral origin:** a state before any transformation [S0208 L512–520].
  - **Z2, the absence lens:** "Zero is a **lens applied to the knowledge state**" [S0721 L49].
    - Its distinctions are older than this file. The 2026-08-22 source already lists UNKNOWN ≠ FALSE, UNKNOWN ≠ INVALID and UNKNOWN ≠ EMPTY [S0208 L189–191].
    - Caution: S0208 is a **prompt** that prescribes its own outcome table [S0208 L5, L99–111, L420–427]. Its rule that Zero is "a research lens, not a kernel primitive" [S0208 L23] is the prompt-writer's instruction, not a finding.
  - **Z3, the gap operator** (2026-08-27): the subject of this section.
- **Requirements and contract (Src).** A goal *G* induces required conditions *R_G* = {r₁ … rₙ}. An **epistemic contract** gives the requirements and their sufficiency rules [S0896 L57–116]. "Zero cannot be computed from the goal alone." [S0896 L105]
- **The gap operator (Src, made precise here).** `[CON-0080 · provisional]`
  - Each requirement *r* has a **status** and a **criticality**. The status set has at least eleven values:
    - Satisfied, PartiallySatisfied, Unknown, Insufficient, Conflicted, Stale, Invalid, Prohibited, NotApplicable [S0896 L155–172];
    - **Missing**, for an expected governed artifact or action [S0896 L247–285];
    - **Failed**, known not to be satisfied ("This is different from Unknown") [S0896 L847–881].
  - The gap Z is the set of items (requirement, status, criticality, evidence, reason) for the requirements not satisfied [S0896 L1237–1276].
  - **The operator detects; it does not plan.** Next actions are left to a separate component; "Zero detects; Lord acts" is the source's own boundary [S0896 L1270–1276, L1281–1315].
  - The source withdrew "distance" as a metric: the operator is a directed discrepancy, "not necessarily a metric" [S0896 L1143–1215].

## 5.4.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. What the gap operator answers, and what it does not (Ded).**
- Ch. 0 §0.9 Q3 asks whether "Zero" and "Gap" formalize **unsatisfiable needs** and **kinds of empty answer**.
- **Kinds of empty answer:** the **absence lens (Z2)** answers this, not the gap operator. Its distinctions feed Ch. 2 §2.8.8 (C4 below).
- **Needs:** the gap operator answers how a need is **represented**: relative to a contract, one item per requirement.
- **"Unsatisfiable" stays unformalized.** The operator records what is **unsatisfied now**, not what **cannot** be satisfied. Prohibited, or Failed under a fixed plan, come closest. That "unsatisfiable" is a union of kinds was already established in Ch. 0 §0.7 and Ch. 2 §2.8.8, before this branch.
- **A requirement is not always knowledge.** *R_G* contains acts (an approval) and states of the world (security requirements met), not only "which understanding must exist" in Ch. 0's sense.
- **A conflict with the book's own rule.** The source's Unknown means no evidence, Evidence(r) = ∅ [S0896 L256–262]. That merges E2 (unspecified), E3 (not looked at), E4 (no carrier) and E5 (does not exist), which Ch. 2 §2.8.8 requires to be kept apart ("gap evidence must record which"). Adopted as it stands, the gap operator would reintroduce that collapse. It must split Unknown first.

**C2. The decision needs criticality as well as status (Ded).**
- **The source's rule.** Execution is permitted only if every **blocking** requirement is satisfied [S0896 L781–784]. Under that rule, *proceed* holds exactly when the count of unsatisfied **blocking** requirements is zero, and the source lists such a count among its derived measures [S0896 L671–709]. So a count **can** decide, if it counts within the right class.
- **What does not decide** is a count, or a score, built from the statuses alone, without each requirement's **criticality**. Two states with the same multiset of statuses, one with Unknown on a minor requirement and one with Unknown on a blocking requirement, give different answers. That proof is valid, and simple.
- **The correct statement:** the decision depends on the multiset of **(status, criticality)** pairs. A summary that drops criticality cannot decide in general.
- This is a case of Ch. 4 §4.1 C3, which requires the class counted to be fixed in advance: here, the class is "blocking requirements".
- The synthesis puts the point well: "a metric may summarize Zero; it may never BE Zero" [S1296 L181].

**C3. The status set mixes kinds (Src + Ded).** `[CON-0081 · invalid]` The source of the absence lens itself warned that the list "should not automatically become a single enum" [S0721 L252–290].

| Kind | Statuses |
|---|---|
| epistemic: what is known | Unknown · Insufficient · Conflicted · Stale · Invalid |
| outcome: known result | Failed · PartiallySatisfied (may be a state of the world rather than of knowledge) |
| institutional: whether an act was done | Missing |
| normative: what is allowed | Prohibited |
| scope | NotApplicable |
| (success) | Satisfied |

- **Missing, the serious problem.** Assigning Missing asserts that the approval **does not exist**, which needs knowledge of absence (E1). If the approval was merely **not found** (E3), the book's authority evaluation gives "cannot determine", not "no" (Ch. 1 §1.14.1, §1.17.3: not found ≠ unauthorized). Neither source gives a rule for telling the two apart.
- **A secondary point:** an approval given but not recorded is, under A1 (only recorded acts change standing), without effect either way. The difference matters only for the remedy: make the record, or make the act.
- **The ratified four-arm summary** (unknown, conflicting, missing, invalid) belongs to the corpus's later synthesis [S1296 L136–149], not to the source. How many statuses it drops depends on whether NotApplicable and Failed are counted.

**C4. The absence lens and the book's kinds of nothing (Src).**
- **Confirmed:**
  - UNKNOWN ≠ ABSENT [S0721 L192–198] is E2/E3 ≠ E1;
  - NOT_ASSESSED ≠ LOW_CONFIDENCE [S0721 L497–503] confirms E3; uncertainty is not a kind of nothing;
  - NOT_APPLICABLE ≠ UNKNOWN [S0721 L537–545] confirms the **distinction** E7 ≠ E2, but not E7's governed character, which Ch. 2 adopted on purpose. The source decides applicability case by case.
- **Candidate E9, invalid evidence** [S0721 L449–456] `[CON-0082 · provisional]`. Raw evidence is not empty; only the **valid** evidence is. Whether this is a new kind of nothing depends on which test Ch. 2 uses for a "kind":
  - "the remedies differ": then E9 is distinct, since its remedy is to re-assess, not to obtain;
  - "a reason for the empty set, not a new way of being empty": then E9 is E2 with a recorded reason.

  Ch. 2 uses both tests, so the test must be fixed first. The boundary with E6(c), "unreadable", is also unsettled for malformed evidence.
- **Candidate E10, unknown dimension** [S0721 L294–330] `[CON-0083 · provisional]`. The distinction holds logically: a dimension missing from the set is not a dimension with an unknown value. Three limits:
  1. **Which set.** The source means **content** dimensions, under its hypothesis of an unbounded knowledge space [S0721 L93–134]. Ch. 2's dimensions are **scope** dimensions of rules. Which set is meant must be said.
  2. **Its licence.** E10 licenses only "no known gap", never "complete" (the source's ZI-10 [S0721 L1293–1353]).
  3. **It lies outside the gap operator by design.** the source's boxed law, completeness requires a defined universe of discourse [S0721 L1419–1423]; the contract is that universe; and the operator reports no gap outside it. So E10 belongs to the derivation of the contract, not to the operator.

  Adding a discovered dimension turns earlier results into "cannot determine". The source calls this a "recalculation trigger" [S0721 L400–445]; compare Ch. 2 §2.8.6.
- **Further distinctions in S0721 not yet in the book** [S0721 L1293–1353]: "previously unknown ≠ previously absent", a temporal kind of nothing the list lacks; "no known gap ≠ complete"; "conflict ≠ invalidity". These are to be assessed when S0721 is read in order.

**C5. Two gaps: knowledge and world (Src).** "Operational Zero" (the world against its target) differs from "epistemic Zero" (knowledge against the contract) [S0896 L520–550]. Even fully understood work can be blocked by an epistemic gap [S0896 L552–597].
- The source's word "distance" was later withdrawn as a metric (5.4.2).
- The corpus's later synthesis keeps only the epistemic gap [S1296 L214–219].
- **Int:** this is Ch. 1 §1.6's distinction between knowing and acting, applied to gaps.

**C6. Requirements may depend on one another (Src, hedged as in the source).** The source suggests that a requirement may imply others, and that a requirement graph "will eventually be needed" [S0896 L1500–1526].
- If r₃ requires r₁ and r₂, reporting r₃ as well is **redundant**, not wrong. The harm falls on attributing the remedy, not on the decision (by C2, counts do not decide anyway).
- A dependency structure gives an order only if it is acyclic (Ch. 3 §3.8 C2).

**C7. The Principal's later direction proposes the same operator (Ext; role: benchmark of intent, not evidence; PROVISIONAL).**
- *Scope, stated first:* the brainstorms EXT-013 to EXT-016 (2026-09-24) are external to the corpus and are direction, not evidence. They are compared here; nothing in them confirms §5.4.
- **The same idea under the same structure (Int):**
  - a gap Δ_t is the set of requirements a knowledge state does not satisfy, relative to an epistemic contract EC_t;
  - Zero holds exactly when Δ_t is empty (EXT-013 L32–34; EXT-014 L477–482);
  - satisfaction must be defined per kind of requirement (EXT-015 L122–185).

  This matches CON-0080 and "Zero cannot be computed from the goal alone" (5.4.2).
- **One point where the direction conflicts with C2 (a fork):**
  - The direction orders states by "improvement iff Δ_{t+1} ⊆ Δ_t" (EXT-013 L35–37), and later by the expected size of the gap (EXT-014 L253–263).
  - By C2, a decision needs (status, criticality) pairs. The size of the gap drops criticality, and inclusion ignores it.
  - So the direction's improvement order does not decide by itself. The corpus position of C2 stands; the outside position does not win automatically (Rulebook §4).
- **A typology to compare, not adopt:** the direction lists ten gap kinds: coverage, value, uncertainty, warrant, contradiction, model, observability, temporal, identity, representation (EXT-013 L106–115). They are not mapped onto C3's kinds of status here. Mapping them is a task for when S0896 is read in order.

## 5.4.4 Open questions

- **Before CON-0080 can be validated:**
  - split Unknown by E2–E6;
  - require E1-grade absence before assigning Missing;
  - define the status as a tuple over the kinds of C3, or state the mixing;
  - reconcile it with Ch. 5's decision-readiness (CON-0012) and gap kinds (CON-0053).
- **For E9:** which test makes a kind of nothing (C4)?
- **For E10:** which set of dimensions, and does the concept need the unbounded-space hypothesis (C4)?
- **ContractDerivation:** where the contract comes from is open in the source itself [S0896 L1237–1276; S1296 L299–303].
