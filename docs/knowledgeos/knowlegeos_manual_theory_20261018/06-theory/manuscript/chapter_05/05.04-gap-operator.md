# 5.4 The gap operator: what a decision still lacks

*From research branch BRANCH-01 (2026-10-19), revised after its independent transition review (`review/BRANCH-01-zero-gap/REVIEW-REPORT.md`).*
- *Sources:* dated 2026-08-22 to 2026-08-28, far ahead in the reading order (S0208, S0721, S0896, S1296). They have been consulted but not yet read in order, and this section will be revisited when they are reached.
- *Status:* the concepts here are **provisional** (Amendment 32a).
- *Notation:* formulas without quotation marks are reproduced in meaning from LaTeX in the sources.

## 5.4.1 In plain language

Before a piece of work can go ahead, certain things must be known or done: the current version, a tested rollback, an approval. The corpus's late "Zero" concept is a way to say, for each of those things, **whether it is in place and, if not, in what way**:
- not known;
- known to have failed;
- conflicting;
- out of date;
- not yet done;
- forbidden.

These call for different responses. A single "no" loses that information, and so does a single number that forgets which requirement is in which state.

The word "Zero" has four meanings in the corpus (three until the consolidation settlement S0036–S0065 added Z4); this section is about one of them.

## 5.4.2 Definitions

- **Three referents of "Zero" (Src).** The corpus's own synthesis keeps them apart: "Kinship acknowledged; identity denied; register enforced." [S1296 L232–233]. The three:
  - **Z1, neutral origin:** a state before any transformation [S0208 L512–520].
  - **Z2, the absence lens:** "Zero is a **lens applied to the knowledge state**" [S0721 L49].
    - Its distinctions are older than this file. The 2026-08-22 source already lists UNKNOWN ≠ FALSE, UNKNOWN ≠ INVALID and UNKNOWN ≠ EMPTY [S0208 L189–191].
    - Caution: S0208 is a **prompt** that prescribes its own outcome table [S0208 L5, L99–111, L420–427]. Its rule that Zero is "a research lens, not a kernel primitive" [S0208 L23] is the prompt-writer's instruction, not a finding.
  - **Z3, the gap operator** (2026-08-27): the subject of this section. Z3 is itself not one thing. Kept apart here:
    - **the operator**, a set: Z_t = Zero(K_t, EC_t), the gap items [S0896 L1237–1262];
    - **Zero-all**, a predicate: Δ_t = ∅, every requirement satisfied [S2377 L356–384]; RESEARCH-PURPOSE's "Zero" is this one;
    - **Zero-blocking**, a predicate: every **blocking** requirement satisfied, Zero_blocking = ∅ [S0896 L800–808]; it is the condition for execution eligibility (C2).

    Zero-all implies Zero-blocking, not conversely. CON-0080 names the operator; the two predicates are derived from it.
  - **Z4, unresolved conflict:** in S0913, Zero "can represent" an unresolved conflict, a state where the evidence does not justify selecting one proposition (S0913 L1165–1174, in meaning). This is close to the opposite of Z3's predicates (the contract, or its blocking part, met). So the word has four meanings, not three.
  - **Z4 re-tested after the split (Ded):** Z4 is a state of the evidence for one proposition; Zero-all and Zero-blocking are states of a contract. Z4 corresponds, at most, to one requirement's status Conflicted, not to either predicate. So Z4 stays a separate sense.
- **Requirements and contract (Src).** A goal *G* induces required conditions *R_G* = {r₁ … rₙ}. An **epistemic contract** gives the requirements and their sufficiency rules [S0896 L57–116]. "Zero cannot be computed from the goal alone." [S0896 L105]
- **The gap operator (Src, made precise here).** `[CON-0080 · provisional]`
  - Each requirement *r* has a **status** and a **criticality**. The status set has at least eleven values:
    - Satisfied, PartiallySatisfied, Unknown, Insufficient, Conflicted, Stale, Invalid, Prohibited, NotApplicable [S0896 L155–172];
    - **Missing**, for an expected governed artifact or action [S0896 L247–285];
    - **Failed**, known not to be satisfied ("This is different from Unknown") [S0896 L847–881].
  - The gap Z is the set of items (requirement, status, criticality, evidence, reason) for the requirements not satisfied [S0896 L1237–1276].
  - **The operator detects; it does not plan.** Next actions are left to a separate component; "Zero detects; Lord acts" is the source's own boundary [S0896 L1270–1276, L1281–1315]. *"Lord"* is the sources' name for that acting component (S0896, S2377); it is a role in the source's model, not a concept of this book.
- **"Requirement" here:** an epistemic requirement of a contract EC_t, met or not by the knowledge state (Sat). In Ch. 9 §9.1, a "required capability" is a state that must be reachable. The two are different objects. Whether "Zero(EC) is reachable" is itself a member of the kernel's Req is open (T9-01).
  - The source withdrew "distance" as a metric: the operator is a directed discrepancy, "not necessarily a metric" [S0896 L1143–1215].

## 5.4.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. What the gap operator answers, and what it does not (Ded).**
- Ch. 0 §0.9 Q3 asks whether "Zero" and "Gap" formalize **unsatisfiable needs** and **kinds of empty answer**.
- **Kinds of empty answer:** the **absence lens (Z2)** answers this, not the gap operator. Its distinctions feed Ch. 2 §2.8.8 (C4 below).
- **Needs:** the gap operator answers how a need is **represented**: relative to a contract, one item per requirement.
- **"Unsatisfiable" stays unformalized.** The operator records what is **unsatisfied now**, not what **cannot** be satisfied. Prohibited, or Failed under a fixed plan, come closest. That "unsatisfiable" is a union of kinds was already established in Ch. 0 §0.7 and Ch. 2 §2.8.8, before this branch.
- **A requirement is not always knowledge.** *R_G* contains acts (an approval) and states of the world (security requirements met), not only "which understanding must exist" in Ch. 0's sense.
- **A conflict with the book's own rule.** The source's Unknown means no evidence, Evidence(r) = ∅ [S0896 L256–262]. That merges E2 (unspecified), E3 (not looked at), E4 (no carrier) and E5 (does not exist), which Ch. 2 §2.8.8 requires to be kept apart ("gap evidence must record which"). Adopted as it stands, the gap operator would reintroduce that collapse. It must split Unknown first.
- **A transfer check is required:** Ch. 2's E-list was built for the scope dimensions of rules. Applying it to evidence sets needs the check C4 asks for (which set of dimensions; which test makes a kind of nothing). Until it is done, this use of the E-list is provisional.

**C2. Execution eligibility needs criticality as well as status (Ded).**
- **The source's rule.** Execution is permitted only if every **blocking** requirement is satisfied [S0896 L781–784], and then it is eligible "subject to authorization" [S0896 L800–815]. Under that rule, execution is eligible **only if** the count of unsatisfied **blocking** requirements is zero (Zero-blocking), and the source lists such a count among its derived measures [S0896 L671–709]. So a count **can** settle eligibility, if it counts within the right class. It never settles the decision itself, which is an act (Ch. 1 A1).
- **What does not settle eligibility** is a count, or a score, built from the statuses alone, without each requirement's **criticality**. Two states with the same multiset of statuses, one with Unknown on a minor requirement and one with Unknown on a blocking requirement, give different answers. That proof is valid, and simple.
- **The correct statement:** execution eligibility depends on the multiset of **(status, criticality)** pairs, subject to authorization. A summary that drops criticality cannot settle eligibility in general.
- This is a case of Ch. 4 §4.1 C3, which requires the class counted to be fixed in advance: here, the class is "blocking requirements".
- The synthesis puts the point well: "a metric may summarize Zero; it may never BE Zero" [S1296 L181].

**C3. The status set mixes kinds (Src + Ded).** `[CON-0081 · invalid]` The source of the absence lens itself warned that the list "should not automatically become a single enum" [S0721 L252–290].

| Kind | Statuses |
|---|---|
| epistemic: what is known | Unknown · Insufficient · Conflicted · Stale · Invalid |
| outcome: known result | Failed · PartiallySatisfied (may be a state of the world rather than of knowledge) |
| institutional: whether an act was done | Missing |
| normative: what is allowed | Prohibited |
| scope | NotApplicable |
| (success) | Satisfied |

- **Missing, the serious problem.** Assigning Missing asserts that the approval **does not exist**, which needs knowledge of absence (E1). If the approval was merely **not found** (E3), the book's authority evaluation gives "cannot determine", not "no" (Ch. 1 §1.14.1, §1.17.3: not found ≠ unauthorized). Neither source gives a rule for telling the two apart.
- **A secondary point:** an approval given but not recorded is, under A1 (only recorded acts change standing), without effect either way. The difference matters only for the remedy: make the record, or make the act.
- **The ratified four-arm summary** (unknown, conflicting, missing, invalid) belongs to the corpus's later synthesis [S1296 L136–149], not to the source. How many statuses it drops depends on whether NotApplicable and Failed are counted.

**C4. The absence lens and the book's kinds of nothing (Src).**
- **Confirmed:**
  - UNKNOWN ≠ ABSENT [S0721 L192–198] is E2/E3 ≠ E1;
  - NOT_ASSESSED ≠ LOW_CONFIDENCE [S0721 L497–503] confirms E3; uncertainty is not a kind of nothing;
  - NOT_APPLICABLE ≠ UNKNOWN [S0721 L537–545] confirms the **distinction** E7 ≠ E2, but not E7's governed character, which Ch. 2 adopted on purpose. The source decides applicability case by case.
- **Candidate E9, invalid evidence** [S0721 L449–456] `[CON-0082 · provisional]`. Raw evidence is not empty; only the **valid** evidence is. Whether this is a new kind of nothing depends on which test Ch. 2 uses for a "kind":
  - "the remedies differ": then E9 is distinct, since its remedy is to re-assess, not to obtain;
  - "a reason for the empty set, not a new way of being empty": then E9 is E2 with a recorded reason.

  Ch. 2 uses both tests, so the test must be fixed first. The boundary with E6(c), "unreadable", is also unsettled for malformed evidence.
- **Candidate E10, unknown dimension** [S0721 L294–330] `[CON-0083 · provisional]`. The distinction holds logically: a dimension missing from the set is not a dimension with an unknown value. Three limits:
  1. **Which set.** The source means **content** dimensions, under its hypothesis of an unbounded knowledge space [S0721 L93–134]. Ch. 2's dimensions are **scope** dimensions of rules. Which set is meant must be said.
  2. **Its licence.** E10 licenses only "no known gap", never "complete" (the source's ZI-10 [S0721 L1293–1353]).
  3. **It lies outside the gap operator by design.** the source's boxed law, completeness requires a defined universe of discourse [S0721 L1419–1423]; the contract is that universe; and the operator reports no gap outside it. So E10 belongs to the derivation of the contract, not to the operator.

  Adding a discovered dimension turns earlier results into "cannot determine". The source calls this a "recalculation trigger" [S0721 L400–445]; compare Ch. 2 §2.8.6.
- **Further distinctions in S0721 not yet in the book** [S0721 L1293–1353]: "previously unknown ≠ previously absent", a temporal kind of nothing the list lacks; "no known gap ≠ complete"; "conflict ≠ invalidity". These are to be assessed when S0721 is read in order.

**C5. Two gaps: knowledge and world (Src).** "Operational Zero" (the world against its target) differs from "epistemic Zero" (knowledge against the contract) [S0896 L520–550]. Even fully understood work can be blocked by an epistemic gap [S0896 L552–597].
- The source's word "distance" was later withdrawn as a metric (5.4.2).
- The corpus's later synthesis keeps only the epistemic gap [S1296 L214–219].
- **Int:** this is Ch. 1 §1.6's distinction between knowing and acting, applied to gaps.

**C6. Requirements may depend on one another (Src, hedged as in the source).** The source suggests that a requirement may imply others, and that a requirement graph "will eventually be needed" [S0896 L1500–1526].
- If r₃ requires r₁ and r₂, reporting r₃ as well is **redundant**, not wrong. The harm falls on attributing the remedy, not on eligibility (by C2, only the blocking class settles it).
- A dependency structure gives an order only if it is acyclic (Ch. 3 §3.8 C2).

**C7. The direction restates S2377 (Ext → Src; PROVISIONAL).**
- *Provenance, decided by the Principal (2026-10-19):* the "Gap theory" that EXT-013 compares with (EXT-013 L5, L28) is **S2377**, the corpus document titled "Formal Theory of Epistemic Gaps" [S2377 L13] (2026-09-02). The brainstorms EXT-013 to EXT-016 (2026-09-24) are AI replies that restate and comment on it.
- **Consequence:** what EXT-013 to EXT-016 share with §5.4 is S2377's content, not an outside view. Its match with S0896 is **one line of thought inside the corpus**, not independent confirmation. The comparison below is therefore between two corpus documents, S0896 and S2377 (C8); EXT-013 to EXT-016 add comments on it.
- **What the brainstorms add beyond S2377:** a comparison of three improvement orders with C2 (EXT-014 L181–193, L253–263):
  - **inclusion,** Δ_{t+1} ⊆ Δ_t (S2377's THM-G2; the source adds "subject to preservation of previously valid requirements" [S2377 L982], i.e. no requirement met before may stop being met): compatible with C2 (Ded). No new requirement becomes unsatisfied, so the count of unsatisfied blocking requirements cannot rise, and eligibility cannot be lost. *The preservation condition is redundant under a fixed R:* Δ_{t+1} ⊆ Δ_t already says that no requirement met at t is unmet at t+1. It is needed only when R changes, and then THM-G4's mapping applies. It loses **status** information (Unknown → Failed is invisible inside Δ), which changes the remedy, not eligibility. Most pairs of states are incomparable;
  - **expected size** E|Δ| (EXT-014 L253–263): conflicts with C2, because it drops criticality;
  - **weighted gap** Σ w_r d_r (S2377 L1432–1438): could encode criticality, for example lexicographically. Open.

  The position of C2 stands.

**C8. S2377 against S0896: the same line, extended and changed (Src + Ded; S2377 read in full, topic-full).** `[CON-0101 · provisional]`
- *Scope, stated first:* S0896 (2026-08-27) was consulted in BRANCH-01, not read in full; S2377 (2026-09-02) was read in full. S2377 replies to "the attached derivation" [S2377 L1] and never names S0896, so **dependence is not shown**; the two share vocabulary (EC_t, Zero, Lord) and one programme. The attached derivation also carries S0721's distinctions (unknown value against unknown dimension, L532–536; the unobserved and unobservable kinds, L738), so a shared origin with **S0721** is likely as well. The three are counted as one line of support.
- *Both sources pair a yes/no predicate with a status or a type; they differ in the content of the status list (table below).*
- **What S2377 keeps from S0896, and what it changes:**
  - the gap is a set of requirements, not a difference: "The epistemic gap is the set of currently unsatisfied epistemic requirements." [S2377 L336];
  - **changed, not kept:** S2377's Zero is Zero-all, Zero_t ⟺ Δ_t = ∅ ⟺ K_t ⊨ EC_t [S2377 L356–384]. S0896's zero condition for execution concerns blocking requirements only (Zero-blocking, L800–808), beside the gap set Z_t (L1237–1262). So S2377 moves the predicate from the blocking requirements to all of them;
  - the gap detects and does not act: "The Lord does not determine whether the Gap exists." [S2377 L2020], matching S0896's boundary (5.4.2).
- **Where S2377 differs from S0896:**

| | S0896 (5.4.2) | S2377 |
|---|---|---|
| satisfaction | a yes/no predicate C_i beside a richer typed **status**, at least eleven values [S0896 L760–778] | a yes/no Sat ∈ {0, 1} [S2377 L297], beside a **failure class** for each unsatisfied requirement (G1–G10, L465–830) |
| what weighs a requirement | **criticality** (blocking or not) | a **priority** field and weights w_r [S2377 L219, L1197–1222] |
| Prohibited, NotApplicable | statuses | absent as statuses; applicability is handled partly by a requirement's validity field and by membership in R_t [S2377 L231] |

- **What S2377 adds (Src; checked here):**
  1. **Direction of a gap** [S2377 L1099–1124]: missing knowledge (Δ⁺) against held knowledge that violates a requirement (Δ⁻); simplified to deficit, conflict and insufficiency [L1164–1175].
  2. **Contract compatibility** (THM-G4) [S2377 L1792–1831]: two gaps are comparable only under the same contract, or through an explicit mapping between their requirements. "Otherwise someone could artificially make Zero happen simply by changing the requirements." [S2377 L1788]. This bears on §5.4's open ContractDerivation.
  3. **A larger gap can be progress** [S2377 L2169–2222], a requirement that was not represented becomes visible, and S2377 proposes a second measure, gap observability, and a progress vector [L2226–2279]. **S2377 leaves open whether this fits its own order**, GapReduction ⇒ EpistemicImprovement [S2377 L1744–1746] (a formula, in meaning) and the frozen ⪰ [L2588]. Its own case is a change of R, so there it is no contradiction. Two cases (Ded):
     - *the requirement was not in R_t before* (L2181): the contract changed, so by THM-G4 the two states are not comparable, and there is no contradiction;
     - *the requirement was in R_t, and wrongly judged satisfied*: the gap grows under a fixed contract, the order calls the new state worse, and S2377 calls it progress.

     Which case applies depends on what Sat refers to: satisfied **in the world**, or satisfied **by what the system believes**. That is a new fork, F15.
  4. **The required state has provenance**, including authority [S2377 L1835–1866].
  5. **The gap order is a lattice:** under a fixed contract the gap lives in the power set of the requirements, with ∅ = Zero at the bottom [S2377 L2329–2441]. *(Ded: true and elementary; a power set ordered by inclusion is a lattice.)*
  6. **Classes may overlap:** "we should not falsely claim that these categories are naturally mutually exclusive." [S2377 L2319]
- **Proof status of S2377's "theorems"** (checked step by step):

S2377's own g_obs = 1 − O_r (L1400) does not satisfy d_r = 0 ⟺ Sat, so it cannot be plugged into G without breaking it. The freshness deficit fails it for a requirement with further conditions; for a freshness-only requirement it satisfies d = 0 ⟺ Sat |

- **Authority at the decision step:** Sārathi(Δ, Proposal, Authority) → Decision [S2377 L2054–2060] brings authority in only when deciding, not when detecting. This agrees with Ch. 1 A1: the gap confers no standing.
- **What does not hold or needs care (Ded):**
  - S2377 announces "seven primary gap classes" [S2377 L461] and then defines ten (L465–830). This is a source defect, and nothing here relies on the number.
  - The age formula age_r = t − now [S2377 L1346] has the wrong sign for a past time t. The freshness deficit needs now − t.
  - ⊥ means "unknown" at L525 and "the bottom element" at L2395: one symbol, two meanings.
  - **Binary Sat merges the kinds of Unknown,** the collapse that C1 rejects (Ch. 2 §2.8.8). S2377's failure classes partly restore the distinctions (G1 coverage, G2 value, G7 observability), but not E2–E6 as a set.
  - **Its weights do not replace criticality for eligibility:** G = 0 ⟺ Zero-all holds, but G > 0 does not say whether a blocking requirement is unmet. C2 stands.
- **Its own open list** [S2377 L2633–2650] includes the universal semantics of Sat, whether the gap classes are complete, and whether a canonical scalar gap function exists. Its next problem is how Sat is defined for each class of requirement [S2377 L2758], the question EXT-015 and EXT-016 take up.

**C9. The operator's must-do half (Ded).**
- **"Detects, never decides" is a must-never only.** Three trivial models satisfy it:
  - the **empty contract**: R = ∅, so Δ = ∅ and Zero-all holds vacuously;
  - a contract with **no blocking requirement**: Zero-blocking holds vacuously, so execution is always eligible;
  - an operator that **always returns ∅**.
- **What the source requires beside it** [S0896, in meaning]:
  - **no invented gaps:** Zero compares against declared or derived requirements, "not against arbitrary AI expectations" (L1021–1050); a requirement enters only "through an authorized rule";
  - **reproducibility:** the same inputs and model versions give the same result; Replay(K_t, EC_G) = Z_t (L981–1000).
- **The must-do half (the book's proposal):**
  - **ContractDerivation** must produce the contract: R, and each requirement's criticality. It is open in the source itself (5.4.4);
  - **R and criticality need an owner:** setting them is an act in Ch. 1 A1's sense, since it decides what can block. The sources name none;
  - **the empty contract is reported as "no contract", not as Zero**, and a contract without a blocking requirement is reported as such;
  - **replay** is a stated requirement of the operator, with the model versions recorded.
- *What this does not decide:* who owns ContractDerivation; the owner table in the chapter index (05.00) records none.
