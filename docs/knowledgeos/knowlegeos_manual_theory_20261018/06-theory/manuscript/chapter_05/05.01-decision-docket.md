# 5.1 The decision docket: how a decision is prepared

## In plain language

When the corpus reached the point where answers had been found but not yet adopted, its authors wrote: "The bottleneck has shifted from finding answers to deciding which validated answers become canonical." [S0023 L12; E-S0023 Q4]. Their response was a **docket**: an agenda of one-page decision packages, ordered so that the decision that controls the others comes first. Each package puts the evidence for and against side by side, names the risk of saying yes and of saying no, names who decides, and gives a recommendation. The recommendation is not the decision: "Recommendations only — every decision is the named authority's." [S0023 L5; Q1].

Source: S0023, the ARB Decision Docket, REV 2, 2026-08-03. A generated CANDIDATE, "the agenda, not the outcome" [L6], prepared by the ARB's secretary [L8].

## The decision package `[CON-0048 · validated]` (Src)

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Decision | what is to be decided |
| Why it exists | the gap or risk that makes the decision necessary |
| Supporting / Opposing evidence | both sides, in the same package |
| Risk of adopting / of rejecting | both directions |
| Downstream | what depends on the decision |
| Authority | the named body that decides (ARB, sponsor, DA, "the Authority") |
| Recommendation | the secretary's advice, kept separate from the decision |

[S0023 L16–28, L196]

**Not every package has every field** (no "Why it exists" in Package 8; no "Downstream" in Packages 9 and 10). The concept is therefore the **core**: one decision, evidence on both sides, a named authority, a recommendation that is not the decision.

**Int:** this is the decision-readiness of Ch. 1 §1.17.1 in a concrete form: evidence graded, options separated, unknowns carried, the decision left to the authority. It is **one** instance, produced by the platform about the platform. It does not show the corpus's general claim that 77 rulings "arrived shaped" (Ch. 1, RC-40a). Decision-readiness itself stays `[CON-0012 · invalid]` (no governing record; TODO T1-51).

The rule behind it: "Per EEP: the platform does not approve itself" [S0023 L7; Q3]. This is the axiom of Ch. 1 §1.6 (only acts by authorized humans change standing), applied to the platform's own governance.

## Gating order `[CON-0049 · validated]` (Src)

The docket is "ordered so the decision that gates the others comes first" [L12; Q5]:
- **D-9** (the over-evolution check of AIP-14) comes first, because it decides "whether Packages 2–10 are worked now at all" [L26];
- the new **Package 11** (a Platform Architecture workstream) is packaged but **deferred**, because opening it before D-9 would be "the exact AIP-14 exposure under examination" [L168].

**Int:** only Packages 1 and 11 state their gating relation. The order of Packages 3–10 is not derived from stated dependencies. The concept holds; its application in this docket is partial.

## Evidence that presupposes the answer `[CON-0050 · invalid]` (Src)

The most instructive passage is the docket's correction of itself. Package 1 originally supported running the AIP-14 check with "20+ platform-only documents" in 48 hours [L22]. A review then asked whether platform work should be measured by document count, effort, elapsed time or delivered capability [L32]. The docket's answer:
- its own evidence line "presupposed document count as the metric" [L34; Q7], which was exactly the undecided question;
- "That line is DOWNGRADED from trigger evidence to illustration pending metric definition." [L34; Q8];
- D-9 splits: **D-9a** define the basis (recommended: delivered capability + effort, not raw document count) → **D-9b** run the check [L36–41]. "the gate must be built before it can be closed." [L43; Q9].

**Int (a general rule, qualified after review C4 F-09; met once in the corpus read so far):** an item counts as evidence for a decision only if its meaning does not depend on the answer to that decision, **or to any open question the decision rests on** (in the docket's only case, the D-9a metric is upstream of D-9), **unless it supports the same conclusion under every candidate answer** (robust evidence). Otherwise it may stay as an illustration. The rule is the evidential counterpart of Ch. 2's point that a rule's author cannot settle the conflict the rule is part of (§2.5, ground 1).

**Consequence for Ch. 0:** AIP-14 is presented elsewhere as a constraint because it "ships with a per-iteration metric" [S0019 L27]. Here the metric is named but its basis is undecided `[CON-0051 · invalid]` (Ch. 0 §0.2, RC-54; TODO T0-18).

**A missing act shows up as "cannot determine" (Package 8).** The register list is "the authority act CAP-001's INCONCLUSIVE rate measures the absence of" [L127; Q17]. Without it, "the dominant verdict of the only operating capability remains" "no criterion exists" [L130]. **Int:** the absence of an authority act appears as an **undetermined** result, not a negative one. This is the three-valued reading of Ch. 1 §1.14.1 and §1.17.3 ("cannot determine"; missing evidence is never reported as missing authority).

**An evidence bar (Package 11).** Software "services" would collide with the stance that KnowledgeOS is not a software system. The only evidence for them is one capability's manual-adoption failure, "which authorizes a question, not a service layer" [L168; Q22]. **Int:** the strength of the evidence limits the size of the change it can justify. The docket calls the stance "adopted stance N-10 / Reference Architecture §1" [L168], while Package 7 still asks the ARB to ratify the Reference Architecture (TODO T5-03; Ch. 0 RC-57).

**Model amnesia (Package 10).** "discover → model → forget → rediscover" is called "the disease the platform exists to cure", counted at n=11 [L151–152] `[CON-0052 · invalid]` (counts not reconciled with a same-day source; TODO T5-01). It belongs to the knowledge loop, which has no home yet: Ch. 7 is planned and not written. Until it is, this section is the count's home (T5-01).
