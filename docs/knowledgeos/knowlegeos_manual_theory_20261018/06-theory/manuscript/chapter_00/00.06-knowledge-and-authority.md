## 0.6 Knowledge and authority

Between 07-27 and 08-16 a second idea becomes central, initially from engineering governance rather than knowledge research:

- **Knowledge feeds authority; it never holds it.** A verdict is an output, never a decision. This is compiled into code (`Verdict.php::emittable()`) [v3 L284; KT documents].
- **Decisions belong to humans.** The AI provides analysis and recommendations; it never approves, ratifies, authorizes or promotes [DevConst L14–18].
- **Knowledge passes through stages that cannot be skipped** [DevConst L55–56]: *observation → evidence → research → candidate → qualification → decision → adoption → implementation → operational evidence → revision*.
- **"Unknown" and "no" are legitimate answers.** "Evidence not found" is complete and respectable [DevConst L101]; "no new capability" is a success [DevConst L61].

**Connection to the knowledge model (Int → Ded).** In GKC, *governance status* is a property of a claim (draft, canonical, …). The authority idea adds something GKC lacks: **who may move a claim between statuses.** Formally:

> status transitions **Draft → Canonical** and **Canonical → Superseded** are *acts*. An act is valid only if a recorded human decision covers it. A mechanism may *report* that the conditions for the act are met; it may not perform it.

This makes I-2 ("transitions are explicit and reasoned") precise: *explicit* means a recorded act; *reasoned* means a recorded reason is present (a stipulation, Ch. 1 §1.17.2); whether that reason cites adequate evidence is a matter of well-foundedness. **Int.** *Open (added in correction round 3):* whether a missing reason makes the act **invalid** or only **ill-founded** is not settled. Chapter 1 §1.17.2 sets out three readings: (a) validity condition, (b) quality obligation, (c) validity condition for specified kinds of act. It is a reserved decision. I-2's third part, "visible where knowledge is found", is a presentation obligation (§0.1). It also explains why the 08-16 documents insist that absence of a recorded grant is not a denial, only a gap in the record (Chapter T-01 and KT-8, a background reading note in `archive/01-basis/B-10-knowledge-transfer/` on five tactical-model documents of 2026-08-16: aggregate boundaries, invariant matrix, invariant allocation, policy catalogue, domain events; engineering background, not corpus).

> **Revised 2026-10-18 (pilot file 1, Chapter 1 §1.2).** GKC's "status" fuses standing and lifecycle (Ch. 1 §1.2 table); only its standing part is meant above. A claim has immutable **provenance** and act-changed **standing**; a later schema conflated them in one "authority" field. GKC already kept them apart [GKC L19]. Chapter 1 §1.2 gives the precise rules: no direct creation→authoritative edge; provenance never confers standing.

**Status.** **Src** for each statement. That they form **one theory of knowledge plus authority** is **Int**. Whether the corpus treats authority as part of the KnowledgeOS theory or as surrounding governance is **Open**.
