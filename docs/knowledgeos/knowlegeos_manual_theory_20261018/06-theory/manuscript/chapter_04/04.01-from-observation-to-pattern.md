# 4.1 From observation to pattern: how evidence is recorded, counted and promoted

## 4.1.1 In plain language

Knowledge in the platform should change when experience shows that something works, fails or keeps recurring. The corpus's Operational Evidence Register (S0027, 2026-08-03/04) is its first systematic attempt to record such experience. Its design answers three questions:
- how to write down what happened without mixing in opinion or authority;
- how to get from single events to recurring patterns;
- what a count of events can prove.

Its design is largely sound. Its own counts, however, do not yet meet the standards its design implies. That is the main lesson of this section.

## 4.1.2 Definitions

- **Evidence entry (Src).** Each entry has five fixed sections: **observation** · **provenance** · **claims affected** (and not affected) · **interpretation** · **promotion decision**, which is always "left to the Decision Authority" [S0027 L113–121]. The reason given: "an entry mixes three epistemic kinds unless the sections are fixed." [L113] `[CON-0068 · validated]` (restated in C1)
- **Two streams (Src).** Stream A is operational evidence, one entry per event, append-only. Stream B holds learning candidates, "generated PERIODICALLY from Stream A · reviewed by the DA · NEVER written directly" [L130]. "Patterns are extracted FROM entries by a distinct act, after several exist — never written INTO them." [L132] `[CON-0069 · validated]` (restated in C2)
- **Independent instances (Ded, defined here).** Two instances are independent when they do not share a **generating process**: the same actor, the same instructions, the same reviewer or the same work item. A different context alone is not independence.

## 4.1.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. An entry holds four kinds of statement, not three (Ded).**
- The five sections map to:
  - **fact** (observation);
  - **provenance**;
  - **inference on claims** ("claims affected", where the register raises its counts, L61, L99);
  - **interpretation**;
  - plus a fixed **disclaimer** of authority (promotion decision).
- Inference on claims is the fourth kind. It sits outside the marked interpretation section by design.
- So the sections make the mixture visible, but they keep inference out of the place marked "interpretation".
- *Counterexample attempt:* a reader takes a count raised in "claims affected" for a fact. The design does not prevent this; only labelling counts as inferences would.

**C2. Separating the streams is necessary, not sufficient (Ded).**
- A pattern is a hypothesis induced from observations. If the same observations both suggest and confirm it, the confirmation is empty. Separate streams are a precondition for avoiding this.
- They do not prevent it on their own. The register counts the **proposing** instance as the pattern's first observation:
  - MO-2's single count is the entry that suggested it [L183];
  - the explanatory-precision heuristic counts "the minting instance" [L193].
- **Rule (Ded):** the instance that suggested a pattern does not count toward its recurrence. Recurrence starts at the first independent instance after the proposal.

**C3. A count needs a fixed class, a fixed membership test and a fixed unit (Int: a methodological norm).** `[CON-0070 · validated]`
- A count *n* of instances of a class is meaningful only if, before the instances are classified, three things are fixed:
  - the class;
  - the test that decides membership;
  - the unit of counting.
  Otherwise any *n* can be reached. This is the reference-class and forking-paths problem.
- **Applied to the register's count of three for the edge "guides product engineering" (R-6)** [L34, L61, L99]:
  - the **label** was fixed in advance ("activity-level", L175);
  - the **membership test** was not: whether a refused plan or a refusal to decide counts as "guided" was judged afterwards;
  - the **unit** was not either: OE-KOS-1 and OE-KOS-2 are the planning and the implementation of one work item, WP-4B, so they may be one instance, not two.
- The count is therefore **unverified**, not established.

**C4. Evidenced is not measured (Int, statistics).**
- The source: "the edge is evidenced, not measured"; there is "no unguided baseline" [L34].
- **No baseline:** three cases of work that obeyed the architecture cannot show that the architecture caused it.
- **The rival explanation:** the developing agent was **instructed** to obey these very rules. Under the hypothesis that the architecture adds nothing beyond the instructions, obedient behaviour is exactly what is predicted. A guidance effect must beat this alternative.
- **Selection:** the records were chosen, written and reviewed by the platform's own process. The "demonstrated" grades come from an "ARB-persona review" [L17], an AI role, not the ARB.
- The count supports "this happens". It does not estimate "how much difference it makes".

**C5. Recurrence must be independent, and the corpus's counts are not (Ded).**
- The corpus already requires a second independent use for structure (Ch. 1 §1.8). The register adds that a modelling heuristic needs "recurrence across MULTIPLE bounded contexts" [L189].
- A different bounded context is **not** independence when the actor, instructions and reviewer are the same: the common cause is the process. By the definition above:
  - **the R-6 count fails:** all three records come from one agent, one instruction set, one review process and one product area (Adjudication and Contestation);
  - **the volume watch counts singletons:** "10–15 candidates" [L197–201] counts distinct n=1 candidates, which measures recording activity, not recurrence. This conflicts with "Pattern extraction begins only when recurrence exists" [L140].

**C6. An obligation is monitored only where its monitor looks (Ded), and may lapse there (Hyp).** `[CON-0071 · validated]` (definitional part)
- **Definitional, valid:** where an obligation's scope exceeds its monitor's coverage, monitored compliance is confined to their intersection. Coverage is a set of path patterns, so this can be checked statically.
- **Empirical, n=1, a hypothesis:** compliance itself falls off outside the coverage. In OE-KOS-3 the developer-guide obligation was skipped across a stream of work while the reminder saw nothing; "The gap was discovered by USER AUDIT, not by the platform." [L74]. Skipping and silence co-occurred; that the silence caused the skipping is not shown.
- **Instance or counterexample:** the register counts this failure as the first **instance** of "implementation accompanied by developer guidance" (DG-1, L158). By its own lifecycle (candidate → evidence → **counterexamples** → promotion, L199) it is a counterexample.

**C7. The feedback from evidence to the platform: counts disagree (Src; reconciliation Int).**
- The corpus gives three counts within about two days:
  - "0 traversals" (S0028, 08-02);
  - "n≈3" (S0026, 08-03);
  - "formally traversed for the first time … formal n=1" (S0027, 08-04).
- See the table in Ch. 1 §1.8. **Int:** they likely use different definitions of "traversal" and "formal". This is a hypothesis, the case of C3 applied to the corpus's own counts.
- The one "formal" traversal was a reminder hook's file pattern fixed after a user found the gap. As a pass of the **platform's** improvement loop, it is weak evidence: the platform did not find the gap itself.
- Guides-edge and improvement-edge must not be conflated [L36].
