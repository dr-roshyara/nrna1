## 10.4.0 The research question, the participants, and what this turn does not do

**Question.** For each relationship among the areas of §10.2: its direction (which side is upstream), which side is authoritative, how they are integrated **in domain terms**, the language both sides share, what changes downstream when the upstream changes, and its evidence. Then the direction law, handled as a candidate law (§2b).

**Participants (from §10.3.6):**
- **the context:** S Standing (admitted);
- **pending contexts:** W Well-foundedness (G1); N Norms (G11, T10-10);
- **doubtful:** C Catalogue;
- **areas that are not contexts:** D Delivery (a role of consumers); M Method, WM Work management and A Assessment methods (applications);
- **outside:** AU, the people who perform authority acts; U, the users and AI agents; P, the products whose knowledge is held (PublicDigit is the one instance).

**Pattern names** (customer/supplier, conformist, published language, anti-corruption layer, separate ways) are used **as labels for a stated relation only**. Each row says in words what the relation is. DDD literature is a benchmark, not a reason; it was not opened.

**Not done:** the published language itself (turn 5); any technical interface, transport or format; operational failure.

## 10.4.3 The direction law, a candidate law (§2b)

**Statement under test:** "applications depend on the kernel, never the reverse" (§6.6 C8, Src; Obs).

**Its exact meaning (Int; for review):**
- **X depends on Y** iff some rule of X cannot be stated, or some answer of X cannot be computed, without Y's published language or Y's **state**.
- The **kernel** is what the boundary test placed there (§9.6–§9.17): authority recording's contract and the evaluator's rule. *Where CE runs is open, §9.6 L219.*
- **Applications** are areas outside the kernel.
- **The law:** the kernel depends on no application. In particular, the kernel reads no application state to decide. Whatever it needs from outside arrives **as an input accepted through its own steps**.

**Its falsifier (observable):** a corpus source or a book invariant in which **a kernel decision needs information that only an application holds, and that does not arrive as a recorded input**. A kernel invariant that cannot be enforced without such information would also count. If one is found, the law is false or too broad. If one is found only for inputs that are recorded, the law is underspecified (it must say "except through recorded inputs").

**Evidence:**

| Kind | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **for** | CE*'s answers are a function of the record's entries only (§9.13: "Φ reads no carrier"; D's kinds); ARCH-INV-1 (the recorder decides nothing); K3's "correct" is defined relative to "the relation the kernel's own steps accepted as input", so coverage and identity enter as inputs (R8, R9) |
| **against, or narrowing** | **K3:** the kernel **assumes** the covers relation and the identity link are correct, and those come from areas outside it (N, C). The law holds only as "through recorded inputs it assumes correct", so the narrowing is needed. **Law 18** (a held act applied once its object exists, §5.6): the kernel step needs to know that a deliverable exists, which is outside the record (§10.3.3 G). Unless that existence arrives as a recorded input, this **meets the falsifier**. Not modelled. **Law 24:** prevention across applications needs one coordination point per grant (K4); if an application provides it, the kernel's guarantee depends on it. **Law 27:** the session holds the current rule at START; the rule's version comes from N |
| **does not decide** | that S0196's PKS keeps assessment "informing, never binding" authority: another product |
| **scope limits** | one estate; the boundary test has not placed CE (§9.6 L219); W's and N's state are not yet in the record (G1, G11) |

**Result (for review):**
- The law holds **in a narrowed form**: *the kernel reads no application state; what it needs from outside arrives as inputs accepted through its own steps, assumed correct (K3).*
- **Law 18** is a standing **candidate falsifier** until held acts and object existence are modelled.
- **Laws 24 and 27** bound the law's scope: coordination and rule versions arrive from outside the kernel.
- The law is **not established**. It is a candidate law at S0.

## 10.4.5 Checked against S0162's warning

**"Relationships, not a pipeline" (S0162 L458, in meaning).** The map is a **star with inputs**, not a chain:
- **S** is upstream of every other area: D, W (who may record), N (standing of rules), WM (the contract) and M.
- **W** and **N** are upstream of D, which is a **common consumer**.
- Every relationship **into** S is an input accepted through S's own steps (R1, R8, R9), never a dependency on a downstream model.

**No relationship runs D → S, W → S or N → S as a dependency.** That is the narrowed direction law, and it is the claim to test.

*Layering risk, as in §10.2.2:* the map reads "S below everything", which resembles a layer. **Its defence** is that each arrow carries a stated domain relation, not a technical call; whether that defence holds is the reviewers' question.
