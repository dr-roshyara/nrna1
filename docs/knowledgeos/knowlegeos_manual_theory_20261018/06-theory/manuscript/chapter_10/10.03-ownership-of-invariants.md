## 10.3.2 Owner, enforcer, and the five relations (the §2c question)

**The mandate refuses "assuming the place that enforces a rule owns it".** The two roles are therefore kept apart.

**Owner: two readings, compared (Int; restated at review).**
- **R-a, the book's (§6.5.2; (o2)):** the owner is the context whose rules an **amending act** changes. The act itself is held by a person, outside the system (A1).
- **R-b, a procedure this turn can apply:** the owner is the candidate whose **concept list (§10.2) defines every term of the invariant**. If the terms come from two lists, the invariant is **shared**; if from none, there is **no owner established**.
- **R-a cannot be applied yet:** no recorded act names a context, since the candidates are not admitted. So the table uses **R-b**, and R-a is applied once contexts are admitted. Where R-b gives a single owner, R-a would agree only if the amending act's scope is that candidate's rules. *This is untested; recorded as the condition under which the table holds.*
- **S0091's two owners:** under R-b they would be two concept lists, so the invariant is shared. Under R-a they are joint or two invariants (§6.5.2 L31). The table records such cases as **shared**, never resolved by choosing the enforcer. *(A's reading: on S0091's case the strict definition yields no owner, not two; recorded.)*

**Enforcer:** the candidate whose **operation** prevents a violation, by a step or a guard.

**The five relations, with the book's ground (restated at review: the draft's four overlapped and merged owner with enforcer).** *Not mutually exclusive (consolidation S0226–S0252, A-03): "owns" combines with one enforcement mode (prevents, observes, respects, assumes), as row A's "acting under ended authority" (owner 1, 1 observes) and law 26 (owner 2, 2 observes) show; the four others exclude each other per (candidate, invariant). Two orthogonal fields (owner status × enforcement mode) are T10-08's option.*

| Relation | Meaning | Ground in the book |
|---|---|---|
| **owns** | its concept list defines the invariant (R-b); its rules state it | §10.3.2 |
| **enforces for another** | its operation prevents violations of an invariant it does not own | **CON-0132's "preserves"** ("enforced on behalf of an owner elsewhere"); §6.5.2's "enforces but cannot amend" |
| **respects** | its own operations never violate a rule owned elsewhere, and it enforces nothing | the mandate's §2c "preserves" (a second sense of the word, so renamed here) |
| **observes** | it detects a violation after the fact and cannot prevent it | "detect" in the Principal's decision 1 (§9.16 R3a); K1's detectability (§9.15) |
| **assumes** | it relies on a condition it cannot establish | the W and E laws (§9.11.2); L1–L11, K1–K7 (§9.15) |

**Statuses of an invariant** (not relations): **shared** (two owners), **disputed ownership** (the sources disagree; renamed from "contested", T10-21, the Principal, 2026-10-31, so as not to share the evidence class's name), **no owner established**, and **held by convention** (no candidate enforces it; CON-0102's "held").

**Answer to §2c:** supported by the book and useful as **five** relations. The mandate's four are kept, with "preserves" split into two senses that the book and the mandate use differently. The mandate's rule is kept: L1–L11 are never owned.

## 10.3.4 The admission test (T10-05)

**The test as the Principal frames it:** a candidate is admitted as a bounded context if it owns an invariant that no other candidate owns.

**The draft's falsifier does not hold (A F-01, B B-01, critical).**
- The draft argued that a "designation" part of candidate 1 owns the designation rule exclusively and has no language of its own.
- But by the owner definition, ownership requires defining the invariant's terms, and a part that shares its whole language owns nothing. The counterexample did not meet the definition.
- Under that definition, "an exclusive owned invariant" already contains a language condition, so the draft's options (i) and (ii) were one option.

**What does hold (Ded, under R-b):**
1. **At the level of single terms, refinement still defeats the test.** A part that coins one term ("tombstone"; "prev_use") and states one invariant in it would own that invariant exclusively (B-01 (2)). Read term by term, "its own terms" blocks nothing.
2. **At the level of a model**, read as "a vocabulary not contained in another candidate's", refinement is blocked by construction, and the test becomes S0150's pair: language and invariants. *(This reading is a choice, not a derivation.)*
3. **The corpus did not treat exclusive invariants as making contexts:**
   - S0036 gives five capabilities "Each … its own invariant" (L138), then "Capabilities are not contexts" (L143);
   - S0033 refused Knowledge Validation, with "invariants only" (L83–86). That is a weak case: its stated ground is the refusal of a parent abstraction (H-CAT-1, L85, L130).

   *One estate, one period; one line of support (F-10, B-20).*

**Result:**
- **Sufficiency is not established.** The test works only with a model-level language clause added, and whether more is needed (owned state, lifecycle) is a choice the evidence does not make.
- *The draft's "standing and correctness are separate because their laws differ" was the Professor's interpretation, not the book's (B-20). It is withdrawn as a ground.*
- The choice goes to the Principal as **one question (T10-05)**, to be put after this settlement.

*When should a candidate count as its own bounded context?*
- **(A)** When it owns an invariant that no other candidate owns, stated in a vocabulary that is not part of another candidate's.
  - Candidates 1, 2 and 4 qualify (2 by its model, without a record).
  - Candidate 5 is doubtful: its supersession and identity invariants have no record support, and its other rows are shared.
  - Candidates 3, 6, 7 and 8 do not qualify: 3 owns no invariant; 6 owns only non-consultation, and its vocabulary is unexamined; 7's invariants are disputed; 8 owns none.
- **(B)** As (A), and it also keeps a record of its own (S0150's "independently owned state").
  - Candidates 1 and 4 qualify.
  - Candidate 2 waits until its evidence record exists (G1).
  - Candidate 5 is doubtful, as under (A).
  - Candidates 3, 6, 7 and 8 do not qualify.
- **(C)** Never by a rule. All eight remain candidates, and the map (turn 4) works with candidates.

**Recommendation: (B).**
- It is the corpus's own pair plus its owned-state question (S0150), so it adds nothing the book did not already contain.
- It ties admission to having a record, which is exactly what G1 shows to be missing for well-foundedness.
- It admits fewest, the safe direction for a baseline that will be audited against.

*Consequence in business terms:* under (B), only standing and the rules about rules are separate areas today. Well-foundedness becomes one as soon as it has its own record. Presentation, method, work management and assessment are roles and applications that use the core, not separate areas of it.

## 10.3.6 The Principal's decision (B), the K2 condition, and its application

*Added 2026-10-29 and **settled after review** (`review/SD4-CONTEXT-MAP/SETTLEMENT.md`; reviewers A, Opus, and B, Sonnet). The decision (decision record addendum 2026-10-29T09:36:01Z, `4fa0dc39d`), verbatim from the Principal: "(B), with the K2 condition". In the record's words: an area counts as a bounded context when it "owns a rule no other area owns, stated in its own vocabulary, and keeps independently owned state"; the condition: state what "a record of its own / independently owned state" means and show that it is compatible with "the kernel law of one designated record (K2, §9.17 R2; CON-0261)", "for example, entries of the one record that only that area may append, not a second record". **Every line is S0.***

**What the review changed (A-01, critical; B-01):**
- The draft's rule required "the context that appended" an entry, but the book records and checks only an appending **application** (the `app` field; L10; §9.16 R4a; §9.17 (c′)).
- Applications that are not contexts append standing's kinds: work management through standing's contract, and the owner application of a grant through the grant-owner rule. So standing failed the draft's rule as written.
- The rule is restated below over **acceptance**, which the book does record: the guard's condition and D's clause for a kind.

**Definition (Int; restated at review).** A context X **keeps independently owned state** iff:
- **(d1)** there is a non-empty set K(X) of **entry kinds** of the one designated record, the sets K(X) of different contexts being disjoint;
- **(d2)** the **acceptance rule** of each kind in K(X) (the append guard's condition for it, and D's clause for it) is **X's rule**, stated in X's model. Whoever appends does so under X's rule, as work management appends standing's kinds under standing's contract (F22 = (C + O)). The appending application is recorded and trusted as declared (L10);
- **(d3)** D holds every entry of a kind in K(X) to X's acceptance rule, so that an entry accepted elsewhere against X's rule takes a merged record out of D.

**What it is not (A-04, B-05).**
- The partition governs **acceptance** only. Record-level kinds reach every kind: `remove` and `annul` target any entry, and `designate` moves the whole record (§9.13). *Since T10-16 (b) and `814b4608a`: `designate` and `remove` are the record element's, `annul` is standing's (§9.18.7; §10.3.7).*
- So **under one record (K2), no area's state is independent of standing's record-level operations.** "Independently owned" can mean only **exclusively governed acceptance**.
- And K(X) is non-empty only because D's kind list, which is standing's schema, contains X's kinds.
