## 10.3.2 Owner and enforcer kept apart; the four relations (the §2c question)

**The mandate refuses "assuming the place that enforces a rule owns it".** So two relations, defined apart (Int, a definition; for review):
- **owner:** the candidate whose model **defines the invariant's terms** and whose own rules **state it**, so that changing the invariant changes that candidate's rules. *(CON-0132's "reason lives in", made operational by these two conditions.)*
- **enforcer:** the candidate whose **operation** makes the invariant hold: a step or guard that **prevents** a violation, or an evaluation that **detects** one.

**The §2c question: are owns / preserves / observes / assumes supported by the book, and useful?** **Answer (Int, grounded in the book's own classes; for review): yes, as four relations a candidate can have to an invariant, each matching a distinction the book already makes:**

| Relation | Meaning | The book's ground |
|---|---|---|
| **owns / enforces** | defines and states it; its operation prevents violations | T and S laws with a guard or a step (§9.11.2); "prevent" in the Principal's decision 1 (§9.16 R3a) |
| **preserves** | does not violate a rule owned elsewhere, in its own operations | CON-0132's "preserves" |
| **observes** | can detect a violation after the fact, cannot prevent it | "detect" in decision 1 (§9.16 R3a: law 26, role exclusivity, acts under ended authority); K1's detectability (§9.15) |
| **assumes** | relies on a condition it cannot establish | W and E laws (§9.11.2); the world assumptions L1–L11 and the kernel assumptions K1–K7 (§9.15) |

**Rule kept (the mandate):** a candidate that relies on L1–L11 is **never** described as owning or guaranteeing them. *Usefulness:* without "observes", the detect-decided laws would look owned and guaranteed; without "assumes", L1–L11 would look like nobody's gap, when they are by design nobody's guarantee.

**Test against S0091's two declared owners (AIP's I-4, "registry ≠ authority": BC-7 / BC-5).** Under the definition, two owners means the invariant's terms are defined in two models: a **shared** invariant. That is recorded as a gap, never resolved by choosing the enforcer.

## 10.3.4 The admission test (T10-05): is "owning an invariant that no other candidate owns" sufficient?

**The test, as the Principal frames it:** a candidate is admitted as a bounded context if it owns an invariant that no other candidate owns.

**What the table gives (Ded from §10.3.3):** candidates 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 each own invariants no other owns. 6 owns one (the binding contract), 7 owns one (law 27, partly assumed), and 8 owns none.

**Evidence for it (Src and Int):**
- invariants are one of S0150's six questions and one of CON-0085's nine criteria (S0150 L584–592; S0033 L29), each in meaning;
- the book's own separation of the core rests on invariants: standing and correctness are separate because their laws differ (CON-0231; §9.13);
- an owned invariant answers "who is responsible", which the mandate's ownership element exists to make visible.

**Evidence against it, and its falsifier:**
1. **Refinement defeats it (Ded).** Any candidate can be split until each part owns an invariant no other part owns. For example, the designation rule alone is owned by a "designation" part of candidate 1, and that part has no language of its own: carrier, record, entry and designation are standing's terms. The test therefore admits whatever partition it is given; it **presupposes the candidate list it is meant to decide**. *Falsifier, met:* a sub-area that owns an exclusive invariant but shares its whole language with another candidate. "Designation" is one.
2. **The corpus's own application treats invariants as insufficient.** S0033 classed Knowledge Validation, which had "invariants only", as a capability family, not a context (S0033 L83–86, in meaning).
3. *Benchmark only (DDD, not opened, never the reason):* invariants usually bound aggregates within a context, while contexts are bounded by models and language. This agrees with points 1 and 2 and adds nothing to them.

**Result (Ded from 1, Src from 2):** **"owns an exclusive invariant" is not sufficient** on the book's and the corpus's evidence. It is necessary-like, and it is the strongest single criterion, but it overadmits. A sufficient condition needs a second clause that blocks refinement. **Which clause is a choice the evidence does not make**, so it is prepared as **one question for the Principal (T10-05)**, to go after review:

*When should a candidate count as its own bounded context?*
- **(i)** when it owns an invariant no other candidate owns **and** the terms of that invariant are defined only in its own model;
- **(ii)** when it owns an invariant no other candidate owns (overadmits: falsified above);
- **(iii)** never by rule: keep all eight as candidates and let the map (turn 4) work with candidates.

**Recommendation, for the reviewers to test first: (i).**
- The second clause blocks the refinement counterexample: designation's terms are standing's.
- It uses only the owner definition of §10.3.2.

**Under (i), as far as this turn can tell (Int):**
- **Candidates 1, 2, 3 and 4 qualify.** 2 qualifies by its model, even without record support.
- **5 is doubtful:** "item" is used by 1's acts too.
- **6 and 7 are doubtful:** S0034 found 7's words to be D-1's.
- **8 does not qualify.**
