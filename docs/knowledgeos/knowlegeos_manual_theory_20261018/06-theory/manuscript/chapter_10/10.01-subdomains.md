## 10.1.0 The research question, and what this turn does not do

**Question.** Which candidate areas of KnowledgeOS are **core** (the reason it exists), **supporting**, or **generic**? For each: the evidence, the level, and, where the evidence does not decide, what would.

**Not done here:** no bounded contexts (turn 2), no ownership (turn 3), no map (turn 4), no published language (turn 5). No class is taken from the size, complexity or structure of any code; no EKS file was opened.

**Three stipulations (Int; choices stated, not deductions; restated at review, A-04, B-05).**
1. **The reference product is KnowledgeOS itself.** A subdomain class is relative to a reference product `[CON-0089 · validated]` (§6.3). In the estate, canon says "The Election System is the Core Domain; this platform is a Supporting Subdomain" (S0035 L30, in meaning, quoting a DA clarification of 2026-07-27), and KnowledgeOS is "potential product", its gate shut (S0035 L71, in meaning). **This section is therefore a counterfactual analysis: it asks what the classes would be if KnowledgeOS were the product.** Canon gives one answer to that question itself: "engineering governance/knowledge **would be** its Core Domain" (S0035 L72, in meaning). That answer is recorded as a rival in §10.1.1 and compared in §10.1.2; it is not set aside (A-05, B-04).
2. **Core is not kernel.** A subdomain class is a strategic class, not an architectural layer (§9.6 D1; 09.99 L47, TT1). **No class below is decided by kernel or application membership.** Where the boundary test bears on an area, it is shown in a separate column as context, not as a reason (A-06, B-03).
3. **What KnowledgeOS is for was not fixed when this turn was written; it is now decided (§10.1.3).** The book states the problem as usable(k, t) = current(k, t) ∧ well-founded(k, t) ∧ visible(k, t), where visible means "the user can see both values" (§0.1, the book's own development, Int). **That states the problem; it does not decide what KnowledgeOS promises to solve.** The mission is disputed (T0-02: "Awaiting Human Decision"), so the promise was the open variable of this section. *Decided since: (b).*

## 10.1.2 The test, and the classes it gives

**The test (Int, a definition of this book; restated at review, A-01, B-01).** An area is:
- **generic** if it is not specific (below), provided the promise's invariants hold at its boundary *(defined as ¬specific so the classes exclude each other: consolidation S0226–S0252, A-06)*;
- **core** if it is specific to KnowledgeOS **and part of what KnowledgeOS promises**;
- **supporting** if it is specific to KnowledgeOS, needed by the promise, and **not part of it**.

The three are mutually exclusive by construction: generic against specific, and within specific, part of the promise or not. **Necessity is not a criterion of core**; the draft's test used it, and so made every necessary area core (A-01's model; B-01). *Terms:* the **promise** is what KnowledgeOS guarantees to its users; **specific** means that no solution made without KnowledgeOS's model can serve.

**The promise is open (stipulation 3).** Three options, from narrowest to widest, each consistent with §0.1's problem statement to a different degree (A-09, B-07):
- **(a) Standing:** the current standing of knowledge is right and visible, with its reasons. Well-foundedness is left to those who use the knowledge. *This narrows §0.1's "visible", which covers both values.*
- **(b) Standing, and well-foundedness shown:** (a), plus KnowledgeOS shows what is known about each item's well-foundedness (refuted, not attested, or supported by recorded evidence; and, since T9-17 (b), contested) **without guaranteeing it**. *This keeps §0.1's "visible" as written.*
- **(c) Standing and well-foundedness guaranteed:** (a), plus KnowledgeOS guarantees that what it serves is well-founded.

Canon's conditional answer, "engineering governance/knowledge" (S0035 L72), maps onto rows 1, 2 and 8 below; it fits (c), or (b) with the method included.

## 10.1.3 The result, and the question for the Principal

- **Independent of the promise:** rows 14 and 15 are generic; rows 4–7 and 9–13 are supporting under every option; row 1 is core under every option. *These rows are still S0 until a later review confirms them; row 1's non-substitutability is a hypothesis.*
- **Dependent on the promise:** rows 2, 3 and 8, now classified under (b) (below).
- **Rivals recorded:** the method (S0036, S0175), the product's lifecycle (S0178), governance and knowledge (S0035 L72, conditional), context assembly (§0.7), and PKS's own classes (S0196, another product).
- **Not decided here:** KnowledgeOS's class within the estate (canon: a Supporting Subdomain of PublicDigit).

**The question put to the Principal (T10-01):** *When KnowledgeOS serves a piece of knowledge, what does it promise the user: (a) only that its current standing is right and shown with its reasons; (b) that, and an honest display of how well-founded it is (refuted, not attested, or supported by recorded evidence), without a guarantee; or (c) that it is well-founded?*
**The Principal's answer (verbatim):** "i accept your recommendation" (decision record addendum 2026-10-29T08:50:11Z, `e73b8e1cb`). **Decided: (b)**, in the record's words: KnowledgeOS promises that a piece of knowledge's "current standing is right and shown with its reasons, together with an honest display of how well-founded it is (refuted, not attested, or supported by recorded evidence), without a guarantee that it is well-founded".

**The classes under (b), the baseline's classes** (each S0 until reviewed):
- **Core:** current standing (row 1); **showing** the well-foundedness status (row 2's display); presentation at the point of use (row 3).
- **Supporting:** the methods that assess well-foundedness (row 2's assessment); rows 4–13, the engineering method (row 8) included.
- **Generic:** rows 14 and 15.

*The recommendation, kept as the record of what was recommended:* **(b).**
- It keeps the book's statement of the problem as written: the user sees both values (§0.1).
- It promises only what the record can support. The record can show a refutation but cannot establish independence (CON-0127), and adoption does not establish correctness (CON-0231). So a guarantee, as in (c), would promise more than the record can carry.
- Under (b), showing the status is core, and the methods that assess it are supporting.

## 10.1.5 What does not hold

*Draft `bde3c61cd`, refuted at review SD1:*
- **The test** ("core if usable cannot be delivered without it and no non-specific solution delivers it"): necessity does not separate core from supporting, so every necessary area came out core (A-01, B-01, critical). Restated in §10.1.2.
- **"One core area, current standing"** as a result: it came from an unstated "delivers by itself" criterion, not the test. Restated as conditional on the promise.
- **"visible(k, t) is what CE\* answers":** visible is a presentation obligation and covers both values (A-03, B-06).
- **"The boundary test places … the evaluator in the kernel" and "the kernel plane, F22":** where CE runs is open, and F22 = (C + O) (A-02).
- **Kernel arguments as reasons for classes** (rows 7 and 8 of the draft), against the draft's own premise 2 (A-06, B-03).
- **"The book's own form of the DDD test":** neither text was opened (A-10, B-15).
- **The recommendation "standing only" (option (a)), resting on CON-0127:** CON-0127 concerns independence, not well-foundedness, and (a) narrows §0.1 (A-09, B-07).
