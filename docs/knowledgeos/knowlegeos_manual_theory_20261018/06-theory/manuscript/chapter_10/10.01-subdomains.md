## 10.1.0 The research question, and what this turn does not do

**Question.** Which candidate areas of KnowledgeOS are **core** (the reason it exists), **supporting** (needed, made for it, but not the reason), or **generic** (needed, and replaceable by something not made for it)? For each: the evidence, the level, and, where the evidence does not decide, what would.

**Not done here:** no bounded contexts (turn 2), no ownership (turn 3), no map (turn 4), no published language (turn 5). No classification is taken from the size, complexity or structure of any code; no EKS file was opened.

**Three premises, stated before any classification (Ded).**
1. **A subdomain class is relative to a reference product** `[CON-0089 · validated]` (§6.3). This turn takes **KnowledgeOS itself as the reference product**, as the mandate asks ("the reason KnowledgeOS exists"). It does **not** decide KnowledgeOS's class within the estate: there, canon says "The Election System is the Core Domain; this platform is a Supporting Subdomain" (S0035 L30, in meaning, quoting a DA clarification of 2026-07-27), and KnowledgeOS is "potential product" with its gate shut (S0035 L71, in meaning). That frame stays as canon has it; nothing here changes it, and T0-02 (the mission) stays open.
2. **A subdomain class is a strategic class, not an architectural layer.** "Core" is not "kernel" and "supporting" is not "application" (§9.6 D1; corrected at TT1, 09.99 L47). Kernel membership is the boundary test's result (§9.6–§9.17); this turn uses it as evidence, never as a definition of "core".
3. **What KnowledgeOS is for** is taken from the book's statement of the problem, not from a corpus product description: "Knowledge must be valid at the moment it is used, by a human or an AI, and the user must be able to see whether it is", with usable(k, t) = current(k, t) ∧ well-founded(k, t) ∧ visible(k, t) (§0.1, Int, the book's own development). Its level is the level of §0.1 (Int, reviewed in the whole-book reviews; no CON-ID).

## 10.1.2 The test applied to each candidate

A candidate area is **core** if usable(k, t) cannot be delivered without it **and** no solution not made for KnowledgeOS delivers it; **supporting** if it is needed by the core and specific to KnowledgeOS's model but does not by itself deliver usable(k, t); **generic** if it is needed and a solution not made for KnowledgeOS can serve, provided the core's invariants are preserved at its boundary. *(The book's own form of the Evans/Vernon test, Ded; premise 3.)*

## 10.1.3 The result

- **Core: one area, current standing (item 1).** It is the only area whose absence leaves usable(k, t) undeliverable and that no non-specific solution provides. *Ceiling S1.*
- **Open: well-foundedness (item 2)**, between core and supporting, decided by what KnowledgeOS promises (below).
- **Supporting: items 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11. Generic: items 6, 7.**
- **Rivals recorded, not adopted:** the engineering method as core (S0036), the knowledge product as core (S0178), admission or transformation as the core act (S0365, S0392; unsettled in the corpus, S0612).
- **Not decided here:** KnowledgeOS's own class within the estate (canon: Supporting Subdomain of PublicDigit; premise 1).

**For the Principal (normative; prepared, not asked yet):** *Does KnowledgeOS promise that the knowledge it serves is well-founded, or does it promise only that its current standing is right and visible, leaving the judgement of well-foundedness to the applications that use it?* Recommendation, for the reviewers to test first: **the second**, because the book separates standing from correctness throughout (CON-0231; CE* reads no correctness), and a promise of well-foundedness would make the core depend on the factors of independence, which the record can refute but not establish (CON-0127). If the reviewers confirm the fork, it goes to the Principal after this turn's settlement.
