# 3.3 Kinds (archetypes), the category test, and the knowledge space

## In plain language

Besides its six properties, everything in KnowledgeOS is **some kind of thing**: a domain, a process, a capability, a piece of knowledge, a document, a runtime asset, a knowledge space, a derived view, or a purpose. The corpus did not invent this list. Five earlier classification schemes persist, with shared vocabulary; they classify different things, so they do not agree on one list. It also found a test for rejecting false categories: **if a category changes when only the encoding or the scope changes, it is not a real kind.**

## The kinds `[CON-0008 · invalid]` (Src)

*Sense:* "kind" here is an **archetype** of element (S0009). It is not a **subject kind** K of Ch. 2 §2.8.6 (the kinds of subject a rule's scope dimensions apply to), not S0021's commission-defined kinds (§3.6), and not a **kind of change** (Ch. 4).

The 2026-08-03 meta-model discovery extends §3.1's formula with a kind [S0009 L65–69]:

> element = KIND × NATURE × REPRESENTATION × GOVERNANCE-STATUS × AUTHORITY-SCOPE (× EVIDENCE-STATUS × OPERATIONAL-STATE)

| Kind | Grade in the source |
|---|---|
| domain · process · capability · knowledge · artifact · runtime asset · projection | confirmed as kinds, read as **candidate archetypes** pending MM-1 [S0009 L24, L72–73, L83] |
| container (knowledge space) | **demoted to candidate**: its distinguishing edge (generating projections) is seen in one space only [S0009 L25] |
| purpose | **candidate**: "found twice, exercised little" [S0009 L84] |

**Where they come from:** convergence across five classification passes [S0009 L36–46]. The source calls them independent. This book reads the convergence as **persistence across passes**: two of the passes are from the same programme on the same day (TODO T0-11).

**What the passes classify (Src → Ded).** The five passes do not classify the same things [S0009 L38–44]: RQ-002 classifies claims (nature × representation × governance status, with types as archetypes); the platform metamodel, knowledge classes; L-A, **document** types; the Relationship Ontology, concepts by kind (purpose · domain · capability · knowledge · representation · container · runtime element · projection [S0021 L48]); the Progression Model, kinds of **change**. So "five schemes agree on the nine kinds" does not hold. What they share is the move from a flat list to dimensions with archetypes, and some vocabulary.

**The category test, applied to the kept kinds (Ded).** The source applied its test ("one thing cannot change type by being encoded differently" [S0009 L46]) to the rejected categories only. Applied to the kept ones:
- **runtime asset:** knowledge in an executable representation, operating at run time. Re-encode it as prose and it stops being a runtime asset, so by the test it is a **representation** value (or a representation × operational state value), not a kind;
- **artifact:** a carrier (§3.4). Under §3.1's reading (B) it is the bearer of representation, not a kind of knowledge; as a kind beside "knowledge" it repeats the fusion the test rejects in "knowledge asset" [S0009 L55];
- **projection:** a derived view. What makes it a projection is how it was produced, a **provenance** value (derived), not what it is;
- **domain, process, capability, knowledge, purpose:** no re-encoding or change of scope changes them in the cases in the source; they pass, in this reading;
- **container (knowledge space):** passes, if a space is not identified by its folder (§3.3 below).

So the kept list does not pass its own test: three of the nine fail it, two as dimension values (runtime asset, projection) and one as a carrier (artifact) (TODO T3-03). This is the book's application of the source's test; the source does not make it.

**Why invalid here:** consistency is open. If "kind" is itself a value of nature (the open question U-MM-1), the formula counts nature twice and is not well-formed [E-S0009 §7]. Partly valid (TODO T3-03).

## The category test (Src)

Applied to a proposed flat list of seven categories, the test **rejected** three [S0009 L55–58]:
- **"knowledge asset"** fuses knowledge with its carrier (§3.4);
- **"governance asset"** is a *nature* value, normative force (graded MEDIUM-HIGH);
- **"product asset"** is a *scope* value.

Its basis is RQ-002's rule: "one thing cannot change type by being encoded differently" [S0009 L46].

**Why scope must stay a dimension:** the kinds are platform knowledge, but their *instances* lie on both sides of the platform/product line. A hospital adopter "inherits the kinds and none of the instances" [S0009 L110–111].

## The knowledge space `[CON-0011 · invalid]` (Src)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | "a set of artifacts with a DECLARED boundary, an owner and a governing constitution; spaces NEST" [S0008 L37] |
| **Not a folder** | the boundary is declared (`scope.include`), "not by a folder" [S0016 L30]. The same field is also the checker's coverage (S0020 L34–35); membership boundary and coverage are two scope concepts (Ch. 2 §2.8.1; §3.10.2) |
| **Product knowledge space** | a container that holds one product's knowledge and bindings; it must never hold reusable method (I-7; breached; Ch. 1 RC-15) [S0008 L23] |

**Why invalid here:** the definition is clear, but two of its claims are contested:
- **nesting**: resolved on 2026-08-02 ("a deployment IS a Knowledge Space", S0012 L93), yet still "unevidenced" on 2026-08-03 (S0013 L75; Ch. 1 RC-26a);
- **generating projections**: seen in one space only [S0009 L25].

Partly valid (TODO T3-04).
