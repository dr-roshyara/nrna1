# 3.5 Three ontology layers, and the scope of each

## In plain language

The corpus distinguishes three bodies of vocabulary:
1. one that describes **documents** (types, statuses, trust levels, links), which exists and is enforced by machine checks;
2. one that would describe the **platform itself** (its domains of governance, method, runtime and evidence), which does not exist yet;
3. one that describes the **product's** business domains, which partly exists.

Keeping them apart matters: putting a platform domain named "governance" into the enforced **bounded-contexts** list would collide with an existing key [S0020 L69]; a separate vocabulary file would avoid the collision [S0020 L74] (narrowed after review C3 F-18).

## The layers (Src)

| Layer | State | Content | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L-A documentation ontology** | "EXISTS · EXECUTABLE · ENFORCED" | 31 document types, 8 statuses, 5 authority levels, 9 audiences (including `ai`), 11 typed edges between documents, 18 lint rules, a generated graph [S0020 L22–30, L46]. **Overlap** (review C3 F-19): the 12 product contexts (`bounded-contexts.yaml`) are a factor of L-A [L46] and also L-C [L48] | `[CON-0029 · invalid]` (partly valid; T3-07) |
| **L-B platform ontology** | "DOES NOT EXIST" | its seed is the domain list D-1…D-7 (governance, method, runtime, capability, evidence, the PKS type); its verbs would be *governs · produces · consumes · observes* [S0020 L47, L108–112]. **Membership inconsistent** (review C3 F-20): there is no D-4 row and D-6b is rated product-side [L57–63], yet "ALL SEVEN belong to L-B" [L146]. S0016 (which cites S0020) models six platform concepts and calls them "an ontology" [S0016 L31, L144], so "DOES NOT EXIST" may be stale | `[CON-0030 · invalid]` |
| **L-C product ontology** | "PARTIALLY EXISTS" | the product's 12 bounded contexts, plus four unindexed catalogues (aggregates, contexts, invariants, decision ownership) [S0020 L48] | `[CON-0031 · invalid]` |

**A fourth layer is declined.** A proposed "engineering knowledge ontology" is not separate: L-A "already covers engineering-knowledge kinds" (adr, research, review, playbook, decision …) at document granularity [S0020 L49].

**Direction of governance:** "L-B would OWN and CLASSIFY L-A; L-A would never contain L-B." L-A's subject is the document; L-B's subject is the platform [S0020 L147]. **Int:** this has the same shape as "knowledge feeds authority, never holds it": the higher model governs the lower, never the reverse.

## Why the platform concepts cannot go into the document vocabulary (Src)

| Proof | Strength (as given) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **key collision** | DECISIVE | the product contexts already contain a `governance` key ("Authority models, approval workflows, committee hierarchy"). Adding "Engineering Governance" would collide in an enum that the lint enforces [S0020 L69] |
| **missing verbs** | STRONG | the edge vocabulary has `depends_on`, `derived_from`, `verified_by`, but no `governs`, `produces`, `consumes` or `observes` [S0020 L70] |
| **subject mismatch** | STRONG | every L-A field is a property of a document; "Engineering Method" has no reviewer, no review date and no code references [S0020 L71] |

**Int:** the key collision is a mechanical case of the identifier-namespace problem of Ch. 1 §1.10 (TODO T1-35). One short key cannot name both a product context and a platform domain inside one enforced list.

## A declared scope is a decision `[CON-0032 · invalid]` (partly valid: the source says only "a scope decision that someone made" [L35], with no record of the act or its author; T3-08)

L-A's graph covers about 13% of the documents because its scope is **declared**: `scope.include = docs/knowledge/**/*.md`. "THE 13% COVERAGE IS A DECLARED BOUNDARY, NOT AN OVERSIGHT"; extending it "would amend a declared scope, not fix a bug" [S0020 L34–35].

**Int:** a declared boundary is the result of an **act**, so changing it is a governance act that needs authority (Ch. 1 §1.6; scope in Ch. 2 §2.8). "The graph covers only 13%" is therefore not, by itself, a finding of a defect.

## Projection, in the vocabulary (Src)

The design policy DP-2 of **CAP-002**, a capability that was **DEFERRED** [S0020 L100], defines what a projection is (owner corrected after review C3 F-14): "every projection regenerates from its sources and is cited as authority by nothing" [S0020 L89] `[CON-0033 · validated]`. It is operating: the graph and the portal indexes are generated [S0020 L88].

For a **PKS** as a projection, the source finds it "SUPPORTED IN PRINCIPLE … BLOCKED IN PRACTICE": there is nothing to project from inside the declared scope [S0020 L78, L96]. A later annotation says the projection reading is "FALSIFIED for every artifact that exists today", "STRONGER THAN 'blocked'": the existing PKS artifacts are authored and positively authoritative [S0020 L80] (source's word restored after review C3 F-15). Ch. 1 §1.14.4 (RC-21a) sets this beside the **proposed** provenance/standing split (PM-1, undecided); both readings are kept.
