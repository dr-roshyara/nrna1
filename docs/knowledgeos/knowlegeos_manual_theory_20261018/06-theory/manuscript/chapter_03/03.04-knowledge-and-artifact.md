# 3.4 Knowledge is not its artifact

## In plain language

A rule and the document that writes it down are two different things. Deleting the document does not delete the rule, unless the document is its only carrier (§3.8 C6; qualified at the consolidation S0131–S0150, B-30), and a missing document does not mean the rule is missing. The corpus calls this split the meta-model's "most important edge", because it makes a recurring mistake distinguishable from the correct statement.

> "ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE ≠ ENGINEERING ARTIFACT. One is the thing; the other is a carrier of it." [S0016 L88]

An engineering artifact's responsibility is to "CARRY knowledge in a representation … it is never the knowledge" [S0016 L82]. Engineering knowledge is "rules, methods and decisions that transfer between products; represented by artifacts, never identical to them" [S0008 L24].

## What depends on it (Src)

- **The deletion test.** The engineering decision model's lifecycle decision asks "can this be deleted without loss of governed knowledge?". That question "is UNASKABLE unless knowledge and its file are different things" [S0017 L68].
- **"Knowledge without an artifact".** The corpus classifies "Capability Mapping" as knowledge "whose ARTIFACT does not exist" [S0009 L90–94]. That is the book's empty case E4, knowledge with no carrier (Ch. 2 §2.8.8).

## Relation to the earlier chapters

This is Ch. 1 §1.5 ("knowledge is not its carrier") at its corpus source. Under §3.1's meaning (B), it is the split between the bearer of nature and evidence status (the claim) and the bearer of representation (the carrier); under (A), it is the constraint that a claim's own dimensions are equal across its instances (§3.1, fork F37).

**Int:** the absence-claim test is the practical rule that follows:
- "X does not exist" is admissible only after checking that X has no carrier **and** that X is not known in another representation (e.g. tacit, or enacted in behaviour);
- otherwise the correct statement is "no artifact carries X" (compare "not found ≠ proven absent", Ch. 1 §1.17.3).
