# 3.9 What executes (fork F11)

*Written under Trial T1 (topic first, 2026-10-19), from five sources read in date order: S0043 (2026-08-02), S0166 (08-19), S0889 (08-27), S1052 (08-28) and S1524 (08-30). Revised after the trial's two independent review reports (`review/T1-F11-execute/REVIEW-REPORT.md`, `REVIEW-REPORT-2.md`). S1524 is a later verification report about the step series to which S1052 belongs. Its own observations and its general criterion are used; it does not confirm any earlier source, and its own counts are graded as reported (Src), not as machine evidence.*

## 3.9.1 In plain language

§3.8 C5 left open what it means to "execute". Three answers were on record:
- **A:** whatever executes is a capability.
- **B:** an executable script is still an artifact.
- **C:** the capability is an interface, and its adapters execute.

Across five later sources, the word covers **two activities**, each of which can be talked about in **two ways**:
- **The activities:**
  - a program **runs**;
  - an authorized action is **carried out** on an outside system.

  The action can be **dispatched** by the platform and **performed** by the outside system.
- **The two ways:**
  - something is **specified** precisely enough that it could be carried out;
  - it **was** carried out, and there is evidence that it was.

Fork F11 is **not settled here**. §3.9.3 C1 sets out what each source supports and where the sources conflict. The word "capability" alone means at least four things, and two of them, a technical service and a platform service, are things that run. That collision explains part of the fork, not all of it.

Two further questions came to light:
- **What counts as evidence that something was carried out?** The sources give four different answers.
- **Can a machine's "allow" change what is permitted, or does it only apply a permission someone granted earlier?**

## 3.9.2 Definitions

**Execution: two activities in two modes (Src; the names are this book's).** `[CON-0090 · provisional]`
- *Scope, stated first:* this is a map of the uses in five sources, **not an exhaustive list**. Activities and modes are on **different levels**: "run" and "act" are things done, while "specified" and "performed" say whether they were done. Three further uses lie outside the table and are listed below it: operative effect, transition, technical command.

| | **specified** (could be carried out) | **performed** (was carried out, with evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **run**: a runtime processes an artifact (a script, a check, a hook) | a model is precise enough to run: the aim is to "see whether our current mathematical model can actually execute a sequence of knowledge transitions" [S0889 L9] | a governed rule "became a runnable check" that changed one decision [S0043 L63]; under S1524's criterion, "0 / 22 executed" in one step series [S1524 L891] |
| **act**: an authorized interaction with an external system through a port (an interface to the outside, implemented by an adapter; Ch. 1 §1.16), whether it changes state or only reads | S1052's runtime design: request, then authorization, then "Execution Service", port and adapter [S1052 L482–502]; at request, "Nothing has executed yet." [S1052 L399] | the external system returns an execution fact, which the platform records with its action id [S1052 L510–526] |

- **The act has two parts:**
  - **dispatch**, in which the platform sends an authorized command through a port and adapter;
  - **performance**, in which the external system carries it out. The engineering environment's responsibility is to "Execute and expose real engineering state." [S1052 L80].

  S1052 uses "execute" for both.
- **Reads count.** S1052 deliberately makes its first execution a read ("deliberately keeping the first execution operation read-only" [S1052 L1655]), classed LOW risk [S1052 L977]. So an act need not change anything.
- **One execution per action id.** The platform "can determine whether A42 has already executed." [S1052 L1046]. A retry with the same action id is not a second physical execution [S1052 L1056–1058]. This fixes what counts as **one** execution.
- **Assembling is neither.** "The runtime executes." "The composition root creates the dependency graph." [S0166 L655–657]. "DI assembles capability." "Governance establishes legitimacy." [S0166 L795–797].
- **A residual use.** "Nothing in this document executes." [S0043 L112] uses "executes" to mean **having operative effect**, the opposite of advisory text. It is neither a run nor an act.
- **The transition use.** S0889 says the knowledge state evolves by a transition function, so that the model "can actually execute a sequence of knowledge transitions" [S0889 L9]. In this use, knowledge is what the transition **operates on**. The transition is what is executed. I-11 is not contradicted: the operand does not execute; the operation does.
- **The act may have a third layer.** S1524 reports, from a later step, a split into action, execution and technical command (S1524 L623, second-hand).
- **The control/data split is a hedged one.** KnowledgeOS is "Primarily" the control plane and the engineering systems "Primarily" the data plane [S1052 L860, L870]. The adapters are part of the platform's deployment (L1240–1249).

**Four referents of "capability" in these sources (Src).** These extend the three senses of Ch. 1 §1.10.

| Referent | Example |
|---|---|
| a **governed engineering capability** | CAP-001, a governed rule turned into a check [S0043 L25, L63] |
| a **replaceable technical service behind a port** | AI providers, vector databases, policy engines: "many replaceable capabilities" [S0166 L23] |
| a **semantic service of the platform** | "These are platform capabilities, not Claude-specific capabilities." [S1052 L167] |
| a **declared ability of an agent** | "Agent capability does not imply execution authority." [S1524 L286] |

- **The referents overlap at authorization (Open).** S0166 lists "authorization systems" and policy engines among the services replaceable by configuration (L30–31, L541). S1052 makes authorization an enforced boundary inside the platform (L452). Whether authorization is a swappable implementation of a fixed boundary, or the boundary itself, is stated by neither source.
- S0166's own guard bears on this: technical changes and governance changes "must not use the same approval path" (L1015–1038).

## 3.9.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. Fork F11 as it stands: what each source supports (Src; scope: the five T1 sources and the three statements of §3.8 C5; F11 is not settled here).**
- **The three statements:**
  - **A:** whatever executes is a capability.
  - **B:** executability belongs to an artifact's representation.
  - **C:** the capability is a port, and the adapters execute (S0029).

| Source | Supports | Against | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0043 | A's reading of the word | — | places "every engineering capability" among hooks and scripts [S0043 L52]; the principle concerns a rule realized as a runnable check (L63) |
| S0166 | C; A for service capabilities | — | capabilities are replaceable services behind ports [S0166 L23]; "The runtime executes." (L655) |
| S0889 | B, in the transition sense | — | the knowledge state is operated on; the transition executes (L9) |
| S1052 | C; A for platform services | A for governed capabilities is not addressed | execution through port and adapter (L482–502); "These are platform capabilities" (L167) |
| S1524 | none of A, B or C directly | — | reports "Capability ≠ Authority" (L567) and "Agent capability does not imply execution authority" (API-006, L286) |

- **The contradictions:**
  - The corpus uses "capability" both for things that run (services) and for governed responsibilities that do not run themselves (CAP-001's kind).
  - A conflicts with B or C only for the **governed** referent.
  - For service capabilities, A and C agree: they run.
- **The proposal on the table (C2):** a convention that a governed capability is not an artifact. Under it, A is false of governed capabilities. **This is a choice, not a finding.** Adopting it would close F11, and that is for a reviewer's verdict, not for this section.

**C2. A proposed convention: a runtime processes an artifact, and a governed capability is not an artifact (Int; a stipulation; scope: running only).** `[CON-0091 · invalid]` *(as first stated: "neither knowledge nor a capability executes", which the section's own referents refute)* `[CON-0095 · provisional]` *(the restricted convention)*
- **The relation:** runs(*r*, *a*): a runtime *r* (an interpreter, an engine, a CI job, a person, **or a running service**) processes an artifact *a* (program text or data).
- **Knowledge is not an artifact,** so it does not run. This needs only that knowledge and artifact are **distinct** (§3.4).
- **Capabilities.** The technical-service and platform-service referents **are** runtimes: they run. Only the **governed** capability (CAP-001's kind) is, by this convention, not an artifact and not a runtime. It is realized when artifacts run.
- **This is a choice, not a reading of the sources.** The sources use "execute" intransitively, with artifacts and actions as subject: the checker's `execute(input)` (S1524 L496) and "whether A42 has already executed" (S1052 L1046). The convention fits those uses but is not derived from them.
- *Counterexample attempts:*
  - A person carrying out a procedure is runs(person, procedure). Holds.
  - An engine evaluating trained weights holds once data may be the second argument.
  - A policy engine evaluating a rule is runs(engine, rule). The engine is a capability in the service sense, so under the convention it is a runtime, not a governed capability. Holds.
  - **A skill fails it.** Nature Skill is knowledge whose representation is tacit (GKC L16; Ch. 0 §0.3, n = Skill ⇒ r = Tacit). A person exercising a skill performs it, but there is no artifact a for runs(person, a). Two treatments: (i) skill lies outside "knowledge" in this convention; (ii) runs(person, skill), with a skill as a second argument type beside artifacts. Neither is chosen here.
- **Interim scope:** until (i) or (ii) is chosen, C2 is restricted to **explicit** knowledge (representation other than tacit).

**C3. "Knowledge does not execute" is a statement about the model (Int; scope: I-11 as stated).**
- S1524 calls statements such as "Recommendation ≠ Authorization" type distinctions that a running system cannot violate: "They cannot fail; therefore they cannot be checked; therefore they are not invariants." [S1524 L194].
- I-11 ("Knowledge does not execute. Capabilities execute.") is of this kind **as phrased**: it classifies, and nothing in it can be violated.
- **But "cannot fail" is too strong as a general criterion.** A type distinction can be violated as a **conflation**, for example a system that accepts a recommendation as an authorization. S1524 itself rates C-018, "Agent cannot self-authorize" (L413), as behaviourally testable (L446). Whether such a statement is checkable depends on how it is phrased, not on what it is about. This book therefore does not rely on S1524's criterion.
- So I-11 is **not a runtime invariant until its types are realized.** This is a scoped reason for §3.8's finding that it holds by stipulation (CON-0039, CON-0066).
- **A testable counterpart:** running an artifact changes no standing except through a recorded act. This is the runtime face of Ch. 1 §1.9 ("knowledge feeds authority; it never holds it").

**C4. Running a rule is not sufficient for enforcing it (Src + Ded).** `[CON-0092 · validated]` *(scope: S0043's classification of one repository on 2026-08-02. The claim needs one blocking and one advisory case, not the counts; the counts are one line of support with S0024 and S0008, Ch. 2 §2.10)*
- **S0043's three kinds of control** [S0043 L44–50]:
  - a **boundary**: the runtime blocks (`deny`, 19 entries);
  - a **trigger**: the runtime interrupts and hands the decision to a person (`ask`, 22);
  - an **advisory**: it "mentions it and proceeds" (10 hooks and 6 checks).

  A trigger does not block, so the source's "41 enforced" merges blocking with interrupting.
- **Ded:** a `deny` rule runs and stops the action; an advisory check runs and stops nothing. So running is **not sufficient** for enforcement. Enforcement is running plus a stop over the path of the action.
- **Two different non-enforcing cases:**
  - an **instruction** is not run by any runtime; an agent reads and interprets it. Hence, in S1052, a rule in an agent's instructions "is not a security boundary" [S1052 L466].
  - an **advisory check** is run, but has no stop.
- **Enforcement point and authority boundary are different things.** By S0043's count, "Enforcement exists exactly where governance does not, and is absent exactly where it does." [S0043 L53]: the enforced controls sit in the runtime adapter. S1052 denies the agent environment **authority** (RT-001), but keeps **enforcement** there: "Hooks should enforce local properties." [S1052 L1419]. Its platform gate is a target design and is unbuilt.

**C5. Executing as acting: dispatch and performance (Src + Int; scope: the act sense).**
- **In S1052, the platform dispatches.** It contains an Execution Service (L490) and an Action module (L1218), keeps execution identity (L1046), and handles retries of a mutating action (L1023). The external system performs; it is "authoritative for its actual runtime state" and "knows what repositories actually exist." [S1052 L844].
- **The control plane / data plane split** [S1052 L862–876] places performance in the engineering systems. An earlier step's "Centralize authority, not necessarily execution." [S1524 L839] **permits** central execution; it does not forbid it.
- **Int:** the platform dispatches and records, and the external system performs. Whether the platform "executes" therefore depends on which half is meant. Which context owns execution is open in the corpus itself: "Is Execution part of Agent/Action, or an Engineering Execution context?" [S1524 L240].
- **Two open overlaps:**
  - For a **read**, the act and an observation through the same adapter (S1052 L532–546) are the same physical event. The line between execution and evidence vanishes exactly where S1052 begins.
  - Whether the returned **execution fact** is itself evidence, or only the separately captured observation is, S1052 leaves unstated (L510–526).

**C5a. A new fork: can a mechanism change standing? (Open; bears on F9 and Ch. 1 A1.)**
- In S1052, authorization is a **service**. "The Action Service invokes the Authorization boundary" [S1052 L405]; it evaluates principal, policy, scope, context and conditions and returns ALLOW or DENY.
- S0889 has the same shape in the epistemic domain: a commitment gate decides whether an assertion enters the knowledge state under an epistemic contract.
- Under Ch. 1's axiom A1 and ARCH-INV-1 (mechanisms record; they never grant), a machine ALLOW cannot itself be an act. There are two readings:
  1. the ALLOW is a **mechanism applying a prior grant**. The act is the adoption of the policy it applies. This is consistent with A1.
  2. the ALLOW **changes standing** by itself. This contradicts A1.

  The sources do not choose. Registered as fork **F12**.

**C6. Executable is not executed (Ded; connects to evidence grades).** `[CON-0093 · validated]` *(scope: the distinction and the grading rule)*
- **An instance:** S0889's verdict is "PASS — Minimal executable state model is viable" [S0889 L1080]. Its experiments are worked by hand: no code is run and no output is reproduced. Its worked cases also contain indexing slips, where snapshot and event numbers do not match.
- **The source states the narrower claim itself:** "We have shown something narrower" [S0889 L1124], namely that "A coherent executable epistemic state machine can be constructed from the current theory." [S0889 L1128]. That is constructibility in outline.
- **A stronger instance, as S1524 reports it:** result blocks in two later steps print specific counts (47 PASS, 42 PASS) with no run behind them. Being "mutually inconsistent", they are "decorative rather than derived" [S1524 L900].
- **S0889's own cases do not show consistency.** Its worked cases contain indexing slips (snapshot and event numbers do not match, and "Uncertain" and "Unknown" are used for one case). So the result is constructibility **in outline** only.
- **Grading rule:** a specification is never evidence that anything ran. Evidence that something ran is an **observed** run or an **observed effect**: machine output, a block, a changed decision, an external system's record.
- **S1524's own counts** ("0 / 22 executed", grep and md5 results) are reported without transcripts. By its own criterion they are reports, graded Src, not machine evidence present in the document.

**C7. Four criteria for evidence of execution, and what each answers (Src + Ded).** `[CON-0094 · provisional]`

| Criterion | Source | Grade here | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** a decision changed | S0043 L63–64 (n = 1; the check itself was not read here) | observed effect, reported | did running it have an effect? |
| **(b)** replay equality, determinism, idempotency | S0889 L719, L751–754, L303 | required properties, not observed | would a run be correct? |
| **(c)** an execution fact plus a record keyed by action id, with evidence captured separately | S1052 L510–546, L1046 | designed, unbuilt; the external system is authoritative for its own state (L844) | was the act performed, and by whom is that attested? |
| **(d)** machine-produced output (commands, fixtures, transcripts) | S1524 L863 | the strictest criterion; S1524 does not meet it itself | did a run occur? |

- **Ded:** the four do not compete. They answer **three different questions**: whether something was carried out ((c), (d)), whether it was carried out correctly ((b)), and whether it had an effect ((a)).
- Only (b) is a **correctness condition** rather than evidence. Treating (b) as evidence that something ran is the confusion of C6.
- **Connection:** criterion (c) makes the external system's record the evidence of performance. That ties execution evidence to fork F2 (external authority).

**C8. "Knowledge is an executable asset", read through the senses (Src + Ded).**
- S0043 recommends one principle: "Knowledge is an executable engineering asset, not passive documentation." [S0043 L59]. Its evidence is one changed decision: "The principle is demonstrated, not proven" [S0043 L63–64].
- **Ded:** by C2, what runs is the **check**, a realization of the rule, not the rule. So the principle means "knowledge can have a runnable realization", which is consistent with B and with I-11.
- *Scope:* S0043 names further checks of the same kind (`knowledge-lint.php`, `link-check.php`, `doc-placement.php --verify` [S0043 L63]); n = 1 applies to **decisions changed**.

## 3.9.4 Open questions

- **F11:** open. The question for review is whether to adopt the convention of C2 (a governed capability is not an artifact), or to keep A's reading of the corpus.
- **The vocabulary.** *Proposal, for review:* qualify every use (run, dispatch, perform; specified, performed). Reserve "execution" for performance of an authorized act, the sense tied to authority.
- **Authorization:** a swappable implementation of a fixed boundary, or the boundary itself (§3.9.2)?
- **F12:** does a machine ALLOW or a commitment gate change standing, or does it apply a prior grant (C5a)?
- **Read and observe:** when an act is a read, are execution and evidence one event (C5)? Is an execution fact evidence?
- **Evidence of execution:** how the four criteria of C7 combine for one act.
- **I-11's testable counterpart** (C3): running an artifact changes no standing except through a recorded act.
- **Who owns execution** (C5): the action context, or an execution context of its own.
- **Composition** as a further activity beside running, acting and authorizing (S0166): where it sits in §3.8's orders.
