## 8.88.1 In plain language

1. **The first adviser draws knowledge's life as a machine of states: proposed, admitted, contested, superseded, reconciled, withdrawn, rejected, insufficient evidence.** In the book a claim has no stored state that moves. What it shows is worked out from the record each time it is asked; only a recorded act changes standing.
2. **The reply objects that some of these "states" are really events, assessments or relations between two claims.** That agrees with the book, where having too little evidence is a grade read from the evidence, not a change of standing.
3. **The first adviser lets the kernel decide whether a change is allowed and another part apply it.** That reopens the gap between checking and acting which the book closes by doing both in one step.
4. **The reply separates five jobs: noticing, proposing, deciding, applying, recording.** In the book applying and recording are one thing: the recorded act is the change.
5. **The first adviser lets a claim be superseded when the other side's evidence weighs more, and lets a withdrawn claim leave the system.** In the book supersession is a further act, evidence never grants, and nothing leaves the record.

## 8.88.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A lifecycle drawn as a state machine over a claim (Src; Int).**
- **S0364 (A):** "the epistemic lifecycle is the **sequence of justified epistemic states** that a knowledge claim can occupy over time, and the **constitutionally governed transitions** between them." (L20); "The epistemic lifecycle is a **constitutional state machine**." (L175); "Every transition must be recorded in history." (L177); "**Admission is the first transition of the epistemic lifecycle.**" (L376).
- **S0364 (B):** "> **Admission is the first transition of the epistemic lifecycle, not the complete lifecycle.**" (L613); "But **I would not freeze these states yet**." (L640).
- **Int:**
  - **No stored state.** In the book a claim's displayed class is a function of the record at a cut, with standing's answers, the purpose and the rule version (§9.18.2). Standing changes only by a recorded act (A1-recognized), and the act recorded is the change (C1 = constitute). *There is no state of the claim that a transition moves; (A)'s machine has no counterpart in D* (as §8.87 C4 found for S0363 (B)'s question).
  - **What agrees.** The displayed class can change as entries arrive (T9-17 (b)), and every change is recorded (L177). *Agreement in direction that knowledge changes over time and its history is kept; not in the mechanism.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. State, event, assessment or relationship (Src; Int, agreement).**
- **S0364 (B):** "Some of these may be **true domain states**. · But some may instead be: · events, · conditions, · assessments, · relationships between claims, · lifecycle statuses, · or derived projections." (L669–678); "EvidenceAssessment = INSUFFICIENT" (L695); "It may describe a **relationship between two previously conflicting claims**." (L720); "Then derive state from events if appropriate." (L886).
- **S0364 (A):** "| ADMITTED | INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE | Evidence is invalidated or removed | Evidence no longer sufficient |" (L136).
- **Int:** *Agreement in direction with the book.* (i) A grade read from the evidence entries changes no standing (§9.18.6 (c2)); INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE corresponds, in the book, to such a grade (shown as "not attested" when there is none), *not a transition of standing*, as (B) proposes (L695). (ii) "derive state from events" (L886) agrees with evaluation over the record (C1 = constitute). (iii) "RECONCILED" as a relationship (L720): the book's "contested" is a displayed class computed from entries on both sides (T9-17 (b)), *not a state of either claim.* (B) leaves its classification open (L997); the book has the answer for these three, not for the whole table (L1001–1018).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. A split between the kernel deciding and another context applying (Src; Int).**
- **S0364 (A):** "The Kernel says: \"Is this transition admissible?\" · The Epistemic Context says: \"Apply this transition if it is admissible.\"" (L494–495); "This separates **decision** (Kernel) from **application** (Epistemic Context)." (L570); "The Kernel is a **pure constitutional evaluator**" (L573); "| **Risk** | Splitting evaluation and application may create consistency issues |" (L425).
- **S0364 (B):** "elegance is not sufficient evidence for a bounded context." (L776).
- **Int:**
  - **The gap.** This is S0361's split (§8.86 C4), now for every transition. *A verdict given in one step and applied in another can go stale between them;* (A) names the risk (L425) and does not answer it. In the book (i) standing is evaluated over the record, so for most kinds no prior verdict exists to go stale; (ii) where a check precedes the append, authority is evaluated in the same kernel step as the append (§9.13 L48; §9.15 G4), one step per grant (K4-k), serialised (K6).
  - **Not pure by its own text.** (A)'s kernel also does "History recording" and "Identity assignment" (L481–483, L560–561), so it is not a pure evaluator (L573) in §8.86's sense.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Trigger, proposal, adjudication, application, recording (Src; Int).**
- **S0364 (B):** "### Trigger · Who detects something? · ### Proposal · Who proposes the transition? · ### Adjudication · Who determines whether it is permissible? · ### Application · Who changes domain state? · ### Recording · Who records the resulting domain event? · Those are **different responsibilities**." (L824–844); "I would **not accept that as established**." (L788), of (A)'s "All transitions that change epistemic state require constitutional evaluation." (L347).
- **Int:**
  - **Application and recording are one in the book.** Under C1 = constitute the recorded act is the change; there is no separate application of a decided transition. *(B)'s fourth and fifth responsibilities have one counterpart in D, the append.* Trigger and proposal lie with producers outside D; adjudication is the evaluation of the act.
  - **(A)'s premise (L347).** In the book every entry passes the kernel's append rules (§9.15 K1, the exclusive write path), but a displayed class can change by an evidence entry, which is not a standing act (§9.18.4). *So (A)'s premise holds for appends and not for standing acts in particular; (B)'s doubt (L788) is right that the two questions differ (L912–918).*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. Supersession by evidence weight; withdrawal as leaving the system (Src; Int).**
- **S0364 (A):** "| **Superseded** | A claim has been replaced by a stronger claim |" (L43); "| CONTESTED | SUPERSEDED | One side wins the contest | Evidence weight difference exceeds threshold |" (L131); "**What is established**: The claim is no longer part of KnowledgeOS (though its history remains)." (L231).
- **S0364 (B):** "> Is \"supersession\" actually replacing a knowledge object, or is it creating a new knowledge state/version while preserving the original identity/history?" (L948).
- **Int:**
  - **Supersession.** In the book supersession is a further act (§1.6 table: a governance-act progression is changed by "a further act (supersession)"); evidence earns, governance grants (§1.6). *(A)'s threshold on evidence weight (L131) makes evidence grant,* as §8.84 C3 found for S0358–S0359. (B)'s question (L948) is answered in the book in its second form: a further act, with the earlier entries kept.
  - **Withdrawal.** The book's withdrawal is withdrawal of reliance for a purpose, an entry (§9.18.4); nothing leaves the record. *(A)'s "no longer part of KnowledgeOS" has no counterpart;* its parenthesis, "though its history remains", agrees.
- **Not a new concept.**
