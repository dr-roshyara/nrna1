## 8.88.1 In plain language

1. **The first adviser draws knowledge's life as one machine of states: proposed, admitted, contested, superseded, reconciled, withdrawn, rejected, insufficient evidence.** The book keeps two things apart that this machine merges: changes that evidence brings and changes that only an act can make. A single machine for both would force either evidence that grants by decree or decisions that wait on evidence that may never come. *(Restated at review: W-01, W-02)*
2. **The reply objects that some of these "states" are really events, assessments or relations between two claims.** That agrees with the book's principle that what is shown is derived from the record; for "rejected" the book already says it is an outcome, not a state. Several of the other mappings remain open in the book.
3. **The first adviser lets the kernel decide whether a change is allowed and another part apply it.** That reopens the gap between checking and acting, which the book closes, for the checks it makes at append, by doing both in one serialised step. *(Restated at review: W-14)*
4. **The reply separates five jobs: noticing, proposing, deciding, applying, recording.** In the book applying and recording are one thing: the recorded act is the change. And for most kinds the book records first and evaluates after, the reverse of the reply's order.
5. **The first adviser lets a claim be superseded when the other side's evidence weighs more, and lets a withdrawn claim leave the system.** In the book evidence never grants standing; supersession is recognized only when recorded, and its kind is not yet admitted. No entry leaves without trace. *(Restated at review: W-07, W-09)*
