## 8.84.1 In plain language

1. **The second adviser treats a sentence the first adviser wrote as the user's own description.** What looks like a second opinion carries the first one's words.
2. **Its worked example starts the adoption review of a stopped work item and marks every check PASS, though its own rule for a stopped item does not allow that action.** The second reply repeats the conflict in its rule language.
3. **It weighs evidence by fixed numbers, gives more weight to recent evidence and longer provenance chains, multiplies by whether the author is in an authority register, and counts a constitutional rule as the heaviest evidence.** The first adviser had said evidence is not authority; the book combines support and refutation without weights.
4. **Its kernel executes the transitions itself and forbids merging identities.** The first adviser put workflow execution outside the kernel; the book unites records by content identity.
5. **It separates duties by role (an owner role may not be among the reviewer roles), not by process.** The estate's bars are by session label.

## 8.84.2 Claims and derivations

- **S0358:** "The most important sentence in your description is: · > **\"deterministically determines whether a proposed knowledge-state transition is constitutionally admissible\"**" (L367–369); "Building on our previous architectural discussion, here's a concrete design for the KnowledgeOS Kernel:" (L53).

- **Int:** The quoted words are S0357's (L473), presented by S0358 as the user's ("your description", L367). *So the user relayed one adviser's text to another, and the second treats it as the user's requirement.* Its "Six Pillars" (L16–25) are S0357's capacities less admissibility and determinism. *Agreement between S0357 and S0358 is relay, not independence* (§8.06 C1, lineage; §8.33: lenses from one line are one voice). §8.34 found a homonym that grows by relay; here a requirement does.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. A worked example that breaks its own rule (Src; Ded).**
- **S0358:**
  - "  proposal: \"Start the Governance adoption review of KOS-OPERATING-MODEL-001.\"" (L159); "    action: START_ADOPTION_REVIEW" (L161);
  - "    history: PASS (entity is in STOPPED state, continuation required)" (L170); "    status: RESOLVED" (L172);
  - "  CHECK: if entity.status == STOPPED then proposed_action in [CONTINUATION, HANDOFF, ESCALATE]" (L204).
- **S0359:**
  - "            STOPPED: proposal.intent.action in [CONTINUE, ESCALATE, SUPERSEDE]" (L151); "            INITIATED: proposal.intent.action in [START_REVIEW, ABANDON]" (L148);
  - "TRANSITION_RULE start_adoption_review:" (L183); "    from_state: status == \"INITIATED\" OR status == \"STOPPED\"" (L185); "        - context_lifecycle_transition" (L194).
- **Ded:** In S0358, the proposal's action is START_ADOPTION_REVIEW (L161) on an entity in STOPPED (L170). Its own context rule allows only CONTINUATION, HANDOFF or ESCALATE from STOPPED (L204). *So the context check should FAIL; the example records PASS and RESOLVED* (L167–172). In S0359, the transition rule `start_adoption_review` may start from INITIATED or STOPPED (L185) and requires `context_lifecycle_transition` (L194), which allows START_ADOPTION_REVIEW from neither state (L148, L151). *So the transition rule can never be satisfied.*
- **Int:** The case is the estate's live one (§8.81 C2; §8.82 C3): the order to start the adoption review on a stopped item. *The adviser's example passes it where its own rule refuses it,* and its "history: PASS … continuation required" (L170) records the blocker as a pass. *The adviser's design is not shown consistent by its own example.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Evidence weighed by number, with authority folded in (Src; Int).**
- **S0359:**
  - "    CONSTITUTIONAL: 1.0 · CONSENSUS: 0.9 · APPROVAL: 0.8 · EMPIRICAL_DATA: 0.7 · EXPERT_OPINION: 0.6 · OBSERVATION: 0.5 · PROPOSAL: 0.4 · REVIEW_COMMENT: 0.3 · REFERENCE: 0.2" (L382–390);
  - "    - condition: evidence.author in authority_register · weight_multiplier: 1.2" (L393–394); "      weight_multiplier: 1.1 (more provenance = more weight)" (L396); "      weight_multiplier: 1.1 (recent evidence = more weight)" (L400);
  - "  - CONSTITUTIONAL: A constitutional rule itself (highest weight)" (L322);
  - "        SUPERSEDE: evidence.weight(claimA) > evidence.weight(claimB) * 1.5" (L178).
- **S0358:** "    - Score by evidence strength" (L92); "         IF proposed_state.evidence > existing_state.evidence THEN supersede" (L211).
- **S0357 (for checking):** "> **Evidence ≠ authority.**" (L91).
- **Int:**
  - **Authority folded into evidence.** An author's presence in an authority register multiplies the weight of the evidence (L393–394), and a constitutional rule is itself the heaviest evidence (L322). *That joins what S0357 separated ("Evidence ≠ authority", L91) and what A1 separates:* authority is a recorded act of a holder, not a weight of support. §8.38 C1 placed the producing process's reliability in Q4's grading rule, not in standing.
  - **Weights without a ground.** The base weights, the multipliers and the thresholds (1.5, 20%) are stated, not derived; longer provenance and recency raise weight by fiat (L396, L400). *A longer chain of custody is more places to be wrong as much as more support.*
  - **The book combines without weights.** Under g1 (T9-17 (b)), live evidence that both supports and refutes gives **contested**, not the heavier side (§9.18). *S0359's supersession by weight ratio is a rival rule;* recorded, not adopted.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. A kernel that executes; identity that may not merge (Src; Int).**
- **S0358:** "    executed_by: urn:kos:actor:system:kernel" (L176); "│  │          5. TRANSITION EXECUTION (if RESOLVED)         │   │" (L98); "  \"An entity must be uniquely identified and cannot be merged or split\"" (L191).
- **S0359:** "  - EXECUTING: Transition is being applied" (L426).
- **Int:**
  - **Execution inside the kernel.** S0357 kept the workflow engine outside the kernel ("Transition X is admissible" against "Execute process Y", §8.83, S0357 L320–326). *S0358 and S0359 put execution inside it and name the kernel as the executing actor* (L176). The two advisers' kernels differ on the boundary S0357 called a separate responsibility. *The book's guard accepts an append (§9.15 G2, G4); it does not itself perform the act.*
  - **No merge.** S0358 forbids merging entities (L191). The book unites records by content identity (§9.16 R2), and §8.83 C3 recorded the tension between act identity from content and S0357's identity never from representation. *S0358 adds a third position on identity:* never merged.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. Separation of duties by role (Src; Int).**
- **S0359:** "    invariant no_self_review: owner not in reviewers" (L94); "    owner: role" (L89); "    reviewers: list[role]" (L90); "      check: evidence.author != evidence.subject" (L372).
- **S0358:** "| Unauthorized actor | Return `UNAUTHORIZED`, do NOT execute |" (L270); "The kernel **never** guesses, **never** approximates, and **never** executes an invalid transition." (L274).
- **Int:** The invariant compares roles (L89–90, L94). *Two processes in different roles pass it; one process in two roles is caught only if its roles are recorded as one.* The estate's bars compare session labels (§8.79.3), and the book's independence has several factors beyond role (§8.06 C1). *Neither the role check nor the label check alone is independence.* The fail-closed table (L265–274) agrees with §9.13's U-with-reasons in direction; *agreement.*
- **Not a new concept.**
