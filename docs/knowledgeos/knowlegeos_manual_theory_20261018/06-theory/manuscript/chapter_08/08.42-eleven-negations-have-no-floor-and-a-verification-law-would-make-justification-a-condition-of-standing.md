## 8.42.1 In plain language

1. **The eleven "kernel laws" say what must never happen, or what must hold of whatever is kept.** The estate's own matrix calls this their strength: each says what must never collapse. None requires that anything be kept at all, so a system that accepts nothing and answers "unknown" to every question breaks none of them. The book rules that system out because it requires inputs to be accepted. The sentence the estate's architect singled out as "the correct constitutional boundary" is exactly this negation-only floor.
2. **One law says no claim may enter knowledge without a preserved justification path.** In the book, standing comes from an act; an adopted item with no evidence keeps its standing, and the display then says "not attested". Read as "an adoption must cite its justification", the law would be a guard, and a stronger one than the choice already open for the Principal: it asks for four things at once, and the book's record has no place for three of them.
3. **The map says research has reached "saturation": ten rounds without a new row.** But the map itself shows new proposals in those rounds, folded into existing rows or not admitted. Proposals did not stop; admissions did. Zero new rows measures the admission choice, not that nothing new remains.
4. **The kernel decision says the laws are "selected" and, on the same line, "not adopted".** The record states that the architect accepts them; the words it quotes from him are an assessment and a recommendation, not the acceptance. Whether an act of acceptance occurred is not visible here.
5. **Several laws agree with the book:** authority is assigned by a recorded human act; conflicting evidence on one item is shown as "contested"; a statement is never valid beyond its delimiting conditions; no value passes from one dimension to another without an explicit act. Others go further than the book: "revision never deletes" holds except for an authorised removal, which drops content and keeps a trace; a conflict between two claims, failed reasoning kept as a state, and relations between items have no place in the book's record yet.
6. **Of 29 rows (25 candidates and 4 Established), 26 "survive", none is rejected; the 22 surviving candidate rows are rendered as 11 laws "without loss of content", and the 4 Established "appear unchanged as the spine".** "The smallest possible set" here means fewer headings, not less content.

## 8.42.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Eleven negations have no floor (Src; Ded against §9.10 F-vac; Int).** *(Premise restated at review: W-06.)*
- **S0239 §4.4:** "A system that applies the eleven laws — and refuses every collapse they forbid. … That is the floor. Remove any one of the eleven and a named corruption appears … Below the eleven there is no KnowledgeOS." (L235–237). §5: "every law is a *negation* — a 'must never collapse X into Y' — which is a directly testable property" (L245).
- **S0240:** the HPA's P5 assessment, quoted as the decision's basis: "The most important sentence from the deliverable is: 'A system that applies the eleven laws — and refuses every collapse they forbid.' That is the correct constitutional boundary." (L3).
- **Ded:**
  - Each of the eleven laws (S0239 L190–200; S0240 L27–37) is universal over knowledge states ("never constitute", "never anonymous", "authority is assigned", "a relationship") or conditional on an event ("never deletes" on revision, "coexists" on conflict). None requires that anything exist.
  - A system that accepts no entry and answers U (or nothing) holds no knowledge state, so every universal holds vacuously; law 9's "UNKNOWN first-class", read as a capability, is met by the constant-U answer. It satisfies all eleven.
  - So the "floor" excludes nothing a trivial system would do (cf. CON-0099, §9.1). The book excludes the empty, refuse-everything and no-op systems by its must-dos: they fail O1–O4, the inputs to accept (§9.10 F-vac); constant answerers fail Q1.

- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. A verification law would make justification a condition of standing (Src; Ded against A1; Int).** *(Reading (ii) restated at review: W-01.)*
- **S0239 law 6, "Verification law":** "no claim enters knowledge without a preserved justification path: authentic evidence (never pseudo-evidence) + acquisition method + inference rule + reasoning path; generation ≠ justification" (L195); carried unchanged into S0240 (L32).
- **Ded, three readings** (as §8.37 C1, §8.38 C1):
  - **(i) a value function** (standing computed from the justification path): breaks A1, only an act confers standing (§1.6).
  - **(ii) a guard on adoption** (an adoption is refused unless it cites a justification path): keeps A1. It is of the guard kind, but **conjunctive and stronger** than the fork recorded in the map ("cites its warrant, **or** an identified reliable source", THEORY-MAP; **a choice for the Principal**, against the proposed clause (c2) *(decided: no guard; c2 kept, (the Principal, 2026-10-31: "No guard, keep c2"; T9-20))*). Of its four parts, three need carriers D does not admit: no warrant field (§8.37 C1), no source-kind field (§8.38 C1; §8.36 C5), and the reasoning path is content, not record (§8.37 C4). "Authentic" is the world assumption that each evidence entry is what it says it is (§9.10). *As a kernel guard it cannot be evaluated on D as it stands.*
  - **(iii) a content rule** ("knowledge" as content that applications treat as known): the kernel has no content value; an application matter.
- **Against the book as it stands:** an adopted item without evidence keeps standing T, shown with "not attested" (promise (b), T10-01; §8.40 C4). Under reading (i) the law contradicts that; under (ii) it would change it, by a decision not yet taken.
- **Not a new concept.**
