## 8.42.1 In plain language

1. **The eleven "kernel laws" are all prohibitions.** The estate's own matrix calls this their strength: each says what must never collapse. But a system that accepts nothing and answers nothing breaks none of them. The book rules that system out because it requires inputs to be accepted. The sentence the estate's architect singled out as "the correct constitutional boundary" is exactly this negation-only floor.
2. **One law says no claim may enter knowledge without a preserved justification path.** In the book, standing comes from an act; an adopted item with no evidence keeps its standing, and the display then says "not attested". Read as "an adoption must cite its justification", the law is a choice that is open for the Principal, not something the book already holds.
3. **The map says research has reached "saturation": ten rounds without a new row.** But its own first rule forbids adding rows, and its rulings fold new proposals into existing rows. Zero new rows is what the rules produce, so it is not evidence that nothing new remains.
4. **The kernel decision says the laws are "selected" and, on the same line, "not adopted".** It is an assistant-written record of the architect's acceptance, quoting him. The act is the acceptance; the document is a record about it.
5. **Several laws agree with the book:** authority is assigned by a recorded human act; conflicting knowledge stays "conflicted" until a recorded resolution; a statement is never valid beyond its delimiting conditions; no value passes from one dimension to another without an explicit act. One goes further than the book: "revision never deletes" holds except for an authorised removal, which drops content and keeps a trace.
6. **Of 29 candidate rows, 26 "survive", none is rejected, and the 29 are rendered as 11 laws "without loss of content".** "The smallest possible set" here means fewer headings, not less content.

## 8.42.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Eleven negations have no floor (Src; Ded against §9.10 F-vac; Int).**
- **S0239 §4.4:** "A system that applies the eleven laws — and refuses every collapse they forbid. … That is the floor. Remove any one of the eleven and a named corruption appears … Below the eleven there is no KnowledgeOS." (L235–237). §5: "every law is a *negation* — a 'must never collapse X into Y' — which is a directly testable property" (L245).
- **S0240:** the HPA's P5 assessment, quoted as the decision's basis: "The most important sentence from the deliverable is: 'A system that applies the eleven laws — and refuses every collapse they forbid.' That is the correct constitutional boundary." (L3).
- **Ded:**
  - Each of the eleven laws (S0239 L190–200; S0240 L27–37) is a prohibition ("never constitute", "never determines", "never deletes", "never anonymous") or a duty to keep ("preserved", "coexists").
  - A system that accepts no entry and returns no answer holds no knowledge, so no collapse can occur in it: it satisfies all eleven.
  - So the "floor" excludes nothing a trivial system would do. The book excludes the empty, refuse-everything and no-op systems by its must-dos: they fail O1–O4, the inputs to accept (§9.10 F-vac); constant answerers fail Q1.
- **Int:** the estate's line already knew the pattern (S0217 (C)'s "a kernel defined only by what it makes impossible", §8.35 C7; §8.39 C4) and did not apply it here. The decision singles out the very sentence that has it. *Not a refutation of any law; a missing half.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. A verification law would make justification a condition of standing (Src; Ded against A1; Int).**
- **S0239 law 6, "Verification law":** "no claim enters knowledge without a preserved justification path: authentic evidence (never pseudo-evidence) + acquisition method + inference rule + reasoning path; generation ≠ justification" (L195); carried unchanged into S0240 (L32).
- **Ded, three readings** (as §8.37 C1, §8.38 C1):
  - **(i) a value function** (standing computed from the justification path): breaks A1, only an act confers standing (§1.6).
  - **(ii) a guard on adoption** (an adoption is refused unless it cites a justification path): keeps A1, and is the guard fork already recorded in the map, **a choice for the Principal**, against the proposed clause (c2).
  - **(iii) a content rule** ("knowledge" as content that applications treat as known): the kernel has no content value; an application matter.
- **Against the book as it stands:** an adopted item without evidence keeps standing T, shown with "not attested" (promise (b), T10-01; §8.40 C4). Under reading (i) the law contradicts that; under (ii) it would change it, by a decision not yet taken.
- **Not a new concept.**
