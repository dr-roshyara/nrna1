## 8.15.1 In plain language

A claim the platform can stand behind is a claim about its own record ("an acknowledgement was recorded"), not about the world ("the session acknowledged"). Something that cannot be settled must stay unsettled, and must never be rounded up to "valid" or down to "missing". A test that requires a history cannot be passed at any single moment, so establishment comes in levels. A decision is recorded by what it says, not by its letter in a list. And a name for a criterion is not yet the criterion.

The existence decision for C-10 rested on the fact that none of its own activities had happened. That corrects what §8.14 said about it.

## 8.15.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. An adopted invariant never exercised does not establish existence, and C-5's two-limb test is not a general rule (Src; narrows §8.12 C7).** `[CON-0215 · provisional]`
- Class C, the evidence that "MUST NOT establish existence by themselves" [S0116 L23], includes "an adopted invariant that has never been exercised" [S0116 L23]. D1 itself is put in Class C: "an adopted semantic never exercised" (S0116 L25, in meaning).
- The work statement forbids carrying C-5's test over: "DO NOT import C-5's existence test automatically. DO NOT assume 'adopted invariant + performed capability' is a universal rule." [S0116 L98]. The two-limb test "was derived from C-5's evidence, never adopted as a general rule" (S0116 L100, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.12 C7, CON-0199; §8.14 C1, CON-0213).** §8.12 C7's sufficient condition holds for C-5, as the source treated it; it is not a law for every capability. S0116 also reverses the reading of D1 made in the C-10 preparation: a decided semantic is Class C, which agrees with §8.14 C1 (the decided boundary was not counted).
- A single instance cannot show "a distinct reason to change", which "requires a HISTORY of changes" [S0116 L41]. And the incidental-versus-owned test: "does the acting context OWN the outcome, or does it fall out of another context's mechanism?" [S0116 L42].

**C2. A handoff only from the current owner can leave work unreachable (Src; evidence for §9.16 R4a's transfer rule).** *(No concept: an observation, bearing on F22.)*
- Under the workflow engine's rule, a handoff to a new lane "must come from the current mutation owner" (S0116 L63, in meaning). The current owner holds a refusal, so a handoff from it would set it to handed off, "misrepresenting an OUTSTANDING verification as concluded" [S0116 L65].
- And "a lane with no recorded handoff can never legally START" [S0116 L67]. So "Registering an assignment now would create a permanently unreachable lane." [S0116 L67].
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** This is the corpus case of the rival R4a rejected: S0071's "Only the current mutation owner may hand off" [S0071 L276]. Here an owner that cannot honestly hand off blocks every successor. Under the Principal's rule (after C20), ownership of a grant is moved by the issuer or an authority above the issuer's scope, never by the holder. So such a block can be lifted without the owner. One case; it supports the rule's reason, not its necessity. *(Corrected at C23, §8.16 C2: S0121 withdraws the "permanently unreachable" claim as too strong. The lane was reachable by releasing the owner, at the cost of a misleading state. So this is weaker evidence: of a forced release, not of a deadlock.)* *(Amended at the C23 review, §8.16 C2: the release was legitimate, and it was recorded by Governance; the engine's "from the current owner" names the lane. So the case is not evidence against the rival rule at all.)*

**C3. A revisit trigger needs an observer and an authority (Src).** `[CON-0216 · provisional]`
- The trigger must be "SELF-EVIDENCING", since "a condition requiring an observer will never fire" when monitoring is owned by nobody (S0116 L43, in meaning).
- Strengthened to four fields: "Trigger · Evidence · Observer · AUTHORITY" [S0116 L156]. "an observer who notices the trigger but holds no authority to reopen D2 does not close the loop." [S0116 L158].
- S0117's answer (window C22): the evidence source is "the governed record itself", and the observation path piggybacks on an existing mandatory read. That is "not observer-free — it is observer-minimised" (S0117 L257–258, in meaning). The authority path has two parts: Governance must register a detection, as a duty; only the PO/ARB may reopen. "Detection is never automatic revision" (S0117 L259, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15; §9.16 R3a).** Detection (R3a's "detect" laws) has the same need. A detected violation is corrected only by a recorded act of an authority entitled to make it. A detection with no observer, or with an observer who has no such authority, is not a detection that closes. For the kernel, a mark is the observation; who acts on it is not given by the mark.

**C4. Four orthogonal dimensions of evidence; independence is not one of them (Src; relates §8.12 C2, C6).** `[CON-0217 · provisional]`
- The governing rule: "a model describing a capability is not evidence that the capability exists" [S0117 L11].
- The commission's list of evidence classes mixes a provenance value (GOVERNED), an epistemic value (OBSERVED) "and two proposed properties of the OBSERVATION METHOD" [S0117 L53]. The proposal separates four dimensions:
  - **subject** ("what is it about?");
  - **epistemic** ("how well is it known?");
  - **provenance** ("whose artifact is it?");
  - **reproducibility** ("can anyone re-derive it?").

  It adds the fourth only after testing it as orthogonal, necessary and sufficient (S0117 L57–63, L79–82, in meaning). *Orthogonality is the source's claim, not shown (W-8):* its own classification puts a "proposed schema" in Class C while I-K1 "does not even reach Class C, being PROPOSED" (S0116 L25; S0117 L92, in meaning). So the subject dimension is not independent of the epistemic one in its use.
- A decided semantic shows why the split matters: "A DECIDED semantic is a strong claim on the EPISTEMIC dimension and an empty one on the SUBJECT dimension." [S0117 L93].
- Independence is kept out. It is C-5's "subject matter, not an evidence class" [S0117 L68], and requiring it would make C-10 unestablishable "for reasons that have nothing to do with C-10" [S0117 L70].
- Reproducibility does the work instead: "A reproducible observation cannot be certified away: anyone can re-run it." [S0117 L72]. And "recording identity ≠ attesting identity": provenance reconstruction needs re-derivation, not an attested observer (S0117 L190, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.12 CON-0196, CON-0201; §9.13 marks).** CON-0196 grades authorship evidence, and CON-0201 has two levels: holds, and can be established. Both sit on the epistemic and provenance dimensions here. CE*'s marks are provenance marks (transcribed, unsigned, removed). Reproducibility is a fourth axis that the book has not used. Under it, a check whose evidence any party can re-run does not depend on trusting **who computed** it. *Limited at review (W-9):* re-deriving re-runs the computation, not the truth of the record's fields. A field such as the recording application still depends on L10 (§9.13 check 24k). This bears on the kernel: an answer re-derivable from the record is checkable without trusting the evaluator, not without trusting the record. *(One producer, the barred author of the analysis, writing the test for its own model; S0117 L8, in meaning.)*

**C5. A conjunction that includes a history cannot be met at first realization; establishment comes in levels (Src; restated at review, W-1, W-2).** `[CON-0224 · provisional]` `[CON-0218 · invalid as stated]`
- E10, operation over time, "is the only criterion that cannot be satisfied at a point in time" [S0117 L213]. The source concludes that the full conjunction would make the capability "PERMANENTLY UNESTABLISHABLE" [S0117 L225].
- **That conclusion does not follow (W-1, the critical finding).** It holds only if the test is applied to evidence from a single moment. Applied to the record up to a time t, E10 becomes true once a history exists. The source's own L3 contains E1–E10 (S0117 L236, in meaning). So the conjunction is unsatisfiable **at the first realization**, not permanently. Option A is near the source's own L3. *(Version 1 adopted "permanently" and offered a "Mathematically" step whose premise, a snapshot of one moment, was missing.)*
  - *A fork, added at C24 (§8.17 C2):* if E10's history is counted from establishment, Option A as the threshold can never be met, and the source's "permanently" holds. The sources do not say where the history begins. The CAP-01 counterexample below needs CAP-01's recurrences to count as E6's "independent governed acts"; the safer ground is E6 necessary, E7 discriminating (S0117).
  - *Stated correctly:* if one conjunct is a property of a history and the test reads only the state at one instant, it is false at that instant. If the test reads the record up to t, the conjunct can become true as the record grows. So a test indexed by the record up to t is satisfiable later; one read at the moment of first realization is not. *This is a source defect (S0117 L225) and is listed separately.*
- The proposal stratifies: prototype, operational and authoritative levels, "each a conjunction within the level" (S0117 L230–236, in meaning). One instance gives L1 only (S0117 L240, in meaning).
- **Repetition is not the discriminator (W-2).** The source says that repetition across at least two acts "is the discriminator between an owned capability and an incidental artifact" [S0117 L163]. Its own worked case refutes this: CAP-01's bootstrap delivery is live (S0120 L59, in meaning) and recurs at every session start (S0117 L179, in meaning), yet it is incidental. What discriminates is the counterfactual test, "remove C-10 — does the receipt still appear?" (S0117 L178, in meaning). Repetition (E6) is necessary at the operational level, not sufficient for ownership.
- **Int (§8.12 C7; §8.15 C1).** What replaces C-5's two-limb test for C-10 is a level structure, with the counterfactual test for ownership. A threshold that no history can meet would be §8.14 C7's trivial case. Option A is not that case, since its history conjunct can be met later.
- **Undisposed acts block downstream definitions.** E2's content "cannot be finally specified while the D1 two-act conflict is undisposed" (S0117 L122, in meaning): §8.13 C5's later act, which is not a supersession, now blocks a later criterion.

**C6. A decision is identified by its proposition, not by its label (Src; the content identity of §9.16 R2).** `[CON-0219 · validated]`
- A third act on the D1 slot reuses the option letters: "The labels are not merely colliding — they are CROSSED." [S0118 L51]. Its Option B matches the earlier Option C's headline, and its Option C matches that option's body (S0118 L52, in meaning).
- So "a future instruction "adopt Option C" is UNRESOLVABLE on the record" (S0118 L53, in meaning), and "Any selection must therefore be recorded by its PROPOSITION TEXT, not by its letter." [S0118 L53].
- The slot now holds three acts, and the first two remain undisposed (S0118 L8, L39, in meaning). §8.13 C5's rule holds again: the later act does not supersede, and the decided text stands (S0118 L39, in meaning).
- The act states an anti-corruption rule: "reference ≠ ownership · recording ≠ asserting · correlation ≠ authority" (S0118 L19, in meaning). This extends §8.13 C1 (CON-0202) with a third pair.
- **Int (§9.16 R2).** The book identifies an act by its content: the identity is derived from the fields CE* reads, so two copies with one identity must agree. S0118 is the same rule for decisions. A label (a letter, a position in a list) is not an identity; across acts it can denote different propositions. A record that stores the label and not the proposition cannot be resolved. *Not covered by content alone (W-12):* the act's **force** (decision or recommendation). S0120 registers a text headed "RECOMMENDED" as a selection, on structural grounds, and flags the reading (S0120 L638, in meaning). Force is a separate field, and content identity does not fix it. *Limit:* one case, recorded by the author of the recommendation the act answers (S0118 L5, in meaning).

**C7. A referent gap is caused by requiring the reference, not by recording it (Src; resolves §8.13 C2's case).** `[CON-0220 · validated]`
- The structural point: "The referent gap is caused by the reference being REQUIRED, not by its being recorded." [S0119 L33]. The recommended proposition makes the reference optional and non-authoritative: a receipt "MAY contain external correlation references" for "traceability only" (S0119 L67, in meaning).
- Under it, references are "an annotation, not a claim" (S0119 L39, in meaning), and a receipt can be populated before the referenced act has ever occurred (S0119 L30, in meaning).
- The two rival propositions fail for opposite reasons. One keeps the required field, so the gap persists. The other places applicability "fully outside", which contradicts the decided rule that the capability "MAY record references" (S0119 L29, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.13 C2, CON-0203; §9.13 D).** CON-0203 named the gap; this is its resolution in the source. For D: a required field that must name an entry is a gap whenever the entry may not exist, while an optional pointer is not. D's forward targets and application names (§8.13 C2 v2) are in this sense annotations: D does not require their referents to exist, so they create no gap in D. The question left is how Q1 reads them, which is §8.14's failed-read question, not a D question.
- **Supersession done additively, twice in one record.**
  - The earlier decided act is "SUPERSEDED IN ITS PROPOSITION, RETAINED AS HISTORICAL", and "No prior record is silently replaced." (S0119 L16, L20, in meaning).
  - A finding true when measured is corrected "additive"ly: it was "accurate at the time" and is "superseded by events" (S0119 L55–57, in meaning).

  This is §8.14 C3 (CON-0214 (b)): a correct historical record stays true, and a later state changes what it is good for.
- The session reference is "RECORDED, NOT ATTESTED" (S0119 L51, in meaning): §8.14 C6 again, now as a field rule.

**C8. The existence decision's own grounds: non-realization, under the incumbent conjunctive test (Src; corrects §8.14 C1 v2).** *(Bears on CON-0213, restated.)*
- The PO/ARB's reasoning, as registered verbatim: "no distinct C-10-owned applicability, acknowledgement, receipt, or invalidation activity is currently realized. Existing delivery behavior is incidental and belongs to other contexts." [S0120 L15]. And: "This decision does not reject the C-10 capability hypothesis" [S0120 L15].
- **The authority's grounds and the registrar's reading are kept apart (W-3).** The PO/ARB's grounds are the line above: non-realization and incidental delivery, while noting "a governed receipt semantic" (S0120 L15, in meaning). The two-limb test is **Governance's** reconstruction, in its registration, from the canonical analysis: "The estate's test is conjunctive, and I applied half of it." [S0120 L40]. Governance's errors were treating "a decided claim-boundary semantic" as an adopted invariant, and that it "ignored the performance limb entirely" (S0120 L37–38, in meaning).
- *Drift (W-13):* the grounds count "C-10-owned applicability" activity, which the next decision, D3, places outside C-10 (S0120 L103, in meaning).
- NOT YET ESTABLISHED "is not NO" [S0120 L19]: "the hypothesis is not rejected" [S0120 L19]. The decision fixes "its current epistemic status" (S0120 L23, in meaning).
- **What this changes in §8.14 C1.** §8.14 C1 v2 said the act "is silent on performance". That is false: the authority's reasoning names non-realization of the capability's own activities. The C21 review (W-1) read only S0112, which states limb ①; S0120 gives the decision's grounds. So **the authority's decision rests on non-realization**; that it applies both limbs is the registrar's reading (W-3). CON-0213's first part stands: D1 was not counted (S0120 L15; S0112 L14). Its second part is withdrawn.
- **And then the presumption of the test was removed.** The order of acts (W-3, corrected):
  - D2 was decided, and its registration reads it under the two-limb test;
  - D3, D4.1, D4, D4.2 and D4.3 followed;
  - then the D5 work statement (AMD1) forbade presuming the test for C-10, "a refinement of the open question, not its answer" (S0116 L102, in meaning).

  *Change over time,* recorded; whether D2's grounds survive under C-10's own test is the D5 question.
- **Int (§8.12 C6, CON-0201).** NOT YET ESTABLISHED is CON-0201's "can be established" level: plausible, not shown. It is neither F nor "not applicable".

**C9. An unresolved status may never be laundered into a resolved one, in either direction (Src; the kernel's U).** `[CON-0221 · provisional]`
- A compensating control "MUST NOT convert NOT ATTESTED into PROVEN or COMPLIANT" (S0120 L206, in meaning), "or silently convert an unresolved governance condition into an ordinary successful result" (S0120 L207, in meaning).
- The register notes one prohibition on three surfaces, "an unresolved status may never be laundered into a resolved one" [S0120 L219], and then on a fourth. The one decided mapping bars both directions: a failure to establish binding "must remain unresolved rather than being converted into either "no receipt" or "valid receipt"" (S0120 L406, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13), stated at review (W-7).** The rule the corpus states is a rule against **rewriting a status**. M4 is never classified as missing or as valid. It still allows **action under an authorized exception** while the status stays unresolved (the compensating control). CE* has the same split. Inside Φ, U is never counted as F. But a consumer that must decide may map U to F at the output (fail-closed use; §9.13, defect 1). So the parallel is: the answer stays U, and a decision taken under it is a separate act, which must not rewrite the answer. *(Version 1 said "the two roundings CE* forbids", which overlooked the fail-closed use.)* The corpus counts four surfaces, "evidence, not a standard" (S0120 L219, in meaning). In the book the rule is stated once, for CE*, by construction.

**C10. A claim about the record, not about the world (Src; for §9.15 L10 and CE*'s scope).** `[CON-0222 · provisional]`
- The final selection says the receipt "claims that governed knowledge delivery occurred and acknowledgement was recorded" (S0120 L643, in meaning). It changes "acknowledgement occurred" to "acknowledgement was recorded".
- The registration's reading: "It moves the claim from a fact about the world to a fact about the record — and the platform can hold that." (S0120 L663, in meaning). And "identity binding was established" is listed among the explicit non-claims (S0120 L656, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13; §9.15 L10).** CE* answers about recorded acts: whether an act on record is authorized and current, given the record. Its claims are about the record. L1–L10 are the assumptions under which they also hold of the world. S0120 shows the same move in the estate's language: the claim the platform can carry is the one about its record, and the world claim (who acknowledged) needs an assumption that is unmet (§8.14 C6). So the kernel's answers should be worded as claims about the record, with the world premises named, which is §9.15's separation. *Two qualifications (W-14):*
  - the selected text keeps "delivery occurred", a world claim, so the move is partial;
  - "acknowledgement was recorded" is satisfied trivially by the receipt itself unless the acknowledgement is a separate record. *This is the corpus's form of the book's line between internal guarantees and world assumptions.*

**C11. A name is not a criterion; an undelivered deliverable is not closed (Src).** `[CON-0223 · provisional]`
- The adopted criteria arrived as labels: "the record holds nine LABELS, not nine criteria" [S0120 L492], and "an undefined name is not a satisfied definition" (S0120 L500, in meaning). Hence "A NAME IS NOT A CRITERION" [S0120 L616].
- And "A good model is not the same as an authorized architectural deliverable." [S0120 L558]. The commission stays open, because closing it "would record the false state commission completed when the producer never delivered" (S0120 L588, in meaning).
- *A separate distinction (W-15; CON-0225):* effects that are **permitted** are not effects that are **enabled**: "Vocabulary ≠ enabled set." (S0120 L273, in meaning). This is a deontic distinction, not one about names. `[CON-0225 · provisional]`
- **Int.** C6 (a decision is identified by its proposition) and C11 are one discipline: a record holds content, not names for content. For the kernel, a kind admitted to D is a set of required fields with a meaning in Φ, not a label. A kind that is named but not defined, like supersession, is not admitted.
