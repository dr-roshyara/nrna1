## 8.26.1 In plain language

These summaries add no new theory. They give three warnings about reading the corpus.

1. **A summary can break its own rules.** One summary defines "Established" as "backed by direct repository evidence", then marks positions, principles and an asserted practice as established.
2. **A summary can misstate its source.** One summary describes an earlier source as being about something it is not about.
3. **A document's own "status: proposed" is not the decision.** A decision act may have decided what the document still calls proposed. The act decides; the document's label is a claim about itself.

## 8.26.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A status tag must meet its own definition (Src; Int for §8.25 C7; an attack on S0179).** *(No new concept.)*
- **S0179's rule:** "Established" requires direct repository evidence as current fact or decision (S0179 L16–20, in meaning).
- **S0179's tags:** it marks as ESTABLISHED a "position, corpus-wide", that KnowledgeOS is a governed knowledge-product platform (S0179 L33, in meaning), two "principles" (S0179 L39–40, in meaning), and Hexagonal Architecture as "lived practice, asserted — no formal ADR recorded" (S0179 L49, in meaning).
- **Int.** A position, a principle and an asserted practice are not "current fact or decision" backed by direct repository evidence. By its own definition these tags overreach. S0178 and S0181 do the same: they tag Track-2 principles relayed by an adviser (S0178 L87–96, in meaning) and Hexagonal Architecture "stated as already adopted" (S0181 L27–29, in meaning) as ESTABLISHED.
- *Agreement with §8.25 C7, one line:* the tag records the summarizer's classification and adds no evidence.

**C2. A summary can misstate its source (Src; Int from §8.22 C7; evidence for §8.25 C7).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0181 summarizes S0154 as exploring LCOM4 for "the multi-language KnowledgeOS binding": "KnowledgeOS pieces (kernel, runtime, intelligence, studio) may live in different languages; the binding between them must stay cohesive and measurable" (S0181 L55–60, in meaning).
- §8.22 C7 records what S0154 is about: one cohesion-metric engine with language bindings, "language-specific understanding, language-neutral calculation", and a PHP engine compared with a Python one. It is about measuring code written in several languages, not about binding KnowledgeOS components to each other.
- *Int, the Professor's reading against the book's record of S0154 (S0154 was not reopened):* S0181's summary misstates its input. So a claim "the corpus says X", resting on a summary, must be checked against the source. A summary is not even reliable evidence of what its sources say.

**C3. An artifact's own status is not the act that decides it (Src; Int for §8.17 and the C23 rule).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0177 and S0179 record a tension: the durability ADR "is PROPOSED" ("recommends an architecture and decides nothing"), while "a separate PO/ARB DECISION ACT (2026-08-19)" decided B′ and R-CONFLICT (S0177 L55–61, L140, in meaning; S0179 L154, in meaning). Both say the two "must be read together or they appear to contradict" (S0177 L140, in meaning).
- **Int.** In the book, authority is a recorded act, and a document's text is prose about it (§8.17 C8, recording is not authorizing; §1.13). An ADR's own status line is a claim the document makes about itself, written before or apart from the act. The act decides, and the C23 rule puts its words first. So there is no contradiction to resolve: the document's status was never the decision. *What is reported, not shown:* S0177 cites the decision act "per the cross-reference digest" (S0177 L61, in meaning). The act's text is not quoted, so the book records the decision as reported.

**C4. The most authoritative record was the least durable (Src; evidence for §9.17 R4 and §9.15 K1).** *(No new concept.)*
- "I-10 (record outranks prose) makes the most authoritative artifact the least durable → authority and durability are **inverted**" (S0177 L35–37, in meaning). The authority record lived in an untracked runtime directory (S0177 L31–34, in meaning).
- This is the estate problem behind §9.17 (turn 8): K1's record must survive, and a change of carrier needs CON-0265's gate. **One line** with S0160 and S0164.

**C5. The authority log is the source; folded state is a projection; authority is strongly consistent (Src; evidence for §8.23 C1 and C2).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0180 reports the current state as follows. The authority record is "an append-only transition log", "written synchronously by the executing component, not consumed as an event stream". Derived state is "folded, never persisted". Authority decisions are "strongly consistent today" (S0180 L32–41, L93, all in meaning). "An 'approve' that may silently fail to propagate is not an approval" (S0180 L78–81, in meaning).
- This is §8.23 C2 (the record is the source, state is a projection) and §8.23 C1 (authority change needs strong consistency). It is reported, not shown, and **one line** with S0158 and S0159.

**C6. Two systems, and "kernel" in two senses (Src; routed to T0-03).** *(No new concept.)*
- "T1 KnowledgeOS vs T3 PKS context sets … must not be merged" (S0178 L161, in meaning; S0179 L43, in meaning). These are different systems with different context names.
- S0179 states as current fact that KnowledgeOS is "**not** an operating system, **not** a kernel, **not** event-driven, **not** a Digitalization Robot" (S0179 L119–122, in meaning). *Not a contradiction of the book (the Professor's reading):* the book's kernel is a theoretical core, the part that must hold whatever is built. S0179's "kernel" is a built component. *Recorded in T0-03 as a homonym, with the T1/T3 distinction.*
