## 8.98.1 In plain language

1. **The fresh reviewer finds no sign that it took part earlier, and declares its independence a PASS.** The book holds that independence on the record can be refuted but never established, so the most the evidence shows is that nothing refutes it; the reviewer itself adds that its PASS is evidence, not a ruling on policy, which agrees.
2. **The reviewer re-runs the tests and reads the code itself rather than accepting the verifier's report, and states that a review PASS is not an adoption.** Both agree with the book: re-running adds independent information, and a check never confers standing.
3. **The reviewer records its own registration and handoff, on the human's recorded words, as earlier lanes on this work item did.** The book has seen this before: an appointee recording its own appointment. The authority is the human's act; the recording is not.
4. **The reviewer finds that the session which built an amendment is recorded nowhere by its identifier, so no future verifier can be checked against it.** That follows from the book: an independence bar can only be tested against what the record holds.
5. **It finds that the documented path for appointments was never used and that closing the review with STOP hides the "ready for adoption" case; it does not reopen the lane to fix the display.** Both agree with the book: what is practised is what counts, and no process may make a human act for itself.
6. **The operating model's header now says adopted and authorized, but the records of those decisions come later in the corpus.** In reading order the outcome is reported before its record; the book takes it as reported until those records are read.
