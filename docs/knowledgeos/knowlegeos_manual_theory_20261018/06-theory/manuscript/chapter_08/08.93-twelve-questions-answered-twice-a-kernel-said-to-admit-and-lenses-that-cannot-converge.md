## 8.93.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer says the commitment belongs to KnowledgeOS and that the kernel admits claims; the replies split this into who asserts, who may admit, and who keeps the result consistent.** Within the book's record the split is right, and the kernel is not the one who admits: an authorized person's recorded act does that, and the kernel evaluates it.
2. **The first answer says identity is given by the kernel at admission, never before, so as not to create identities for claims later refused.** Within the record an item can carry its identifier and entries before anyone adopts it; what has no trace is a refused input, not an identity. Whether changing a claim's content makes a new claim is open in the book too.
3. **The first answer says the state is worked out from events and not stored, but also that the kernel keeps an "assessment result" it did not make.** Within the record the book has the first and not the second: the kernel works out both standing and the class itself, by stated rules.
4. **The replies separate four things behind "contested": a relation, an event, a derived state and a governance determination.** Within the record the book has the event and the derived value; a determination is a recorded act; the relation between two claims has no kind yet.
5. **The second reply says that when each lens is used as a vote the answers cannot converge, so lenses should produce questions and test cases, and the decision should come from invariants.** That is how the book has used them. Its test of evidence that supports a hundred claims does not force one large boundary within the record: each piece of evidence is recorded per item.
6. **The replies list cases where something is missing.** Within the record most are answered: a claim with no evidence has standing if an authorized act gives it and is shown as not attested; the evaluator always answers on a record in its domain.
