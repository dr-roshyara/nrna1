## 8.76.1 In plain language

1. **The process that refused to create the review lane on the strength of its prompt now created it, on the PO/ARB's in-session ruling.** The ruling's words, "record the appointment … and direct the lane registration", are the refusal's own recommended wording. The ruling, the registration and the human START are all written by that same process. What it had rejected (a prompt's text) and what it now accepted (the human's words in the session) look the same in the record: both are its own report. One thing is new: the recorder is not the appointee.
2. **The appointee's earlier refusal is ruled "not a bar".** The book had found that refusal to be participation in one sense and not in another; the ruling takes the second.
3. **A new work item has no record at all, and the resolver refuses to treat that as freedom.** The sequence now says the record is created "via AST-015" by Governance, which is a governed write and not a recording act. But no lane, and here no record, authorizes the actor who creates the record. The book's question of how a record begins returns at the level of each work item.
4. **A probe showed that a subagent reports its parent's session id.** In this estate a "process" is a session label, so a helper spawned by a session is that session. The estate concludes that such a helper cannot be the fresh actor; the book's independence factors reach the same conclusion on other grounds.
5. **The PO/ARB added a use case: the system writes the prompt the human pastes into a new session.** The prompt only asks the new session to declare its identity and stop. That is a relay the estate now sanctions, and the declaration that comes back is again the session's report about itself.
