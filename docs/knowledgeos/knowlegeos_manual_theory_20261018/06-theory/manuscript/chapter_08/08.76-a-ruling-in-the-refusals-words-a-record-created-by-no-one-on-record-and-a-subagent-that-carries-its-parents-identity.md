## 8.76.1 In plain language

1. **The process that refused to create the review lane on the strength of its prompt now registered the appointment, on the PO/ARB's in-session ruling, and announced that it would append the lane itself.** The ruling, as registered, is in the terms the refusal had recommended. In the record, what it rejected (a prompt's text) and what it now accepted (the human's words in the session) differ only in its own report of their source. One thing is new: the recorder is not the appointee. And the line the book drew last window, prose records without a lane but no store writes without one, is broken by this recorder while the other recorder, the same day, keeps it.
2. **The registration calls the appointee's earlier refusal "not a bar".** That is the recorder's own judgement, quoting its earlier report and dropping its own earlier doubt; the ruling as quoted only says the refusals were correct.
3. **A new work item has no record at all, and the resolver refuses to treat that as freedom.** The record is to be created by Governance, now called a governed write. Who may perform that first act is an authority question the book stops at a root act. And the same act was registered earlier as mere recording, a ground the PO/ARB relied on.
4. **A probe showed that a subagent reports its parent's session label.** The estate concludes that such a helper cannot be the fresh actor; the book's independence factors reach the same conclusion. The same reasoning bears on this book's own reviewers, which is the Principal's matter.
5. **The PO/ARB commissioned a use case in which the system writes the prompt the human pastes into a new session.** It is commissioned, not implemented or adopted. The prompt only asks the new session to declare its identity and stop; the declaration that comes back is again a self-report.
