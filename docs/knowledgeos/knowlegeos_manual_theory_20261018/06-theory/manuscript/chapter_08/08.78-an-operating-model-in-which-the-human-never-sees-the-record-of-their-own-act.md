## 8.78.1 In plain language

1. **The human never operates the engine and is never required to inspect the record.** The system writes the human's decisions into the record. The commission forbids fabricating the human's act, and it forbids requiring the human to inspect the record in order to route the work. Inspection stays possible, but nothing prompts it, so by default the one party who could attest the act is outside the record's checking path. *(Restated at review: W-01.)*
2. **One choice, "Yes — appoint and activate", produces two records the prompt keeps distinct.** Appointment and activation are listed as different; one answer yields both. Whether a second human act, starting the assigned session, also stands behind activation is read two ways in the source. *(Restated at review: W-03, W-05.)*
3. **Some human decisions are mandatory; the rest depend on the escalator's judgement.** The human decides at appointment of a fresh actor, at adoption and, in the prompt's business model, at every role transition. Whether a scope change, an exception or a "no longer routine" problem goes to the human is judged by the party that would escalate. The first classification of every request is exempt from review. *(Restated at review: W-02.)*
4. **The session whose work ends may perform its own routine handoff, once the human has authorized the transition.** The transition model has no STOP transition; the end of work is recorded, if at all, by the HANDOFF or by the mechanism's `stop` command. *(Restated at review: W-06, W-07.)*
5. **Whether a subagent's review counts as independent is left as a policy.** The same lineage says a subagent cannot be a fresh actor. The book recorded the question about its own reviewers; the estate separates the actor's freshness from the reviewer's independence, and that distinction bears on the Principal's question. *(Restated at review: W-04.)*

(L317–329);
  - "Use business language such as: · \"Start the assigned session.\"" (L331–333);
  - "The human must never need to: … - inspect workflow records to route the work;" (L879–890);
  - "Technical details may be available as optional diagnostics." (L626);

  - "The system handles: · identity discovery · eligibility · assignment · REGISTER · HANDOFF · START · activation verification" (L1258–1266);
  - "Governance records the decision using the canonical mechanism." (L748).
- **Int:**
  - **Who writes, and who could check.** The capability executes the transition mechanics (L301–302) and the system "handles" START (L1265); Governance records the human's adoption decision (L748). The human is never required to inspect the record to route the work (L890), and the record's structure is among what the human need not understand (L67). *So the human's decisions reach the record as the capability's transcriptions (§9.15 R4).* "The system must NOT fabricate a human act" (L326) is a must-never with no check inside the record (§8.77 C6; K3, §8.27 C2).
  - **Not removed, only not required, and not prompted.** The ban is on requiring inspection, scoped to routing (L890); technical detail may be offered as optional diagnostics (L626), and the system may "explain the consequence" (L323). *Inspection is optional and nothing in the design prompts it; by default the one party who could attest the human act is outside the record's checking path.* Whether the human should be prompted to check is a design question for the PO/ARB, not decided here.
  - **Who performs START: two readings** (W-03). (a) The capability executes START on the human's decision (L301–302, L1265; §8.77 C6). (b) The human act at the START boundary is the human starting the assigned session: the system may only "prepare the activation" and must not fabricate the act (L323–326), the business wording is "Start the assigned session" (L333), and the human starts a fresh session physically (L908) and before candidacy (L524). *The source does not fix which; on (b) a second human act stands behind activation.*
  - Commissioned, not implemented (S0343 L9).
- **Not a new concept.**

## 8.78.4 Challenges recorded
