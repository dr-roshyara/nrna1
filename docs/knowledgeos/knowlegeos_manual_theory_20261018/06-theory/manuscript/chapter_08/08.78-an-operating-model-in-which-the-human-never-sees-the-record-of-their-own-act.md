## 8.78.1 In plain language

1. **The human never operates the engine and never has to inspect the record.** The system writes the human's decisions into the record, including the START that stands for the human's act. The commission forbids fabricating that act, and it also forbids requiring the human to look at the record. So the one party whose act is recorded is the one who never checks the record of it.
2. **One choice, "Yes — appoint and activate", does two things the prompt itself says are different.** Appointment and activation are listed as distinct, and a single answer produces both.
3. **The Governance Engineer decides whether a decision needs the human.** The party that should escalate decides when escalation is needed; the first classification of every request is exempt from review.
4. **Any authorized session may perform the routine mechanics, including handing off its own work.** What counts as "routine" is decided by the session doing it.
5. **Whether a subagent's review counts as independent is left as a policy, deliberately not decided.** The book recorded the same open question about its own reviewers. The prompt's identity rules agree with the book.

## 8.78.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. The human never sees the record of their own act (Src; Int).**
- **S0344:**
  - "The human must never be required to understand or manually operate: … - START; … - workflow record structure;" (L56–67);
  - "Human authority must never be manufactured. · The system may: · - prepare the transition; · - validate preconditions; · - explain the consequence; · - prepare the activation. · The system must NOT fabricate a human act. · However, the human must not be forced to understand the technical · transition." (L317–329);
  - "The human must never need to: … - inspect workflow records to route the work;" (L879–890);
  - "The system handles: · identity discovery · eligibility · assignment · REGISTER · HANDOFF · START · activation verification" (L1258–1266);
  - "Governance records the decision using the canonical mechanism." (L748).
- **Int:**
  - **Who writes and who could check.** The system writes every transition, START included (L1258–1266), and records the human's adoption decision (L748). The human is never required to inspect the record (L890), and the record's structure is among what the human need not understand (L67). *So the human's acts reach the record only as the system's transcriptions (§9.15 R4), and the one party who could say whether a recorded human act happened is the one the design keeps from the record.* "Never fabricate a human act" (L326) is a must-never with no check inside the record (§8.77 C6; K3, §8.27 C2), and the design removes the outside check the human could supply.
  - **Not a contradiction.** The prompt does not forbid the human from inspecting the record; it forbids requiring it (L879, "must never need to"). *The check is optional, and nothing in the design prompts it.* Commissioned, not implemented (S0343 L9).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. One choice that appoints and activates, against the prompt's own distinctions (Src; Ded; Int).**
- **S0344:**
  - "role · ≠ process identity · identity · ≠ eligibility · eligibility · ≠ appointment · appointment · ≠ activation · activation · ≠ authorization" (L481–494);
  - "\"A fresh eligible actor is available.\" · 1. Yes — appoint and activate · 2. Write / revise prompt · 3. Stop · Human choice 1 is the authority act. · Do not create authority before the human act." (L551–559);
  - "Human START boundary" (L297, L940).
- **Ded:** The prompt lists appointment ≠ activation (L490–491). Choice 1 is "appoint and activate" (L553), and "Human choice 1 is the authority act" (L557). *So one human act yields both an appointment and an activation:* the two are distinct as records, not as acts.
- **Int:** *This is §8.75 C1's "one Yes, two acts", now written into the operating model's own wording.* Whether the human START (the activation's human act, L297) is the same act as the appointment is not said; on the prompt's terms it is (L557). The distinction list survives as a distinction between records, not between human decisions.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. The escalator decides when to escalate; the intake exempt from review (Src; Int).**
- **S0344:**
  - "The Governance Engineer determines: … - whether a governance decision is required; - whether a scope change needs explicit approval; - whether an exception or conflict must return to the human." (L127–134);
  - "Governance MUST NOT silently change substantive roles. · When a role transition requires human authority, present:" (L265–267);
  - "Communication Engineer must escalate when the problem is no longer · routine." (L708–709);
  - "Every substantive actor's work must be reviewed. … The initial Governance intake / classification is the exception." (L632–648).
- **Int:**
  - **Self-gated escalation.** Whether a human decision is required is determined by the Governance Engineer (L132–134); whether a problem is "no longer routine" by the Communication Engineer (L708). *So the gate on the human's authority is set by the party it gates.* A Governance Engineer that never finds a decision required satisfies every stated must-never (it never silently changes a role it judged to need no authority): *by analogy with §9.19 T7, a prohibition met by inaction.* A1 locates authority in a recorded act of a holder; the prompt locates the trigger for that act in an AI responsibility.
  - **The intake is unreviewed** (L648). The first classification, which decides whether Architecture is needed and which path the work takes (L127–131, L228–259), is the one step exempt from review. *The decision with the widest consequence is the one no reviewer sees.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Routine mechanics by any authorized session (Src; Int).**
- **S0344:**
  - "The Communication Engineer is NOT a permanent person. · It is a TRANSFERABLE OPERATIONAL RESPONSIBILITY. · Any currently authorized actor/session may perform the Communication · Engineer responsibility for routine coordination." (L142–147);
  - "- routine actor handoff; … - routine execution of already-authorized workflow mechanics." (L157–159);
  - "Every actor/session must have access to the Communication Engineer · capability for routine work." (L1010–1011); "Local Communication Engineer may: … - perform routine handoff;" (L1015–1020).
- **Int:**
  - **The actor hands off its own work.** Any authorized session may perform routine handoff (L157, L1020). *So the session whose work ends may append the HANDOFF that ends its lane and starts its successor's:* the self-recording of §8.73 C1 and §8.76 C1, made routine. Where the actor's work ends, §8.77 C3 asked for a recorded STOP; the prompt's transition model has REGISTER → HANDOFF → Human START (L291–299, L934–942) and no STOP.
  - **"Routine" decided by the doer.** What counts as routine, and so may be executed without escalation, is judged by the session itself (L708; C3).
- **Not a new concept.**
