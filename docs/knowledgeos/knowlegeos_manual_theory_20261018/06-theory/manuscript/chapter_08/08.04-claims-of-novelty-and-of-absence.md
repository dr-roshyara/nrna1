# 8.4 Claims of novelty and claims of absence

*Window C9 (2026-10-19). Sources: S0046 (2026-08-03), in index order; S0091 (2026-08-18), added under §12a (read for §9.5; its correction history bears here). **Status: reviewed in C9 (2026-10-19); settled in `review/C9-S0046-S0050/SETTLEMENT.md`.***

*Lineage:* S0046 belongs to the 08-02 … 08-04 programme. S0091 is a later work item. Their subjects differ; both concern how the corpus checks its own claims.

*Existing homes (book search, step 3):* Ch. 8 §8.2 (rediscovery; search miss ≠ absence); Ch. 6 §6.4 C2 (an observation by absence is only as strong as its search). This section adds two disciplines the corpus states for itself.

## 8.4.1 In plain language

Two kinds of claim go wrong in the same way: "this is new" and "this does not exist". Both rest on a search, and both fail when the search was too narrow. The corpus states a discipline for each.

## 8.4.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Novelty and demonstration are two axes, not three classes (Src).** `[CON-0119 · invalid as stated]` (valid as two axes)
- S0046's "honesty table" sorts its claims into [S0046 L55–57]:
  - **rediscovered**, established practice, no novelty claimed (for example policy and enforcement separation, staged promotion, rebuildable projections);
  - **extended**, with "no external instance found — internal until independently validated" (for example the authority model);
  - **unproven regardless**: `generates → PKS`, "*novel AND undemonstrated; the two must never be confused*".
- **Ded:** "unproven regardless" is defined as "novel AND undemonstrated", so the scheme mixes two variables. The axes are:
  - **novelty:** rediscovered, or no external instance found. "No external instance found" is a search result, not novelty (§8.2, §8.4 C3);
  - **demonstration:** demonstrated in use, or not.

  "Extended" items are as undemonstrated in use as `generates → PKS`. And a rediscovered pattern validated elsewhere does not validate this realization of it (Ch. 1 §1.16.8).

**C2. "A governance platform that uses ontologies": three sides, not three independent supports (Src; Int).**
- The verdict "THE SECOND", governance not ontology engineering, rests on internal, external-convergent and external-gap evidence [S0046 L41–47].
- **Int:** the internal side is the programme's own history; the external-gap side is one survey [S0046 L22]. One survey's named gap is evidence that the layer is under-modelled, not that KnowledgeOS fills it.
- **The convergent side is selected, too:** "every strongly-validated edge is a GOVERNANCE edge" [S0046 L46], but the edges were chosen and validated by a governance-oriented programme, through its own matrices. None of the three sides is independent.
- The survey's finding is kept: "*no systematic discussion of validation workflows, curation processes, or **human authority roles***" [S0046 L22].

**C3. An absence claim needs an untruncated search and a statement of what was searched (Src).** `[CON-0120 · validated]`
- S0091 withdrew three absence claims inside one file. The first "rested on a search truncated by `head -5`" [S0091 L401]; the second generalised a verified narrow claim "without searching for a counter-instance" [S0091 L402].
- Its proposed discipline: "**an absence claim requires an exhaustive, untruncated search AND an explicit statement of what was searched.**" [S0091 L404]; "a search that can be truncated is not a survey." [S0091 L404]
- And its own disclosure after the third correction states search scope, method and completeness, including what was not covered ("NOT complete for paraphrases") [S0091 L677–682].
- **Int:** this is the evidence rule behind §8.2 C2 and §6.4 C2, stated as a procedure. A miss counts only for the scope that was searched, and the scope has to be written down. "Exhaustive" is to be read as "scope, method and completeness stated", since S0091 itself records that paraphrases were not covered.
- **The book broke this rule in the same window:** §9.5 was first written as new because a lexical book search found no home (review C9, finding 4). A miss by `book_search.py` is subject to C3 like any other.
- The same pattern of withdrawals (C-14: absent, then live, then contested) involves the homonym "enforced" (§6.4 C3).

**C4. Four classes of evidence with four weights (Src).**
- "absence from a governed registry ≠ evidence of non-existence" [S0091 L433]. The material divides into governed architecture, untracked material, persona language, and executable lane roles [S0091 L426–431].
- The surviving conclusion rests "solely on the absence of an ADOPTING GOVERNED ACT", not on how rare a term is [S0091 L725]. Its scope: the four artifact classes that could adopt (ADRs, decision records, the capability map, the registry); the 104 hits were not classified one by one [S0091 L727].
- **Int:** under A1 (Ch. 1), only an act confers standing. So "is X architecture?" is answered by looking for the adopting act, not by counting mentions.

**C5. A look-alike edit (Src; an observation about method).**
- A correction "landed on the `C-5` row because it was applied with a scripted first-match string replace", because two rows shared a look-alike cell; the repository's rule against automated replacement was scoped to source code, and "**The rule's SCOPE excluded documents; its RATIONALE did not.**" [S0091 L663–665]
- *Observation:* this book's own workspace uses scripted, exact-match replacements with a uniqueness assertion; that guard is what the source's case lacked.
