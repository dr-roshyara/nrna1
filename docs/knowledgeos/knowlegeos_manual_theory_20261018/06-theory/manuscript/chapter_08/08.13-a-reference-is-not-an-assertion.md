## 8.13.1 In plain language

A record can carry a pointer to a judgement without vouching for the judgement. A decided receipt was required to point to an act that the estate never performs, so no receipt could yet meet the decision. A decision is entitled to state what it rests on, and when the basis is unverified it must say so, not pass the question on silently. Supplying a selection that the decider's act does not contain is making the decision. A recorder may transcribe the decider's selection, but must not supply one.

A second act on a slot that is already decided does not replace the first just because it came later: it may be an amendment, a re-issue that crossed with it, or a duplicate, and until someone with the authority disposes of it the first stands. A strict gate can leave no one eligible. That is the same cost as refusing a grant's use while its owner is away.

## 8.13.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A reference is not an assertion; "reference" has three senses (Src; restated at the consolidation S0101–S0130, A-03, I-03).** `[CON-0238 · provisional]` `[CON-0202 · invalid as stated]`
- The decided receipt establishes an "applicability *reference*" and does not establish "the **correctness of applicability**" (S0109 L38–39, in meaning). The record's reason: "A pointer to a judgement is not an assertion that the judgement was sound." [S0109 L41].
- The invariant decided with it, "AUTHORITY IS CLAIM-SCOPED" [S0109 L107], is CON-0154's rule (§8.10 C4), restated by this source; it is not part of this concept (v2; W-10).
- A second act offers two readings (S0110 L30–31, in meaning): keep the reference as a recorded field that the receipt asserts nothing about, or drop it. They "differ in whether the receipt still CARRIES a pointer it does not vouch for" [S0110 L33].
- **Int (§1.13; §9.13), restated (v2; W-8).** *(The universal of this line was refuted at the consolidation S0101–S0130: A-03, I-03.)* Three senses: **(i)** a **citation** in D asserts that its target exists and is earlier, since D refuses a dangling or later citation; **(ii)** a **target** or an **application name** in D asserts neither (§9.13: targets need not be present); **(iii)** a **reference in the estate** shows only that it is present, a claim (§1.13.2; §1.16: presence is not validity). No reference of any sense asserts that its referent is **correct**. §1.13's grant has this shape: it holds a pointer to an act and no authority of its own. CE*'s citations have it too. D refuses a citation whose target is not an earlier entry, so a citation asserts existence and order. It asserts nothing about the target's authorization, which CE* evaluates. *(Version 1 said a citation "asserts nothing"; it asserts existence, which is C2's point.)* I15's recorder, required by D and read by no clause of Φ, is a field the record carries without vouching for it (§9.13).
- *Limit:* one decision of one work item, recorded by one process.

**C2. The referent gap: a requirement that points to an act never performed (Src; the dual of §8.12 C7).** `[CON-0203 · provisional]`
- After the decision, the applicability reference is one of eight required properties, while the act it must point to "does not occur" and has no owner (S0109 L130, in meaning). The consequence: "a receipt conforming to this decision is NOT PRESENTLY POPULATABLE" [S0109 L132].
- Two dispositions, both the PO/ARB's (S0109 L54, in meaning): the reference is optional or null until the act has an owner, or the decision creates a standing obligation to establish the act and its owner.
- The second act removes the clause from the proposition. Then "the gap closes at the level of the claim" [S0110 L24].
- **Int (§8.12 C7).** §8.12 C7 gave a sufficient condition for existence: an adopted invariant presupposes the capability, and the capability is performed. Here an adopted requirement presupposes an act that is not performed. So the requirement creates a gap instead of evidencing a capability, and S0109 ties it to the existence question (S0109 L133, in meaning).
- **What the gap is (v2; W-7).** The act the receipt must point to is defined and can occur; nobody performs it, and nobody owns it. So it is a **performer or owner gap**, not a kind that cannot occur.
- **For the kernel (Int, v2; W-7).** D already admits references that may have no referent:
  - annulment targets and ended grants may point forward to entries that never arrive (§9.13);
  - the `owner` and `new_owner` fields name applications, which are never entries of D (§9.13 v11).

  So D has referent gaps by design. How Q1 answers when a required reference has none is the open question in §8.13.3. *(Version 1's "D's present kinds meet this" was false.)*
- **A required property with open content.** The decision makes invalidation semantics required while their content is left to a later decision: "the receipt MUST HAVE invalidation semantics" [S0109 L125]. A requirement can fix that a property exists and leave what it is open (v2; coverage).

**C3. A decision's basis carries its assurance status (Src; an extension of §9.13's marks).** `[CON-0204 · provisional]`
- The option set of C-10 Decision 1 is "a product of the unverified artifact itself" [S0108 L38]. Its contrast: C-5 Decision 1 "was defensible on grounds independent of those artifacts" [S0108 L38].
- "Not an argument against deciding" [S0108 L39]. The basis is stated "so a DECIDED act does not silently inherit an unresolved provenance question" [S0108 L39].
- Outside material cited in support: "citing it does not convert it" [S0109 L63].
- **The status travels without retraction (v2; coverage).** Adopted findings stand, "but they now rest on a provenance-conflicted verification" [S0107 L53]. Every C-5 conclusion in the correction inherits the provenance answer (S0106 L59, in meaning). A party that records an adverse disposition about itself discloses this; it does not cure it (S0107 L16, in meaning).
- **The sufficiency side.** A classification whose stated reason holds one incorrect element "STANDS — it does not depend on the incorrect element" [S0107 L49]. The conclusion follows from the other ground alone (S0107 L50, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13; decision 2 after turn 6).** CE*'s answer is **marked, not refused**, when its support holds transcribed, unsigned or removed entries. S0108 asks the same of a decision: state what the basis is, and do not refuse to decide. S0107 adds what CE*'s marks lack. CE*'s support is a conservative closure, so it may mark an answer for an entry the value does not need (§9.13; T1-16, open). S0107's rule follows only the grounds the conclusion needs. That is the tighter rule, and it is not yet defined for CE*.

**C4. The origin of a selection, not the writing of it, makes the decision (Src; restated at review, W-6).** `[CON-0207 · provisional]` `[CON-0205 · invalid as stated]` *("selecting is deciding", as a case for P-perf.)*
- "Selecting is the decision itself" [S0108 L7]. And: "a process supplying one would be making the decision, not recording it" [S0108 L8]. The act the recorder refused contained no selection; to supply one would have been to decide.
- In the next record the same process **writes** the PO/ARB's selection, and does not thereby decide: "The selection is the PO/ARB's own act, and the record must not show a process having made it" [S0109 L28]. So writing a selection is not deciding. Supplying one that the decider's act does not contain is.
- The refusal is of a kind of its own: "This is a decision-authority boundary, not a refusal of an assignment." [S0108 L8]. No independence gate was reached (v2; coverage).
- A later act asks "PO/ARB must decide whether…" and carries no selection (S0110 L5, in meaning); the recorder again selects nothing.
- **Int (§9.15).** S0109's case is a **transcription**: the decider's act, written by another (signed-as-other, §9.15 R4). It is not a case for P-perf, which asks which acts are performed by the writing of their entry. *(Version 1 offered it as one; it is not.)*

**C5. A later act is not automatically a supersession (Src; a corollary of I-7, for F24).** `[CON-0206 · provisional]`
- Two acts of the same authority address one decided slot with different propositions (S0110 L6, L12–15, in meaning). "A later act is not automatically a supersession." [S0110 L37]. The second act may be an amendment, a re-issue that crossed with the first, or a duplicate. Until Governance disposes of it, the decided text stands (S0110 L37, in meaning).
- The work item records the pattern: "acts and records have crossed four times" [S0110 L38]. Two Governance lanes worked on it, and "neither lane saw the other's assignment" [S0107 L17].
- **Int (I-7; §9.13).** I-7 makes supersession a link. Its **contrapositive**, not its converse (corrected at the consolidation S0101–S0130, I-11, B-18), follows: without the link, a later act does not supersede. S0110's own content is what remains: until the later act is disposed of, the decided text stands, and the later act may be an amendment, a crossed re-issue or a duplicate. Without a link, and the two stand as a conflict for disposition (F24's "preserve" side). CE* agrees **for adoptions only** (v2; W-9). D has no supersession kind, and no clause of Φ lets the later of two adoptions win: they are joined by "or", and their contents are not read. But CE* does not represent the conflict F24 needs. And a revocation names an **item**, not an act, so a later revocation defeats every earlier adoption of the item with no link to any of them.
- **Supersession done with its link, in the same window (v2; coverage).** S0109 appends the formal decision text after its draft, marks the draft "superseded as to wording", and records the diff, "recorded so the diff is not silent" [S0109 L114]. That is I-7's own form, an instance beside S0110's conflict.
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** One writer per grant does not exclude this case. Here one authority produced two acts on one slot, and ordering them did not decide between them.

**C6. A strict gate can leave no one eligible (Src; a parallel to F22's cost).** *(No concept: an observation.)*
- Applied to every known process, the fresh-verification gate admits none: "no known process satisfies the gate" [S0107 L97]. And criterion 7 "is currently NOT MECHANICALLY DETECTABLE" [S0107 L98].
- One case falls outside the criteria. Whether "the party that registered the assignment" may verify its execution is "an AUTHORITY-SEPARATION question that criteria 1–7 do not reach" [S0107 L95]. S0106 refuses for the same reason: "C-5 operating correctly forbids its own analysis being written by the party that governs the record it rests on" [S0106 L43].
- **A refusal is evidence, not clearance (v2; coverage).** S0106 records its own refusal as evidence of the capability, with no ownership inferred: "This refusal is itself C-5 evidence" [S0106 L45]. And "A refusal is not clearance" [S0107 L68]: the assignment stays open to a qualified process (S0107 L68, in meaning). Where something cannot be established, NOT ATTESTED, never PASS (S0107 L85, in meaning), as already in §8.12 C1.
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** The shape is (C + O)'s cost: prevention at a gate trades away availability. A grant cannot be used while its owner is unavailable; an assignment cannot be verified while no eligible process exists. Both sources keep the gate and record the cost ("no relaxation proposed", S0107 L97, in meaning), as S0183 does at L196.

**C7. An attribution amended beside an append-only record (Src; an extension of §8.12 C8).** *(No concept: an observation, with an open question.)*
- Of the engine's transition types, "none of them amends a prior transition's attribution" [S0107 L18], and transitions are append-only (S0107 L18, in meaning). So "Seq 21 is not rewritten, and its text is not altered." [S0107 L24]. The amendment stands beside it, as a document.
- The cause: the designation "was recorded before the assignment executed and was not updated when a different process performed it" [S0107 L31].
- **Int (§9.13).** D has the same gap. I15's recorder is a recorded claim (§9.13), and no kind of D corrects it: a removal drops content, and an annulment voids an act. An attribution recorded before the act, as here, cannot be corrected within D. Whether D needs a correction kind for attribution is open (§8.13.3).

## 8.13.3 Open questions

- Does the kernel need the tighter rule for marks, following only the grounds an answer needs (C3; §9.13, T1-16)?
- Should D admit a kind that corrects a recorded attribution, citing the entry it corrects (C7)?
- Which acts are performed by recording (P-perf)? C4 is not a case of it (v2).
- When a required reference has no referent yet, is the kernel's answer "not applicable", "not established", or refused (C2; §8.12 C6)?

## 8.13.4 What does not hold

*Version 1, refuted at review C20:*
- **CON-0205, "selecting is deciding", as a case for P-perf** (C4). The window holds the counterexample: the recorder wrote the PO/ARB's selection without deciding it (S0109 L28). Restated as CON-0207 (W-6).
- **"D's present kinds meet this"** (C2's kernel Ded). D admits forward targets and application names with no referent, and the source's gap is a performer gap (W-7).
- **"The citation asserts nothing about it"** (C1). A citation asserts existence and order (W-8).
- **"CE* agrees"** (C5), unscoped. True for adoptions; a revocation is item-wide (W-9).
