## 8.13.1 In plain language

A record can carry a pointer to a judgement without vouching for the judgement. A decided receipt was required to point to an act that the estate never performs, so no receipt could yet meet the decision. A decision is entitled to state what it rests on, and when the basis is unverified it must say so, not pass the question on silently. Writing an option into a decision slot is making the decision, so a recorder that is not the decider must not write it.

A second act on a slot that is already decided does not replace the first just because it came later: it may be an amendment, a re-issue that crossed with it, or a duplicate, and until someone with the authority disposes of it the first stands. A strict gate can leave no one eligible. That is the same cost as refusing a grant's use while its owner is away.

## 8.13.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A reference is not an assertion (Src; an extension of §1.13 and §8.10 C4).** `[CON-0202 · provisional]`
- The decided receipt establishes an "applicability *reference*" and does not establish "the **correctness of applicability**" (S0109 L38–39, in meaning). The record's reason: "A pointer to a judgement is not an assertion that the judgement was sound." [S0109 L41].
- The invariant decided with it: "AUTHORITY IS CLAIM-SCOPED" [S0109 L107]. The receipt "is authoritative only for the claim that delivery and acknowledgment occurred" [S0109 L108].
- A second act offers two readings (S0110 L30–31, in meaning): keep the reference as a recorded field that the receipt asserts nothing about, or drop it. They "differ in whether the receipt still CARRIES a pointer it does not vouch for" [S0110 L33].
- **Int (§1.13; §9.13).** This is the shape of §1.13's grant, which holds a pointer to an act and no authority of its own. It is also the shape of CE*'s citations. A citation names a claimed basis, and CE* evaluates that basis; the citation asserts nothing about it. I15's recorder, required by D and read by no clause of Φ, is a field the record carries without vouching for it (§9.13).
- *Limit:* one decision of one work item, recorded by one process.

**C2. The referent gap: a requirement that points to an act never performed (Src; the dual of §8.12 C7).** `[CON-0203 · provisional]`
- After the decision, the applicability reference is one of eight required properties, while the act it must point to "does not occur" and has no owner (S0109 L130, in meaning). The consequence: "a receipt conforming to this decision is NOT PRESENTLY POPULATABLE" [S0109 L132].
- Two dispositions, both the PO/ARB's (S0109 L54, in meaning): the reference is optional or null until the act has an owner, or the decision creates a standing obligation to establish the act and its owner.
- The second act removes the clause from the proposition. Then "the gap closes at the level of the claim" [S0110 L24].
- **Int (§8.12 C7).** §8.12 C7 gave a sufficient condition for existence: an adopted invariant presupposes the capability, and the capability is performed. Here an adopted requirement presupposes an act that is not performed. So the requirement creates a gap instead of evidencing a capability, and S0109 ties it to the existence question (S0109 L133, in meaning).
- **For the kernel (Ded, trivial).** If a kind of D required a field that must cite an entry of a kind never recorded, no entry of that kind could be in D. So every required reference field of D must name a kind that can occur. D's present kinds meet this: every required reference names a grant, a root or an entry.

**C3. A decision's basis carries its assurance status (Src; an extension of §9.13's marks).** `[CON-0204 · provisional]`
- The option set of C-10 Decision 1 is "a product of the unverified artifact itself" [S0108 L38]. Its contrast: C-5 Decision 1 "was defensible on grounds independent of those artifacts" [S0108 L38].
- "Not an argument against deciding" [S0108 L39]. The basis is stated "so a DECIDED act does not silently inherit an unresolved provenance question" [S0108 L39].
- Outside material cited in support: "citing it does not convert it" [S0109 L63].
- **The sufficiency side.** A classification whose stated reason holds one incorrect element "STANDS — it does not depend on the incorrect element" [S0107 L49]. The conclusion follows from the other ground alone (S0107 L50, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13; decision 2 after turn 6).** CE*'s answer is **marked, not refused**, when its support holds transcribed, unsigned or removed entries. S0108 asks the same of a decision: state what the basis is, and do not refuse to decide. S0107 adds what CE*'s marks lack. CE*'s support is a conservative closure, so it may mark an answer for an entry the value does not need (§9.13; T1-16, open). S0107's rule follows only the grounds the conclusion needs. That is the tighter rule, and it is not yet defined for CE*.

**C4. Selecting is deciding: recording can perform the act (Src; a case for §9.15 P-perf).** `[CON-0205 · provisional]`
- "Selecting is the decision itself" [S0108 L7]. And: "a process supplying one would be making the decision, not recording it" [S0108 L8].
- When an act attributed the selection to the recording process, the record corrected it: "The selection is the PO/ARB's own act, and the record must not show a process having made it" [S0109 L28].
- A later act asks "PO/ARB must decide whether…" and carries no selection (S0110 L5, in meaning); the recorder again selects nothing.
- **Int (§9.15 P-perf).** P-perf asks which acts are performed by recording. Here the corpus treats writing a selection into a decision slot as performing the decision, so the writer's eligibility is the decider's. This is one case. It shows that P-perf holds for some decisions; it does not say which, and P-perf stays open.

**C5. A later act is not automatically a supersession (Src; the converse of I-7, for F24).** `[CON-0206 · provisional]`
- Two acts of the same authority address one decided slot with different propositions (S0110 L6, L12–15, in meaning). "A later act is not automatically a supersession." [S0110 L37]. The second act may be an amendment, a re-issue that crossed with the first, or a duplicate. Until Governance disposes of it, the decided text stands (S0110 L37, in meaning).
- The work item records the pattern: "acts and records have crossed four times" [S0110 L38]. Two Governance lanes worked on it, and "neither lane saw the other's assignment" [S0107 L17].
- **Int (I-7; §9.13).** I-7 makes supersession a link. S0110 adds the converse: without the link, a later act does not supersede, and the two stand as a conflict for disposition (F24's "preserve" side). CE* agrees: D has no supersession kind, and no clause of Φ lets the later of two adoptions win. Standing changes only through an act of a kind that names its target (revocation, annulment, ending).
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** One writer per grant does not exclude this case. Here one authority produced two acts on one slot, and ordering them did not decide between them.

**C6. A strict gate can leave no one eligible (Src; a parallel to F22's cost).** *(No concept: an observation.)*
- Applied to every known process, the fresh-verification gate admits none: "no known process satisfies the gate" [S0107 L97]. And criterion 7 "is currently NOT MECHANICALLY DETECTABLE" [S0107 L98].
- One case falls outside the criteria. Whether "the party that registered the assignment" may verify its execution is "an AUTHORITY-SEPARATION question that criteria 1–7 do not reach" [S0107 L95]. S0106 refuses for the same reason: "C-5 operating correctly forbids its own analysis being written by the party that governs the record it rests on" [S0106 L43].
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** The shape is (C + O)'s cost: prevention at a gate trades away availability. A grant cannot be used while its owner is unavailable; an assignment cannot be verified while no eligible process exists. Both sources keep the gate and record the cost ("no relaxation proposed", S0107 L97, in meaning), as S0183 does at L196.

**C7. An attribution amended beside an append-only record (Src; an extension of §8.12 C8).** *(No concept: an observation, with an open question.)*
- Of the engine's transition types, "none of them amends a prior transition's attribution" [S0107 L18], and transitions are append-only (S0107 L18, in meaning). So "Seq 21 is not rewritten, and its text is not altered." [S0107 L24]. The amendment stands beside it, as a document.
- The cause: the designation "was recorded before the assignment executed and was not updated when a different process performed it" [S0107 L31].
- **Int (§9.13).** D has the same gap. I15's recorder is a recorded claim (§9.13), and no kind of D corrects it: a removal drops content, and an annulment voids an act. An attribution recorded before the act, as here, cannot be corrected within D. Whether D needs a correction kind for attribution is open (§8.13.3).

## 8.13.3 Open questions

- Does the kernel need the tighter rule for marks, following only the grounds an answer needs (C3; §9.13, T1-16)?
- Should D admit a kind that corrects a recorded attribution, citing the entry it corrects (C7)?
- Which acts are performed by recording (P-perf), beyond decisions written by their decider (C4)?
- When a required reference has no referent yet, is the kernel's answer "not applicable", "not established", or refused (C2; §8.12 C6)?
