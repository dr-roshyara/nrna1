## 8.64.1 In plain language

1. **The extraction lists seven "knowledge dimensions" as the "knowledge kernel" and ends "The kernel is complete."** By §9.19 no kernel of KnowledgeOS can be stated, let alone a complete one, until the requirements and the parts are fixed. The extraction also says "the kernel enforces truth conditions". The book's kernel answers standing, not the truth of a claim's content. It marks its own proposals "Established" without an act.
2. **The performance part is labelled a "theoretical projection" and concludes the compiler is "practically implementable".** Its "overall" accuracy is the parser's row copied, not a combination of the stages. If the stages' errors compound, the typical figure would be about 43%, not 82%. Its throughput summary does not match its own per-page time.
3. **The benchmark answer reports two runs on a CPU.** The second run reports 74.2% accuracy on 120 test sentences, "not good enough". The figures are internally consistent, but the code, cases and output are not in the file, so they are reported, not shown. The answer reads the failure as validating the compiler analogy. The failure refutes the flat rule set. The proposed hierarchy is untested.
4. **The answer calls the deterministic validator "the epistemic authority" and lets it mark a candidate "accepted".** In the book, a check is not an authority: standing needs a recorded act. "The LLM does not get to directly write KnowledgeOS knowledge" agrees with the book, but the answer replaces the LLM with a validator, not with an act.
5. **The type check is now said to "enforce kernel invariants"**, with "Decision cannot exist without evidence". That moves §8.63 C2 toward its first reading: a kernel refusal, which is the guard the Principal declined. The test cases that keep "after the audit", "despite the audit" and "yesterday" apart agree with the book's "context is part of identity".
