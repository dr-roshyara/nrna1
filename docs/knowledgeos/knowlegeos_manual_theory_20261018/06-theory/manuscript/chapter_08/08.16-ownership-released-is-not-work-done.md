## 8.16.1 In plain language

When ownership passes from someone who refused the work, the new state can make it look as if the work was done. The record keeps "released" apart from "completed"; a reader of a summary may not. A field can have different owners for its meaning, its data, its rule and its authority. Storing a field does not make you its owner, and needing it does not either. A review that checked only completeness can be cited for completeness, not for correctness. And a decision's basis should be on the record, not only in someone's message.

## 8.16.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Releasing ownership is not completing the work (Src; for §9.16 R4a's transfer).** `[CON-0226 · provisional]`
- After the handoff from the lane that had refused, the fold shows that lane as handed off. The record keeps the two apart: "HANDED_OFF denotes OWNERSHIP RELEASED, not VERIFICATION PERFORMED." (S0121 L115, in meaning). The engine has a separate state for completion, and the lane "has never been COMPLETEd" (S0121 L113, in meaning).
- The risk lies in reading, not in the record: "the fold alone would mislead a later reader", because a lane that refused "now reads, in the state column, indistinguishably from lanes that finished their work" (S0121 L116, in meaning).
- The remedy is a clarification, not a transition: "rewriting or re-STARTing S5 would corrupt the history" (S0121 L118, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R4a; §9.13).** In the book's sketch a transfer moves a grant's owner and carries the last use. It records nothing about whether work was done. So a transfer must never be read as a use, or as the old owner's work being finished. CE* keeps them apart by kind: a transfer is not a use (`single_use_grants` excludes it), and no clause of Φ reads a transfer as an ending. What the book lacks is S0121's warning for the **reader**: a summary that shows only "current owner" can hide that the previous owner refused. *Adversarial check (before review):* is there a case where the record itself conflates the two? Not in D. The conflation is in a projection that drops the reason, which is a marks question: a mark "ownership moved; the earlier holder's work not completed" would carry it. Not proposed for the kernel; recorded.

**C2. An unreachability claim withdrawn as too strong (Src; corrects §8.15 C2).** *(No concept: a correction.)*
- Governance had said that no new lane could be made reachable while the refusing lane held ownership. That claim is "WITHDRAWN — this was too strong." [S0121 L127]. The accurate statement was "not reachable without releasing S5's ownership" (S0121 L127, in meaning), and the release then happened at seq 29 (S0121 L100, in meaning). Its cost is C1's misleading state, not unreachability.
- **What this changes in §8.15 C2.** §8.15 C2 cited S0116's "permanently unreachable lane" as evidence that a handoff only from the owner can leave work unreachable. S0121 withdraws that claim. The surviving evidence is weaker: an owner-only handoff forces the release of an owner that cannot honestly hand off, and the release leaves a state that misleads. That still bears on the transfer rule's reason, but not as a deadlock. *(Additive correction pointer added to §8.15 C2.)*

**C3. Ownership of a field has four parts; storing is not owning, and owning is not requiring (Src; for the book's owners and holders).** `[CON-0227 · provisional]`
- Each field gets four owners: semantic, data, invariant and authority. They "diverge" on the knowledge reference, where the four answers are BC-1, C-10, BC-1 and knowledge governance. They "converge" on the receipt's own identifier, where all four are C-10 (S0122 L142, in meaning).
- Without the split, "C-10 stores it" reads as "C-10 owns it" (S0122 L144, in meaning). "NONE is a legal value, and an informative one" (S0122 L146, in meaning): the receipt carries the reference and nobody vouches for it.
- Owning is not requiring. The knowledge version is required although its meaning is BC-1's. The objection "conflates ownership with requiredness" (S0122 L152, in meaning).
- **Adversarial check (before review).**
  - (a) *Orthogonality.* The diverge/converge test shows that the four are not one function: they differ on one field and agree on another. It does not show that they are independent. That needs, for example, every combination to be possible. The source's "independent, not a relabelling" (S0122 L142, in meaning) is stronger than its test.
  - (b) *A required reference into another context's unbuilt mechanism.* The version is required, while BC-1's publication mechanism "is not built" (recorded in earlier sources: S0114 L59, in meaning). That is the shape §8.15 C7 (CON-0220) traced the referent gap to: a required reference whose referent may not exist. Is a conforming receipt populatable before BC-1 publishes versions? **Pending:** S0124 is the governance review of this proposal, so a later source in the window may answer it.
- **Int (Ch. 9).** The book has several roles on one act or grant: actor, recorder, holder, issuer and (since F22) owner application. None of them is a four-part ownership in this sense. The owner application of R4a holds the **data** of uses, and the kernel holds the **invariant** (single use, R4c). The authority to move it is the issuer's. So R4a is already a split ownership in S0122's sense, unnamed. *Not proposed as a kernel change (the Principal's rule for windows).*

**C4. A claim's two parts can differ in kind: evidence toward a world fact, evidence of a record fact (Src; settles §8.15 C10's qualification).** *(Extends CON-0222.)*
- The decided claim is asymmetric: "delivery occurred" is a world fact, and "acknowledgement was recorded" is a record fact (S0122 L38, in meaning). The PO/ARB keeps the asymmetry, and the receipt "does NOT independently prove the underlying event" (S0122 L41, in meaning).
- A seventh anti-corruption rule makes this safe: "Receipt Evidence ≠ Independent Proof of Underlying Event" (S0122 L75, in meaning). "The receipt's delivery element is evidence toward a world-fact; its acknowledgement element is evidence of a record-fact." (S0122 L43, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.15 C10, CON-0222).** The C22 review qualified C10: the move to the record is partial, because "delivery occurred" stays a world claim. S0122 records the authority's answer. The partiality is intended, and it is made safe by stating that the record is evidence toward, not proof of, the world part. For the kernel this is §9.15's separation in one sentence: a claim's internal part (the record) and its world part (resting on L-assumptions) are named apart, not merged.

**C5. Absence of a field concluded, not assumed; a field forbidden because it invites a forbidden inference (Src).** `[CON-0228 · provisional]`
- The actor field is "FORBIDDEN as a C-10-owned field" (S0122 L110, in meaning), with the rationale argued: absence "is not assumed to be required; it is concluded" (S0122 L115, in meaning).
- The reasons, in short:
  - an actor field invites the inference "identity binding established", which the decision lists as a non-claim;
  - it would import C-5's subject;
  - it could carry only a self-declaration;
  - the session association already carries what is needed (S0122 L111–114, in meaning).
- Drift is "blocked structurally, not by warning": no field asserts understanding, application or compliance (S0122 L188, in meaning).
- Conditional requirement: the invalidation reference is "REQUIRED when lifecycle_state = invalidated", since an unconditional requirement would demand it on every valid receipt (S0122 L132, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13 D).** D's required fields are per kind, not per state. S0122's conditional field (required in one state) has a D analogue: the tombstone, which must cite a removal act only when removed. Forbidding a field to block an inference is the book's choice for I15. The recorder is required but read by no clause of Φ, so the record cannot be read as attesting who acted. *Adversarial check:* does forbidding the field lose information the estate needs? The source says the need is real and routes it elsewhere, through C-5 attestation or as correlation content (S0122 L115, in meaning). So the claim is about where the information lives, not that it is unneeded.

**C6. A review is citable only for the questions it checked (Src; CON-0154's rule for reviews).** `[CON-0229 · provisional]`
- The governance review is "bounded". It checks completeness, provenance and decision-boundary preservation only, and "does not evaluate architectural correctness" (S0124 L7–9, in meaning). It "must NEVER be cited as independent verification of E2" (S0124 L9, in meaning).
- So its verdicts are three narrow ones: complete, traceable, preserved (S0124 L85–87, in meaning). It includes a provenance check of the proposal's claims "mechanically verified against the record, not accepted on assertion" (S0124 L45, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.10 C4, CON-0154; §8.15 C4).** CON-0154 asked "authoritative for which claim". S0124 applies it to a review: a review's result is evidence for the questions it checked and for nothing else. A completeness review that finds a model "tested, not asserted" (S0124 L43, in meaning) has checked that a test is present, not that the test is adequate. So §8.16 C3's adversarial point stands unanswered. The diverge/converge test shows that the four ownership parts are not one function, not that they are independent. S0124 does not reach it.
- **The pending point of C3 (b) stays open.** S0124 does not examine whether a receipt with a required version is populatable before BC-1's publication mechanism exists; its bound excludes it. *Open after this window* (§8.16.3).
- **Basis outside the record.** The resolution of the decided claim's asymmetry, and the seventh rule, came "from an act absent from the registered record" (S0124 L99, in meaning). The review counts this as the fourth occurrence of input "reaching a governed decision from outside the governed channel" and recommends registering it "so the artifact's basis is readable from the record alone" (S0124 L100, in meaning). *Int (§9.13 D):* D's rule that a citation must name an earlier entry present in the record is this recommendation for acts. A basis that is not on record is not a basis.
- *Source defect (S0122, found by S0124):* "four of them are classified SUPPORTING", while the table has three (S0124 L92, in meaning). Not relied on.

**Skimmed: S0125 (one line).** S0125 registers the Flag-O resolution and the seventh rule, whose content is S0122's, already in C4. That closes S0124's registration gap (C6), so E2's basis is now on record. *What it adds:* the consumption rule that a downstream artifact "must consume D1 exactly — it may neither weaken nor strengthen it" (S0125 L37, in meaning). And a fact: E2 is "ADOPTED WITH RECORDED FOLLOW-UP ITEMS" (S0125 L60, in meaning).
