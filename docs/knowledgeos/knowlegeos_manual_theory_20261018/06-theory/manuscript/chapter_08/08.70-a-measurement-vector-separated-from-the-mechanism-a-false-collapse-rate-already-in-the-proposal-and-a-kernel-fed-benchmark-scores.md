## 8.70.1 In plain language

1. **The first layer is a literature review whose evidence cannot be checked from the file.** In its bibliography the title and the link of an entry often name different works, several quotations carry no citation, and one topic is rated both "Strong" and "Moderate". Its accuracy hypothesis, "74.2% → 85-90%" on a 120-case corpus, starts from figures identical to a benchmark the book graded as a report without a transcript. It is the book's earlier projection pattern again.
2. **The second layer makes the proposal more modest, on the book's lines.** SNF is any normalization that passes tests, not one pipeline. But until the distance, the reference relation "same meaning" and the thresholds are fixed, that is still a schema. A hash fingerprint shows that two representations are equal, not that two meanings are, and not that two pieces of knowledge are the same. The uncertainty of the interpreter stays on the interpreter's side. Its "context" argument, though, may carry the knowledge base back in; the first layer itself calls the knowledge base "context".
3. **The new false-collapse rate renames the proposal's own complement.** The first layer's non-collapse score counts the distinct pairs that stay apart. The false-collapse rate counts the distinct pairs that do not: one minus the old score, as the book already saw in S0317's form. The critique rejects the old score for its arbitrary threshold, and then needs a threshold to define "collapse". The rate that is new is the opposite error, equal meanings split apart. The continuous separation measure restores the first layer's own "current" formula.
4. **A distance that understands meaning can do the normalizer's work.** The distance used, NSID, is meant to be semantic. A "normalizer" that returns every expression unchanged then looks good: the distance, not the normalizer, puts equal meanings close and different ones apart. Only the equality-based forms of the error rates test the normalizer itself.
5. **No single score: the metrics must each pass.** That agrees with the book: what blocks cannot be traded for what does not. But there are two acceptance rules. The selection rule uses the table's "Target" column while calling it "minimum", and it has no abstention criterion at all. The table's abstention precision would fail a system that abstains on everything, if precision is measured against "humans agree it is ambiguous". That definition is in the earlier source S0317, not in this one. Convergence on equal meanings is a second route, depending on how an abstention is counted. The gap the book recorded at §8.65 and §8.68 is narrowed only under these conditions.
6. **The third layer agrees with everything and then draws the kernel deciding epistemic state from the metrics.** The metrics describe a mechanism over a test corpus. They cannot decide the state of one claim. The drawing goes against the critique it endorses on what the kernel takes as input. It stands in tension with the first layer's "Do not design the kernel".
