## 8.25.1 In plain language

This window restates what the book holds, with three points.

1. **A robot that acts on its own must act under a recorded grant, or it is acting without authority.** An adviser's worked example has a robot run its own checks ("PASS"), implement a change and record new knowledge, with no human approval shown. In the book, a check produces evidence, never authority. An autonomous action is legitimate only as an act under a grant that covers it.
2. **A business translation is a view of the record, not the record.** An adviser asks that translations "simplify language, but … not simplify truth". In the book's terms, a translation is a projection: a decision must cite the governed state, not the translation.
3. **"ESTABLISHED" in a summary is a claim, not evidence.** Two non-authoritative summaries tag some claims "ESTABLISHED" because an adviser stated them as fact. The tag does not make them established.

## 8.25.2 Claims and derivations

### S0171: the Digitalization Robot

**C1. The robot is an application of the kernel, not its core (Src; evidence for §8.24 C8 and T0-03; a tension recorded).** *(No new concept.)*
- "The robot is not the core domain. The robot is a consumer and operator of governed knowledge." [S0171 L23].
- **A tension in S0171, recorded.** Four lines earlier, "KnowledgeOS becomes a Digitalization Robot" (S0171 L19, in meaning). The two statements conflict unless "KnowledgeOS" means the whole platform at L19 and the kernel at L23. S0176 resolves it that way: the robot is a "distribution", and the kernel stays free of AI (S0176 L149, in meaning). This is T0-03's identity question again.
- Agreement with §8.24 C8 (S0167: applications reach knowledge only through the kernel). It is the same evening thread, so it is **one line**.

**C2. An autonomous action is legitimate only as an act under a grant that covers it (Src; Int from §9.13 and §1.14.2-1; an attack on the source's example).** *(No new concept.)*
- **S0171's modes:** Advisor (the human decides), Developer Assistant (the human approves), Controlled Executor (approval needed), and Autonomous Operator, "Only for limited domains" (S0171 L297–355, in meaning).
- **S0171's worked example:** a request, then the robot's design, its own "Governance" checks reporting "PASS", implementation, and storage of a "New Knowledge Product". No human approval is shown, and the mode is not named (S0171 L359–454, in meaning). The robot's loop includes "Decide" (S0171 L81–98, in meaning).
- **Int.**
  - In the book a check produces evidence and never standing (§8.06 C6), and the mechanism records authority and never grants it (§1.14.2-1).
  - So the example's "PASS" is evidence only. Recording a new knowledge product needs an adoption whose authorization is T (§9.13). Evidence must also be qualified before governance uses it (§8.23 C6).
  - For the Autonomous Operator, the book's form is an act under a recorded grant whose holder is the robot's actor and whose scope covers the domain ("limited domains"). Outside that scope its acts are F.
  - S0171 does not say that its modes are grants. **Recorded:** S0171's modes are compatible with the kernel only if each mode is a grant with a scope.
- *Scope:* S0171 is a vision, not a mechanism. The point is about what the kernel requires of any such robot, not about an existing one.

### S0173: the business translator

**C6. A translation is a projection: a decision cites the governed state (Src; Int for §8.17 and the C23 rule).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0173's translator makes no decisions and "should never become another authority layer" (S0173 L89, L118, in meaning). It "must preserve uncertainty" (S0173 L132, in meaning). "The business translation may simplify language, but may not simplify truth." [S0173 L322]. "Internally we keep the exact governed state. Externally we provide the interpretation." (S0173 L262–264, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.17; the C23 rule).**
  - A translation is computed from the governed state and is not part of it: "a projection, never a source" (§8.17).
  - S0173's business statuses merge governed states. For example, "Ready for decision" means "No technical blocker remains" (S0173 L252–253, in meaning), which covers both ADDRESSED and CLOSED findings. So the translation loses information, and the governed state cannot be recovered from it.
  - *Deduction:* a decision taken on a translation must cite the governed state it rests on, as the C23 rule requires: the authority's words first, every gloss labelled.
- *Agreement.* It is also the Principal's own reporting rule for this book: plain business language, with the governed record behind it.
