## 8.25.1 In plain language

This window restates what the book holds, with three points.

1. **A robot's act is effective only if it is an act under a recorded grant that covers it.** An adviser's worked example has a robot run its own checks ("PASS"), implement a change and record new knowledge, with no human approval shown. In the book, a check produces evidence, never authority. An autonomous action is legitimate only as an act under a grant that covers it.
2. **A business translation is a gloss on the record, not the record.** An adviser asks that translations "simplify language, but … not simplify truth". In the book's terms, a translation is a derived claim: a decision must cite the governed state, not the translation.
3. **"ESTABLISHED" in a summary is a claim, not evidence.** Two non-authoritative summaries tag some claims "ESTABLISHED" because an adviser stated them as fact. The tag does not make them established.

**C2. An autonomous act is effective only if it is T: an act under a grant that covers it (Src; Int from §9.13 and §1.14.2-1; an attack on the source's example; restated at review, W-09).** *(No new concept.)*
- **S0171's modes:** Advisor (the human decides), Developer Assistant (the human approves), Controlled Executor (approval needed), and Autonomous Operator, "Only for limited domains" (S0171 L297–355, in meaning).
- **S0171's worked example:** a request, then the robot's design, its own "Governance" checks reporting "PASS", implementation, and storage of a "New Knowledge Product". No human approval is shown, and the mode is not named (S0171 L359–454, in meaning). The robot's loop includes "Decide" (S0171 L81–98, in meaning).
- **Int.**
  - In the book a check produces evidence and never standing (§8.06 C6), and the mechanism records authority and never grants it (§1.14.2-1).
  - So the example's "PASS" is evidence only. A new knowledge product is **effective** only through an adoption whose authorization is T (§9.13). D records an F adoption too; only removals, designations and single-use uses are refused at append. Evidence must also be qualified before governance uses it (§8.23 C6).
  - For the Autonomous Operator, the book's form is an act under a recorded grant whose holder is the robot's actor and whose scope covers the domain ("limited domains"). Outside that scope its acts are F, or U where coverage is undefined (§9.13, check 15).
  - S0171 does not say that its modes are grants. **Recorded:** S0171's modes are compatible with the kernel only if each mode is a grant with a scope. Capability (what the robot can do) and authority (what is effective) stay separate (§8.24 C3).
  - *Recorded (coverage):* S0171's closing loop, from business goal to "New Organizational Knowledge" (S0171 L593–636, in meaning), has no step for retracting what proves wrong.
- *Scope:* S0171 is a vision, not a mechanism. The point is about what the kernel requires of any such robot, not about an existing one.

### S0173: the business translator

**C6. A translation is a derived claim; a decision cites the governed state (Src; Int for the C23 rule; restated at review, W-08).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0173's translator makes no decisions and "should never become another authority layer" (S0173 L89, L118, in meaning). It "must preserve uncertainty" (S0173 L132, in meaning). "The business translation may simplify language, but may not simplify truth." [S0173 L322]. "Internally we keep the exact governed state. Externally we provide the interpretation." (S0173 L262–264, in meaning).
- **Int (the C23 rule).** A translation is a **derived claim**, a gloss on the governed state. It is not a projection in the book's sense, which is regenerable from its sources (CON-0260): an interpretive text is not regenerable. So, for **any** translation, a decision taken on it must cite the governed state it rests on: the authority's words first, every gloss labelled (the C23 rule). This holds even for a mapping that loses nothing. Where the mapping loses information, the governed state also cannot be recovered from the translation.
  - *The Professor's reading [I]:* S0173's five business statuses collapse nine governed states (S0173 L225–260, in meaning), but S0173 defines no state-by-state mapping. Reading "Ready for decision" ("No technical blocker remains", S0173 L252–253) as covering both ADDRESSED and CLOSED is the Professor's interpretation.
- **A tension in S0173, recorded:** it names "Business Meaning Truth" beside technical and assurance truth (S0173 L303–307, in meaning), while saying the translator is no authority layer. It also gives three different templates: five questions (L43–55), nine sections (L157–193) and a six-item review ending (L344–351), all in meaning. So its "fixed template" is not fixed (W-12).
- *Agreement.* This is also the Principal's own reporting rule for this book: plain business language, with the governed record behind it.
