## 8.65.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer calls the earlier benchmark's speed "already demonstrated".** The book graded that benchmark Src: no transcript was kept, and whether a run occurred is open. The same answer begins by saying the verification "correctly questions the latency". It also simulates a future version with star ratings, and says one result validates the incompleteness-theorem lens.
2. **The middle answer projects the next version to "93–95%" from gains measured stage by stage.** The next answer reads this as adding the gains to 74.2%, and rejects it: gains overlap, so they are "not additive". The middle answer's own figures do not hold together:
   - its cumulative column follows no stated rule;
   - five of its six stage tables do not sum to their totals;
   - its "failure contribution" column is the earlier per-category accuracies relabelled;
   - its stage speeds allow at most about 13,500 parses per second in series, not the 20,000–30,000 it states.
3. **The third answer separates semantic accuracy from epistemic correctness.**
   - Semantic accuracy asks whether the compiler understood the sentence.
   - Epistemic correctness asks whether there are grounds to conclude the sentence is true.
   - A sentence can be parsed correctly while its status stays UNKNOWN for lack of evidence.

   This agrees with the book: understanding a claim is not having evidence for it. The book also keeps a third answer that the split lacks, standing: an adopted claim keeps its standing even without evidence.
4. **The same answer says KnowledgeOS should "decide what happens to that meaning".** It lists outcomes from VALIDATED to SUPERSEDED, and names no act or actor.
   - Some of these outcomes are, in the book, effects of acts; others are display classes.
   - "UNKNOWN because there is no evidence" is the book's "not attested", not standing's U.
   - A system that always says UNKNOWN would have no false acceptances, and no metric in the source guards against that.
5. **Several statements agree with the book:** context takes part in a claim's identity; "probably approved" must not collapse into "approved"; similarity is not identity.
   - The revision example is the exception. It adds "after the security review", which by the answer's own rule (and S0309's) is a change of context. It then calls the result "identity continuity": a contradiction within the line, not an agreement.
   - The compiler is placed "outside the core" by a decision cited but not in this window. That placement is compatible with both branches of the open fork.
