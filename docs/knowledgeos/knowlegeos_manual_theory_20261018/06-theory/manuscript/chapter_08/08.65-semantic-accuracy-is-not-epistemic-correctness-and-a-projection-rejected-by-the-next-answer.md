## 8.65.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer treats the earlier benchmark as having "demonstrated" its speed.** The book graded that benchmark Src, because no transcript was kept, and it is still not known whether a run occurred. The first answer also "simulates" a future version with star ratings and ranges, and treats a single result as validating the incompleteness-theorem lens.
2. **The middle answer projects the next version to "93–95%" by adding stage gains to 74.2%.** The arithmetic fails on its own terms:
   - the first step, 74.2 plus 20, is shown as 85;
   - added through, the gains would pass 188%;
   - its stated speeds give about 11,000 parses per second for a serial pipeline, not the 20,000–30,000 it states.

   The third answer rejects these numbers as evidence: accuracy gains are "not additive". That is the source correcting itself, as S0307 did.
3. **The third answer separates two things:**
   - **semantic accuracy:** did the compiler understand the sentence?
   - **epistemic correctness:** may KnowledgeOS conclude that it is so?

   A sentence can be parsed correctly while its status stays UNKNOWN for lack of evidence. This agrees with the book: understanding a claim does not settle its standing or its evidence.
4. **The same answer says KnowledgeOS should "decide what happens to that meaning".** It lists admission outcomes: VALIDATED, UNKNOWN, CONTESTED, CONFLICTED, REJECTED, SUPERSEDED. No act or actor is named for any of them, so the act gap of §8.63–§8.64 remains. Some of these outcomes are acts in the book, such as superseding. Others are display classes.
5. **Several statements agree with the book:**
   - context takes part in a claim's identity;
   - a revision keeps the earlier version rather than overwriting it;
   - "probably approved" must not collapse into "approved".

   The third answer also places the compiler outside the core, citing a decision that is not in this window. It renames the "Gödel stage", noting that the theorem's insight is not a JSON format.
