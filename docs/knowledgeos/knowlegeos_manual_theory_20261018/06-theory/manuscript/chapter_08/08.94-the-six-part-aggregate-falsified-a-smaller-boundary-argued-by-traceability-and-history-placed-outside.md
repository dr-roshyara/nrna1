## 8.94.1 In plain language

1. **The answer tests the six-part aggregate pair by pair, finds that none of the pairs needs to change together, and declares the aggregate falsified; it then proposes a smaller boundary of identity, evidence references and justification history.** The reply points out that the new boundary is argued from traceability, the same mistake. Within the book's record both points hold: nothing shown needs a joint update, and the smaller boundary has no invariant behind it either; the boundaries the book does need join a check to the entry it guards.
2. **The answer states as its one invariant that every claim in KnowledgeOS must be justified by evidence and reasoning.** Within the record that is too strong for standing: an authorized act can give standing to a claim with no evidence, which is then shown as not attested.
3. **The answer says history is the source of truth and state is derived from it, but then places the full event history outside the kernel, in an audit context.** Within the record the book has the first: the record is the kernel's state, and history is the record. So history cannot be outside the kernel.
4. **The reply lists nine scenarios to test the smaller boundary: new evidence, evidence invalidated, one source behind a thousand claims, and others.** Within the record most are answered by the book: each is an entry appended, and what is shown is worked out at each moment, not caught up later. Contest and reconciliation between two claims have no kind yet, and whether content can change is still open.
5. **The answer's "Zero" section says meaning is resolved before a candidate exists, candidates do not persist, an inconsistent state makes the kernel refuse, and an empty knowledge state is valid.** Within the record the last three have counterparts; the first is the reply's objection too: the book takes the reading of a claim as an input, and says nothing about where unresolved readings are kept.
6. **The answer lists what the kernel cannot establish about itself and calls the kernel "the set of these gates".** The list agrees with the book within the record, read as self-correctness. Of the gates, the one that admits no claim without evidence conflicts with the book, as in point 2.
