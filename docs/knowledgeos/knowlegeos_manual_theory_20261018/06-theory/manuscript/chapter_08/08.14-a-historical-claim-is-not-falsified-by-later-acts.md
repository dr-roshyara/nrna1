## 8.14.1 In plain language

A record that says "this was delivered at time T" never becomes false. Later events can only change what the record is good enough for, so withdrawing reliance on it is a decision about one purpose, not a correction of the past. A dispute is a separate flag that can sit on a record in any state. Raising one is cheap and resolving one is expensive, so who may raise a dispute has to be settled together with what the dispute suspends.

The authority declined to count a decided boundary as an adopted commitment to the capability. Deciding to enforce a rule is itself a decision about what the rule's owner is. And "there is no record of it" counts as evidence only when the store the record would be in actually exists and was read.

## 8.14.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A decided artifact boundary is not counted as an adopted commitment to the capability (Src; restated at review, W-1).** `[CON-0213 · validated]` *(validated at the C22 review, in its restated form: the decided boundary was not counted; the act's grounds are non-realization, §8.15 C8)* `[CON-0208 · invalid as stated]` *(CON-0208's reading, "the estate requires performance", is withdrawn.)*
- S0111 recommended YES. Its "load-bearing argument" was that a decision fixing the receipt's authoritative claim "presupposes a subject for that artifact" (S0111 L46, in meaning), with "realization NONE" (S0111 L44, in meaning). Of the capability's five activities, "one occurs and is owned elsewhere; four do not occur at all" [S0111 L21].
- The authority decided otherwise: "D2 = `NOT YET ESTABLISHED`" (S0112 L12, in meaning).
- **What the act shows, and what it does not.** S0112 states the estate's existence test: adopting the receipt as authoritative state "would supply limb ①" (an adopted commitment about the capability) and so would "decide existence by the back door" [S0112 L14]. So D1, the decided claim boundary, **was not counted as limb ①**. And supplying limb ① alone would bear on existence. The act is **silent on performance**. *(Corrected at C22, §8.15 C8: false. The decision's own reasoning, registered in S0120, names non-realization of the capability's activities, and Governance records that the incumbent test is conjunctive. The C21 review read S0112 only.)* *(Version 1 read it as evidence for §8.12 C7's performance limb. S0112 L14 points the other way: the estate's test is about an adopted commitment, not about performance.)* The authority's own grounds are in its decision record (S0120), read at C22 (§8.15 C8).
- **Decision by invariance, a sketch (W-8).** S0111 argued that existence holds whichever way the open scope question is resolved: "Existence survives either resolution" [S0111 L54]. The cases are not exhaustive. A third resolution, applicability outside **and** the receipt owned elsewhere, leaves the capability empty. The second branch also assumes that the receipt is C-10's, which is D3's open question. So the form is a sketch with a missing case, not a proof.

**C2. Enforcement decides category; a decision can be kept non-constitutive (Src; for §9.16 R3a).** `[CON-0209 · validated]` *(validated at the C21 window review: a definitional consequence of the source's criterion; one producer.)*
- "Selecting HALT or BLOCK for any tier decides the category by consequence." [S0113 L18]. The source rests this on the analysis: advisory today, "control-plane if it gates START" (S0113 L16, in meaning).
- A boundary specification framed as constitutive "would decide existence by the back door" [S0112 L14]. The remedy is a conditional form: the capability "would own the receipt lifecycle and state described above if and when C-10 is realized" (S0112 L69, in meaning).
- And: "enable any enforcing behaviour in the same act that defines it" is the one move Governance would not make (S0113 L91, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R3a).** R3a chose prevention or detection per law by whether the effect can be undone. S0113 adds a structural consequence: choosing prevention puts the law's guard in the control path, which is itself a decision about where the law lives. For the kernel, this is what §9.16 R4 derived for single use: prevention needs a coordination point.

**C3. Two operations under one word: correcting an error and withdrawing reliance (Src; restated at review, W-7).** `[CON-0214 · provisional]` `[CON-0210 · invalid as stated]` *(CON-0210 merged the two.)*
- **The source's claim, and its scope.** The receipt's claim "is a time-stamped historical fact, so it never becomes FALSE — only INSUFFICIENT or UNRELIED-UPON" [S0114 L15]. This holds for a claim **recorded correctly**. The same source lists "receipt issued in error" among the triggers for invalidation [S0114 L78], and there the claim was false from the start.
- **So "invalidated" covers two operations:**
  - **(a) correcting an error**, where the claim was false from the start. CE*'s annulment ("void from the start") is the analogue;
  - **(b) withdrawing reliance for a purpose**, where the claim stays true: "invalidation is PURPOSE-SCOPED, not global" [S0114 L85]. The worked instance kept the findings as evidence and withdrew only their use as assurance (S0115 L54, in meaning). The same instance is cited twice (S0114, S0115), so it is one instance.
- The three-way cut stands for (b). Superseded is a fact about the version, "act-independent"; stale is "relational"; invalidated is "an authority act" (S0114 L64–69, in meaning). "Reversible?" is mis-framed for (b): the question is whether reliance may be restored (S0114 L80, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13), restated.** CE* separates the record of an act from its standing, and it has (a). For (b), one adoption per claim already allows reliance to be withdrawn for one claim and kept for another. What CE* lacks is a way to **partition one act by claim**: an annulment of an act voids every claim it carries. *(Version 1 said "CE* lacks purpose"; that overstated it.)*
- *Change over time:* S0112 had invalidation "triggered when the referenced context version is superseded" (S0112 L48, in meaning). S0114 separates the two.

**C4. A dispute is a flag orthogonal to the lifecycle, and its effect needs a gate on who may raise it (Src; compares with §9.13's marks).** `[CON-0211 · provisional]`
- "DISPUTED is a FLAG over the lifecycle state, not a terminal state within it." [S0114 L19]. A receipt may be valid, superseded, invalidated or missing, and disputed as well (S0115 L60, in meaning).
- The effect proposed: "A dispute suspends the receipt's use FOR THE DISPUTED CLAIM ONLY." [S0115 L71].
- The asymmetry: a dispute is "cheap to enter, expensive to exit" (S0114 L96, in meaning). So "The effect and the "who may raise" gate must be decided together." [S0115 L76].
- "A dispute cannot make an unenforceable condition enforceable." [S0115 L78].
- **Int (§9.13).** CE*'s marks have the same shape: they are orthogonal to the value and change no value. A dispute that only marks would be safe against the asymmetry. One that suspends use changes standing, and then the raiser's authority must be evaluated, as for any act that changes standing. This is the kernel form of S0115's "decided together".

**C5. Absence is evidence only in a realized store (Src; bears on §9.13's C1 and §9.15 L4).** `[CON-0212 · provisional]`
- "absence of a record in an UNREALIZED store is not evidence of absence" [S0114 L27]. *Change over time:* S0113 had the missing condition detectable "in principle" (S0113 L28, in meaning); S0114 and S0115 make absence non-evidential while the store is unrealized. A retrieval failure "must NEVER be reported as M1", the missing case (S0114 L29, in meaning), since that "laundders an infrastructure failure into a governance fact" (S0114 L29, in meaning; the source's spelling).
- No enforcement on "an inference from absence alone" (S0115 L33, in meaning), and M1, the missing case, "is that inference" (S0115 L33, in meaning).
- A present receipt that cannot be bound to its session is NOT ATTESTED, never MISSING: calling it missing "asserts "no receipt", which the evidence does not support" (S0114 L34, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.13 C1; §9.15 L4).** CE*'s current-authority gives F when an item has no adoption on record. That F is an inference from absence, and §9.13 rests it on L4 (the record is complete). S0114 sharpens the premise. The F holds only if the record **is** the authoritative store and was **read**. A failed read must not yield F: it is a refusal, not an answer. CE* has no input for a failed read, so a Q1 implementation must keep "the record was not read" apart from "the record has no adoption". **A partial read** (C21, W-9) is worse: if it happens to be a prefix in D, it gives F silently. It needs §9.16 R4 (iii)'s rule for cuts (answers "as of the entries received"), and the F needs L4 (completeness) besides a realized store. *Proposed for the next §9.13 revision; not applied in this window.*
- **Two senses of NOT ATTESTED (W-10).** S0115 uses it for an identity that cannot be bound (M4) and for an applicability that cannot be resolved (M2, STALE; S0115 L34, L38, in meaning). Only the first is §8.12 CON-0201's "can be established" level; the second is an open upstream judgement.

**C6. The binding of a record to its subject is not attestable in the estate today (Src; bears on §9.15 L10).** *(No concept: an observation of the estate's state.)*
- "The platform cannot attest "a specified session execution."" [S0112 L20]. So identity binding "must NOT be placed inside C-10's authoritative state" (S0112 L24, in meaning). Even the missing-receipt check "cannot verify that a presented receipt belongs to the session presenting it" (S0113 L44, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R4a; §9.15 L10).** (C + O)'s guard rests on L10: the kernel authenticates the application that submits an entry. These sources record that the estate cannot attest the identity of a session. In this estate the recording parties **are** sessions: each record is by a "Recording process" that is self-declared (S0108 L4, in meaning; C21, W-12). So L10's kind of binding is **unmet for the estate's actual recorders today**. These sources do not refute L10 as an assumption; they show it is a real one, not a formality.
- **A possession claim with a self-declared subject.** S0112 keeps the receipt authoritative for delivery and possession (S0112 L24, in meaning) while its subject session cannot be attested (S0112 L20). So the possession claim's subject is self-declared (W-11 b).
- **A contradiction recorded (W-11 d).** S0112 would have the receipt own "completeness" (a receipt for each version an act requires; S0112 L49, in meaning), while applicability, which "requires" needs, is excluded (S0112 L55, in meaning). G4 under (C + O) holds only where L10 is met.

**C7. Prevention can stop everything when its condition is universal (Src; a parallel to F22's cost).** *(No concept: an observation.)*
- While no receipt exists, "Every session, for every required context version, is in state `Missing`." [S0113 L40]. So "Any blocking response to Missing halts the entire estate on the day it is enabled." [S0113 L42].
- An enforcing response needs a defined trigger, an authoritative trigger, an evidence source and an existing authority: "No row satisfies all four." [S0115 L25].
- A warning on every act "would be noise, not notification" (S0115 L84, in meaning).
- **A third way between prevent and detect (W-11 a).** S0113 names a compensating control: work proceeds with an explicitly recorded exception, and the estate already does this, since the recorded human START act substitutes for absent machine enforcement (S0113 L75–77, in meaning). *Int (§9.16 R3a):* R3a has two values, prevent and detect. A recorded authorization of the exception is a third. It bears on the cost of R4a: a grant whose owner is unavailable could proceed under a recorded exception, which is a normative choice the book has not made. *Change over time:* the floor moves from WARN (S0113) to INFO (S0115 L84).
- **Int (§9.16 R4a).** The same shape as (C + O)'s cost and §8.13 C6: prevention trades availability, and when the guarded condition holds everywhere the trade is total.

## 8.14.3 Open questions

- Does the kernel need **purpose-scoped standing**, so that reliance can be withdrawn for one use and kept for another (C3)?
- Should a dispute be a **mark** (no effect on standing), or an act that suspends use, with its raiser's authority evaluated (C4)?
- How does Q1 report a **failed read** of the record, as against an absent adoption (C5)?
- Is "the answer is the same under every resolution of the open question" an accepted form for deciding under an open question, and does S0111's case list hold (C1; the authority's grounds, S0120)?
- Should D partition one act by claim, so that reliance can be withdrawn for one claim of an act (C3)?
- Is a recorded exception a third value beside prevent and detect (C7)?

## 8.14.4 What does not hold

*Version 1, refuted at review C21:*
- **CON-0208, the NOT YET ESTABLISHED act "as one instance of the estate requiring performance"** (C1). The act shows that the decided boundary was not counted as an adopted commitment (S0112 L14); it is silent on performance (W-1). *(C22, §8.15 C8: this refutation is itself withdrawn. The authority's grounds name non-realization.)*
- **CON-0210, "a historical claim never becomes false"** without its scope, and invalidation as one operation (C3). The source's own trigger, "issued in error", is a claim false from the start (W-7).
- **"CE* lacks purpose"** (C3). It lacks a partition of one act by claim (W-7).
