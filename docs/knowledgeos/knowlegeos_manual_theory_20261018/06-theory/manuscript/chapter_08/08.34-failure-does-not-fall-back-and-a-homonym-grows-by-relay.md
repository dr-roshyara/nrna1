## 8.34.1 In plain language

1. **The estate says a failed change leaves the last trusted state in force.** Inside one kernel the book agrees: the bad entry is refused and the record is unchanged. But if a bad entry reaches a merged record, the book does not fall back to the earlier state. It refuses every answer on that record, earlier points included.
2. **A word with two meanings gets stronger each time it is relayed.** EKS's "lifecycle record ≠ grant" became "evidence ≠ authority", then "means of knowing ≠ valid knowledge", and each step is cited as more support. None adds evidence.
3. **"Meaning is not valid knowledge"** agrees with the book's two separate values. The same chat, though, allows "assessment → authority" in its pipeline while listing "assessment ≠ authority" as an invariant. The two fit only if that step is a person's act.
4. **"Canonical knowledge is always historical"** is what the book's Q1 already does: it answers at a recorded point.
5. **"Make forbidden transformations impossible"** is the book's guard class only; the book detects some violations instead. And the kernel does give answers: it derives them and never stores them.
6. **The chat declares collection complete twice and keeps adding lenses.** It also numbers new candidates "INV-KOS-004…007" while saying "no INV-005…N added": two numbering series.

## 8.34.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A breach does not fall back to the last trusted state: inside one kernel it is refused at append; on a merged record every cut is refused (Src; Ded against §9.13; Int).**
- **S0212:** "Failure pauses time, not the world": "Trusted Snapshot T1 remains canonical" after a failed synthesis (L86–139). The candidate INV-KOS-005: "Failed transformations should preserve the last trusted knowledge state and should NOT create partial authoritative states" (L441–447).
- **Ded, against §9.13:**
  - **(i) Within one kernel:** a failing entry is refused by the append guard, and nothing is appended (§9.13.2; PL-2). The record, and every answer on it, is unchanged. **Agreement.**
  - **(ii) On a merged record or a bypass:** a bad entry puts **the whole record** outside D. "D is closed under prefixes but **not under appending**", and "Q1 then refuses every question on it, although earlier cuts were answered" (§9.13.2, availability). Membership of D is checked on the whole record, also for an earlier cut (§9.13 v9; `ce_star_cut`). **So the book does not fall back to T1; it refuses.** The book's reason: refusing is fail-closed, while dropping a failing entry could turn the acts it targets to T (§9.13.2).
- **Int:**
  - S0212's model holds for (i) only. For (ii), "preserve the last trusted state" would require reading an earlier cut **despite** a later breach, which the book rejects as not fail-closed.
  - §9.18.6's partial fault containment is the one place the book narrows the refusal, for the evidence area's own clauses.
  - *Agreement within one kernel; a stated difference on merged records.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. A homonym grows by relay: "transitions ≠ grants" → "evidence ≠ authority" → "Pramāṇa ≠ Pramā" (Src; Int).**
- **The chain:**
  - S0206 (EKS): "transitions (evidence) ≠ grants (authority)": the lifecycle log against the authority record (S0206 L1150; §8.32 C2: the book's state ≠ authority).
  - S0213 and S0214: "The EKS extraction showed: Transitions ≠ Grants. Meaning: Evidence movement ≠ Authority assignment. This maps directly: Vedanta: Pramāṇa ≠ Pramā. KnowledgeOS: Evidence method ≠ Authority state. This strengthens INV-001 Authority Separation" (S0213 L140–184; S0214 §4).
  - S0213's reply: "Evidence ≠ Authority — Already established by EKS" (L824).
- **S0214 §12.1** then cites "the EKS evidence (transitions[]≠grants[], 126/126 humanActRef, content-derived ids)" as evidencing "the 'who'" of knowing. **Against S0206:**
  - humanActRef is present on 126 grants but is prose only on 63 and resolvable for at most half (S0206 L404, L733);
  - content-derived identity belongs to recommendations (L718), while grant ids are not even unique (L405).
- **Int:**
  - Each relay renames the same EKS fact and counts it as **further** support for INV-001. The lifecycle log is not a "validation method", and the grant record is not "valid knowledge". **No relay adds evidence.**
  - This is the mechanism by which an "Established" row grows in the estate's map without new measurement (§8.31 C4; §8.33 C3).
- **Not a new concept.** Recorded for T10-18.

**C3. Meaning ≠ valid knowledge agrees with promise (b); the pipeline "assessment → authority" fits INV-003 only as a person's act (Src; Int).**
- **S0213's example:** "'The server is healthy'": expression ("Text exists"), meaning ("A health condition is described"), validation ("Monitoring evidence confirms it"), authority ("Authorized person/system accepts it"). "These are separate" (L356–389).
- **Agreement:** in the book, **current authority** (an accepting act, Q1) and **well-foundedness** (evidence, the class) are separate values, shown together (promise (b); §10.5 PL-14).
- **Tension in the line:**
  - S0213's allowed pipeline is "Observation → Evidence → Assessment → **Authority** → Publication" (L490–502);
  - the Established row it relies on is "INV-003 Assessment ≠ Authority" (S0212 L81; S0214 §12.1).
  - **Int:** the two fit only if the step from assessment to authority is a **recorded act by a person who holds authority**, which informs the act and does not become it. That is the book's line: evidence informs, an act by AU decides (§10.4 R1). S0213 does not say so.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. "Canonical knowledge is always historical" is Q1 at a cut; "publication" is a recorded act (Src; Int).**
- **S0212:** "Canonical knowledge is always historical, never present-tense" (L228); the candidate "Trusted State Is Historical: A knowledge state becomes authoritative only after successful governed publication" (L250–252); INV-KOS-006 "Historical Authority" (L456–462).
- **The book:**
  - Q1 answers for a **cut s**, a recorded prefix (§9.10 Q1).
  - Answering "now" across applications without a sealed cut is not order-free (§9.16 L80).
  - **Authority comes from a recorded act** (an adoption, a kind of D), not from a separate step called "publication".
- **Int:** S0212's "always historical" is what the book's Q1 already does. Its "publication gate" is, in the book's terms, the acceptance of an authorised act at append (§10.5 PL-2). S0212 itself rules that publication is "a governed transformation", not a dimension (L218, §14.2), and applies the estate's model-integrity question to reach it (L220). *Agreement in substance.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. "Make forbidden transformations impossible" is the guard class only; the kernel derives answers and never stores them (Src; Ded against §9.16; Int).**
- **S0213:** one mechanism, "Structural Enforcement: The kernel must make forbidden transformations impossible" (L1028–1040). "KnowledgeOS does not store answers. It preserves the conditions under which answers can be trusted" (L575).
- **The book:**
  - **(i)** per law, some violations are **prevented** and others **detected** (§9.16 R3a): acting under ended authority is detected.
  - **(ii)** the kernel **gives answers** (Q1, Q3; §10.5 PL-6) with their conditions (PL-12). It never **stores** them: S0206's "every interpretation is recomputed on demand" (L705).
- **Int:** "impossible" fits the guard class only. "Does not store answers" is right only in the sense of "derives, never persists".
- **Not a new concept.**
