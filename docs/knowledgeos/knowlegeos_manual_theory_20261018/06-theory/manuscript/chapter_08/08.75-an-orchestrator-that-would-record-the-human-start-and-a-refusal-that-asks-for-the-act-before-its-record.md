## 8.75.1 In plain language

1. **The PO/ARB commissioned a tool that, after a human says "Yes", sets up the next actor's lane automatically, including the human START.** The tool would write the record that a human started the work, on the strength of the human's answer in the session. That is what the estate's earlier processes refused to do, and what the appointed verifier did. The commission forbids treating an AI message as a human act, but it does not say how the human's "Yes" becomes a record that is not the tool's own.
2. **The commission's distinctions agree with the book.** It separates recommendation from appointment, appointment from activation, and activation from completion of the work. It asks the tool to "verify independence", which the record can refute but not establish.
3. **The commission answers the human's burden, not the gap that stopped the work.** The refusals showed that what was missing was a recorded appointment and an actor entitled to record it. The tool would remove the need for the human to know the mechanics; it would still need someone to record the human's decision.
4. **A process refused to create the review lane because the appointment existed only in its prompt.** It asked for the human act to be in the record before anyone transcribes it. But the record can only ever hold transcriptions; the human act itself is outside it. The book stops that regress at a root act, by stipulation.
5. **The refusing process holds up the earlier appointments as the standard: a recorded human act plus a registration.** Those records were written by the processes they appointed. The standard was met in form only.

## 8.75.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. An orchestrator commissioned to record the human START on the human's "Yes" (Src; Int).**
- **S0334:**
  - "When the human selects: · 1. Yes — handle the governed setup automatically. · The system should: · resolve candidate · → verify eligibility · → prepare appointment · → REGISTER · → HANDOFF · → record human START according to G-3 · → verify ACTIVE · → report success" (L253–266);
  - "10. require/record the human START according to the authority model;" (L231);
  - "The application MUST NOT manufacture the human decision. · The following are NOT valid: … - treating an AI message as humanAct without an explicit human act;" (L237–242);
  - "The human may decide: · \"Yes, appoint one.\" · That is the authority act." (L162–166);
  - "Ports: … HumanDecisionPort" (L415–420).
- **Int:**
  - **The step the estate refused, made a function.** After the human's "Yes", the application records the human START (L231, L264). *That is §8.73 C1's pattern (a process appending `recordedBy: human` on a human's in-session statement), commissioned as the normal path.* The estate's earlier processes refused exactly this: "a chat instruction is not a human START" (§8.29 C1). The prompt's own bar (L242) excludes "an AI message as humanAct", not a human's chat answer transcribed by the application.
  - **The open interface.** The human's "Yes" reaches the application through a `HumanDecisionPort` (L420), which the prompt names and does not specify. *Whether the human's act is recorded by anything other than the process that then acts on it is the port's question, and it is left open.* K3's legitimacy matter (§8.27 C2: whether a recorded START had a human act behind it) is moved into that port, not answered.
  - **One "Yes", two acts** (W, step 1). The commission distinguishes "Appointment ≠ Activation" (L150), yet in Option 1 one human "Yes" drives both the appointment and the recorded START (L255–265); step 10 reads "require/record the human START" (L231). *Whether the START needs a second human act is not resolved by the source.*
  - **The trivial solution** (W, step 1). Thirteen of the fifteen minimum tests (L443–459) are "no transition" or "no selection" properties, which an orchestrator that refuses everything satisfies; only tests 2 and 12 are must-do properties. *By analogy with §9.19 T7.*
  - **No law forbids it** (§8.71 C3; §8.73 C1). *It is the PO/ARB's commission, as recorded; the book records the tension with the estate's earlier practice and with S0336 (C4), and decides nothing.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. A refusal that asks for the human act to exist in the record before its transcription (Src; Int).**
- **S0336:**
  - "the PO/ARB appointment of `d31ea60f` is **not confirmable from any authoritative record** — it exists only as text inside the prompt; and (b) this process is **not a verified governance-recording actor** … Recording a `recordedBy: human` START on the strength of a prompt would fabricate a human authority act (`G-3` boundary)" (L9);
  - "This instruction asks a process to write the human `START` itself — but the human act must *already exist* in the record before it is lawful to transcribe it; a prompt is not evidence that the PO/ARB acted." (L56);
  - "The prompt's assertion *\"You are Governance Architecture\"* does not create the role." (L68).
- **Int:**
  - **A correct refusal on the prompt:** *agreement* with §8.72 C5 and §8.74 C1 (the prompt is not the lane) and with §8.29 C1 (a chat instruction is not a human START). "You are Governance Architecture" (L68) is a statement of a role, not an act that grants it (A1; §1.12).
  - **A rival reading: two objects** (W, step 1). The standard is coherent if "the human act … in the record" means an **appointment record** (a registration artifact) distinct from the START transition that transcribes it; S0336's unblock path names both (L84). *On that reading the regress moves to the appointment record, whose recorder is again the question* (C5).
  - **The regress in the standard.** "The human act must *already exist* in the record before it is lawful to transcribe it" (L56) asks for a recorded human act as the precondition of recording the human act. *The record holds only transcriptions;* the human act itself is outside it (K3, §8.27 C2). So the standard is met only by a first transcription that is not itself preceded by one. The book stops this regress at a root act, by stipulation (§1.14.1; §8.51 C1; N4-15, T1-17 open). *S0336 does not name where its regress stops; the prior chain stopped it at the beneficiary's transcription (C5).*
- **Not a new concept.**
