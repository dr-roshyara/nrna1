## 8.104.1 In plain language

1. **Within the estate, one research commission went to three providers, and an adviser counted their agreement as independent convergence.** The commission itself listed the topics the three agree on (identity, boundary, authority, context, the hidden reasoner, knowing and acting) and asked each reply to challenge the architecture. The agreement on topics is therefore the commission's agenda, not a finding; the book holds again that agreement within one line is not independence. The third reply attacks a domain model that the commission, as recorded, does not contain.
2. **Within the estate, the third provider's report, which §8.103 could not read, is now read directly.** Its questions mostly land where the book already stands: the record's boundary is a designation, so "conventional" describes it rather than refutes it; the kernel within D checks no sufficiency, so a single-gate sufficiency test is a check the book does not have; the evaluator's three values are discrete by construction and are determinations of standing, not epistemic states. Identity through change, and who may set or amend the admission rules, remain open in the book.
3. **Within the estate, the report names five places where interpretation might hide in the kernel.** Within D none of the five is a kernel judgement: the clauses read recorded fields, identity of an entry is derived from its fields, sufficiency has no counterpart, the evaluator computes its value from recorded acts, and meaning-identity is an input.
