## 8.39.1 In plain language

1. **"Two equally supported, contradictory conclusions stay unresolved; the state is CONFLICTED, not FALSE."** This agrees with the Principal's decision of 2026-10-29: the display shows "contested". One difference: in the book, more evidence never ends a contest. As long as one live support and one live refutation remain, the item is contested, however many more entries are added on either side. A contest ends only by a recorded act that removes one side, a correction or a withdrawal. Nor does the book weigh evidence: "equally supported" plays no part.
2. **The same source treats one situation two ways.** A passed audit against a new critical vulnerability is "conflicted" in one place and "superseded by stronger evidence" in another, with no rule to choose. The book keeps three things apart: a conflict (contested), a defeat by stronger evidence (no home: evidence has no strength, and nothing is replaced), and an expiry (the end of a validity period set by an act).
3. **"Knowledge identity includes its authority and time"** does not hold in the book. An entry's identity comes from its content. Authority is computed, and a stored authority has no standing.
4. **The sources list states (VALID, UNKNOWN, CONFLICTED, SUPERSEDED, INVALID or REFUTED) but never say how one state may follow another.** Their rules are all prohibitions or duties to keep; a store that does nothing would satisfy them.
5. **Ontological categories, knowledge atoms, typed relations and accountable agents** are either content matters or already placed in the book.
6. **Two of the three sources are one template; the three give three different definitions of KnowledgeOS within nine minutes, under three numbering prefixes; and one claims a graph database is "almost mandatory", which is false.**

## 8.39.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. "Contested" agrees with the sources, but is not resolved by more evidence, and is not weighted (Src; Ded against §9.18.5 g1; Int).**
- **S0226's H-KOS-Competing-Reasoning-001:** "Equally supported contradictory inferences SHALL remain unresolved until additional evidence changes the epistemic state" (L254–258). Its example: "Security audit passed" against "Critical vulnerability discovered", and "Correct state: KnowledgeState = CONFLICTED", "not: KnowledgeState = FALSE" (L224–247). It rejects choosing the "Highest probability answer" (L210–216).
- **S0228's KOS-ERROR-001:** "Contradictions are knowledge objects, not failures", with "Conflict detected — Resolution pending" for two recorded database versions (L415–447).
- **Ded, against g1 (T9-17 (b), decided):**
  - **Agreement:** with a live support and a live refutation, the class is **contested**, not refuted (§9.18.5 g1). The sources' rejection of a forced FALSE is the Principal's decision.
  - **Not resolved by more evidence:** g1's input is "some refutation is live" and "some support is live". Adding entries to either side leaves both T, so the class stays contested. It changes only when one side ceases to be live, by a recorded act: an annulment (correction, voids from the start), or a withdrawal for the purpose asked by a holder of a grant over the scope (T9-16 (b)). So "until additional evidence changes the epistemic state" does **not** hold under g1.
  - **Not weighted:** g1 has no strength. An item is contested whatever the balance; "equally supported" is not a condition.
- **Int:** the sources' "resolution pending" implies a later resolution act; the book's form of it is the annulment or withdrawal above. Two adoptions of conflicting **content** (S0228's two database versions as adopted facts) are a different case: both stay current, and the conflict is neither recorded nor reported (§8.36 C4 (ii)).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. Conflict, defeat and expiry are three things; the source merges two of them (Src; Int).**
- **S0226** uses one evidence pattern twice:
  - as conflict: audit passed against a critical vulnerability gives CONFLICTED (L224–241);
  - as defeat: "Version 1.0 is secure" against "Critical CVE discovered" gives "VALID → SUPERSEDED_BY_EVIDENCE" (L350–373), under H-KOS-Evidence-Override-001, "Higher authority evidence may invalidate a conclusion while preserving historical lineage" (L380–383).
  - No rule chooses between them; "equally supported" (L256) and "higher authority" (L382) assume an order of strength that is not defined (W's S0226 item 5).
- **S0228** adds a third: KOS-TIME-001, "Knowledge has lifecycle and temporal validity", with valid-from and valid-until (L535–567).
- **The book:**
  - **Conflict:** contested (C1).
  - **Defeat by stronger evidence:** no home. g1 has no strength; producer authorisation decides only whether an entry counts (T9-12 (c)), not its weight; an evidence entry changes no standing value ((c1); (c2) proposed); and supersession is not admitted (Θ_f σ). Under g1, the CVE against the earlier support gives **contested**, not superseded.
  - **Expiry:** for authority, a validity set by the act and grant periods are recorded, and CE* reads them (§9.13). For content, validity is the content's.
- **Int:** "higher authority evidence" mixes authority (standing's) with evidence strength (none). If v1.0 was never secure, the claim was refuted, not superseded: the sources merge refutation with change over time (W's S0226 item 6).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Authority and time are not part of an entry's identity (Src; Ded against §9.16 R2).**
- **S0226:** "Knowledge Identity = (Entity, Property, Context, Relation, Time, Authority)", "Not: Entity + Text" (L484–502); its avacchedaka example puts "authority: operations" in the bounds of a property (L460–470).
- **Ded:**
  - In the book, an entry's identity is derived from its content: the fields CE* reads, not the recorder or the status (§9.16 R2; law 29; §10.5 PL-4). Since v7 a grant's owner and recording application are also part of its identity for the merge (T9-18).
  - Authority is not a field. It is computed by CE* at a cut (§9.10 Q1), and a stored derived value has no standing (CON-0141). Making authority part of identity would make standing an attribute of the entry, and would change identity whenever standing changes.
  - Time: an act's time and validity are fields of the act; they bear on standing but do not make the claim's content a different item.
- **Int:** "authority: operations" in S0226's bounds is a content delimiter (§8.37 C3), the organisational owner of the property, not standing: "authority" once more as a homonym (§8.38 C5).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. State vocabularies without transitions; prohibitions only (Src; Int).**
- **The vocabularies:** S0226: CONFLICTED, SUPERSEDED_BY_EVIDENCE, VALID (L237–373); S0227: "VALID / UNKNOWN / CONFLICTED / SUPERSEDED / INVALID" (L372–380); S0228: "VALID / UNKNOWN / CONFLICTED / SUPERSEDED / REFUTED" (L399–411). Two siblings, three minutes apart, differ in their last value.
- **Int:**
  - Each is one chain over several axes (§8.38 C2): standing (VALID), evidence (CONFLICTED, REFUTED), change over time (SUPERSEDED), and the open world (UNKNOWN; §8.33 C1). No source states which transitions are allowed.
  - **Prohibitions only:** the invariants of all three are duties not to do something or to keep something ("SHALL NOT", "SHALL preserve", "cannot"). No source states a must-do capability; the "Revision Engine" (S0227 L514) has no rule. A store that holds nothing satisfies them all (W's S0227 item 13). The book excludes that trivial solution with its must-dos (Q1 answers on every record in D, §9.10; §8.35 C7).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. Ontology, atoms, relations and agents: content, or already placed (Src; Int).**
- **Ontology:** S0227's H-KOS-Ontology-001, every object has a category "entity, property, relation, event, or absence" (L58–62); S0228's KOS-ONTOLOGY-001 (L94–98). The book's kinds are **act** kinds (§9.13.2), not a content ontology; a content category is the application's. *S0228's mapping drops generality, particularity and inherence (W's S0228 item 3); not used.*
- **Atoms:** S0228's KOS-ATOM-001, knowledge "decomposable into independently addressable semantic units" (L511–515). The book's act-object granularity is a parameter (§9.12; CON-0190). "Independently addressable" pulls against context-bounded meaning (S0226 L476–502): an atom's meaning depends on its context (W's S0228 item 9).
- **Relations:** S0227's KnowledgeGraph-001 and S0228's KOS-GRAPH-001, relationships as first-class (S0227 L331–335; S0228 L297–301): authority relations are the record; item relations have no home (§8.37 C3).
- **Agents:** S0227's AgentReasoning-001, "identity and capability of the reasoning agent" (L470–474); S0228's KOS-AGENT-001, an agent "accountable for the reasoning chain it produces" (L633–637). The actor of every act is recorded; reasoning has no kind of act (§8.36 C5); "capability" is not a grant held (§8.38 C5).
- **Not a new concept.**

## 8.39.4 Challenges recorded

- **C1, rival:** "additional evidence" in S0226 means evidence that settles the matter, for example a fix.
  - *Answer:* under g1 a further entry cannot settle it; only removing one side's liveness by a recorded act can. A fix shown by new evidence leaves the old refutation live until it is withdrawn or annulled.
- **C2, rival:** "superseded" means only that the newer evidence is shown first.
  - *Answer:* that is an ordering of the display, which g1 does not have (recency was a rival of T9-17, not chosen).
- **C3, rival:** S0226's "Authority" in the identity tuple is the organisational owner, not standing.
  - *Answer:* then it is content (a delimiter), and C3's point stands for standing; the source does not say which it means.
