## 8.39.1 In plain language

1. **"Two equally supported, contradictory conclusions stay unresolved; the state is CONFLICTED, not FALSE."** This agrees with the Principal's decision of 2026-10-29: the display shows "contested". One difference: in the book, more evidence never ends a contest. As long as one live support and one live refutation remain, the item is contested, however many more entries are added on either side. A contest ends only by a recorded act that stops one side from being surely live: annulling an entry, annulling or ending the grant its producer relied on, or withdrawing it for the purpose asked. Nor does the class rule weigh evidence: "equally supported" plays no part in it.
2. **The same source treats one situation two ways.** A passed audit against a new critical vulnerability is "conflicted" in one place and "superseded by stronger evidence" in another, with no rule to choose. The book keeps apart: a conflict (contested); a defeat by stronger evidence, which the class rule does not have and whose possible home, the evidential grade, is not yet defined; a defeat by an act that takes a statement out of use (a counted withdrawal, or annulment), which has a home; and an expiry (the end of a validity period set by an act). Replacement by a newer statement (supersession) is required by the book's list of acts, but not yet built.
3. **"Knowledge identity includes its authority and time."** In the book, an entry's identity comes from its content, and that content includes the act's time and its validity. Authority, however, is computed, not stored: a stored authority has no standing. Whether a later version of a statement is a different item is still open.
4. **The sources list states (VALID, UNKNOWN, CONFLICTED, SUPERSEDED, INVALID or REFUTED) but never say how one state may follow another.** Their rules are prohibitions or duties to keep; a store that does nothing would satisfy them. The book excludes that, because it requires inputs to be accepted.
5. **Ontological categories, knowledge atoms, typed relations and accountable agents** are either content matters or already placed in the book.
6. **Two of the three sources are one template; the three give three different definitions of KnowledgeOS within nine minutes, under three numbering prefixes; and one claims a graph database is "almost mandatory", which is false.**

## 8.39.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. "Contested" agrees with the sources, but is not resolved by more evidence; g1 does not weigh (Src; Ded against §9.18.5 g1; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-03, W-02.)*
- **S0226's H-KOS-Competing-Reasoning-001:** "Equally supported contradictory inferences SHALL remain unresolved until additional evidence changes the epistemic state" (L254–258). Its example: "Security audit passed" against "Critical vulnerability discovered", and "Correct state: KnowledgeState = CONFLICTED", "not: KnowledgeState = FALSE" (L224–247). It rejects choosing the "Highest probability answer" (L210–216).
- **S0228's KOS-ERROR-001:** "Contradictions are knowledge objects, not failures", with "Conflict detected" and "Resolution pending" (two lines) for two recorded database versions (L415–447).
- **Ded, against g1 (T9-17 (b), decided):**

  | Step | Justification |
  |---|---|
  | (1) an entry is live iff its producer is authorised, it is not annulled, and it is not withdrawn for the purpose asked | §9.18.5 g1 |
  | (2) contested iff some refutation and some support are both surely live | g1 |
  | (3) a new evidence entry is neither an annulment nor a withdrawal | §9.18.4 |
  | (4) a new evidence entry changes no standing value of the other entries | premises (c1) and (c2), §9.18.6 ((c2) proposed, since adopted (T9-19, T9-20); result 1 empirical on a sample) |
  | (5) Kleene "or" is monotone, so both sides stay T | g1 |

  - **So more evidence never ends a contest** (under (c1), (c2)). *Agreement* in the display; S0226's "until additional evidence changes the epistemic state" does **not** hold under g1.
  - **What can end it:** a recorded act that makes every entry on one side not surely live: an annulment of the entry, an annulment or ending of its producer's basis (liveness is the entry's own standing value), or a counted withdrawal for the purpose asked (T9-16 (b); none applies with no purpose named). An act of standing can also turn a contest into U, for example a grant ending at the entry's act time (W's probes on v24, cases, not proofs). This matches §10.2 contested(k, t): "a correction, a withdrawal, or an act of standing".
  - **Not weighted:** g1 has no strength, so a contest holds whatever the balance. *For the book as a whole this is provisional:* Q4's evidential grade, where strength could enter, has no reference definition yet (C2).
- **Int:** two adoptions of conflicting **content** (S0228's two database versions as adopted facts) are a different case: both stay current, and the conflict is neither recorded nor reported (§8.36 C4 (ii)). Read as a change over time (an upgrade), S0228's example supports C2's split instead.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. Conflict, defeat and expiry are three things; the source merges two of them (Src; Int).** *(Restated at review: W-02, W-07.)*
- **S0226** uses one evidence pattern twice:
  - as conflict: audit passed against a critical vulnerability gives CONFLICTED (L224–241);
  - as defeat: "Version 1.0 is secure" against "Critical CVE discovered" gives "VALID → SUPERSEDED_BY_EVIDENCE" (L350–373), under H-KOS-Evidence-Override-001, "Higher authority evidence may invalidate a conclusion while preserving historical lineage" (L380–383).
  - No rule chooses between them; "equally supported" (L256) and "higher authority" (L382) assume an order of strength that is not defined (W's S0226 item 5). Its H-KOS-Inference-Consistency-001 ("Every inference SHALL preserve validated relation strength", L122–129) is a further strength assumption.
- **S0228** adds a third: KOS-TIME-001, "Knowledge has lifecycle and temporal validity", with valid-from and valid-until (L535–567).
- **The book:**
  - **Conflict:** contested (C1).
  - **Rebutting defeat by strength:** no home in g1, which has no strength; producer authorisation decides only whether an entry counts (T9-12 (c)), not its weight. Its possible home is Q4's evidential grade, a required question whose reference definition is absent (§9.10 Q4). *Not built, not refuted.* Under g1, the CVE against the earlier support gives **contested**, not superseded.
  - **Defeat by an act (undercutting):** has a home: a counted withdrawal for a purpose by a holder of a grant over the scope (T9-16 (b)), which is S0888's "resolution", or an annulment, or an act of standing on the producer's basis.
  - **Supersession** (a newer statement replacing an older one): a required kind (O1; I-7) that D does not yet admit (Θ_f σ, open). *Required and not yet built.*
  - **Expiry:** for authority, a validity set by the act and grant periods are recorded, and CE* reads them (§9.13); the book already keeps several kinds of ending apart (§1.13; §1.16). For **evidence**, an entry has an act time but no validity period (§9.18.4): a refutation of "v1.0 is secure" never expires; only a withdrawal or an annulment ends it. KOS-TIME-001 for evidence has no home: *a gap, not a refutation*.
- **Int:** "higher authority evidence" mixes authority (standing's) with evidence strength. If v1.0 was never secure, the claim was refuted, not superseded: the sources merge refutation with change over time (W's S0226 item 6).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Authority is not part of an entry's identity; time is; item identity over versions is open (Src; Ded against §9.16 R2).** *(Restated at review: W-01, W-06.)*
- **S0226:** "Knowledge Identity = (Entity, Property, Context, Relation, Time, Authority)", "Not: Entity + Text" (L484–502); its avacchedaka example puts "authority: operations" in the bounds of a property (L460–470); its type "SecurityStatus : (system, audit-context, timestamp, assessor) → EpistemicClaim" (L602–606).
- **Ded:**
  - **(a) Entry identity.** An entry's identity is derived from its content: "kind and D's required fields other than the recorder and the status" (§9.16 R2; law 29; §10.5 PL-4). Every kind requires an actor and an act time; an adoption requires its validity (§9.13). So **time is part of an entry's identity**; since v7 a grant's owner and recording application are too (T9-18). **Authority is not:** it is computed by CE* at a cut (§9.10 Q1), and a stored derived value has no standing (CON-0141). Making authority part of identity would make standing an attribute and change identity whenever standing changes.
  - **(b) Item identity.** S0226 speaks of the *knowledge* (item), not the entry. Q1's k is a governed identifier (§9.10 Q1). Whether a time-indexed version of a statement is a different item is open (T9-14; G10).
- **Int:**
  - S0226's type reads its tuple's "Authority" as the **assessor** (L602–606). The book carries the assessor as the evidence entry's actor, its producer, a required field (§9.18.4). On that reading S0226 partly agrees with the book.
  - "authority: operations" in the bounds of a property (L466) is a content delimiter (§8.37 C3), an organisational owner of the property, not standing: "authority" once more as a homonym (§8.38 C5).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. State vocabularies without transitions; prohibitions only (Src; Int).** *(Citation corrected at review: W-05.)*
- **The vocabularies:** S0226: CONFLICTED, SUPERSEDED_BY_EVIDENCE, VALID (L237–373); S0227: "VALID / UNKNOWN / CONFLICTED / SUPERSEDED / INVALID" (L372–380); S0228: "VALID / UNKNOWN / CONFLICTED / SUPERSEDED / REFUTED" (L399–411). Two siblings, three minutes apart, differ in their last value.

  - Each is one chain over several axes (§8.38 C2): standing (VALID), evidence (CONFLICTED, REFUTED), change over time (SUPERSEDED), and the open world (UNKNOWN; §8.33 C1). No source states which transitions are allowed.
  - **Prohibitions only:** the invariants are duties not to do something or to keep something ("SHALL NOT", "SHALL preserve", "cannot"); S0226's Evidence-Override-001 is a permission ("may"). ## 8.39.4 Challenges recorded

- **C1, rival:** "additional evidence" in S0226 means evidence that settles the matter, for example a fix.
  - *Answer:* under g1 a further entry cannot settle it; only an act that stops one side from being surely live can (C1). A fix shown by new evidence leaves the old refutation live until it is withdrawn or annulled, or its producer's basis ends.
- **C2, rival:** "superseded" means only that the newer evidence is shown first.
  - *Answer:* that is an ordering of the display, which g1 does not have (recency was a rival of T9-17, not chosen).
- **C2, rival (added at review, W-02):** defeat has a home as undercutting.
  - *Answer:* yes, and C2 now says so: a counted withdrawal, an annulment, or an act of standing. Rebutting defeat by strength still has none in g1.
- **C3, rival (restated at review, W-06):** S0226's "Authority" in the identity tuple is the assessor.
  - *Answer:* then it is the evidence entry's producer, a required field, and S0226 partly agrees with the book. Read as standing, it does not.
