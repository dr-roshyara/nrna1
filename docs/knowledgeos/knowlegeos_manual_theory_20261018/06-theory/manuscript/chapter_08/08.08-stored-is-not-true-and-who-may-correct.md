## 8.8.1 In plain language

Three points come out of the verification chain of one domain model:
1. **A record can store a value that never becomes true.** The mechanism keeps unknown fields, but nothing ever reads them. An invariant can therefore hold because nothing consults the value, not because the value is refused.
2. **A number is only evidence with its method.** Two verifications in a row found counts that could not be reproduced because the counting rule was not stated.
3. **The practice of who may correct changed.** Earlier, the verifier was the "wrong pen" for a repair. Here, the verifier of round two made the correction, and only verifying its own correction was barred.

## 8.8.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Stored is not true: an invariant can hold by non-consultation (Src; Ded).** `[CON-0141 · provisional]`
- **The evidence:**
  - the mechanism has "**no key whitelist** — a submitted key is persisted as given" [S0073 L77];
  - the fold "reads only its defined fields; **no derived key is ever read from a record**" [S0073 L79];
  - the estate holds 20 stored keys the mechanism never reads, among them `note` ×16, which "appears nowhere in the mechanism" [S0073 L84].
- **The rule as the verifier states it:** "The rule is not that such a value is refused at the boundary; it is that it can never become truth." [S0075 L47]. And "storage acceptance ≠ semantic authority" (S0074 L37, in meaning).
- **Four ways an invariant can be kept** [S0073 L122]: refusal (at the boundary), overwrite (a submitted value is replaced), non-consultation (the derivation never reads it), and writer obligation (a practice, measured).
- **Ded:** if every derived value is computed by a fold that reads only a fixed set of fields, then a value outside that set cannot affect any derived value, whatever is stored. So "no derived value comes from a stored projection" holds **by the fold's definition**, without refusal. *Premise:* the fold is fixed. The interpreter can be swapped (§6.5 C4), which is the same premise as CON-0134.
- **Int:** this explains why §6.5 C4 holds, and it separates **holding** an invariant from **enforcing** it at the boundary (CON-0102). It is the category set of Ch. 2 T2-05, met in a mechanism.

**C2. A count is evidence only with its selection rule and its measurement point (Src; an extension of §8.5 C9).**
- **Third and fourth instances:**
  - a delivery note's "5 removed · 14 added" against git's "16 insertions / 5 deletions", where a grep-based count drops blank lines [S0075 L11];
  - "6 of 37 STARTs" that "is **not independently reproducible**": a plain criterion gives 14 of 38, and "The selection criterion is unstated" [S0075 L12].
- **The verifier's proposed rule:** "**A census must state its selection rule, not only its result.**" [S0075 L87]. And the pattern "is the risk, not any instance" [S0075 L212].
- **A metric offered as an acceptance criterion is a hazard of its own.** When the figure "was offered to this verification as an acceptance criterion", a verifier using the natural method sees a mismatch: "That is how a whitespace discrepancy becomes a false tampering signal" [S0075 L132].
- **Dated counts survive drift:** denominators moved 133 → 135 during the verification "and the proportions held exactly", because the corrected cell carries its measurement point [S0075 L201].
- *Count only* (entry 46): four instances in one estate (V-D, R-1, F-1, F-2), as the source counts them [S0075 L212]. **Int:** this is the method requirement of §8.5 C9 and CON-0120, applied to every census.

**C3. Who may correct: exclusion against a forward constraint (Src; a conflict with §8.7 C5).**
- **The earlier rule (S0056):** a verifier who repairs becomes the producer and is barred from the next verification, the "wrong pen" (§8.7 C5).
- **The practice here:** the correction was performed by the author of verification #2, against a prompt that described "the **fresh** Architecture correction terminal" [S0074 L19]. The registered grant says the act "**bars no one** … the safeguard is the bounded scope plus the independent review that follows, not exclusion" [S0074 L17]. The record adds a "FORWARD CONSTRAINT: whoever performs this correction MUST NOT verify it" [S0074 L18].
- **Verification #3's verdict:** "the disclosure is adequate, and I do NOT judge the work should be redone by a fresh pen", because the corrections are "mechanically re-checkable" and were re-checked [S0075 L159–161].
- **Int:** two forms of one law (§1.14.3, producer ≠ verifier):
  - **exclusion:** a verifier may not produce, because then it would verify its own production later;
  - **a forward constraint:** anyone may produce, and the producer may not verify its own product.

  Both forbid verifying one's own work. Only exclusion also uses up the pool of independent verifiers. The corpus holds both, a day apart, in two work items of one estate. *Open (F25):* which form is the law, and does the choice depend on whether the work is mechanically re-checkable?
- **The cost is stated by the verifier:** "a same-pen correction plus a same-model verification means two of the three independence factors in this chain are weakened" [S0075 L163].

**C4. The model factor can be unknown even to the verifier (Src; an extension of §8.6 C1).**
- "MODEL SEPARATION — Unknown, and material … My system prompt identifies this process as Fable 5, while a /model selection in this session named "Opus 5 (1M context)" … I cannot attest which model executes this turn." [S0073 L34–38]
- **Int (§8.6 C3):** the record cannot attest a shared factor. Here the process itself cannot attest one of its own factors. So the model factor of independence can be undeclarable, not only unattestable.

**C5. An amendment completes; a changed verdict needs a new act (Src).**
- Verification #3 was amended after delivery, and it states what that permits: "this is **restatement and completion, not re-decision** — a changed verdict would have required a new verification act, not an amendment" [S0075 L250].
- **Int (§5.6, the object of an act; I-7):** an amendment may add classification and measurement to a verdict, but not change it. This is the act-level form of I-7 (supersession is a link, never an edit).

**C6. Further instances, recorded (Src).**
- **A stopping rule, a second time:** "Another correction cycle would cost more in lifecycle overhead than the defects cost in trust" [S0075 L226] (§8.7 C3; S0056 L109 was the first, in another work item).
- **The roles law RL, a third time:** the correction lane recorded its own seq-18 START, "per the practice accepted on this same record at seq 3 and seq 12" [S0074 L83] (§9.6.5).
- **`recordedBy` on STARTs:** "38 of 38 STARTs are `recordedBy: human`" [S0075 L79], while their own text can say "registering a START is a GOVERNANCE act" [S0075 L78]. This is the OQ-10 referent problem (§9.6.5), now counted.
