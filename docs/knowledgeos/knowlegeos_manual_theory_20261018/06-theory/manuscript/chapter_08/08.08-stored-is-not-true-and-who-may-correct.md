## 8.8.1 In plain language

Three points come out of the verification chain of one domain model:
1. **A record can store a value that never becomes true.** The mechanism keeps unknown fields, but nothing ever reads them. An invariant can therefore hold because nothing consults the value, not because the value is refused.
2. **A number is only evidence with its method.** Two verifications in a row found counts that could not be reproduced because the counting rule was not stated.
3. **The practice of who may correct changed.** Earlier, the verifier was the "wrong pen" for a repair. Here, the verifier of round two made the correction, and only verifying its own correction was barred.

## 8.8.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Stored is not true, for a given reader: an invariant can hold by non-consultation (Src; Ded; scoped after review C13).** `[CON-0141 · provisional]` (a lemma of CON-0134, shown for BC-7 only)
- **The evidence:**
  - the mechanism has "**no key whitelist** — a submitted key is persisted as given" [S0073 L77];
  - the fold "reads only its defined fields; **no derived key is ever read from a record**" [S0073 L79];
  - the estate holds 20 stored keys the mechanism never reads, among them `note` ×16, which "appears nowhere in the mechanism" [S0073 L84].
- **The rule as the verifier states it:** "The rule is not that such a value is refused at the boundary; it is that it can never become truth." [S0075 L47]. And "storage acceptance ≠ semantic authority" (S0074 L37, in meaning).
- **Four ways an invariant can be kept** [S0073 L122]: refusal (at the boundary), overwrite (a submitted value is replaced), non-consultation (the derivation never reads it), and writer obligation (a practice, measured). *Lineage (corrected at the settlement):* S0073 confirms this list as "drawn correctly" from the refinement it verifies, S0078 (its OQ-12, which Ch. 2 T2-05 also cites). **One origin, not a convergence**, and shown for one mechanism only.
- **The distinctions it rests on, from S0072 and S0073:** "absence in practice ≠ refusal by contract" (S0072 L34, in meaning); and, for the `authorized` query, an answerability precondition, a verdict function and a consistency invariant are three different things, of which only the first involves the session (S0073 L98–108, in meaning).
- **Ded:** if every derived value is computed by a fold that reads only a fixed set of fields, then a value outside that set cannot affect any derived value **of that fold**, whatever is stored. So "no derived value comes from a stored projection" holds by the fold's definition, without refusal. **Premises, stated after review C13:**
  - the fold is fixed (the interpreter can be swapped, §6.5 C4);
  - **the fold is the only reader whose output counts.** BC-7 has a second reader: the resolver holds its own `mutationOwner` references ("**5** in `session-resolve.php`" [S0071 L48]) and carries an open specification gap, V-3 (S0067 L76, in meaning). People also read the stored `note` texts. So "can never become truth" holds for the fold's output, not for every reader.
- **Trivial case:** a fold that reads nothing meets "derived values come only from the fold". The lemma needs a must-do partner: what the fold **must** read. So it is a lemma, not a law.
- **Int:** this explains why §6.5 C4 holds, and it separates **holding** an invariant from **enforcing** it at the boundary (CON-0102). It is the category set of Ch. 2 T2-05, met in a mechanism.

**C2. A count is evidence only with its selection rule and its measurement point (Src; an extension of §8.5 C9).**
- **Third and fourth instances:**
  - a delivery note's "5 removed · 14 added" against git's "16 insertions / 5 deletions", where a grep-based count drops blank lines [S0075 L11];
  - "6 of 37 STARTs" that "is **not independently reproducible**": a plain criterion gives 14 of 38, and "The selection criterion is unstated" [S0075 L12].
- **The verifier's proposed rule:** "**A census must state its selection rule, not only its result.**" [S0075 L87]. And the pattern "is the risk, not any instance" [S0075 L212].
- **A metric offered as an acceptance criterion is a hazard of its own.** When the figure "was offered to this verification as an acceptance criterion", a verifier using the natural method sees a mismatch: "That is how a whitespace discrepancy becomes a false tampering signal" [S0075 L132].
- **Dated counts survive drift:** denominators moved 133 → 135 during the verification "and the proportions held exactly", because the corrected cell carries its measurement point [S0075 L201].
- *Count only* (entry 46), re-tallied at the settlement: §8.5 C9 lists six cases (S0051–S0055); this window adds F-1 and F-2 [S0075 L11–12]; S0072's "all 13 live workflow records" against 15 in the other sources (S0072 L26, in meaning); and S0073's "6 of 37" against its own "37 of 37" (S0073 L88, L155, in meaning). **Ten cases in one estate**, the source's own tally running from V-D through R-1 to F-1 and F-2 (S0075 L212, in meaning). **Int:** this is the method requirement of §8.5 C9 and CON-0120, applied to every census. *Hyp:* that it generalises beyond this estate.

**C3. Who may correct (Src; restated after review C13).**
- **The earlier rule (S0056):** a verifier who repairs becomes the producer and is barred from the next verification, the "wrong pen" (§8.7 C5).
- **The practice here:** the correction was performed by the author of verification #2, against a prompt that described "the **fresh** Architecture correction terminal" [S0074 L19]. The registered grant says the act "**bars no one** … the safeguard is the bounded scope plus the independent review that follows, not exclusion" [S0074 L17]. The record adds a "FORWARD CONSTRAINT: whoever performs this correction MUST NOT verify it" [S0074 L18].
- **Verification #3's verdict:** "the disclosure is adequate, and I do NOT judge the work should be redone by a fresh pen", because the corrections are "mechanically re-checkable" and were re-checked [S0075 L159–161].
- **Int (restated after review C13):** the two cases do **not** differ on the forward rule. S0056 also says that repairing "would make this process the producer of that correction" and so bar it from the next verification (§8.7 C5), and S0074 obeys the same bar. They differ on two other things:
  - **a grant:** S0056 had none for a further correction ("No grant exists"), while S0074 worked under a grant that "bars no one";
  - **role against process:** §1.14.3 forbids the *Verification* **role** to repair. S0074's process repaired in a new *architecture* lane, a different role on the same object.

  *Corrected:* the first version said only exclusion uses up independent verifiers. It is the other way round: when verifiers repair, each repair bars that verifier from the next verification (§8.7 C5).
- **Open (F25, restated):** may a process that verified an object then take a producer lane on the same object, and is a grant that permits it sufficient? Mechanical re-checkability is S0074's justification, not a variable shown to decide.
- **The cost is stated by the verifier:** "a same-pen correction plus a same-model verification means two of the three independence factors in this chain are weakened" [S0075 L163]. *Mapped onto §8.6 C1's seven factors:* S0075's "pen" is the process and its lineage (same process as the producer), and its "model" is the model factor. Its third, process separation from every artifact, is role/process.

**C4. The model factor can be unknown even to the verifier (Src; an extension of §8.6 C1).**
- "MODEL SEPARATION — Unknown, and material … My system prompt identifies this process as Fable 5, while a /model selection in this session named "Opus 5 (1M context)" … I cannot attest which model executes this turn." [S0073 L34–38]
- **Int (§8.6 C3):** the record cannot attest a shared factor. Here the process itself cannot attest one of its own factors. So the model factor of independence can be undeclarable, not only unattestable.

- **The model is also declared inconsistently (added after review C13):** S0071 declares its author "Claude Opus 5" (S0071 L11, in meaning), while S0075's commit table lists the proposal under "Fable 5" (S0075 L139, in meaning). Declared model labels across the chain disagree, and none is attestable.

**C5. An amendment completes; a changed verdict needs a new act (Src; one self-description).**
- Verification #3 was amended after delivery, and it states what that permits: "this is **restatement and completion, not re-decision** — a changed verdict would have required a new verification act, not an amendment" [S0075 L250].
- **Int (§5.6, the object of an act; I-7):** an amendment may add classification and measurement to a verdict, but not change it. This is the act-level form of I-7 (supersession is a link, never an edit).

**C6. Further instances, recorded (Src).**
- **A stopping rule, a second time:** "Another correction cycle would cost more in lifecycle overhead than the defects cost in trust" [S0075 L226] (§8.7 C3; S0056 L109 was the first, in another work item).
- **The roles law RL, a third time:** the correction lane recorded its own seq-18 START, "per the practice accepted on this same record at seq 3 and seq 12" [S0074 L83] (§9.6.5).
- **`recordedBy` on STARTs:** "38 of 38 STARTs are `recordedBy: human`" [S0075 L79], while their own text can say "registering a START is a GOVERNANCE act" [S0075 L78]. This is the OQ-10 referent problem (§9.6.5), now counted.

**C7. Two different A–D defect taxonomies (Src; added after review C13).**
- **S0073's**, for an invariant finding: (A) an invariant violation, (B) missing mechanism enforcement, (C) a missing conceptual distinction, (D) a mis-scoped invariant statement (S0073 L151, in meaning).
- **S0075's**, for a correction: A, a measurement error *about* the artifact; B, an evidence defect *inside* it; C, a scope violation; D, an architecture model defect [S0075 L173–176, in meaning].
- **Int:** same letters, two meanings. S0075's split between a defect *about* an artifact and a defect *inside* it bears on §8.7 (what a correction must repair).

## 8.8.3 Open questions

- **F25 (restated):** may a process that verified an object then produce on it in another lane, and is a permitting grant sufficient (C3)?
- Is non-consultation sufficient where several readers derive values from the same record (C1)?
- Should every invariant in the kernel carry its enforcement class (refusal, overwrite, non-consultation, writer obligation), as S0078's OQ-12 asks (C1; Ch. 2 T2-05)?

## 8.8.4 What does not hold

- **"Can never become truth" without a reader set** (C1). It holds for the fold's output only; the resolver and people are further readers (review C13, finding 3).
- **"Only exclusion also uses up the pool of independent verifiers"** (C3). Inverted (finding 1).
- **F25 as "exclusion against a forward constraint".** Both cases share the forward rule; they differ on a grant and on role against process (finding 2).
- **"Third and fourth instances"** (C2). Re-tallied as ten cases (finding 8).
- **"Four ways" as a convergence of S0073 and T2-05.** One origin, S0078 (finding 7).
