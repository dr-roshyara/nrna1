## 8.24.1 In plain language

Most of this window restates what the book holds. *(Corrected at the consolidation S0151–S0181, B-08: the draft said "one deduction is new"; C7 is K2-w's contrapositive under K2-d, a restatement applied to rebinding, with S0166 as an analogy.)*

1. **A role is read from the record.** A terminal or a prompt is where work runs, not who is authorized. This is the book's rule that a reader must not resolve "the record" by its own default (CON-0239), applied to roles.
2. **Being able to do something is not being allowed to do it.** Two advisers say it in their own terms: a session that knows how to change code still needs a grant, and wiring components together creates no authority.
3. **Changing the store that carries the record requires a designation.** An adviser separates technical changes (swapping a reasoning engine) from governance changes, each with its own approval path. Read for a store that carries the record, the book says: sending the kernel's writes to another store without a designation entry breaks K2-w. So such a change needs a designation entry, an act in the record.

## 8.24.2 Claims and derivations

### S0165: four sessions as roles

**C1. A role is resolved from the authoritative record, not from the terminal (Src; evidence for §1.14.2-1 and §1.14.3-2; restated at review, W-05).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- "A terminal is an execution context; it is not an authority context." [S0165 L594]. Role is resolved "from the authoritative workflow assignment, not from the physical terminal, process, prompt, or conversational claim" (S0165 L590, in meaning). The mechanism records an execution context and does not derive identity from it (S0165 L126, in meaning, reported as an earlier session's finding).
- **Int (§1.14.2-1; §1.14.3-2).** A session that takes its role from its terminal claims standing from a source other than the recorded acts. That breaks the rule that standing comes only from recorded acts (ARCH-INV-1). Activating itself breaks §1.14.3-2 (C2). S0165's rule names the record as the only source.
- *Analogy, not instance (W-05):* CON-0239 concerns readers that resolve **the record** by their own default. A terminal-resolved role does not resolve the record at all. The likeness is in shape only.
- **Scope.** S0165 asks for a resolver that does not yet exist (S0165 L738, in meaning). It does not show that the record it resolves from is itself designated. That is K2-d's presupposition (§9.15).
- *Agreement within the estate's lineage; one work item (n = 1).*

**C2. No role activates itself (Src; evidence for §1.14.3-2; challenged by §6.5 C2).** *(No new concept.)*
- "HANDOFF ≠ ACTIVE" and "HUMAN START + HANDOFF = ACTIVE" (S0165 L297–299, in meaning): REGISTERED, then CREATED by the predecessor's handoff, then ACTIVE by a human START (S0165 L312–326, in meaning).
- **Home.** This is §1.14.3-2's role laws, stated as a lifecycle. §6.5 C2 records that the estate breached them in practice: lanes recorded their own STARTs.
- **A gap in the rule as stated.** The first role in a work item has no predecessor handoff, so the formula does not say how it becomes ACTIVE. This is the same shape as the book's root designation, which lies outside D (§9.15 L11).
- *Recorded:* every rule here is a "must never". A model in which no human START is ever given satisfies them all and does nothing. Liveness rests entirely on human acts.

**C3. Capability is not authority (Src; evidence for §1.14.2-1).** *(No new concept.)*
- "A session can know how to perform something without being authorized to perform it" (S0165 L340, in meaning). An authorized mutation needs the role, the ACTIVE state, mutation ownership, an implementation grant and a scope match together (S0165 L352–363, in meaning).
- This is ARCH-INV-1: "The mechanism records authority. It never grants authority" (§1.14.2-1). S0165's conjunction is one estate's form of it.
- S0168 lists, among the risks of role-first modelling, "role assignment is mistaken for authorization" (S0168 L165, in meaning), and keeps roles as an operating model, not a decomposition of the domain (S0168 L172–182, in meaning). Same reading; S0168's standards are reported, not checked.

**C4. "Workflow state authorizes" is a homonym; S0165's Governance session that "determines" authority is an open tension (Src; Int; restated at review, W-04).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0165's closing list says "Workflow state authorizes." [S0165 L674] and, two lines later, "Human acts create authority." [S0165 L675].
- **Int.** Read alone, the first line contradicts ARCH-INV-1. Read with the second, "authorizes" means that the recorded state **admits** an action because it records a human act, not that the state creates authority. In that sense it agrees with §1.14.2-1. *Recorded as a homonym.*
- **But S0165 is mixed (W-04).** Its Governance session "determines whether and under what authority work may proceed" (S0165 L26, in meaning), asks "May this work exist?" (S0165 L233–235, in meaning) and "Is the work legitimate?" (S0165 L392, in meaning). Elsewhere the same session registers human acts (S0165 L399, L703, in meaning). If "determine" means deciding, a session decides, which ARCH-INV-1 forbids (§1.17). If it means checking the record for a human act, it agrees. *Open tension, recorded in §8.24.3. S0165 as a whole is not counted as agreeing with ARCH-INV-1.*

**C5. Do not change a mechanism while it is being measured (Src; practice).** *(No new concept; recorded only.)*
- Adding a role-discovery mechanism during the qualification "would contaminate the qualification" (S0165 L776–778, in meaning).
- This is the practice the estate applies to reviews (§8.20, a review in flight), and the Principal's own rule for this book: apply decisions after a review settles. It is methodology, not kernel theory.

### S0166: dependency injection

**C6. Composition is not authority (Src; evidence for §1.14.2-1 and §8.06 C6; tensions recorded).** *(No new concept.)*
- "The container cannot decide governance ownership." [S0166 L787]. "Dependency Injection assembles capabilities. It does not create authority." [S0166 L1099]. This is ARCH-INV-1 for composition, and §8.06 C6's "automation produces evidence; it never produces standing".
- **Tensions inside S0166, recorded:**
  - **Where the root sits.** The review separates the composition root from the runtime (S0166 L615–657, in meaning). The third part makes the runtime the root again (S0166 L1184, L1310, in meaning).
  - **Authority by composition.** The third part has the root "establish identity and authorization boundaries" (S0166 L1189, in meaning) and enable "the runtime to intercept and govern every agent action" (S0166 L1275, in meaning). That merges composition with enforcement, against L1099.
  - **An overclaim.** "necessary" (S0166 L1332, in meaning) does not follow from sources that show compatibility.

**C7. For the store that carries the record, rebinding the kernel's writes requires a designation entry (Src; Ded from §9.17 R1, §9.15 K2-d and K2-w; restated at review, W-02).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0166: changing the reasoning engine "is technical", changing the evidence authority model "is governance", and "They must not use the same approval path." [S0166 L1038]. Its bindings include an evidence store bound to an implementation (S0166 L888–901, in meaning).
- *The Professor's reading, [I]:* S0166 names only the reasoning engine as technical. That a storage rebinding would also be "technical" is an inference from its binding examples, not its words. S0166 puts the evidence authority model on the governance path, which is close to C7's own view.
- **Ded.**
  1. A rebinding changes **where the kernel's appends go**. By R1 it does not change which store is designated, because the carrier is fixed by designation, not by where writes land.
  2. K2-d (CON-0261, PROVISIONAL; the Professor's presupposition, not the Principal's words): the initial carrier is set by the root designation, and every later carrier by a designation entry in D.
  3. K2-w requires every kernel append to go to the carrier the designation in force names.
  4. So a rebinding without a designation entry sends appends somewhere other than the designated carrier: a K2-w failure.
  5. Therefore a change of the carrier **requires** a designation entry, appended as the new carrier's first append, with no append between the rebinding and that entry (K6).
- **What does not follow (W-02):** that the rebinding *becomes* a designation, or that any change is governed beyond requiring the entry. The designation's authorization is evaluated like any act's. The Principal's ruling of 2026-10-27, refusing an F or U designation at append, is applied after this window as changed theory for C32.
- **Scope.** This covers only a store that carries the record, and only changes after the initial binding (the root designation, L11, is outside D). Swapping a reasoning engine, or a store that holds projections (§8.17), is not a change of carrier.

- **The kernel/application boundary.** "KnowledgeOS applications do not directly access knowledge storage." (S0167 L1412, in meaning). They call kernel operations, including queries (S0167 L1416–1422, in meaning). This agrees with §9.17 R3 (c): a write that bypasses the kernel's steps is a K1 or K2-w failure. ### S0168: outside research on role separation

**C9. A locator is not an identity (Src, reported from W3C PROV; evidence for CON-0264).** *(No new concept.)*
- "A file path is a locator, not a durable identity." [S0168 L896]. An evidence record should survive a file move, repository split or storage migration. The old location remains historical provenance, and the new location is a locator "for the same or a derived entity, depending on whether content changed" (S0168 L925–950, in meaning).
- **Int (CON-0264; §9.17 R2; CON-0240).** CON-0264: carried-across copies are the same members. S0168 states the same for evidence entries moved between stores. Its condition, the same entity only if content is unchanged, agrees with D's unique identifiers (R2) and with CON-0240's content identity of fields.
- *A homonym, qualified (cf. §8.23 C4):* this is the identity of an **entry**, not of the **carrier**. It says nothing about which store is designated.
- **Two levels, not one (W-06).** S0168 separates a stable logical identity ("Evidence ID") from an immutable version and from a content hash (S0168 L898–921, in meaning), and leaves open whether hashes suffice for semantic equivalence (S0168 L966, in meaning). This is evidence for T9-08 (f), CON-0240's two levels of identity, which the turn-8 settlement left untouched.

**C10. Independence is graded; the record can show only that recorded identities differ (Src, reported; evidence for §8.06 C1, C3 and §8.15 C4).** *(No new concept.)*
- A platform "can prove a limited proposition": that the recorded producer and verifier identities differ (S0168 L233–236, in meaning). It cannot prove from metadata alone that there was no collaboration, conflict or bias (S0168 L248–256, in meaning). A system reporting "independent = true" from different actor IDs alone "would overclaim" (S0168 L1213, in meaning).
- This is §8.06 C3 (on the record, independence can be refuted but not established) and §8.06 C1 (a new identifier is not a factor of independence). S0168's graded levels (S0168 L218–227, in meaning) sit beside §8.15 C4, where independence is kept out of the evidence dimensions.
- *The cited standards (NIST SP 800-53 and 800-53A) were not opened. What S0168 says they require is reported.*
- *Recorded (coverage):* S0168 separates five assurance claims that are "not interchangeable": internal check, internal assurance, independent verification, external certification, governance acceptance (S0168 L835–845, in meaning). It holds that a platform that defines, runs and certifies its own conformance makes a structurally limited claim (S0168 L1248–1250, in meaning).

**C11. Enforcement needs an effect; publication is not delivery (Src; evidence for §1.14.5-1, §8.22 C3 and §9.05).** *(No new concept.)*
- "A script that prints a warning is monitoring or advisory control, not enforcement." [S0168 L530]. Enforcement needs a decision point, a protected operation, a defined effect, a failure mode, an override model and auditability (S0168 L1146–1152, in meaning). This agrees with §8.22 C3: a warn-only checker is not a gate, and a blocking one is a governance object (§1.14.5-1).
- "approved decision exists but execution context does not contain it" is a distribution failure (S0168 L374–378, in meaning), and published, delivered and applied are different states (S0168 L1239, in meaning). This is §9.05: a recorded rule must reach the session that acts.
