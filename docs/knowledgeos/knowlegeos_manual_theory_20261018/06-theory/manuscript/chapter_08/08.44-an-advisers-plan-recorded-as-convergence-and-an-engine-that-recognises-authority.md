## 8.44.1 In plain language

1. **The "eight-lens analysis" that the research record attributes to the architect reads as an assistant's answer to him.** It speaks to him as "you" and ends with "my recommendation" and "my expectation". Its recommendation to study four kinds of source becomes, in the record, "the convergence of four independent sources"; its expectation becomes the result. Whether the architect adopted the answer is not visible.
2. **The record runs the necessity test with a rule that any "yes" counts as confirming an existing article, never as a new law.** With that rule, the test cannot find a kernel law. "Seven for seven, each resolves to an existing article" is what the rule produces.
3. **The second answer proposes an engine that "recognises" which document has higher authority, and detects identity.** The Constitution of the same morning says authority is only ever assigned by a recorded human act, and identity is never derived from similarity. The book agrees with the Constitution: an engine may write evidence; it cannot find authority in documents.
4. **Several points agree with the book:** the kernel protects and the engines reason; revision is not deletion; the system holds what is justified, not what is real; evidence is not authority. Others go beyond what the book's record can carry: a "retirement" stage, relations between items, and a confidence number on each claim.
5. **The closure says "no further intake" and calls the marginal value of more research "collapsed"; nothing measures it.** Its list of what KnowledgeOS is not is said to be "the same five refusals" as the Constitution's, but two of the five differ. A Kashmir Śaivism extraction written after the closure appears in its survey without an intake record.

**C3. An engine that recognises authority and identity (Src; Ded against A1 and S0241 Art 1.3, Art 3.2; Int).**

- **Ded:**
  - In the book, authority exists only as a recorded act by a holder of a grant over the scope (A1; decision C1). Nothing an engine computes from documents is authority; it can be at most an evidence entry, which decides no class (§10.2).
