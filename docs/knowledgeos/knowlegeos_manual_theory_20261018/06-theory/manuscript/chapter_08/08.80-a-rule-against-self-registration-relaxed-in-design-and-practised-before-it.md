## 8.80.1 In plain language

1. **A rule said a session must never register itself.** The PO/ARB, as recorded, called that too strong and designed a narrow capability by which a fresh session may register itself for a role already commissioned; the capability was recorded as not decided and not implemented. A REGISTER naming the verifier's own identity then followed, on an instruction to it to "register yourself", before any such capability existed. *(Restated at review: W-01.)*
2. **When a session's registered label is its own runtime identity, the later identity check holds for it by construction** if the check reads the runtime value; if it reads a label the caller supplies, it holds for anyone who supplies that label. The design also moves appointment from a named session to a role. The role is to come from a commission that, unless it enters the store, is prose or the prompt itself. *(Restated at review: W-02, W-05, W-12.)*
3. **The design lets the very first session register itself as Governance Engineer on a typed instruction,** with nothing earlier to compare against, and its flow shows no human START and no predecessor for its HANDOFF. *(Restated at review: W-10.)*
4. **The verification calls its independence "formal" because a governed lane exists.** That lane names the verifier and was registered on an instruction to it; its bars are the producer-recorded ones, and the verifier's passing of them is its own declaration. For the general behavioural claims, "VERIFIED" is stronger than what the probes show: they failed to falsify. *(Restated at review: W-03, W-07.)*
5. **The verifier's lane, and the CONTINUATION out of STOPPED, live only in a git-ignored runtime file.** After the HANDOFF the producer's lane is HANDED_OFF, which ends §8.79's possible stale entitlement from then on; which branch held before is still not shown. *(Restated at review: W-08.)*
