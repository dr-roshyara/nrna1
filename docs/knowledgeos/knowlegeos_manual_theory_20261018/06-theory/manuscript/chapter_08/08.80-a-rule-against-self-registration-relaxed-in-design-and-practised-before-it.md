## 8.80.1 In plain language

1. **A rule said a session must never register itself.** The PO/ARB, as recorded, called that too strong and designed a narrow capability by which a fresh session may register itself for a role already commissioned. The capability was recorded as not decided and not implemented. The verifier then registered itself on an in-session instruction, before any such capability existed.
2. **When a session registers its own runtime identity, the later identity check compares that identity with itself.** It still catches a different session; it cannot catch anything about the registration. The role is to come from "the authoritative commission", which is a prose registration the mechanism does not read.
3. **The same design lets the very first session register itself as Governance Engineer because a human typed "I want Governance Engineer."** There is no earlier commission to compare that against; the authority chain would start from a chat line.
4. **The verification calls its independence "formal" because a governed lane exists.** The lane's entries appear to have been appended by the verifier itself, under a human instruction the verifier quotes; the independence policy is a placeholder. The verdict "VERIFIED" is stronger than what its probes show: they failed to falsify.
5. **The verifier's lane, and the CONTINUATION out of STOPPED, live only in a git-ignored runtime file.** The HANDOFF moved the producer's lane to HANDED_OFF, which settles §8.79's open branch for this case.
