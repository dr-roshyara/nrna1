## 8.97.1 In plain language

1. **The adviser says the research is done and the next step is to define the kernel's boundary, from nine responsibilities, by asking what must change together; the kernel is "not a generic" rules evaluator.** Within the book's record the method agrees. The book's kernel does evaluate fixed rules over one record, so the claim that it is not a rules evaluator holds only in the sense that its rules are specific and decided outside it. Of the nine responsibilities, the book has counterparts for some (one path for changes, recording, evidence entries, working out standing and the class) and none for others (justification, confidence, assigning identity).
2. **The prompt records a test: a subagent started from a session reports that session's identifier, so it is said to be the same session and cannot be an independent reviewer.** The book already holds that a session identifier is a label and that independence has several factors; a shared label shows one shared factor, enough to refute independence, but calling it the same session says more than the label shows.
3. **The candidate passes every listed bar and still declares its independence undetermined, because it had looked at the work item and advised on it before being asked, and no policy says whether that counts.** The book's result fits: on the record, independence can be refuted but never established; and "fresh" cannot mean knowing nothing of the estate. The book does not decide which reading the PO/ARB should take.
4. **Both records say a process may declare its own identity but never choose its own role, scope or authority.** Within the book that agrees: identity is self-declared and nothing attests it, and eligibility is read from the record.
5. **A later notice corrects the prompt's stated workflow state by adding to it, not rewriting it; and "verification ≠ adoption".** Both agree with the book: history is kept, and a check produces evidence, never standing.
