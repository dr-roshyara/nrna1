## 8.86.1 In plain language

1. **One file holds three texts: a review by one provider and two replies by another.** The replies agree with the review's direction and reject its final definition of the kernel. All three are advice. The review's seven bounded contexts are declined by the reply as not yet shown, which agrees with the book's way of admitting contexts. *(Restated at review: W-01.)*
2. **The review defines the kernel as a pure function from a command, a state, rules and evidence to a verdict.** Its counterpart in the book is the append guard, which judges a proposed entry by reading the evaluator; the review also contradicts its own purity by having the kernel record its adjudications. *(Restated at review: W-05.)*
3. **The review lets the kernel apply rules "as provided by the Governance Context"; the reply objects that the kernel would then accept whatever someone calls the constitution.** The reply's questions (who sets the rules, under whose authority) are the book's own open question F31; in its model the evaluator's rules are fixed by the Principal's decision. *(Restated at review: W-03.)*
4. **The reply warns that if one step judges a transition admissible and another applies it, the state can change in between.** The book has the same form; where it checks before an append, check and append are one serialised kernel step, and otherwise it records the act and evaluates it over the record afterwards. *(Restated at review: W-04.)*
5. **The review puts evidence admissibility outside the kernel; the later reply, citing the estate's F-1…F-5 results, puts evidence admission and the sufficiency of a justification inside.** On confidence the halves are compatible, by the review's own distinction between mechanism and epistemic confidence. *(Restated at review: W-02.)*
