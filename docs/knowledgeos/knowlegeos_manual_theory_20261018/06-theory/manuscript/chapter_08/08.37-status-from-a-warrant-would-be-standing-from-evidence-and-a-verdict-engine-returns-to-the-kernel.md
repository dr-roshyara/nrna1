## 8.37.1 In plain language

1. **One source says that a conclusion without a stated reason "remains a hypothesis".** In the book, standing comes from acts of authority, not from reasons. An adopted item with no evidence keeps its standing, and the display says "not attested". Making standing depend on the reason would let evidence decide standing, which the book forbids.
2. **A "reasoning verification engine" with five verdicts is proposed as a new kernel capability.** The sibling sources had just placed such checks outside the kernel. In the book they are applications. The kernel's answer is standing: T, F or U.
3. **"Relations are first-class, not attributes" and "a statement is limited by its context"** both agree with the book. The record is made of acts that cite and cover; every act has a scope; nothing stores a level of authority on evidence.
4. **"A challenge must not delete the challenged claim"** agrees: nothing is deleted, and conflicting claims both stay in force until the conflict is resolved where they are used.
5. **"Pseudo-evidence"** splits in two. Inside the record, a citation to an entry that does not exist cannot be entered. Whether an outside source is real is not checked by the kernel.
6. **The chat repeats its candidate wording from source to source, reverses a correction it had made ("knower and known cannot be separated"), and again chooses a technology ("knowledge graph").**

## 8.37.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Status from a warrant would be standing from evidence; the book refuses it (Src; Ded against §9.18; Int).**
- **S0223's H-KOS-Vyapti-001:** "KnowledgeOS SHALL preserve the warranting relation that connects evidence to conclusion. A conclusion without an explicit inference relation SHALL remain a hypothesis" (L69–71).
- **Ded:**
  - The first sentence asks to *record* a relation. The book can hold it as an evidence entry that cites what it supports (§9.18).
  - The second sentence makes a *status* depend on the presence of that relation.
  - **(i)** If "hypothesis" is a standing value, the rule computes standing from evidence. That breaks non-interference (§9.18 (c1), with (c2) proposed; §8.35 C2).
  - **(ii)** If it is a display beside standing, it fits promise (b): an item with no recorded evidence shows "not attested" (T10-01), whatever its standing.
- **Int:** the source does not say which. Only (ii) is consistent with the book.
- **Relay:** S0220 L288–290 has the same name, with only the first sentence; S0219 TARKA-002 asks that the relation be *established* (W's C43 inventory, S0220 item 7). One name carries three strengths in three sources.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. A verdict engine returns to the kernel (Src; Int).**
- **S0223:** "New Kernel Capability — Reasoning Verification Engine", with output "VALIDATED / QUESTIONABLE / CONFLICTED / INVALID / UNKNOWN" (L153–174). Its layers end in "Truth Determination" (L483–489).
- **The same line had placed this outside the kernel:**
  - S0218 (C): the kernel "Does not: … decide" (L779–784);
  - S0219: "Tarka as kernel primitive — Rejected" (L296);
  - S0220: "mostly in the reasoning layer, not the kernel identity layer" (L11).
- **S0222:** a "Reasoning Gate" leading to an "Accepted reasoning path" (L323–359).
- **The book:**
  - CE* answers standing (T, F, U), not truth (§9.10 Q1).
  - Assessment methods are applications (§10.2).
  - Acceptance is a recorded act by an authorised actor; a passing check is not one (§8.36 C1, as settled).
- **Int:** S0223 reverses its siblings without argument. The five verdicts mix a truth verdict (VALIDATED, INVALID) with the book's undetermined (UNKNOWN, CONFLICTED).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Relations and delimiters are already in the record (Src; Int).**
- **The sources:**
  - S0221's H-KOS-Relation-001: "SHALL treat epistemic relationships as first-class entities and SHALL NOT reduce them into attributes" (L121–123). Its "Wrong: Evidence { authority_level: high }" (L91–97).
  - H-KOS-Context-002, the avacchedaka: "SHALL preserve the delimiting conditions under which a proposition is valid"; "Context is not metadata. Context is a logical boundary" (L181–191).
- **The book:**
  - The record's entries are acts that cite earlier entries, cover scopes, and name holders (§9.13). These relations are the record.
  - No entry stores a level of authority. Standing is derived, and a stored derived value has no standing (CON-0141). *Agreement with "Wrong: authority_level".*
  - Every act carries a scope, and Q1 is asked per scope through covers (§9.13 Θ_f ω; §8.35 C4 (iii)). *Agreement: the book's delimiter of standing is the scope.*
- S0221's delimiter is wider (application layer, network layer, threat model, L157–175): it delimits a proposition's *content*. The book delimits *standing* by scope. Content delimiters belong to the item's content.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Challenge without deletion; a counterbalanced reason (Src; Int).**
- **The sources:**
  - S0223's H-KOS-Dialogue-001: claims "may be challenged without destroying knowledge identity" (L230–232); a "Conflict Space", not "A wins / B deleted" (L259–281).
  - S0221's H-KOS-Failure-001: "SHALL preserve reasoning failure modes … not discard failed reasoning attempts" (L380–382).
- **The book:** nothing is deleted. An annulled or revoked act stays recorded, and a removal keeps every field D requires (§9.13.2). A refutation is an evidence entry (§9.18). Conflicting adoptions of content both stay current, and the conflict is resolved at use (§9.13 Θ_f, C6 row; I-4); a conflict in the authority record gives U with its reason (§9.15 G6). *Agreement* *(corrected before review after C42 W-02)*.
- **Satpratipakṣa** (a reason counterbalanced by an equal reason) is "Equal opposing evidence" in S0221 (L362, L653), but "Ignoring equal evidence" in S0222 (L374). One term, two meanings, in sibling sources. The first fits T9-17's open "contested" class.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. Pseudo-evidence: inside the record and outside it (Src; Ded against §9.13.2; Int).**
- **S0223's H-KOS-EvidenceAuthenticity-001:** "distinguish evidence from pseudo-evidence by evaluating whether the evidence possesses the logical force required" (L342–344). Its example: "Evidence: Generated citation — No real source exists" (L318–326).
- **Ded:**
  - Inside the record, a citation must name an earlier entry (§9.13.2). A citation to an entry that does not exist cannot enter. *Prevention.*
  - That an outside source exists, or says what it is quoted as saying, is not checked by the kernel. The book's outside commitment covers loss of the record (§9.17, K1), not the truth of outside sources.
  - "Logical force" is content assessment: g0 has no strength (§9.18), and the class vocabulary is open (G7, §10.2).
- **Not a new concept.**
