## 8.96.1 In plain language

1. **The answer makes the "KnowledgeClaim" the aggregate root that owns the commitment, and says splitting it would make the duty unenforceable; the reply rejects this as not yet shown.** Within the book's record the reply is right: an authorized person's recorded act admits, and a duty is not a container.
2. **The answer gives identity as opaque, not derived from content, assigned at admission, and "not a relation".** As in the last window, that fits an item's governed identifier, not an entry's identity; "at admission" conflicts with the book, where an item has its identifier before adoption.
3. **The answer lists ten invariants.** Within the record some agree (one path for changes, unique identifiers, no identity or confidence from mechanisms, failed candidates leave no trace); some conflict (every admitted claim must have evidence; admission only if a contract is satisfied); some have no counterpart (justification, confidence). The last one, that failed candidates leave no trace, is exactly why the record cannot tell a refusal from no claim.
4. **It weighs four boundaries and calls an event stream per identity the absolute minimum.** Within the record the book has one stream across all items, because order across items matters; evidence need not be contained for standing, since standing needs no evidence; the checks happen when an entry is added.
5. **The reply asks whether evidence shared by many claims, and the "supports" link itself, can change on their own.** Within the record the link is exactly what the book records: an evidence entry about one item, which can be withdrawn, restored or annulled without touching the item's standing.
6. **The answer says the invariants are given from outside and the kernel only enforces them; the reply asks what an item with an identity and nothing else is.** Both have answers within the record: the rules are decided outside the kernel, and such an item is an item without standing, not a candidate; a refused candidate leaves nothing.
