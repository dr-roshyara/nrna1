## 8.38.1 In plain language

1. **"Knowledge is accepted or rejected by checking how reliable the producing process was."** Standing in the book does not work that way. It comes from recorded acts of authority. How reliable a process is belongs to the evidence and its assessment, and it is not yet classified in the book.
2. **One proposed "confidence state machine" puts four different things in one line:** what kind of source it was (observed or inferred), whether it was accepted, whether it is challenged, and a final verdict. The book keeps these apart.
3. **"Trust by default; investigate only on doubt."** In the book, an item that no act has recognised is not trusted at all. An item that has been adopted keeps its standing without any evidence; the display then says "not attested". So no review is needed to keep standing, but nothing is trusted without an act.
4. **"Never overwrite; keep the line of interpretation."** The book agrees that nothing is overwritten. But the book cannot yet record that one statement interprets, extends or replaces another.
5. **The word "authority" has three meanings in these two sources and the book:** the trustworthiness of a speaker, a legal capability to act, and the book's recorded standing. They must not be mixed.
6. **"A term means something only within its context"** agrees with the book, which has found the same problem over and over as homonyms.
7. **The sources write "ADR candidates", although their own line said not to; and they call KnowledgeOS an "Epistemic Operating System", which an earlier reply had denied.**

## 8.38.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Reliability of the producing process is not standing (Src; Ded against §9.18; Int).**
- **S0224:**
  - "Knowledge is the output of a reliable process" (L41–44).
  - Its checking chain is "Generate claim → Identify production process → Check process reliability → Accept / reject knowledge" (L151–168).
  - A "Knowledge Source Registry" gives reliability by kind. Testimony's reliability "depends_on: authority, trust_level" (L184–208).
- **S0225:** "Word + Reliable Source + Correct Understanding = Knowledge" (L158–166), with a record field "trust: Validation" (L192–193).
- **Ded:**
  - In the book, an item's standing is fixed by recorded acts of authority, evaluated by CE*. Acceptance is a recorded act by an authorised actor (§8.36 C1).
  - Process reliability is a property of *evidence*. An evidence entry changes no standing value (§9.18 (c1); (c2) proposed).
  - So "accept / reject by reliability" can be an actor's reason for an act. It cannot be the act.
- **Int:**
  - The book has no reliability scale: g0 has no strength, and the class vocabulary is open (G7, §10.2).
  - S0224's "testimony depends_on authority" turns governance standing into an input of *content reliability*. Evidence ≠ Authority is usually read the other way (evidence does not make authority), but the same separation forbids this direction too: being authorised does not make a statement more likely to be true.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. One state chain carries four axes (Src; Int).**
- **S0224's "Knowledge Confidence State Machine":** "OBSERVED → INFERRED → ACCEPTED → CHALLENGED → UNDER REVIEW → VALIDATED / REJECTED" (L318–340).
- **Its KnowledgeEvent record** has "validity: verified/rejected" (L103–120).
- **Int:** the chain mixes four things the book keeps apart:
  - **(a)** the kind of source (observed or inferred). For content this is an open gap (G7; §8.35 C3).
  - **(b)** acceptance, which is standing: T, F or U by CE*.
  - **(c)** challenge. A refutation is an evidence entry, shown in promise (b)'s display.
  - **(d)** a verdict (VALIDATED, REJECTED).
- **The verdict:** stored as a field, it is either a stored derived value, which has no standing (CON-0141), or a content assessment, which belongs to an application (§10.2).
- **The chain:** it is a lifecycle, and a lifecycle state is not authority (§8.32). It is the seventh lifecycle vocabulary in §8.35–§8.38.
- *Model integrity (an observation, not promoted):* the chain is **one overloaded dimension**. Each of (a)–(d) is a separate dimension, and the book has a separate home for each, or a recorded gap.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. Default trust against the closed world (Src; Ded against §8.33 C1).**
- **S0224:** "Normal: Trust → Action … When doubt appears: Doubt → Investigation → … Resolution" (L274–307). ADR-KOS-EPI-004: "Claims SHALL enter deeper verification workflows only when triggered by contradiction, uncertainty, or challenge" (L530–532).
- **Ded:**
  - **(i) For standing:** an item that no act recognises is F (decision C1; §8.33 C1). Nothing is trusted by default.
  - **(ii) For an adopted item:** standing needs no evidence. The display shows "not attested" (promise (b)). In this sense "not every fact needs review" agrees with the book.
  - **(iii) The trigger:** when to investigate is a workflow, an application concern (§10.2).
- **Int:** S0224 also checks reliability on every claim (C1), not only on doubt. The source does not reconcile the two (W's C45 inventory, S0224 item 8).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Lineage without overwrite; links between items have no home (Src; Int).**
- **S0224:** "Original Decision → Interpretation → Extension → Challenge → Resolution", "Git for institutional reasoning" (L412–440). ADR-KOS-EPI-005: "preserve interpretive lineage rather than replace historical reasoning states" (L538–540).
- **S0225:** "Meaning lineage" (L399–440); ADR-KOS-SEM-004: "Changes in terminology SHALL preserve historical meaning transformations" (L526–528).
- **The book:**
  - *Agreement on non-overwrite:* nothing is overwritten. Acts are appended, and an annulment voids from the start but stays recorded (§9.13.2).
  - *Gap:* the record admits no link between items ("link" is not admitted, CON-0188), and supersession is not admitted (Θ_f σ; §8.35 C1). That a statement interprets, extends or replaces another cannot be recorded in the book's record today. It is content, or it is missing.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. "Authority" has three meanings; three "kernels" and a "truth engine" (Src; Int).**
- **The three meanings:**
  - S0224: testimony's reliability "depends_on: authority" (L206). Here it means the **trustworthiness** of a source.
  - S0225: "Authority" defined in context as "Legal capability to perform action", implemented as "AuthorizationPolicy" and "CapabilityResolver" (L414–439). Here it means a **capability**.
  - The book: authority is **recognised standing** derived from recorded grants and adoptions (Q1). A capability check is a consumer of Q1, not authority itself (§10.2).
- **The three kernels:**
  - S0225 has "KnowledgeOS Core" hold a Semantic, an Epistemic and a Governance Kernel, above "Agent Execution" (L474–494).
  - The book places acting agents outside KnowledgeOS (§10.2).
  - Splitting evidence ("Epistemic") from authority ("Governance") has the shape of non-interference. Calling each a kernel widens "kernel" again (§8.35 C5).
- **The "truth engine":** S0225 says "The first book gives you the **truth engine**" (L583). The line had said "The kernel does not answer 'What is true?'" (S0216 L720–726). Q1 is "Not the truth of k's content" (§9.10 Q1).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C6. Meaning is bounded by context: agreement (Src; Int).**
- **S0225:** "What does this expression mean in this context?" (L215–221). Its example is "Release" with four meanings (L228–246), and the rule "Meaning requires: Term + Bounded Context + Usage" (L250–256). ADR-KOS-SEM-003: "Terms SHALL be interpreted within their bounded context" (L518–520). "A corrupted vocabulary creates corrupted knowledge" (L347).
- **The book:**
  - It records homonyms as findings in nearly every window: evidence, compute, mechanism, projection (§8.34 C2), validation (§8.36), KnowledgeOS (§8.35 C5) and authority (C5 here).
  - Its published language is split into the record's language and standing's language (§10.5.2 A/B).
  - *Agreement.* DDD's ubiquitous language is a benchmark, not the reason (T10-03).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C7. ADR candidates against the line's own method; the reversal of "not an EOS"; finality again (Src; Int).**
- **ADR candidates:**
  - S0224 proposes "ADR-KOS-EPI-001…005" (L498–540), and S0225 "ADR-KOS-SEM-001…004" (L498–528), all in "SHALL".
  - S0219, in the same line, said "Do not create ADRs" (L362).

- **Finality:** S0224's "almost a blueprint for the Epistemic Kernel" (L550) continues the finality claims of §8.35 C5, §8.36 C6 and §8.37 C6.
- **Analogy:** both sources drop analogy (upamāna) from the four reported sources without comment (S0224 L210–215 against L188–208; W's inventory, S0224 item 5).
- **Not a new concept.**
