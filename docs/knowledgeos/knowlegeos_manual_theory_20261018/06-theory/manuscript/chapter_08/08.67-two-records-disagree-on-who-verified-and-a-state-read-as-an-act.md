## 8.67.1 In plain language

1. **The two records disagree on who did what.**
   - The verification report gives its own process id as one value and names a second process as the producer.
   - The registration then names that second process as the verifier, and a third process as the producer.
   - The two records also use different kinds of identifier (a full session id and a short label).
   - The registering process says its own id was "in genuine doubt" after a resume, and settled it by reading its transcript.

   On the record, it cannot be established that the producer and the verifier were different processes. On S0315's own reading it is refuted. This is the book's rule: independence can be refuted from the record, never established from it.
2. **The resolver reported that a human had started a lane whenever the lane's state was anything but "created".** A lane cancelled straight from "created" was reported as started, though no start was ever recorded. That reads an act off a state. The book holds that a state is not an act, and that an act is known only from its recorded entry.
3. **When the resolver answered AMBIGUOUS, it listed 62 candidates as its reason, while only 2 actually matched.** The book counts AMBIGUOUS as a success, but only with its correct reason. A second defect: one advisory field changed depending on how the same lane was asked for, so the same question received two answers.
4. **The verification supplies two premises that §8.66 C5 found unstated:** the resolver reads no model setting, and its output is deterministic. That makes the byte-identity across providers a property of the code. The registration then says this "supports" the hypothesis that the original problem was legibility, not the models. A property that holds by construction cannot support a claim about how the two providers behaved.
5. **The work item that built the self-governing workflow engine has no record in that engine.** Governance declines to create one, because creating it would decide the open question. It also notes that a wrong key on creation forks the record silently. In the book's terms, the record cannot show this work's standing, and a silent fork is two records where there should be one.

## 8.67.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Two records disagree on who produced and who verified (Src; Int against §8.06 C3).**
- **S0315:**
  - "| Verifying process (`CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`) | `d1612e03-7df3-4969-9e7e-881ccbed5641` | · | Producing process (per boundary proposal + registry notes) | `claude-code-session:8a525719` | · | Producer ≠ verifier | ✅ **satisfied** — different process |" (L16–18);
  - "**Independence gate — result: PARTIAL, declared honestly rather than assumed.**" (L22);
  - "The *substantive* independence requirement (the producer must not verify its own work) **is** satisfied; the *formal* one (a REGISTERed verification lane) **is not available to satisfy**" (L29).
- **S0316:**
  - "| implementation / diagnostic-and-activation registration | **`a8ce5a39`** | · | independent verification | **`8a525719`** | · | this recording | **`b64828fe`** (Governance) |" (L16–20);
  - "**Verification artifact:** … verifier `claude-code-session:8a525719`" (L5);
  - "⚠️ **Identity was in genuine doubt and was resolved mechanically before acting.** This session's system-prompt scratchpad reported `a8ce5a39` after a resume, while its transcript reported `b64828fe`. **Discriminated by transcript content and recency** …" (L22).
- **S0314** (§8.66): the "running processes were **`8a525719` / `c11a5a12`**" (L20).
- **Int:**
  - **The disagreement.** S0315 names `8a525719` the **producer** and gives its own id as `d1612e03-…`. S0316 names `8a525719` the **verifier** and `a8ce5a39` the producer. The two records do not use the same kind of identifier: a full session id (S0315 L16) and short labels (S0316). The estate shows no mapping between the two kinds.
  - **Three readings:**
    - (a) S0316 attributes the verification to the producer's label. Then S0316's map is wrong.
    - (b) S0315's "per boundary proposal + registry notes" producer is wrong. S0314 names `8a525719` a running process during the diagnosis, with no role.
    - (c) The two kinds of identifier name the same sessions in a way the record does not show.

    The window does not decide which.
  - **Against §8.06 C3.** On the record, independence can be refuted and not established (CON-0127). Under S0315's own map with S0316's verifier, producer and verifier are one label, so independence is **refuted**. Under S0316's map alone it is not refuted. *In no reading is independence established.* S0315's "satisfied — different process" (L18) rests on a different identifier, and §8.06 C1 holds that a new identifier is not a factor of independence.
  - **Labels drift on resume.** S0316 reports that its own label changed between the scratchpad and the transcript after a resume (L22). That is a mechanism by which a self-declared label can misattribute work. It *agrees* with INV-ATTR-1/2 ("self-declared, not attestable"; §1.14.2) and with treating attribution as evidence only (§8.66 C5). S0316 settles its own identity by reading its transcript, which is the producer's record of itself (§8.50 C3's pattern).
  - **Substantive against formal independence** (S0315 L29). The verifier holds "no registered governed lane, therefore **no workflow-derived authority**" and calls its review "**advisory technical evidence only**". *Agreement* with A1: evidence is not an act (O2), and authority needs a recorded grant.
- **Not a new concept.** *Recorded as one instance under CON-0127.*

**C2. A state read as an act (Src; Ded; Int, agreement).**
- **S0315:**
  - "**Finding V-1 (F14).** `recorded_human_start_act` is **inferred** as `state !== 'CREATED'` (l.360). AST-015 accepts `CANCEL` directly from `CREATED`. … REGISTER S-c → CANCEL S-c (no START ever recorded) → workflow_state = 'CANCELLED' → recorded_human_start_act = true ← FALSE STATEMENT ABOUT THE RECORD" (L148–154);
  - "The registry's own V-3 text asserts truthfulness \"by entailment (CREATED implies no START)\". That entailment holds in the **forward** direction only; the implementation relies on the **reverse** (`not CREATED ⇒ START recorded`), which this probe falsifies." (L156).
- **Ded:** "CREATED ⇒ no START" does not imply "not CREATED ⇒ START". That would be the converse, and one counterexample, the probe, refutes it. *The source's own derivation is correct.*
- **Int:** the field reports an act ("recorded human start act") by reading a state. In the book, a state is not authority (§1.14), and an act is known from its recorded entry (A1; Q2 returns the entries). States are derived from entries (§9.8). Reading entries back from states runs the derivation the wrong way and fails where two entry sequences reach one state. *Agreement*, with a corpus instance of the failure. It "fails safe" (L156) because authorization also requires `ACTIVE`, but the field is still false about the record.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. AMBIGUOUS, a success, with a reason that misstates its set; an answer that depends on how it is asked (Src; Int).**
- **S0315:**
  - "`meta.disambiguation_required` renders `describeLanes($candidates)` — **all** candidates, not the **matching** set. Live: it listed **62** lanes when **2** matched. S-4 cannot catch this: its fixture contains exactly two lanes total, so \"all\" and \"matching\" coincide." (L138);
  - "**V-2** … `recommended_next_actor` is not stable across argument sets selecting the same lane: a HANDED_OFF lane yields `verification` (successor role) without `--session`, `po/arb` **with** `--session`" (L265);
  - "**Live `a8ce5a39` case — the plan's prediction was wrong and the resolver was right.** … **This is a correct fail-closed result, not a defect.**" (L127–136).
- **S0316:** "> ### ⛔ **The `a8ce5a39 → AMBIGUOUS` behaviour MUST NOT CHANGE. It is a success of the mechanism, not a defect.**" (L47).
- **Int:**
  - **The verdict is right; its reason is wrong.** AMBIGUOUS is a success (§1.14.2-5; §8.66 C4). §9.10.2 requires an E6 answer to "carry its recorded bound or reason". Here the reason lists the wrong set, 62 where 2 matched. *A U-type answer with a misstated reason fails the reason clause while passing the verdict.* The test's blind spot, a fixture where the two sets coincide, is a degenerate case of the kind §9.19 T7 warns about (a test whose input cannot tell the cases apart).
  - **One question, two answers (V-2).** The same lane, asked for with and without `--session`, gets two different next actors. Two readings:
    - (a) these are two calls, since the arguments differ, so §9.10 Q1's "the same call, the same answer" is not breached;
    - (b) the question "who acts next on lane L" is one question, and K5 (one answer per item, scope and time, whoever asks; §9.7) is breached.

    The source treats it as a defect (L265), which fits (b).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. The premises supplied by a report; a hypothesis "supported" by a property that holds by construction (Src; Int).**
- **S0315:**
  - "**Structural:** the asset reads **no** provider or model variable. Exhaustive scan for `ANTHROPIC*`, `MODEL`, `BASE_URL`, `API_KEY`, `$_ENV`, `$_SERVER` returns **only** `KOS_MECHANISM_PATH` and `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`. Provider-independence is a property of the code, not a property of a passing test." (L174);
  - "Ordering is deterministic by construction (`sort($workItems)` after `glob`). No timestamps, no randomness, no unordered traversal in the output path." (L192);
  - "**Live (F10)** … → **byte-identical, 6829 bytes**" (L176);
  - "The original defect — two providers diverging inside one harness — cannot be reproduced through this asset." (L178).
- **S0316:** "⭐ **The architectural hypothesis is therefore supported: the problem was coordination / session-state legibility, not that DeepSeek or Claude inherently cannot resolve the workflow.**" (L33).
- **Int:**
  - **§8.66 C5's premises (2) and (3) are now stated:** no model or endpoint variable is read (L174), and the output is deterministic (L192). They are stated **in a report**: the scan and the runs are described, not transcribed, so they are graded Src (§3.9 C6). *R3 note for §8.66 C5: premises (2) and (3) are reported by S0315, Src.* With (1)–(3), byte-identity across providers holds by construction, as S0315 itself says ("a property of the code").
  - **"Supported" (S0316 L33).** If byte-identity holds by construction, it would hold whatever the providers do. So it cannot support a claim about the providers ("not that DeepSeek or Claude inherently cannot resolve"). S0315's own sentence is narrower and correct: the divergence "cannot be reproduced **through this asset**" (L178). *The registration reads a property of the code as evidence about the models.* It is the same step §8.66 C5 found in S0314 L9, now made by a different process.
  - **Re-execution.** "Every green result below was independently re-executed" (L48), and the producer's counts were "confirmed exactly" (L243). This is a report of independent runs, still without transcripts (Src). Under §8.06 C2, agreement between two runs shows no disagreement, not correctness. *This is stronger than the producer's self-report (§8.66 C5) but of the same evidential class.*
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. The engine that governs itself has no record of the work that builds it; a silent fork on `init` (Src; Int).**
- **S0315:** "**V-8** … `KOS-SESSION-BOOTSTRAP-001` has **no authoritative workflow record** — no lane, no REGISTER, no G-3 gates for the work item that built the lane resolver." (L271); "V-8 is a governance question about whether meta-work on the workflow engine must itself be recorded in it." (L273).
- **S0316:**
  - "> ⭐ **The observation stated plainly: the workflow engine is being used to govern itself, while the bootstrap work item that implements that self-governance is not registered in it.**" (L55);
  - "**Governance has NOT created one either**, deliberately: initialising a work-item record decides V-8, and per `C-9` an `init` with a wrong key forks silently while `append`/`grant` merely refuse. **⇒ V-8 requires a PO/ARB decision**" (L59);
  - "**The verification artifact is UNTRACKED in git.** … **Governance does not commit another process's artifact.**" (L76–78).
- **Int:**
  - **No record, no standing in the record.** The activation act (S0314 L66–70) exists only as a registration in prose. In the engine's own record the work item has no entry, so within that record its standing cannot be shown: under decision C1, within the kernel, F ("never adopted"; §1.17 E4). Outside the kernel, C1-a against C1-b is open (§1.13 L134). *An instance; nothing decided.*
  - **Declining to act where the act would decide** (L59). Governance does not create the record, because creating it would answer the question reserved for the PO/ARB. *Agreement* with the role discipline: a recorder does not decide (§1.14.3).
  - **A silent fork on `init`** (L59). Creating a record with a wrong key makes a second record without refusal, while appends and grants refuse. In the book this is §9.17's case of two records where one is designated. K2-d's presupposition (one designated record) is broken silently, and readers may resolve different records. *A corpus instance of §9.17's hazard; recorded.*
  - **Durability, the fifth occurrence** (L74–78). The artifact that returned the implementation exists only in the working tree. This continues §8.62 C4's open observation, which the record now counts as the fifth occurrence. "Governance does not commit another process's artifact" treats the commit as an act of authorship. *Recorded.*
- **Not a new concept.**
