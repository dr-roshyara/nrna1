## 8.92.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer says identity, evidence, justification, state, confidence and history define each other and so must sit in one aggregate that changes as a whole; the reply answers that being related does not mean having to change together.** Within the book's record the reply is right: state and class are worked out each time and cannot drift, a claim without evidence is allowed and shown as not attested, and the boundaries the book does need join a check to the entry it guards.
2. **The first answer also says events come first and states are worked out from them; the reply separates recording events from storing everything as events.** That is the book's form within the record: the recorded acts are the record, and what is shown is derived. The book does not choose how the record is stored.
3. **The first answer has the kernel keep supersession, contest and reconciliation as relations and keep assessments of evidence, but in one table says the kernel should not derive states and in another that it should.** Within the record the kernel itself derives standing and the class; an assessment kept as a stored value would be exactly the kind of copy that goes stale.
4. **The first answer makes identity unchangeable, assigned only at admission, and also says a claim cannot be identified without its evidence.** The two disagree. For recorded entries, the book takes identity from the entry's content, never from its standing or evidence; for an item across versions, identity is open.
5. **The first answer lets evidence be invalidated and restored, moving a claim to "insufficient evidence" and back.** The book has kinds for withdrawing and restoring reliance on evidence; they change the class shown for an item, never its standing, and there is no threshold.
6. **The first answer, citing the incompleteness theorem, says the kernel cannot establish its own correctness from inside.** In that reading the book agrees, for reasons of its own; the theorem is an analogy, not a proof.
