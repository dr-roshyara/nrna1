## 8.92.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer says identity, evidence, justification, state, confidence and history define each other and so must sit in one aggregate that changes as a whole; the reply answers that being related does not mean having to change together.** Within the book's record the reply is right: state and class are worked out each time and are not part of the state the theory keeps, a claim without evidence is allowed and shown as not attested, and the boundaries the book does need join a check to the entry it guards.
2. **The first answer also says events come first and states are worked out from them; the reply separates recording events from storing everything as events.** That is the book's form within the record: its entries (acts and evidence entries) are the record, kept in one order, and what is shown is derived; the theory's state has the shape of an event log. How it is stored is an engineering choice the book does not make. *(Restated at review: W-10, W-11)*
3. **The first answer has the kernel keep supersession, contest and reconciliation as relations and keep assessments of evidence, but in one table says the kernel should not derive states and in another that it should.** Within the record the kernel itself derives standing and the class; an assessment kept as a stored value would be exactly the kind of copy that goes stale.
4. **The first answer makes identity unchangeable, assigned only at admission, and also says a claim cannot be identified without its evidence.** The two disagree. Within the record, an entry's identity comes from its content, never from its standing or evidence; an item can carry its identifier and entries before any adoption, so admission does not give it identity; for an item across versions, identity is open. *(Restated at review: W-02, W-05)*
5. **The first answer lets evidence be invalidated and restored, moving a claim to "insufficient evidence" and back.** Within the record the book has three nearer things: annulling a mis-recorded evidence entry, withdrawing reliance on it for a purpose, and recording evidence that refutes it. They change the class shown for an item, never its standing, and there is no threshold. *(Restated at review: W-03, W-05)*
6. **The first answer, citing the incompleteness theorem, says the kernel cannot establish its own correctness from inside.** In that reading the book agrees within the record, for reasons of its own; the theorem is an analogy, not a proof. *(Restated at review: W-05)*
