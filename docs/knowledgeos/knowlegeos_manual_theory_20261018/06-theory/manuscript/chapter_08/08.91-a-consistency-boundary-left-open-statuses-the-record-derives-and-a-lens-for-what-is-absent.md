## 8.91.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer asks whether identity, evidence, justification, state, confidence and history must all change together, and leaves it unresolved; the reply asks which rule would break if they changed separately.** Within the book's record, what is shown is worked out from the record each time, so it cannot fall out of step with the record (given the unrecorded inputs), and history is the record itself. The boundaries the book does need are of another kind: a check and the entry it guards must happen in one step, one per grant for single use and one for designation. *(Restated at review: W-02, W-08)*
2. **The first answer asks whether "superseded", "reconciled", "contested" and "insufficient evidence" are states, relations, events or derived values; the reply says a relation, an event and a status can all exist together.** Within the record that is the book's form: an entry is the event, the entries it cites are its relations, and the status is worked out from them. Revocation is the nearest instance of the reply's supersession sketch; the new claim that supersedes the old has no counterpart, and for supersession and reconciliation the book has no kind of entry yet. *(Restated at review: W-07)*
3. **The reply asks first what a "knowledge claim" is, and draws it as one object with identity, evidence, justification, status, confidence and history.** Within the record, if "history" means something other than the entries, the book has shown that such an object cannot be the state; if it means the entries, the object is the record sorted by item plus stored values, which go stale and lose the order across items. What a claim is, as an item, the book leaves open. *(Restated at review: W-01)*
4. **The reply separates owning evidence, referring to it, its provenance and acquiring it.** Within the record the kernel holds a named producer's recorded claim that evidence supports or refutes an item; it does not acquire the evidence, vouch for its truth or test whether it is enough.
5. **The first answer says the kernel cannot check its own invariants from inside, citing the incompleteness theorem.** Read as saying that the kernel cannot check the record, that is wrong within the book: the kernel checks the record's rules at each append. Read as saying that it cannot vouch for its own correctness, the book agrees: that rests on assumptions met by testing, replay or outside evidence, and on whether recorded acts really happened. The theorem is an analogy, not a proof. *(Restated at review: W-03)*
6. **The last reply keeps a "Zero" lens that asks what is missing, and lists six things not to confuse.** Within the record, a candidate refused at the gate leaves no trace, so it cannot be told apart from no claim at all: the collapse the lens warns about. The book does separate a case that cannot be determined (a refusal, with a reason) from an unknown (a value that names its reason). It has no counterpart for abstention or for insufficient evidence as a threshold. *(Restated at review: W-04, W-05, W-06)*
