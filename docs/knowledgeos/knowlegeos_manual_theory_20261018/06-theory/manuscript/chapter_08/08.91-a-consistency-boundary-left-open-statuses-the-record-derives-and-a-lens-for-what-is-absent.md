## 8.91.1 In plain language

1. **The first answer asks whether identity, evidence, justification, state, confidence and history must all change together, and leaves it unresolved; the reply asks which rule would break if they changed separately.** Within the book's record the reply's question has an answer: what is shown is worked out from the record each time, so it cannot fall out of step, and history is the record itself. What is left is which entries must be recorded together, and that the book has not fixed.
2. **The first answer asks whether "superseded", "reconciled", "contested" and "insufficient evidence" are states, relations, events or derived values; the reply says a relation, an event and a status can all exist together.** Within the record that is the book's form: an entry is the event, the entries it cites are its relations, and the status is worked out from them. For supersession and reconciliation the book has no kind of entry yet.
3. **The reply asks first what a "knowledge claim" is, and draws it as one object with identity, evidence, justification, status, confidence and history.** The book has just shown that such a table per item cannot be the state: the kernel's fields sit on entries, and an item's attributes are worked out from them or still open. What a claim is, as an item, the book leaves open.
4. **The reply separates owning evidence, referring to it, its provenance and acquiring it.** Within the record the kernel holds a named producer's recorded claim that evidence supports or refutes an item; it does not acquire the evidence, attest its truth or test whether it is enough.
5. **The first answer says the kernel cannot check its own invariants from inside, citing the incompleteness theorem.** The kernel checks the record's rules each time an entry is added; what it cannot establish is whether a recorded act really happened. The theorem is an analogy here, not a proof.
6. **The last reply keeps a "Zero" lens that asks what is missing, and lists six things not to confuse: no claim, unknown, insufficient evidence, abstention, rejected, withdrawn.** The book keeps some of them apart, and some only in an item's list of entries, not in its standing. It has no counterpart for abstention or for insufficient evidence as a threshold.
