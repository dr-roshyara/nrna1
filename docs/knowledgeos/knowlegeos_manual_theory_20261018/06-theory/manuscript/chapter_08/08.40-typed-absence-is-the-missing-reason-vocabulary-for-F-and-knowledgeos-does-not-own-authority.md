## 8.40.1 In plain language

1. **S0231 sorts absence into kinds:** something never existed, something existed and was destroyed, something is impossible, something is different from something else, or nobody knows. The book's "F" (not current authority) lumps several of these together: no act ever recognised the item; it was revoked or its validity ended; it was annulled, which voids it from the start. The book has not yet said which reasons F should give. S0231's kinds are a ready list for that gap, and for the naming question already put to the Principal.
2. **"I found no evidence" is not "the evidence proves it does not exist."** The book agrees: "not attested" means "not attested in the record at this cut", with no claim about the world. For authority the book is stricter: an item no act recognises is "not recognised", not "unknown".
3. **One source says KnowledgeOS "owns lifecycle, validation and authority."** In the book, KnowledgeOS records acts of authority and never grants authority. Authority belongs to whoever performs the act.
4. **"Knowledge without evidence is only intention."** In the book, an adopted item keeps its standing without any evidence; the display then says "not attested". Standing and well-foundedness are kept apart.
5. **The sources describe pipelines:** from purpose to reality; from thought through an evidence check to trusted knowledge; a "purification" that retires outdated knowledge. These are applications. In the book, a check does not make knowledge trusted, and "retirement" is a kind of act the record does not yet admit.
6. **S0231 says absence is "not a seventh dimension", only a different value.** That is the right kind of move: a new value, not a new dimension. A removal in the book leaves a trace, which is S0231's "destroyed, with provenance".

## 8.40.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Typed absence is the missing reason vocabulary for Q1's F (Src; Ded against §9.13; Int).**
- **S0231:** absence is to be typed: "it never existed? it existed but was deleted? it exists somewhere else? it is forbidden? it is unknown? it is impossible?" (L49–62). The Nyāya kinds: prior absence, "Customer never existed" (L100–134); destroyed absence, with "cause: deletion_event" and "provenance: audit_record" (L138–165); absolute absence as a constraint (L169–188); difference (L192–208). H-KOS-Abhāva-001: absence "SHALL carry identity, context, relation, temporal scope, and provenance" (L815–817).
- **Ded, against the book:**
  - Q1's F reads "refuted or not recognized" (§9.13; T10-21 (1)). On D it arises in at least these ways:
    - **(a) never recognised:** no act adopts the item (closed world, decision C1): S0231's prior absence;
    - **(b) ceased:** an adoption revoked, a cited grant ended, or a validity period closed (§9.13 Θ_f P): destroyed absence;
    - **(c) void from the start:** the adopting act annulled (§9.13.2): not "ceased" but "never was", an absence with an act that explains it.
    - *(A record outside D is refused, not F; a tie or a negative cycle gives U, not F.)*
  - The book requires reasons for T and F but has not defined them (§9.13 L166). (a)–(c) are three different reasons with three different acts (none; a revocation or ending; an annulment).
- **Int:** S0231's kinds give a ready vocabulary for that gap: F with a reason "never recognised", "ceased (revoked / ended at …)", or "voided (annulled by …)". It also bears on T10-21 (1), Q1's F beside the evidence class "refuted": the reasons would say which F it is. *Recorded as input to the reasons gap and to T10-21; nothing is decided here.* S0231's "forbidden" and "impossible" are constraints (C6), and "different" is content (DDD), not reasons for F.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. No evidence found is not proof of absence (Src; Ded against §9.18; Int).**
- **S0231:** LLMs confuse "I found no evidence" with "Evidence proves non-existence"; KnowledgeOS must separate "Unknown ≠ Absent ≠ False" (L363–389). A search with no result gives "Feature Y status: UNKNOWN, because: absence of evidence" (L415–427).
- **The book:**
  - **Evidence axis:** "not attested" is read and shown as "not attested in the record at this cut"; no completeness rule is needed, and "no evidence exists anywhere" is a world claim, never part of a value (§9.18.5 (v)). *Agreement.* Under g1, U (a possibly-live entry), "not attested" and "refuted" are three values (§9.18.5 g1): S0231's three-way separation.
  - **Authority axis:** an item no act recognises is F, "not recognized", not unknown (decision C1; §8.33 C1). S0231's "UNKNOWN because absence of evidence" is the open-world reading the book rejects for authority.
- **Int:** per axis again (§8.36 C4 (iii)): S0231 is right for evidence and for content, and differs from the book on authority.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. KnowledgeOS records authority and does not own it (Src; Ded against A1 and ARCH-INV-1).**
- **S0229:** "Term: Knowledge Sovereignty — Definition: KnowledgeOS owns lifecycle, validation and authority" (L200–212).
- **S0230:** "Liberation → Autonomous Knowledge Governance" (L44); Level 5, "Knowledge Sovereignty: trusted knowledge base, deterministic decisions, governance" (L255–265).
- **Ded:**
  - The mechanism records authority and never grants it (ARCH-INV-1, §1.14.2-1; a relayed ruling, one line of support). The act is the authoriser's, and the recorder decides nothing (§10.4 R1; §1.14.3). Only an act confers standing (A1).
  - So "KnowledgeOS owns authority" contradicts the book. Read as "KnowledgeOS owns the record of authority", it agrees: the record element owns record-keeping (T10-16 (b)).
  - "Autonomous governance" without a person's act is against the book's norm that acceptance is a human act (§1.13.1; §8.36 C1).
- **Int:** S0230's own image points the other way: the witness "that sees but does not act" (L60–65), which is close to the roles law. Benchmark only.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C4. Standing without evidence is not "only intention" (Src; Ded against promise (b)).**
- **S0229's KOS-CH1-001:** "Knowledge without evidence is only intention" (L453–457).
- **S0230:** three states, "Valid Knowledge: Evidence → Observation → Validated Knowledge", "False Knowledge" and "Uncertain Knowledge" (L109–161).
- **Ded:** an adopted item without evidence keeps its standing (T); the display shows "not attested" beside it (promise (b), T10-01). Standing and well-foundedness are two answers, shown together, never merged. S0230's "valid = evidenced" merges them.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C5. The pipelines are applications; a check does not make knowledge trusted; retirement has no home (Src; Int).**
- **The pipelines:**
  - S0229's seven layers, purpose → vision → language → trust → execution → feedback → reality, with the law "Knowledge degradation happens when a transformation layer is skipped" (L548–550).
  - S0230's "Agent Thought → Evidence Check → Trusted Knowledge" (L536–546), and its "Knowledge Purification Process": detect outdated knowledge → challenge → validate against reality → retire obsolete knowledge (L411–429).
- **The book:**
  - S0229's law has the shape of §1.2's rule: no lifecycle edge from *created* to *authoritative* without a recorded act. Its layers, though, are an engineering delivery workflow, an application's.
  - S0230's evidence check before "Trusted Knowledge" has no act: a passing check is not acceptance (§8.36 C1; §8.38 C8).
  - **Retirement** is one of O1's kinds that D does **not** admit (registration, link, retirement, establishment, declassification; CON-0188; §9.13.2). The book's ways for an item to lose standing are revocation, ending and annulment. "Retire obsolete knowledge" has no home as a kind; as supersession it is not admitted either (Θ_f σ).
  - S0230's KnowledgeClaim has "type: observed | inferred | assumed" (L169–186): the source-kind gap recorded with G7's row (§10.2), with a new value "assumed".
- **Not a new concept.**

## 8.40.4 Challenges recorded

- **C1, rival:** S0231's kinds are kinds of non-existence of things, not reasons for an authority value.
  - *Answer:* the mapping is by structure, not by subject: no recognising act, an act that ended it, an act that voided it. The book's acts already distinguish them; only the reason vocabulary is missing.
- **C3, rival:** "owns authority" means owns the authority *record*.
  - *Answer:* then it agrees with T10-16 (b); the source's definition says "owns … authority", and the book records that difference.
- **C5, rival:** "retire" is only a revocation by another name.
  - *Answer:* possibly; the book's O1 lists retirement as a kind of its own, not admitted, so whether retirement reduces to revocation is open.
