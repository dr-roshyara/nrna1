## 8.32.1 In plain language

1. **EKS says it cannot answer two things:** whether the grant's holder is the right one, and whether an act falls under a grant's scope. The second is exactly an input of the book's evaluator. The first is not: EKS's "holder" is a work lane, not a person, and its grants lack the holder field the book requires, so the book would refuse such a record.
2. **When EKS separates "evidence" from "authority", its "evidence" is the log of lifecycle steps.** The book draws that line too, as state ≠ authority, not as evidence ≠ authority. So it is not evidence for the book's separation of evidence from standing.
3. **EKS's guards keep its own record well-formed and prevent nothing in the world.** The book draws the same line: its guards prevent a second recorded use; a second actual use is prevented only if work waits for the record.
4. **The growth from 216 to 218 transitions is plausible, but nothing records it, and losses cannot be excluded.** §8.31's "one record at later cuts" is too strong.
5. **The estate's mathematics proposals agree with the book more than they first seem to.** The authority step is an act in both; a jump is bad when it has no recorded history in both. What remains in conflict is only the idea that evidence moves knowledge forward.
6. **"Expression is not meaning"** agrees with the book. As a kernel law, preserving meaning is outside the book's kernel, which never judges content; that is a matter for evidence.
