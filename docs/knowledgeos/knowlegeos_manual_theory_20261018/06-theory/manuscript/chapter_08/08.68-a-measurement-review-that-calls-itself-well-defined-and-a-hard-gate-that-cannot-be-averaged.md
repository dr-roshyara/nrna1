## 8.68.1 In plain language

1. **A review signed as a "Senior Mathematician" says its metrics are "now well-defined, measurable, and have known statistical properties".** It leaves the distance between representations as a choice ("could be"). It also asserts that one score is a consistent estimator, without saying what is sampled. Its final decision rule has overlapping cases, so one input can be both ACCEPT and REJECTED. Its status rule never looks at whether distinct meanings stayed distinct. The file contains the review twice, word for word.
2. **The next answer corrects it on the book's lines.** A distance between graphs is structural, not semantic: equal structures do not decide equal meaning. Entropy over the model's own candidate readings measures the model's uncertainty, not the sentence's. Three uncertainties are kept apart: which reading, whether the representation captures the meaning, and whether the claim is justified.
3. **The same answer makes "distinct meanings stay distinct" a hard gate that no average can hide.** It holds that merging two different meanings is worse than splitting one meaning in two. This matches the book: what blocks cannot be summed away with what does not, and a false "yes" is worse than an honest "unknown". The answer also calls its thresholds experimental, to be calibrated, not justified.
4. **The later answers report runs that confirm nothing about the method.**
   - The first pilot "detected" collisions that it had itself injected, so the detection holds by construction.
   - Two later runs returned no output. The answers correctly refuse to invent numbers, but they still say the runs "established" or "confirm" the evaluation structure. A run without output confirms nothing. The structure rests on the design argument made before the runs.
5. **Zero is again given an operational form:** keep the unresolved state rather than invent a canonical identity. The answers count "false certainty" (an UNKNOWN made confident) as a failure class. Both agree with the book's reading of U as an honest answer with its reason.

## 8.68.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A review that calls itself well defined; a decision rule that is not a function (Src; Ded; Int).**
- **S0317 (L2–360, repeated at L362–720):**
  - "**Reviewer:** Senior Mathematician — Formal Systems & Information Theory" (L6);
  - "**Implementation:** For SNF as a graph (URDNA2015), this could be: d(G₁, G₂) = 1 − |E(G₁) ∩ E(G₂)| / |E(G₁) ∪ E(G₂)| · Or more richly: weighted edit distance with semantic weights." (L70–76);
  - "**Statistical Property:** For sufficiently large m, this is a consistent estimator of semantic invariance." (L132);
  - the decision criteria: "ACCEPT if C_snf > 0.9 and H_sem < 0.2 · TENTATIVE if C_snf > 0.8 and H_sem < 0.4 · UNKNOWN if H_sem > 0.5 or C_snf < 0.6 · REJECTED if NC_snf < 0.5 or T_snf < 0.5" (L328–336);
  - the status rule: "CONFIDENT → ACCEPT · CONVERGED → TENTATIVE · RESOLVED → UNCERTAIN · otherwise → UNKNOWN" (L595–599);
  - "**Key Improvement:** All metrics are now well-defined, measurable, and have known statistical properties." (L358).
- **Ded:**
  - **Not a function.** C_snf = 0.95, H_sem = 0.1 and NC_snf = 0.3 satisfy both "ACCEPT" and "REJECTED" (L331, L334). The rule gives no precedence. Some inputs, such as C_snf = 0.7 with H_sem = 0.45, NC_snf and T_snf ≥ 0.5, meet no case. *The rule is neither total nor single-valued.*
  - **The status rule ignores non-collapse.** "DISTINCT" (NC_snf > 0.8) and "STABLE" are computed (L589–591), but the result mapping (L595–599) reads only CONFIDENT, CONVERGED and RESOLVED. So a status of ACCEPT is reachable with NC_snf = 0, collapsed meanings.
  - **The units of H.** H_sem ∈ [0, log₂ n] (L465) is compared with 0.2 and 0.5 (L332–336) and enters "(1 − H_sem)⁻¹" (L574). Unless the normalized form is meant, the term can be negative, or undefined at H = 1. The text does not say which form is meant.
- **Int:**
  - **"Well-defined."** Taking the claim at the level of the review's own choices, every metric depends on d, which the review leaves open ("could be … Or more richly"). The metrics are defined once d is chosen. "Consistent estimator" (L132; L647–651) needs a sampling model: of what population are the m expressions a sample, and what is "the true semantic invariance"? None is stated. *The claim exceeds what is shown, as with §8.65 C1's projections. The review is a specification, never evidence that anything ran (§3.9 C6).*
  - **Against the book's evaluator.** The book's CE* is total on D, with every T and F forced (§9.13). A decision rule with overlapping and missing cases fails exactly what that construction secures.
  - **The role label.** "Senior Mathematician" is a role assigned in a generated text, not a person and not a grant. *Recorded.*
  - **The duplicate.** L2–360 and L362–720 are identical (a line comparison). For any count of support the review counts once (§8.33).
- **Not a new concept.**
