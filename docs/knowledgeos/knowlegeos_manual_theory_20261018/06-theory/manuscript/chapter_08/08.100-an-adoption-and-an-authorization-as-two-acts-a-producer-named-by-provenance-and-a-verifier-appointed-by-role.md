## 8.100.1 In plain language

1. **Adopting and authorizing were two decisions, recorded separately.** The PO/ARB first adopted the three verified layers and, later, authorized them for future use. Within the estate the two records keep these apart. Within the book's record, adoption has a kind (`adopt`); "authorized for future use" of a model has none, and the estate records it only in a document, so nothing in a record would answer "is this authorized?" — as the source itself says.
2. **Within the estate, the producer of the held capability is now named, by someone else, from transcripts.** The book already has this method (a write in exactly one session's transcript, matched to the commit). What is new is that the producer never confirmed it. The bar can now be checked against the recorded name; the grounds of the name lie outside the record. "If wrong, it only excludes an innocent session" is too strong: a wrong name also leaves the true producer off the bar.
3. **Within the estate, sixteen identities are barred, and only a new session can still verify.** The book has seen this pool run out before. The list can exclude; it cannot show independence.
4. **Within the estate, the verifier is told to declare before reading anything, and the appointment names a role, not a process.** Ordering the steps avoids the earlier failure without deciding the open policy question. Within the book's record, an appointment with no holder is not a grant; it authorizes nothing until a holder is named.
5. **Within the estate, the capability has never written a transition outside its tests.** That is the book's earlier observation of a guard shown on a documented path, not on the practised one.
