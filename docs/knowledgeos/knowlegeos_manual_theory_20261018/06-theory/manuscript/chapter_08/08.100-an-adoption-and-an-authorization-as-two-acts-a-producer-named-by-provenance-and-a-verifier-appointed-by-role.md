## 8.100.1 In plain language

1. **Within the estate, adopting and authorizing were two decisions, recorded separately.** The PO/ARB first adopted the three verified layers and, later, authorized them for future use. How they map onto the book's record is open: either adoption is the book's adoption and the authorization has no kind, or the estate's *authorization* plays the role the book gives adoption (it is what makes the model bind future work). "Authorized" has four senses across the book and the estate.
2. **Within the estate, the producer of the held capability is named, by someone else, from transcripts.** This is not new: the book records two earlier provenance attributions in the estate, one of them by Governance for a bar. The name is written in a governance document, not in the workflow record, so the bar can be checked against a document. "If wrong, it only excludes an innocent session" is true of who is barred, but not of what the bar is said to show.
3. **Within the estate, fifteen process labels are barred** (the sixteenth "identity" is the human). Under bars by label the pool does not run out: a new session passes them trivially. In the factor sense independence is already refuted for every fresh session in the estate.
4. **Within the estate, the verifier is told to declare before reading anything, and the appointment names a role, not a process.** Ordering the steps avoids the earlier failure without deciding the open policy question. Within the book's record an appointment with no holder is not a grant; the book has this case already. The prompt still asks the candidate to report its own independence as PASS, which the book rules out.
5. **Within the estate, the capability has never written a transition outside its tests,** and the adopted contract test reaches its decision case only on a fixture the governed flow never produces. Both are the book's earlier observation: a property shown on a documented path, not on the practised one.
