## 8.50.1 In plain language

1. **The session now says wisdom is not another kind of knowledge and cannot be stored; it is a process applied to knowledge, and "KnowledgeOS does not become wise".** The subject of "mature" moves from the system to knowledge ("enabling knowledge to mature into wisdom"). Some wording still has the system do things ("request evidence", "do not promote the conclusion"). Those are behaviours, not recorded acts; who performs them has three readings, as in §8.49, and the window's own text ("Wait for validation", "human / agent action") favours people or engines, not the kernel.
2. **A research extraction labels sentences of the earlier answer "[User's analysis]".** Three readings fit: the user pasted the answer, the user adopted it, or the sentences were the user's own to begin with (the answer quotes the user's extraction it reviews). Which is not shown; under each, the extraction's input reappears as its confirmation. A third text maps its components to the Constitution's articles: two names are exact, two match by content, one is a loose label, and two are wrong (consolidation S0253–S0277, C-07; Article 5 is not "Provenance", Article 6 is not "Temporal").
3. **The operational line records the protocol's adoption by the product owner, quoting the decision.** The session that registers it is the protocol's own producer, which the independent review had said must not register its own proposal. The registration discloses this and reads the rule more narrowly (only "acceptance of its own work" is barred). That it quotes "verbatim" and that adoption "accepts" the review's three findings are the record's own sentences.
4. **The registration says: "The adoption's authority is the human PO/ARB act, not this record."** That is about where authority comes from (provenance, §1.2), not about standing within the kernel; so it does not contradict the book. It also says agents may follow the protocol "only after Governance has accepted it": registration and acceptance are different acts, and the record shows only the registration.
5. **Several points agree with the book:** humans remain the knower and the decider; an uncertain conclusion is not promoted and stays unknown; a revision keeps what was believed before; popularity is "treated as evidence, not authority"; "outside research is not KnowledgeOS architecture". Others go beyond it: confidence and "calibrated humility" are grades the book has not built; "Authority" as an "approved source" combines a grant with a confidence grade; and the session again proposes the kernel reduction, which the record had already proposed (three readings, §8.49 C3).
