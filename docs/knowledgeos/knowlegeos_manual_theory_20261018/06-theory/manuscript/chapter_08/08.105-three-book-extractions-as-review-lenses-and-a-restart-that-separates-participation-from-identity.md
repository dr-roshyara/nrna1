## 8.105.1 In plain language

1. **Within the estate, an adviser turns a classical logic manual into a lens for testing claims about the kernel's boundary**: is the definition too narrow, too wide, or empty; is the reason established; does the claimed dependency hold both ways; what exactly is absent. The tests agree in form with how the book already argues. Two of its distinctions have homes: the book separates unknown values by their cause, and states every absence relative to a stated domain.
2. **Within the estate, the adviser extracts nine principles from a book that puts knowledge first.** The most used, that knowledge is factive and that knowing does not imply knowing that one knows, concern knowledge in the philosophical sense, which the book does not record: the kernel decides standing in a record, not truth. The non-iteration point has a counterpart in the book: a check's standing comes from a recorded act by someone else, not from the check itself.
3. **Within the estate, a restart gave one working process a second identifier, and the estate concluded that independence must be judged on prior participation, not on a different identifier.** This answers, for the estate, the question the book left open at §8.11: whether a continuation of a barred process is barred. A separate record keeps one manager's choice for one repair apart from a rule, so that no general prohibition is created by accident.
4. **Within the estate, the adviser extracts ten principles from an epistemology textbook,** of which three have direct homes: where a claim came from, what supports it, and whether it is adopted are three things, as in the book; and a coherent body of claims from one source is not independent corroboration, the book's own rule about one lineage, which the window before this one applied to the adviser's own three-provider matrix.
