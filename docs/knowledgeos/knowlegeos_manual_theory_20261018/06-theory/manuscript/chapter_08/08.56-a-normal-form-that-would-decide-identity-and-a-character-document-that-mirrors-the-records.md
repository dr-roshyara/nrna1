## 8.56.1 In plain language

1. **The word-order record proposes a "Semantic Normal Form": different sentences are parsed into one stored meaning structure, so "two different sentences → same knowledge object; two similar sentences → different objects".** The record leaves to a parser the question of which item a sentence is about. Read as a proposal that an act then confirms, it agrees with the book, where an item's identifier is governed. Read as identity decided by the parser, an engine would settle identity with no act. It agrees with the line's own identity model, in which meaning, not surface form, bears identity.
2. **The Character Definition grows by one section per record and restates each record's verdict.** It says of itself "nothing adopted". Its section on the Constitution says "PROPOSED, pending HPA ratification"; the next section mentions "the HPA's ratification of Constitution v1.0" in passing; the closure section says "Constitution PROPOSED" again; from the Quranic section on, every section says "Constitution RATIFIED (no change)". No section records the ratifying act. That is the swing the book found in §8.46, now in one file.
3. **The document states the kernel–engine split in so many words: "The Kernel does not *do* the reasoning. It provides the inviolable playing field where reasoning can happen safely."** That agrees with the book's kernel, which evaluates standing and records acts. Its canonical one-sentence character ("head 43") calls KnowledgeOS "a constitutional epistemic operating system". So the operating-system phrase is in the canonical essence itself, which §8.52 did not compare. Its "begins from Unknown" holds for evidence; for authority, an item no act recognises is "not recognized", not unknown.
4. **One of its rules says "A conclusion without an explicit inference relation should remain a hypothesis".** For the content of a claim, that agrees with the book's evidence classes. As a condition on standing, it is the guard the Principal decided against (T9-20: no guard on adoption).
5. **Several points agree with the book:** a change of evidence is not a change of authority; revision is not deletion; knowledge informs decision and action but does not act; agency is traced, not bound; unknown is not absent and absent is not false.
