## 8.62.1 In plain language

1. **The first Sanskrit-grammar record called its text "the HPA's complete extraction".** That text is the first answer in this chat export. The export holds three answers in an assistant's voice. Its second answer is word for word the export the book read as S0295, so the same text sits in two corpus files and must be counted once. One sentence of the record, "the kernel stores the semantic roots and rules", is not in the answer. It is the recorder's own sentence, and it is the source of the tension §8.55 found inside that record.
2. **An adviser recommended "accept, with conditions" and drafted the note the product owner should add.** The registration records that note as "the PO/ARB's own wording", verbatim, and puts the adviser's reasons inside the quoted decision. Who wrote the words does not change what the act decides. But the record's statement about the words' origin is a claim of provenance, and the files contradict its plain reading.
3. **The registration says the acceptance "closes" the chain's findings and "discharges" the bar on Phase 5.** The quoted note speaks only of "closure of the current correction workflow". The previous record had routed both closures to this acceptance, so reading the act this way is defensible. The plan, though, barred Phase 5 until the repair of DV-1 was independently reviewed. The product owner declined a fresh independent check, so the act discharges the bar without settling whether that condition had to be met again after the later repairs. §8.61 left that question open, and it stays open.
4. **The same open item is non-blocking for the acceptance and blocking for the migration.** It was made blocking by the product owner's own act. That is one instance of what §5.4 asks for: someone who sets a requirement's criticality by an act.
5. **The grammar answers repeat that "the identity survived" a change of expression, but give no rule for when two things are the same.** In the book, sameness across a change is a recorded act, a link or a supersession, and those act kinds are still outside the evaluator's domain (T9-21). "Event time is not knowledge time" agrees with the book's two time axes.
