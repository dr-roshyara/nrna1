## 8.12.1 In plain language

Who wrote a file was settled by the record of the write itself, not by what anyone declared, and not by the names in a grant or the authors in git. By that evidence:
- the barred refusal really was the barred process's;
- the "correction" that called it false was itself false;
- one process had written both the findings and the correction that answered them.

The estate's authority then set a rule for the next verification of this work item: a continuation, or a process that inherited the barred one's working context, is barred too. The earlier case §8.11 describes is not decided by it. A refusal on independence grounds is not a verdict, and a verifier reads who acted before it reads what was done, because what it has read it cannot unread.

The analysis also gave the terms a rule about controls needs:
- a control **constrains** the party whose act it conditions, whoever runs it;
- how a result is produced, what it conditions and who answers for it are three separate questions;
- a machine-made result is trusted for its execution conditions, and it is never a ratification.

**C2. Evidence classes for authorship, and what is refused as evidence (Src; an extension of §8.10 C5).** `[CON-0196 · provisional]`
- The reconciliation names four classes: "AUTHORED = issued a write-class operation, timestamp-correlated to the commit · OBSERVED = read/inspected only · ASSERTED = claimed in a document without write provenance · NOT ATTESTED = cannot be established" [S0105 L19].

- **Int (§9.15 R4, R6; §9.13 I15).** These classes map onto the recording modes:
  - a recorded recorder or status filled from a declaration is **ASSERTED**;

  So I15's fields are claims (as §9.15 K5 v4 says of the status): the record can carry ASSERTED attribution, and AUTHORED needs evidence outside the entry (here, the transcripts). The failure mode, a true attribution carried forward to a new artifact, is §8.9 C5's propagation pattern applied to authorship.
- *Assumptions, as in §8.10 C5, and two more (review, CON-0196):*

  - sessions ran concurrently (S0105 L143, in meaning).

  "Settles" below is bounded by these.
- **Two scales, not one (corrected at review, W-8).** C2's classes grade the **authorship of an artifact**. How a process knows **its own identifier** is a different scale. On that scale sit declaration, runtime metadata the process reads itself (S0101 L52), and binding by the live transcript's modification time plus the scratchpad path (S0104 L22). **C4. A refusal is not a verdict, and the gate is read before the artifact (Src; an extension of §8.9 C2).**

**C5. The terms of a control, and three questions about a result (Src; an extension of §8.10 C7).** `[CON-0197 · invalid as stated]` (its interpretation as stated, "§9.15's K-list is their execution conditions", is defeated; the definitions and axes, with the per-condition mapping below: `[CON-0200 · provisional]`)
- **Party** (S0102 L809, in meaning): any process, lane or person that performs a governed act or is answerable for it; it is identified in the record by its assignment and role, and its process identity is self-declared.
- **Constrain** (S0102 L811, in meaning): a control constrains a party when its outcome conditions whether that party's act may proceed, be accepted, or be claimed conformant. "Constraint is a relation between a control and the party whose act is conditioned." [S0102 L811]. It is not a relation to whoever runs the check or reads the result (S0102 L811, in meaning).
- **Machine-generated result** (S0102 L814, in meaning): produced by deterministic execution of an encoded rule, without anyone's judgement. "Its trustworthiness derives from its EXECUTION CONDITIONS — when it runs, what inputs it reads, and who can alter or bypass it — and not from the identity, role or separation of any party." [S0102 L814]. And "Being machine-generated makes an outcome neither independent nor ratified" [S0102 L814].
- **Independent ratification** (S0102 L815, in meaning) needs three things: a party gives it, the party is not the constrained one, and the route is not writable by the constrained party. A mechanism cannot give it: "a mechanism cannot, because ratification is an act of answerability" [S0102 L815].
- **Three axes:** "MODE OF PRODUCTION and EFFECT are orthogonal" [S0102 L817]. With answerability as the third (S0102 L824, in meaning), the analysis concludes: "A machine-generated result may be EVIDENCE that a ratifying party relies on; it can never BE the ratification." [S0102 L824]. And: "authority is about effect, ratification is about answerability" [S0102 L824]. The estate holds all four combinations of the first two axes (S0102 L819–822, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15), restated per condition at review (W-4).** CE*'s answers are machine-generated results. In S0102's terms they are **authoritative (effect)** where a gate reads them, they are **not ratification**, and their trustworthiness rests on their execution conditions. Mapped onto §9.15's kernel assumptions, condition by condition:
  - "what inputs it reads" → K3 (the inputs);
  - the version that runs → K7;
  - "who can alter … it" → K6 (the implementation), and K1 only for alteration of the record;
  - "who can … bypass it" → **no K**: bypassing a gate is a property of the gate's placement, outside §9.15's list;
  - "when it runs" → **no K**: the triggering of an evaluation, which in the type is an input (§9.11, E).

  So S0102's conditions and §9.15's K-list overlap but are not the same list. Two conditions, bypass and timing, are gaps in §9.15 (a further observation, for its next review).
- **Two homonyms, qualified (W-4).**
  - **authority (effect):** S0102's sense, that an outcome conditions an act. It is not standing conferred by an act (§1.14).
  - **ratification (of an outcome):** S0102's sense, a party answering for a result. §1.14.5's "enforcement presupposes ratification" speaks of **ratification (of a rule)**, the act that adopts what a gate enforces. S0102 L824 says an authoritative result "is not thereby ratified": that is about outcomes, so it neither confirms nor contradicts §1.14.5. Version 1 merged the two.
- **The rule, now with its terms.** §8.10 C7 quoted the proposed rule on a control's verdict (S0096 L51) and stated a candidate law once, as Hyp. S0102 supplies the terms the rule needs; the rule itself stays "PROPOSED / OPEN" in the estate (S0102 L826, in meaning). The book's statement of the law at §8.10 C7 is unchanged, with C5's definitions as its vocabulary.
- **Its hardest exception.** In a single-operator estate "a blanket invariant may be unsatisfiable in this estate, and an unsatisfiable invariant is worse than none." (S0102 L664, in meaning). *Int (§9.4; the trivial-solution test):* an invariant that no state can satisfy makes every check fail. It is the dual of a must-never rule that refusing everything satisfies (§8.11 C4).

**C6. Four states for an assurance dimension (Src; an extension of §8.6 and §8.10 C7).** `[CON-0198 · invalid as stated]` (its three-way interpretation is defeated; restated below on two levels: `[CON-0201 · provisional]`)
- The four states are not applicable, not established, established, and externally attested (S0102 L520–525, in meaning). "Not established" means "it applies, and the system cannot currently establish it" [S0102 L522].
- Not applicable "must never collapse into" a pass [S0102 L529]. The original analysis had written organisational independence as "not applicable", and the amendment corrects it to "not established" (S0102 L527–529, in meaning).
- **Intra-platform third party ≠ external.** The amendment says: "intra-platform third-party ≠ external attestation" [S0102 L838]. Another process under the same operator, on the same filesystem, shows process separation, not externality (S0102 L838, in meaning).
- **Nine dimensions at three maturity levels:** separation "is not one property with one gap. It is nine properties with three different maturity levels" [S0102 L137]. It is strong where it is authority-shaped (mechanically enforced) and absent where it is identity- or access-shaped (S0102 L137, in meaning).
- **The vocabulary is incomplete (review, W-3).** The same table uses "PARTIAL" and "PROCEDURAL ONLY" (S0102 L514–516, in meaning), which are not among the four states. The states have no stated order.
- **Int, restated on two levels (W-3).** "Not established" ("cannot currently establish it", S0102 L522) and NOT ATTESTED ("lineage cannot be established", S0103 L35) have the **same definition**, so they are one state, not two. And "not established" carries two senses:
  - **unachievable** here: organisational independence in a single-operator estate (S0102 L528, in meaning);
  - **achievable but not done:** external attestation, which "applies" and could in principle be supplied (S0102 L840, in meaning).

  A further state is missing: **refuted**, a property shown false, as NOT INDEPENDENT is (S0103 L34, in meaning). The two levels are:
  - **(a) whether the property holds:** T, F or U, as in CE*;
  - **(b) whether it can be established:** does the requirement apply, is it establishable here, and has it been established, and by whom (the platform, or an outside party).

  §2.8.6's "not applicable" answers the first question of (b); "not established" is a value of (b); "refuted" is F on (a). Version 1's three states mixed the two levels.
- **For §8.10 C7 (W-13).** §8.10 C7 says organisational independence and external attestation are "absent by construction" in a single-operator estate. That holds for the first. For the second, S0102 L840 says it applies and could be supplied, so it is absent in fact, not by construction. §8.10 C7 is corrected.

**C7. A sufficient condition for a capability's existence (Src; Hyp as a general rule; an extension of §8.10 C3).** `[CON-0199 · provisional]`
- "A capability with an adopted governing invariant that has been performed exists." [S0102 L475]. It is said of separation attestation, on two grounds:
  - an adopted invariant presupposes the capability;
  - it was performed once, when Verification #3 cleared a bar from write provenance (S0102 L475, in meaning).
- The same amendment withdraws a categorical denial with the distinction: no platform-wide capability is established and no capability exists "are different claims, and only the first is supported" [S0102 L592].
- **Int (§8.10 C3; §8.4 C3), restated at review (W-9).** The source **treats** this as a sufficient condition; it does not show sufficiency. It joins a normative ground (an adopted invariant) with an empirical one (a performed instance).
- **Ground ① fails the trivial-solution test.** INV-ATTR-2 is a prohibition ("must never be represented as independently attested", S0102 L102, in meaning), which the empty mechanism satisfies. A prohibition presupposes the **concept**, not the **capability**. So the empirical ground carries the weight.
- **How loose the condition is:** the same amendment applies it to communication composition, which "demonstrably occurs" because every governed artifact is a composed rendering, plus an adopted rule (S0102 L478, in meaning). Read so, almost any recurrent activity with a rule about it exists as a capability.
- **Necessity is not existence:** for knowledge distribution "the need is evidenced" but "no instance … has ever been performed": "Its necessity is INFERRED; its existence is not" (S0102 L476, in meaning). This supports CON-0178's reading at §8.10 C3: existence is empirical, necessity counterfactual.
- **"Not established" against "does not exist"** is §8.4 C3's rule on absence claims, applied to capabilities.
- *One source*, so Hyp beyond its case. *Falsifier, on the instance:* an accidental, one-off performance that meets the condition and that nobody would call a capability.

**C8. Four operations on a record, and what the record must not be made to say (Src; for §9.8 H1′ and §9.13 v8's tombstones).**
- The correction separates four operations (S0102 L863–868, in meaning):
  - preserving a historical artifact;
  - additive amendment;
  - replacing text in a file;
  - transcribing into the workflow record, which "changes the workflow record, never the artifact" (S0102 L868, in meaning).
- **Strikethrough is replacement, not annotation.** One supersession pointer struck through original words: "The words remain, but they now read as negated rather than annotated." [S0102 L867]. It was not reverted, because "reverting it would itself be a further edit to historical text" [S0102 L870].
- **A later act's wording governs.** A finding about verbatim wording was disposed of without an edit, since "the later registered wording governs" [S0102 L857] and the artifact already matched it.
- **No entry may assert what is false.** Governance "will not manufacture the START" [S0103 L84], nor record a bootstrap form, because "it would assert that no producer lane precedes this verification, which is false" [S0103 L84].
- **A report that never executed.** An earlier precondition check had said "ALL PRECONDITIONS SATISFIED", but "That output never executed" [S0103 L88]. One of its claims was true on other evidence, "but it was not established by that report" [S0103 L88].
- **Int (§9.8; §9.13 v8; §9.15 R2).**
  - Strikethrough changes how an entry reads without deleting it. It is neither an additive amendment nor a tombstone, which keeps every field D requires and is named by a removal act (§9.13 v8). The book's record model admits only the additive form and the tombstone.
  - The refusal to record a bootstrap form, and to manufacture a START, are instances of H4's soundness half: an entry must not report what did not happen.
  - The never-executed report shows "true" and "established by this report" coming apart, which is §9.15's legitimacy against recognition (R3) for a single claim.
  - *Two objects (review):* S0102 L868 separates the artifact, a prose document, from the workflow record. The book's record model D is about the second. A strikethrough in the artifact is outside D, so the question "annotate without negating" is about artifacts, not about D's entries.

**C9. Further points of the window (Src).**
- **I-K1, reformulated.** "Before a governed act begins, the session must possess a valid receipt for the applicable context version required by the act." [S0102 L562]. Three of its terms are left undefined: valid, applicable, required by the act (S0102 L564, in meaning). *Int (§9.5 C1, CON-0117; §9.11 law 27):* this is §9.5 C1's rule with its evidence named. Law 27's T reading needs START to carry the rule's version, and the receipt is that field. By S0102's own analysis (§8.10 C4) it carries possession authority only, not applicability or compliance. *And it is self-asserted (review, W-12):* ContextAcknowledged is "the session records that it holds a specific version" (S0102 L554, in meaning), so possession authority rests on the constrained party's own record, the failure that S0102 §8.3 names. A receipt the session writes is ASSERTED in C2's terms.
- **Three kinds of dependency, with consequences.** Beyond §8.10 C8: "A semantic dependency constrains MEANING" [S0102 L684]; an evidence dependency constrains what can be proven; and "an implementation dependency constrains only SEQUENCE and may be deferred" [S0102 L684]. So only the third is deferrable (S0102 L721–723, in meaning).
- **Subagents.** A verifier declines to delegate, because "Delegating would create processes whose identity and independence are unattestable" [S0101 L48]. *Int (corrected at review, W-10):* a **forked** subagent, one that inherits the parent's context, fails C1's criterion 7 for the subject. A fresh subagent that receives only a prompt inherits information, not working context (§8.6's information lineage). S0101 says "unattestable", not "not independent".
- **S0101's own disclosure rests on the false correction.** Its independence section states that the morning refusal's declaration was false: "That declaration was false." [S0101 L18]. By S0105's evidence (C3), that statement is wrong. The appended erratum corrects it: the producer's "context is a compacted continuation carrying the conversation of" the barred verifier [S0101 L222]. The erratum is the supported side **on its conclusion** (inherited context) and not on its mechanism, since S0105 finds no compaction (C3; review, W-1).
