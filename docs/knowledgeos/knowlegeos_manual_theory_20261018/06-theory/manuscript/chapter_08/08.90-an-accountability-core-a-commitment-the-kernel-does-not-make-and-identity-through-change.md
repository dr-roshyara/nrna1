## 8.90.1 In plain language

1. **The first adviser says the kernel is where the domain makes and stands behind an "epistemic commitment"; the reply calls this its strongest formulation: the kernel owns the commitment, not the machinery.** In the book no commitment belongs to the kernel: an authorized person's recorded act makes it, and the kernel evaluates what the record holds. What agrees is that the machinery stays outside.
2. **The first adviser says admission creates a historical fact that cannot be undone, even if the claim is later retracted.** The book agrees: nothing leaves the record without trace, and even an annulment is itself a further entry.
3. **The first adviser's accountable object must keep identity, evidence, state, confidence, justification and history co-consistent.** As in §8.89: the book meets the grade part by deriving it from the record; the rest is open or absent.
4. **The reply puts it as "The Kernel removes illegitimate transitions into knowledge".** A kernel that refuses everything removes every illegitimate transition; what must be let through has to be stated as well.
5. **The reply asks what must stay the same while a claim's state changes: its identity.** The book holds that question open.

## 8.90.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A commitment the kernel is said to own (Src; Int).**
- **S0366 (A):** "The core domain act is **epistemic ratification** — the domain's assertion that a particular candidate now occupies a specific epistemic state within the system's governed history." (L11); "**the Kernel is the epistemic commitment itself**, and the aggregate is the accountability structure that surrounds it." (L143); "The boundary is: **the Kernel owns the commitment; it does not own the machinery of commitment-formation or commitment-use.**" (L207); "**Hypothesis D — \"Kernel as Epistemic Accountability Core\"** — is best supported." (L299).
- **S0366 (B):** "> The Kernel owns the **commitment**, not the machinery of commitment formation or commitment use." (L719); "That may be our strongest formulation so far." (L721).
- **Int:**
  - **Whose commitment.** In the book a standing change is recognized only if a recorded act effects it (A1-recognized), and an act counts only if authorized (G2); canon is made only by a human act (§1.6 L48–50), and current authority is F without an adoption act on record (§9.13 L129). *The commitment is an authorized actor's recorded act; the kernel evaluates it (C1 = constitute) and owns no commitment of its own.* (A)'s "the domain's assertion" (L11) and "the Kernel is the epistemic commitment itself" (L143) *put the commitment in the kernel; the book puts it in the act.*
  - **What agrees.** "it does not own the machinery of commitment-formation or commitment-use" (L207) agrees with the mechanism/epistemic split (§8.86 C5) and with §8.89 C1: the kernel performs no transformation.
- **Not a new concept.**

**C2. Admission as an irreversible historical fact (Src; Int, agreement).**
- **S0366 (A):** "Admission is the **irreversible (in the domain sense) transition from mechanism-space to domain-space**. Irreversible does not mean immutable — epistemic states can change — but it means the candidate now *exists* in the domain's history. Even if later retracted, it remains in history as \"knowledge that was admitted and then retracted.\" Admission creates a **historical fact** that cannot be undone." (L45).
- **Int:** *Agreement* with the book's record: every change is an entry, removed only by a recorded act, by tombstone (§9.15 G3), and an annulment voids its target from the start while itself remaining an entry (§9.18.7). *What cannot be undone is the entry, not its effect:* an annulment can make an admission void from the start, which (A)'s irreversible transition (L45) does not provide for. (A)'s "retracted" has no admitted kind in D (§8.88 C5, the withdrawal homonym).
- **Not a new concept.**

**C3. An accountable object whose parts must be co-consistent (Src; Int).**
- **S0366 (A):** "The invariant is: *\"If KnowledgeOS asserts that X is known with epistemic state S, confidence C, and justification J, then the evidence E supporting that assertion must be co-consistent with S, C, and J, and this co-consistency must be recoverable as historical fact.\"*" (L31); "Justification sufficiency (the knowledge claim is supported by adequate evidence)" (L164); "Confidence boundedness (confidence is within the domain's valid range)" (L166).
- **Int:** The same six-part invariant as S0365's (§8.89 C2). *The book meets its grade part by derivation over the record at a cut (§9.18.2), and "recoverable as historical fact" holds because the record is the history.* "Justification sufficiency" established at creation (L164) conflicts with standing shown as not attested without evidence (§8.87 C2); the book has no justification or confidence concept (§8.87 C4), so confidence boundedness (L166) has no counterpart.
- **Not a new concept.**

- **Int:** (A)'s minimum is asserted, as §8.83 C5 and §8.89 C3 found for the earlier lists; its "What breaks" column names principles. (A)'s argument at L219, that sufficiency evaluated outside would open a second path, *agrees in outcome* with the book's placement of the class rule g1 in the kernel (U2); the book's reason is recorded at U2, not this one.
- **Ded (from §9.19.6, under §8.89 C3's two premises):** refuse-all removes every illegitimate transition and fails Req_O (T7). *So (B)'s principle (L419) alone is satisfied by the empty kernel;* it needs what must be let through (§9.19.8, the corrected F-min).

**C5. What must remain invariant while state changes (Src; Int).**
- **S0366 (B):** "> **KnowledgeOS needs a stable identity/invariant substrate through which epistemic change can occur without losing continuity.**" (L550); "> **allow change without loss of epistemic identity and constitutional coherence.**" (L587); "A changing state does **not necessarily mean changing identity**." (L601); "### What must remain invariant while epistemic state changes?" (L633).
- **S0366 (A):** "**How does the domain handle \"supersession\"?** When new knowledge replaces old, is the old knowledge destroyed, retracted, or marked superseded?" (L318).
- **Int:** In the book what persists through change is the record: entries are appended, never rewritten (§9.15 G3), and the class at each cut is read from all of them (§9.18.2). *Whether an item keeps one identity across versions is open (T9-14);* so (B)'s question (L633) is open in the book, and (A)'s supersession question (L318) too (§8.88 C5). (B)'s states for one identity, (provisional, validated, contested, superseded, retracted; L606–616), are a third list in the conversation (S0364's eight, S0365's five, §8.89).
- **Not a new concept.**
