## 8.18.1 In plain language

These five sources plan how to move the authority record to a durable place. Five points come out of them. First, one program writing the record does not make one record. Each script found "the record" by its own default, and a caller could point it anywhere, so two readers could see two truths with no fork in the data. Which code interprets the record was a second such choice, and the estate decided that both belong under one governed boundary (C1, C12, C15). Second, a byte-for-byte copy checked by hashes shows the record is kept. The book's content identity shows only that the answers are kept, because it reads only the fields the evaluator uses (C2). Third, a check that anyone can re-run is not yet a check that someone else has run, and it holds only on the data it was run on (C10, C21). Fourth, a manifest or count taken earlier detects the loss only of what it listed; anything added later can vanish unseen (C20). Fifth, the migration's own rules have gaps. Its checkable test passes the violation it is meant to catch, its ordered steps leave out the reconciliation it calls required, and its proof would at first live where it is not durable (C4, C8, C14). The authority's decision is quoted as registered. A scope may be widened only by its own act (C16), and a boundary decided is not a problem fixed (C18).

### S0131: the implementation design for relocating the authority record

**C1. One writer is not one record: which record is meant is fixed by each reader's default, and two defaults make two truths without any fork (Src; evidence for §9.15 K1 and K2).** `[CON-0239 · provisional]`
- The estate has "exactly one" writer, and its writes are atomic (S0131 L22, in meaning). Yet the path to the record is a default in each of two scripts: the second "defaults `$recordDir` to the SAME path — independently" (S0131 L33, in meaning). So "reconciling one default is insufficient; there are two" (S0131 L33, in meaning).
- Switching one default only gives "one reader sees the old path and one the new — that is a HIDDEN SECOND TRUTH" [S0131 L130]. The source excludes it "only by switching both in one change" [S0131 L157].
- And the path is a parameter: `--dir` "is an unconstrained override. Any invocation may write an authority record into an arbitrary directory, and nothing validates the target." (S0131 L160, in meaning). The source records this as needing its own decision.
- **Int (§9.15 K1, K2).** K2 is "a single view": readers agree on the entries of the cut (§9.15 K2). The book has treated a failure of K2 as a fork in the data. S0131 shows a second way, **with no fork at all**: one writer and one store, read by two readers whose defaults name different stores. Each reader's answer is CE* on a record in D, and the two answers differ. K1 likewise speaks of "the record" as one object. In the estate, which record a write changes is a per-call parameter, so **exclusivity of the writer does not give exclusivity of the record**. A write through the kernel's own steps into an arbitrary directory is not a write "outside" the steps (K1 is met), yet it makes a second record that some reader may take as the record. *Deduction:* K1 and K2 presuppose a fixed **identity of the record**, and nothing in D can supply it, since D is a property of one record. *Recorded as evidence and as an open question for §9.15 (§8.18.3); no kernel change (the Principal's rule: kernel edits only when a source changes the theory).*

**C2. A migration is verified on bytes; content identity certifies that standing is kept, not that the record is (Src; Int for §9.16 R2 and §8.15 C6).** `[CON-0240 · provisional]`
- The source: a migration that re-serialises "could change bytes (key order, escaping, whitespace) without changing meaning" (S0131 L64, in meaning), and then no hash comparison of old and new is possible. Hence "Migration MUST be a byte-preserving copy, never a parse-and-rewrite." [S0131 L66], and verification is "a hash equality per record" [S0131 L66].
- *Attack on the source.* "No hash comparison … is possible" holds for a hash of the bytes. A hash of a canonical form survives re-serialisation. But it needs a parser and a canonicaliser that are themselves trusted, so it is not the cheap check the source wants. *Scoped, not refuted:* the claim is right for byte hashes, and the byte copy is the simpler evidence.
- **Int (§9.16 R2; §8.15 C6, CON-0219).** The book identifies an act by its content, "derived from the fields CE* reads" (§8.15 C6). So a re-serialised copy keeps its identity in the book: two copies that differ only in bytes are joined by the union. That is right for **standing**. It is too weak for a **migration**. A rewrite that drops or alters a field CE* does not read leaves every identity unchanged and every answer unchanged, yet it has lost part of the record. Provenance, one of D2's required properties (S0131 L60), may live in such fields. *Sharper, from the definition (Professor's adversarial pass):* §9.16 R2 derives the identity from "kind and D's required fields other than the recorder and the status, which are not part of it". So a rewrite that changes an entry's **recorder**, which is its provenance in D (L10), or its **status** keeps its identity. The union would then join the altered copy with the original as one entry with one identity. Whether that union is refused or keeps one recorder is R2's merge rule ("joining copies with one identity"), and it is not stated for copies that differ in the recorder. *Deduction:* there are two levels, and the inclusion runs one way. Byte equality implies equal identities. Equal identities do not imply equal bytes. A migration that must preserve the record, not only its standing, is verified at the byte level (or over every field). CE*'s identity shows only that standing is preserved.

**C3. In the window, neither side wins by default; a conflict over a sequence position is not yet a conflict of authority (Src; Int for §9.13 order and §9.16 R2).** `[CON-0241 · provisional]`
- The source: within the migration window "NEITHER by default. The window has no automatic winner — that is what "no silent resolution" means here" (S0131 L102, in meaning). Divergence is recorded in a divergence record "naming both sides, both seq ranges, and the per-record hashes" [S0131 L106], "Never a merge in place" [S0131 L106].
- Divergence "has exactly two shapes" (S0131 L114, in meaning). In a **strict extension**, the first M entries are byte-identical and the runtime holds more; appending its tail is "a UNION that preserves both sides and every sequence number" [S0131 L118]. In a **prefix disagreement**, "Two writers produced different content at the same sequence position" [S0131 L119], which the source calls "a genuine authority conflict" [S0131 L119]: record it, preserve both, escalate.
- **Int (§9.13; §9.16 R2).** CE* agrees with "no automatic winner" by construction: where two acts conflict and are unordered, the answer is U (§9.13, ties unordered, v8), not a choice. Strict extension is the book's union (§9.16 R2). For a prefix disagreement the book and the estate part. The estate identifies an entry by its **position** (record, seq), so two contents at one seq cannot both stand, and only an escalation can settle the record. The book identifies entries by content and names a cut by its entries, not by a position (§9.16 R2 (d)). In the book the two entries are simply two acts: the union keeps both, and CE* then says whether they conflict **in standing**. They may not conflict at all, for example two grants of different scope, and the answer is then T or F with no escalation. *Deduction:* the source's "genuine authority conflict" is first a **positional** conflict, which comes from assigned positions. Whether it is a conflict of authority is a second question, and a content-identified record answers it mechanically, or with U. *Limit:* for a single-use grant, two uses on two sides are a double use. The union records it; it does not prevent it (K4, §9.16 R3a). *Second limit (Professor's adversarial pass):* the estate's record is a transition log under a state machine, which refuses illegal transitions (S0131 L24, in meaning). A union of two branches can be a sequence that machine would refuse. The book's acts carry no such machine, so C3's "mechanically" holds for D and not, without more, for the estate's fold.

**C4. Source defects in the migration's own rules (Src; attacks on S0131's claims, listed separately).** *(No new concept.)*
- **(a) The checkable compliance test passes the violation it is meant to catch.** The source: "after reconciliation the record's seq set must be dense, monotonic, and a superset of both inputs' sequence sets." [S0131 L121]. In a prefix disagreement both inputs hold the same seq numbers with different content. Keeping one side's content gives a dense, monotonic set that is a superset of both inputs' sets, so it passes. The source's second clause ("dropping any input's content, is invalid", S0131 L121, in meaning) is the one that excludes it, and the stated test does not check it. This is the same defect the book found in S0128's detector: density sees gaps, not dropped content at a shared position (§8.17 C5, W-01). *The test is necessary, not sufficient.*
- **(b) The ordered sequence leaves out the step the design calls required.** The five steps are COPY, VERIFY, SWITCH, DEMOTE, REMOVE (S0131 L71–75, in meaning), and "Step 5 is only safe after step 2" (S0131 L79, in meaning). But between steps 1 and 3 "any `append` still writes to the OLD path" (S0131 L83, in meaning), and the source makes reconciliation "a REQUIRED STEP" of the migration (S0131 L90, in meaning). No step of the sequence reconciles. A removal after step 2 and before reconciliation deletes the window's tail, which step 2 never saw. So the safety condition for step 5 is "after the window is reconciled", not "after step 2".
- **(c) "Exactly two shapes" rests on an assumption the source itself undermines.** The two shapes cover the evidence copy as a fixed prefix while only the runtime grows. That holds only if nothing writes to the evidence boundary within the window. The source's own finding that `--dir` lets "Any invocation … write an authority record into an arbitrary directory" (S0131 L160, in meaning) does not exclude a write there. If the evidence side also grows, there is a third shape, extension on both sides after a common prefix. It is a prefix disagreement only if the "common prefix" covers the shorter side's whole length. *Scoped:* the claim holds under quiescence of the evidence side, which the design does not secure (C1).
- *Recorded, not a defect:* the source's own ordering argument is sound once (b) is repaired. Removing a copy deletes no history only if another verified copy holds all of it (S0131 L79, in meaning). This is the "no deletion" property at the level of copies.

**C5. A design that implements a decision may not amend it (Src; evidence for §8.17 C9 and CON-0231).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*

### S0133: a self-review of the migration plan (completeness and provenance)

*S0133 reviews the migration plan `3817eb2b`, which is not in this window. In index order the plan is S0300, so every claim here about its content is the reviewer's, **pending S0300**. The reviewer is the plan's producer, `1c8b041b`, and says so first: "This is a SELF-REVIEW." [S0133 L6].*

**C10. A re-runnable check is checkable, not checked (Src; for CON-0217 and §9.15 L1a).** `[CON-0242 · provisional]`
- The source divides what a self-review can and cannot establish. It can establish "mechanical completeness" and "mechanical provenance", and both "are re-runnable by anyone" (S0133 L9, in meaning). It cannot establish "that the migration design is sound" [S0133 L11], nor "that nothing was omitted that the author did not think of" [S0133 L11]. Completeness was checked "against the commission's own list. Absence of an item nobody listed would not be detected by this pass." [S0133 L38].
- *Attack on the source.*
  - **"Re-runnable by anyone" is a property of the check, not evidence that anyone re-ran it.** Until another party re-runs it, what exists is the producer's report that it was run and gave this result, which is L1a's testimony.
  - **"Re-runnable" holds only while the input is unchanged.** The corpus "accepts writes at any time" [S0133 L103], and the counts already moved between S0131 and S0133 (17/213/102 to 18/216/109, S0131 L21; S0133 L48, both in meaning). So a re-run tomorrow checks a different cut. The re-runnable claim needs the cut it was run on, named by its entries (§9.15 K2), or a hash of the inputs.
  - **The self-falsification is real.** INFO-1 is a defect the pass found in its own artifact (S0133 L91–94, in meaning). It shows the check was performed adversarially, not that it is independent.
- **Int (CON-0217; §9.15 L1a).** CON-0217 keeps provenance (whose artifact) and reproducibility (can anyone re-derive it) as separate dimensions. S0133 is the estate's own use of the split: a self-run check keeps full reproducibility and has no independence. *Deduction:* reproducibility moves the burden. A reader **can** check without trusting the producer, but until someone does, the result rests on the producer's word. So **checkable** and **checked** are two states, and only a recorded re-run by another party moves a result from the first to the second. That is also why a completeness check against the producer's own list is "close to tautological" (S0133 L11, in meaning). The list is part of what is checked, and its omissions are invisible to the check.

### S0134: OPEN-M3, decision-prep and the appended decision

**C16. Widening an adopted rule needs its own act, never a reading (Src; evidence for §8.9's "what an act covers is fixed by the act").** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- The registrar's argument: "the adopted invariant's TEXT governs LOCATION" (S0134 L39, in meaning). Reading interpretation into it "silently is exactly the failure the v3 commission's Category B rule forbids" (S0134 L39, in meaning). So plain A "asserts the mechanism was already inside the decided boundary — which the adopted text does not support" (S0134 L51, in meaning). Hence the recommended form, an explicit extension.
- After the decision, the registrar records that "extending an adopted invariant required its own act and received one" [S0134 L102].
- **Int (§8.9; CON-0219).** §8.9 found that what an act covers is fixed by the act, and that a recorder who widens it creates scope without an act. S0134 generalises the rule from recorders to everyone. A plan that presumes a wider reading, a reviewer who reads purposively, an authority restating its own rule: none of them widens an adopted text except by a new act. In D this is already how scope works. An act's scope is a field of the act (I2), and a wider scope is a new act with its own act time. So "already inside" and "extended now" give **different answers at earlier valid times**. Under "already inside", P-4 was governed from the adoption of AMD2. Under "extended", it is governed from the extension's act time. *Deduction:* the distinction the registrar insists on is visible to CE*. The two readings differ on cuts between the two acts.

**C17. A presumption in a delivered artifact as a reason for its own adoption (Src; attack on the source; for CON-0231).** *(No new concept: an attack, recorded.)*
- The prep's finding: "the plan implements an answer the review says is not yet given." [S0134 L32]. Its reasons for A over B and C include: "it keeps the delivered Phase 5 valid" (S0134 L53, in meaning). The decision rejects C because it "would leave Phase 5 carrying an architectural assumption nobody had adopted" (S0134 L94, in meaning). It then records: "The presumption is now ADOPTED." [S0134 L100].
- *Attack on the source.* One of the prep's three reasons for A is that a plan **already presumes** A. That is a reason from the cost of revising an artifact, not from the boundary's merits. If it counted, every presumption delivered early would argue for its own adoption. The prep's other reasons stand without it: the purposive argument (S0134 L39), and avoiding a second owner for `C-2` (S0134 L40, L55, in meaning). *Scoped:* the decision is supported, but not by that reason. The C rejection is sound on the authority's own ground. An unadopted assumption in a delivered plan is "hidden architecture" (S0134 L94, in meaning), and leaving it unadopted would not remove it.
- **Int (CON-0231).** Adoption makes a presumption current by authority. It does not show that the presumption was right before adoption. Between the plan's delivery and this act, Phase 5 **rested on an undecided presumption**. It was not invalid: it would have needed revision only under B or C (S0134 L33, in meaning). *(Corrected in the Professor's adversarial pass; the draft said "invalid as written", which is the registrar's phrase for option C only, S0134 L41.)* The registrar's own word is "presumes" (S0134 L22), and a later adoption does not change that history (§8.14 C1, a historical claim is not falsified by later acts).

### S0135: the independent Governance review of the migration plan

*S0135 is Governance's review of the same plan (`3817eb2b`, S0300 in index order). "This reviewer did not author it." [S0135 L5]. The reviewer's identity is "self-declared, not attestable" (S0135 L4, in meaning). So its independence is independence **as declared** (CON-0195; §9.15 L1a).*

**C20. A commitment detects loss only of what it committed to: a stale manifest hides what came after (Src; for §9.15 K1).** `[CON-0243 · provisional]`
- The plan's acceptance criterion 1 reads "Phase 1 inventory vs 18 / 216 / 109." (S0135 L92, in meaning). The corpus "is live and grew during planning" (S0135 L93, in meaning). So at execution time a criterion pinned to frozen figures "either fails against a legitimately grown corpus, or is "met" by comparing to a stale manifest — in which case a record added between planning and execution is not detected as missing." (S0135 L93, in meaning). "The plan states no requirement to re-execute Phase 1 at migration time" [S0135 L93].
- **Int (§9.15 K1).** K1's answer to an uncited lost suffix is an external commitment: "not from the record alone; with an external anchor, yes" (§9.15 K1). S0135 shows the limit of that answer. A commitment covers the entries it committed to, at the time it was made. A record or entry added afterwards is outside it. If such an entry is lost, a comparison against the commitment shows nothing missing. *Deduction:* an external anchor detects loss **relative to its own cut**. To cover a later operation, it must be taken again at the time of that operation, or kept current by every append. So K1's "with an external anchor, yes" needs a qualifier: **yes for the entries the anchor commits to**. The migration, which is the operation the estate fears most, needs a commitment taken at its own start. *Recorded for §9.15 K1 and the open questions (§8.18.3); no kernel change.*
- *Attack on the source.* "fails against a legitimately grown corpus" (S0135 L93, in meaning) assumes growth is legitimate. A count that rose could also hide a removal offset by additions (one lost, two added). A count is not a commitment. Only a list of entries or a hash of them detects which entry is missing. The source's diagnosis is right, and the remedy it declines to give would have to compare **entries**, not figures.

**C21. Checkable becomes checked: the re-run by another party, and what it found (Src; an instance of CON-0242).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*

"The plan was accurate at its commit and the inventory is reproducible." [S0135 L90].
- **Int (CON-0242).** CON-0242 said a self-run check is checkable, and that only a re-run by another party makes it checked. S0135 is that re-run. **C22. Conformity is judged at a cut: an earlier presumption "incorporates" a later decision (Src; for C17 and §8.14 C1).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
