## 8.60.1 In plain language

1. **Several earlier sections waited for the plan itself, because they had only its reviews.** Read now, the plan answers most of what they left open: the window between copy and switch now has its own reconciliation step; a conflict of content is never merged automatically, though the list of post-conditions still does not say whether "superset" is over numbers or content; the inventory comes before the durable place, and the plan leaves that order as an open question rather than reorder it; the migration covers every record, not two work items.
2. **One file, five authors.** The original plan, four amendments, a correction and a repair were written by at least four named processes and one unnamed repair session, all inside one file. The plan labels most of its changes, and lists who wrote which amendment in its header. In the book's terms it is a sequence of entries kept as one changing document.
3. **One sentence stands twice on one line, first as a current claim and then, quoted, as superseded.** The repair added the correction after the sentence instead of replacing it. A reader who stops at the first part reads the old claim as current.
4. **The plan says the human act is the authority and a grant would only be its record.** The book's decision C1 says the same thing at two levels: the act happens in the world; inside the kernel, only the recorded act counts. Four amendments are written under acts that are not recorded; in the book's terms they have no standing yet.
5. **Several points agree with the book** and are already in it: storage location is not ownership; derived state is never a second authority; a removal is not a deletion only if a verified copy holds all of it.
