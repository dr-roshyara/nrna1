## 8.71.1 In plain language

1. **The process that had been acting as Governance declares itself a candidate, and passes the bar "must not be Governance".** The bar names Governance by a session label, `b64828fe`. The candidate is `b51dba91`, so it passes, while disclosing that it registered four governance acts on the same work item. The book had asked whether such a bar binds a role or a session. The PO/ARB answers neither way: what disqualifies is the function performed. Recording is not authorship, verification, acceptance or ownership, so it does not disqualify.
2. **The candidate's census says no prior participation is "recorded anywhere", and its own report lists four registrations.** The census searched only the workflow store. The registrations sit elsewhere. "Anywhere" means "in the store", which presupposes that the store is the record. What carried the question was the candidate's disclosure, not its census.
3. **The appointee registers its own appointment.** The only record of the PO/ARB's ruling is a registration written by the process the ruling appoints. Earlier processes in the estate refused to register their own appointment. Here the registrar says it acts on the PO/ARB's explicit direction. No law the book states forbids it, but the record's truth about the ruling now rests on its beneficiary alone.
4. **The appointment is made on a declared identity.** The candidate "verified" its identity by reading its own environment variable: a self-report. The earlier commission put verification before registration; the ruling's sequence has no verification step. Once the lane is registered with the declared label, the bootstrap's attribution check will compare the running process with the label it declared itself. That match holds by construction. It confirms consistency, not identity.
5. **The resolver's rule text uses identity only to deny.** Its authorization formula requires an identity match, while saying identity is "never a grant input". The two agree if the match can only take permission away, which is how the formula uses it. The resolver is "not adopted", and yet its rules tell every governed session what to do at start. They are in force under the authorization to implement, not under adoption.
