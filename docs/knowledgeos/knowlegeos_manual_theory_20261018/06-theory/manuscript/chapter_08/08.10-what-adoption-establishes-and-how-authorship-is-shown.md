## 8.10.1 In plain language

Adopting a model of roles organises who does what. It says nothing about which capabilities exist, and the act says so itself. A verification that was true when made is not disproven by a later decision that changes the world; it is superseded, and the old answer stays as it was. Whether a capability exists and which kind of thing it is are two questions: a failed test of kind is no evidence that the capability does not exist. "Authoritative" needs an object: authoritative for which claim. And when a report's author seemed impossible to name, the record of who wrote the file traced it to an identifier the bar already named. That evidence shows who wrote an artifact, under assumptions about the record; it does not show that a process was separate.

## 8.10.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Adopting a model is not evidence that a capability exists (Src; an extension of §8.4 C4).**
- The PO/ARB adopts "the six-role model as the AI Engineering Platform's engineering operating model" [S0093 L11], and the registration calls the non-equivalences "the load-bearing half of this act" [S0093 L26]: adoption does not establish role ≠ bounded context, capability, agent, platform service or organisational position (S0093 L28–36, in meaning). "⛔ **The adopted role model must not be used as proof that a capability or bounded context exists.**" [S0093 L46].
- **Both directions:** the verification grant already forbade using "a role title as evidence that a capability exists", and "This act closes the other: **adoption is not that evidence either.**" [S0093 L48].
- **Adoption of a model is not adoption of a document:** the untracked 925-line role document "is **not adopted by this act.** **Adoption of the model is not adoption of that document.**" [S0093 L55]. And "⛔ **Do NOT retroactively convert historical brainstorming documents into governed architecture.**" [S0093 L53].
- **Int (A1; §5.6):** an act confers standing on exactly what it names. Here the object is a model of responsibilities, not a claim about the domain and not any document that contains the model. So the act's object is fixed by the act, and the act states its own limits.

**C2. Superseded, not disproven: a true finding stays true of its time (Src; an extension of §1.16.6 and §9.8).** `[CON-0156 · provisional]` (scoped; premises below)
- **Premises:** (i) the finding's record-relative time s is fixed when it is made; (ii) no act is retroactive, that is, no act changes the state at times before its own. Under a retroactive ratification (ii) fails, and a later act could make the old finding false of its time.
- After the adoption, the thesis a verification was commissioned to test, that the model "is not currently established as governed architecture", is "**SUPERSEDED** by the adoption decision" (S0094 L75, in meaning). "**Superseded, not disproven.** The claim was true of the estate when Verification #2 examined it; a later decision changed the world, not the finding." [S0094 L77].
- "⛔ **DO NOT DELETE OR REWRITE THE HISTORICAL VERIFICATION RESULT.**" [S0094 L79]. "Supersession governs **what is to be verified next**; it does not edit what was found before." [S0094 L81].
- **A homonym in the same chain:** the adoption registration calls the same thesis "**FALSE**" as of the decision (S0093 L63, in meaning). "False as of now" and "superseded, not disproven" describe one event on two time axes.
- **Int (an analogy with §9.8):** a finding of the form "currently X" is a statement about the state at the time it was made, and its act time and recording time can differ (§9.8 H2): in §9.8's terms, a value of Hist(c, x, t, s, u) with t = s = u = the time of the finding. A later act changes the state from then on, so it makes a new question, not a false old answer. Overwriting the old answer would turn a change of the world into a correction of the record (§1.12).

**C3. Whether a capability exists and which kind it is are two questions (Src; for the boundary questions).** `[CON-0153 · validated]` (as a distinction; C3's mapping to the boundary test, restated as an analogy: `[CON-0178 · provisional]`)
- "**Separate capability EXISTENCE from capability CATEGORY.**" [S0096 L13]. Existence takes "**YES · NO · CONTESTED · NOT YET ESTABLISHED**" [S0096 L17]; category, "bounded context · cross-context capability · stewardship · control-plane function · another explicitly justified category" [S0096 L18]. "⛔ **Do not derive one answer from the other.**" [S0096 L20].
- The error it corrects: "It has no aggregate of its own, therefore it is not a capability" is "a **category** test doing an **existence** test's work" [S0096 L22]. In the concrete case: "Absence of a `C-5`-owned aggregate may disqualify a separate bounded context but must NOT be used as proof that the capability itself does not exist." (S0096 L28, in meaning).
- **The rule holds in one direction:** category is posed only "*If it exists*" [S0096 L18], so a failed category test is no evidence of non-existence, while a category answer presupposes existence.
- **Int (an analogous rule, not the boundary test itself):** RESEARCH-PURPOSE's boundary test asks whether a capability is **necessary** (a counterfactual removal test) and whether the **kernel** must provide it. S0096's existence is empirical (is there an instance in the estate?), and its categories (bounded context, stewardship, control-plane function) are placements **inside the platform**, not kernel against application. So existence ≠ necessity and category ≠ kernel placement: four questions, not two. What carries over is the form of the rule: a failed placement test is not evidence against the earlier question (§5.7 C4: compatible with, not identical to). *Hyp:* that the boundary test's answers also need CONTESTED and NOT YET ESTABLISHED beside yes and no.

**C4. "Authoritative for what claim?" (Src; an extension of §9.5 C4 and the homonym of §6.6, §6.7 C6).** `[CON-0154 · provisional]`
- The context lifecycle is split into seven states: "`ContextPublished` · `ContextSelected` · `ContextDelivered` · `ContextAvailable` · `ContextAcknowledged` · `ContextApplied` · `ContextVerified`" [S0096 L34]. "⛔ **Receipt is not proof of application or compliance.**" [S0096 L35].
- The question is reformulated from "is receipt authoritative?" to "**authoritative for WHAT CLAIM?**", across "**delivery · possession · applicability · execution · compliance** authority" (S0096 L37, in meaning). "The old form invited a single authority claim to cover five different assertions." [S0096 L38].
- **Int:** "authoritative" is a relation between an artifact and a kind of claim, not a property of the artifact. Read that way, the homonym of §6.6 and §6.7 C6 ("authoritative state" as the canonical source of a state) is one value of the relation: authoritative for the claim "this is the current state". *Hyp:* that none of the five kinds is standing conferred by an act (§1.14); S0096 calls them kinds of authority and does not say. *One source.* *A count to reconcile:* S0096 names seven context states, and the record-integrity pack a "nine-stage lifecycle" (S0095 L110, in meaning).

**C5. Authorship shown by provenance, not by identifier (Src; for fork F32).** `[CON-0155 · invalid as stated]` (restated form below, `[CON-0179 · provisional]`)
- The registration of the third verification had judged one exclusion unenforceable: a report "**declares no session identifier for itself**" [S0094 L41], and "**A bar that cannot be checked cannot clear it.**" [S0094 L45].
- The verification found the premise false: "🔴 `50d55d26` is **not a session identifier at all**. It is a **git commit hash**." [S0092 L50]; "the exclusion was never missing an identifier — **it was being sought in the wrong namespace.**" [S0092 L62].
- It traced the author by a write-class tool call on the file in exactly one session's transcript, corroborated by that session's own written disclosure [S0092 L66–69]. *Only the first is provenance:* the disclosure is a self-declaration of the kind INV-ATTR-2 says cannot be attested, and the method assumes the transcripts are intact, while the verifier's own mapping to "its" transcript is self-declared (S0092 L13–15, in meaning). Two cautions: "**Window overlap alone proves nothing**" [S0092 L72], and "**A transcript search for authorship is self-contaminating; only the originating `tool_use` block is evidence.**" [S0092 L74].
- Its limits, stated by the verifier: the method "requires transcript retention, covers only tool-mediated acts, is **self-contaminating if searched naively**", and "attests **an artifact's authorship**, never **a process's separation**" [S0092 L350].
- **The case did not do without identifiers.** The trace ended at `2da45a86`, an identifier already on the bar, so "**The seventh exclusion collapses into an exclusion the record already names.**" (S0092 L83, in meaning). The source then labels its inference that the substance "can be verified by a process with no Track-1 authorship" as OBSERVED (S0092 L85, in meaning) and calls the clearance "durable" and not to be re-litigated (S0092 L90, in meaning): both stronger than one trace under these assumptions supports.
- **Int (§8.6 C1; §8.9 C2, F32):** what the case shows is narrower than eligibility without identifiers: **an identifier bar became enforceable through the provenance of the writing act.** It is one case, under the assumption of intact transcripts. It is weak evidence for F32's relational option, not a decision of it.

**C6. Access evidence is short of corroboration (Src; an extension of §8.6, information lineage).**
- Whether the discovery reconstructed an unread document or converged with it independently was classed OPEN. The verifier measured access: the discovery lane's "first tool-mediated access to the 925-line document occurred AFTER both the discovery and Amendment 1 were delivered" (S0092 L175, in meaning).
- It keeps the class: "⛔ **This does NOT discharge `INV-ATTR-2`, and I do not upgrade the classification to `CLOSED`.**" [S0092 L179], because it "cannot exclude: exposure conveyed by the human operator · exposure in a different session" (S0092 L179, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.6 C1):** timing evidence can refute tool-mediated access; it cannot establish information independence, because information can reach a process by channels the record does not see. The verdict "OPEN, with the access evidence recorded" keeps the evidence and the class apart.

**C7. Six kinds of separation, and the single operator (Src; an extension of §8.6).**
- The amendment names "process separation · access separation · artifact isolation · independent execution · **organizational independence** · external attestation" (S0096 L30, in meaning), and: "⛔ **Do not treat organizational independence as established in a single-operator environment.**" [S0096 L31].
- A candidate rule, explicitly PROPOSED: "*A party subject to a governed control may not be the sole authority for issuing, accepting, or finalizing the control's verdict about its own compliance, separation, or conformance.*" [S0096 L51].
- **Int (§8.6 C1):** §8.6's nine factors are about the check and its information; S0096's six add properties of the environment (access, artifacts, organisation, an outside attestor). In a single-operator estate, organisational independence and external attestation are absent by construction, so every independence claim here is relative to the other four at most. The proposed rule is producer ≠ verifier generalised to every governed control; *Hyp, one source, not adopted.*
- **The candidate law, stated once (Hyp; the home for Ch. 8):** *a party constrained by a control may not be the sole authority on its own compliance with it.* This is the producer ≠ verifier family (Ch. 1 §1.14.3: producer ≠ verifier, legislator ≠ evaluator), of which the book has these instances:
  - §8.6 C3: a process cannot attest its own separation (S0091 L257);
  - §8.11 C3: the author of a finding may not judge its uptake (CON-0158);
  - §8.11 C2: a continuation that declines to decide whether it is barred (S0099 L80);
  - §8.11 C3: a disclosure is not a ruling on its own significance (S0100 L137–138);
  - this section: S0096 L51's proposed rule.

  Each instance is a source's statement or the book's reading of one; none is derived from the law, and the law is not derived from them. *Not tested:* whether it is a kernel law or an estate policy. Whether Ch. 8 is a validation chapter or an assurance-governance chapter is decision brief DB-4.

At 09:05 and 09:13 on 08-19 two registrations still record the lane as not started and ask for a START "with the `W-1` / verdict-`G` reconciliation stated in the START act" (S0093 L79, in meaning; S0094 L104). That stands against the verification's own "did occur … (it is the act this report answers)" (S0092 L35, in meaning): the two sources disagree on whether the START preceded the report. *Int:* the act's time rests on the authority's later word, not on the record (§1.12; §9.8 H2).
- **Two differently ordered chains on one day.** The adoption repeats the ARB's order "capability → ownership → bounded context / stewardship → role → agent → service / technology" (S0093 L43, in meaning; §5.7 C4, S0080 L28–33). Three hours later the record-integrity pack gives "capability → bounded context/stewardship → ownership → adopted role → agent → service" and says it "still binds" (S0095 L99, in meaning); the same line says "The analysis method is superseded — not the direction" (S0095 L99, in meaning), and it gives a reason for the later place of ownership: the method's ten questions "force **meaning, invariant, evidence, authority and effect** to be settled *before* ownership rather than alongside it" [S0095 L99]. S0095 also names a "sixteen-step" capability sequence (S0095 L81, in meaning) and an "eleven-stage chain" (S0095 L235, in meaning), which may be different objects. §5.7 C4 rests on the first order.
- **A reachable stuck state.** A handoff to a lane that never started left "`mutationOwner` is **`None`**" (S0095 L13, in meaning); a handoff needs the current mutation owner (Inv C) and a START needs a predecessor handoff and a human act (G-3), so "**The record is not broken. Whether the kernel must guarantee the absence of such states is a liveness question the book has not asked. **Fork F33.**

## 8.10.4 What does not hold

*First version, refuted at review C17:*
- **"This is the separation the book's boundary test already makes"** (C3's Int), and the open question pairing existence with necessity and category with placement. Existence is empirical and necessity counterfactual; S0096's categories are placements inside the platform. An analogous rule only.
- **"It established the author by two routes"** and **"decided by the relation to the object"** (C5; CON-0155 as stated). One provenance route plus a self-declaration; the trace ended at an identifier already on the bar; transcript integrity assumed.
- **"It is not standing conferred by an act"** as a statement (C4). Not supported by S0096; a Hyp.
- **"The third external input … absent from the estate"** repeated uncritically (C9). One of the three is present but untracked.
