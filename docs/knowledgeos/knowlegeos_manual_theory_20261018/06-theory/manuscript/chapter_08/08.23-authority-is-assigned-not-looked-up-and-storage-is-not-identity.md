## 8.23.1 In plain language

This window restates what the book holds, with three points of evidence.

1. **Which store is authoritative is a governance decision, not a lookup.** An adviser says so in the estate's own terms. The book already holds it: a designation picks the carrier, and since 2026-10-25 a designation is an entry in the record. This window adds evidence, not theory.
2. **The carrier is not a property of its entries.** The book proves this for the record (§9.17 R1). S0160 makes an analogous claim for bounded contexts: one store can hold two of them. The two claims use "identity" in different senses.
3. **An open choice was named and returned, not left to the incumbent's defaults.** In the estate's parsing evaluation, library defaults had stood in for a choice nobody had made. The evaluation named the gap and returned it undecided. A reply in the log ruled only the scope of the neutrality claim. A recorded grant had already forbidden treating the current implementation as the definition of correctness. This bears on the fork left open in §8.22. It does not decide it.

## 8.23.2 Claims and derivations

### S0159: event-driven architecture, refined

*S0159's lessons are credited to an outside article that is not in the corpus (S0159 L16, in meaning). They cannot be checked here.*

**C1. Consistency is a domain decision, and authority change needs strong consistency (Src; evidence for §1.16.1).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- "Consistency requirements are domain decisions, not technology decisions." [S0159 L297]. S0159 lists authority change, approval legality and aggregate invariants as needing strong consistency, and projections, search and notifications as tolerating lag (S0159 L241–293, in meaning).
- **Int (§1.16.1, §1.16).** §1.16.1 asks which object owns an invariant and which change may alter it. §1.16 puts grant records inside the same consistency boundary as the claims they support. S0159's "authority change is strong" is the same placement.
- *Agreement within the estate's lineage.*

**C2. Reconstruction as a domain requirement: in the kernel the record is the source and state is a projection (Src; Int for §8.22 C9; restated at review, W-12).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0159 keeps event sourcing for cases where historical reconstruction is itself a domain requirement, and names governance decisions, authority changes and conflict resolution as likely candidates (S0159 L372–386, in meaning).
- **Int, with its premise.** *Premise:* the kernel's authority is defined as CE* over the record (§9.13). Then reconstruction is required by construction: the record is the source, and any stored state derived from it is a projection, "a projection, never a source" (§8.17, from S0128). This is §8.22 C9's scope stated from the other side.
- *A homonym, qualified (W-12):* S0159's "event sourcing" persists domain events, which it treats as facts passed between contexts (S0159 L52–61, L338–386, in meaning). The book's record holds recorded acts. The deduction uses only "the record is the source", not S0159's event model.
- *A tension in S0159, recorded:* its kernel diagram includes "Domain Events" (S0159 L614–621, in meaning), while its text treats events as the mechanism **between** contexts (S0159 L52–61, in meaning). Kernel content and the collaboration mechanism are merged in the diagram. See C10 for the drift between kernel inventories.

**C3. The idempotent consumer: compatible with either order; S0158 and S0159 are one line (Src; restated at review, W-13).** *(No new concept.)*
- "The producer owns the business fact; the consumer owns the reaction." [S0159 L20]. S0159's consumer checks whether it has already processed an event, then acts (S0159 L454–477, in meaning).
- S0159 asks for an idempotency boundary (S0159 L462, in meaning) and does not say whether the check and the effect are separate steps or one atomic change. So S0159 is **compatible with either order**. It is not, on its own, an instance of the check-then-effect order that §1.16.1 rejects, which S0158 states (§8.22 C11).
- S0158 and S0159 read the same article, so they are **one line, not two**.

### S0160: a missing boundary behind the durability problem

**C4. The carrier is not a property of the entries; S0160 makes an analogous claim for contexts (Src; Int for §9.17 R1; restated at review, W-08).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- S0160's diagnosis is "Same storage ≠ Same bounded context" (S0160 L59–63, in meaning, a diagram). The concepts sharing one store differ in lifecycle, ownership, invariants, rate of change and authority model (S0160 L49–55, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.17 R1, CON-0264).** §9.17 R1 proves that which store carries the record is not a property of its entries, and CON-0264 keeps members across a carrier change. So one record can live in several stores over time.
- **S0160's claim is analogous, not derived.** One store can hold more than one bounded context. That is S0160's assertion about contexts. It is not a converse of R1 and not deduced here.
- *A homonym, qualified:* "identity" in R1 is which store carries a record. In S0160 it is the identity of a bounded context. The book does not hold that content never fixes identity: D's identifiers are unique (§9.17 R2), and content identity of fields is CON-0240. R1 says only that the entries do not fix the **carrier**.

**C5. Which artifact is authoritative is a governance decision, not a technical lookup (Src; evidence for CON-0261 and the decision of 2026-10-25).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- "Which artifact is authoritative?" [S0160 L358], and then "is not a technical lookup." [S0160 L360], "It is a governance decision." [S0160 L362]. The second pass repeats it: "Authority resolution is not a technical lookup." [S0160 L875]. The ownership is the domain's, and an implementation may be a resolver service (S0160 L340–352, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.17 R1, CON-0261).** R1 proves that no predicate on entries can decide which store is the designated carrier. S0160's "not a lookup" is an ownership claim that still allows a resolver as the implementation (S0160 L346–352, in meaning). So it is **compatible with** R1, not the same claim (W-04). The Principal's decision of 2026-10-25 makes the designation a kind in D, so the governance decision is on the record. A resolver follows the designation entries' names from the root designation, which lies outside D (§8.22 C2 as scoped; L11).
- **Scope.** S0160's "authoritative artifact" is broader than the carrier: it covers authority resolution in general (S0160 L322–352, in meaning). The book's claim holds only for the carrier of the record.
- *Agreement within the estate's lineage.*

**C6. Evidence is qualified before governance uses it (Src; evidence for §0.6).** *(No new concept.)*
- "Evidence may enter governance only after qualification criteria are satisfied." [S0160 L815]. Evidence answers "What happened?" and Governance "What is accepted?" (S0160 L1046–1052, in meaning).
- This is §0.6's sequence, which puts qualification before decision and adoption (DevConst L55–56).
- *Change over time inside S0160, recorded:* the first pass orders the contexts Governance → Evidence → Products (S0160 L104–118, in meaning). The second orders them Execution → Evidence → Governance → Product (S0160 L1022–1040, in meaning).
- *Not a contradiction (the Professor's reading):* "I would freeze this as the working model" (S0160 L1062, in meaning) and "Do not freeze the final bounded contexts yet." [S0160 L1147] speak of different things: a working model and the final contexts.

### S0162: a verdict on the DDD model

**C7. An aggregate is a consistency boundary; an adviser names an owner for the ACTIVE invariant (Src; evidence for §1.16.1; for §8.21 C13).** *(No new concept.)*
- An aggregate "is valid only if it owns invariants that must be maintained atomically", and "it is a consistency boundary" (S0162 L118, in meaning). This is §1.16.1.
- **For §8.21 C13 ("the ACTIVE invariant has no owner").** S0162 moves lifecycle from the product to the product version: "A ProductVersion cannot transition to ACTIVE unless all required governance controls for that version are satisfied." [S0162 L291].
  - This is an adviser's proposal, and no act adopts it. §8.21 C13 stands.
  - It conflicts with S0160, which places Lifecycle in the Governance Context (S0160 L370–380, in meaning).
  - The question is about the application, not the kernel. The kernel stays neutral about which context owns lifecycle.

**C8. Several sources of truth; a projection is not authoritative unless assigned (Src; Int for CON-0261).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- S0162 lists one authority per context (product lifecycle, evidence provenance, approvals, semantics) and makes the graph "a federated projection" of them (S0162 L237–245, in meaning). The graph "should not silently become authoritative … unless that responsibility is explicitly assigned" (S0162 L259, in meaning).
- **Int.** "Explicitly assigned" is a designation (CON-0261). Several authorities are several records, and the book orders entries across records only by citation (§8.20; §9.15 K2). A projection that is not designated is a projection (§8.17).
- *Agreement within the estate's lineage.*

**C9. AI proposes; authorized actors approve (Src; evidence for §1.14).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0162's chain is: AI proposes, domain rules validate, authorized actors approve, runtime policy enforces, and evidence records the result (S0162 L341–346, in meaning). AI may not establish approval, ownership, policy, evidence authority or activation (S0162 L327–334, in meaning).
- This is §1.14's "state is not authority" (ARCH-INV-1) and §8.17 C8, "recording is not authorizing".

## 8.23.3 Open questions

- **T8-04 (the §8.22 C7 fork, with C12's evidence):** may the incumbent serve as the specification for choices the specification leaves open? If it may, matching the incumbent on those choices is conformance by definition. If not, an open choice must be decided, by an act, before a conformance claim covers it. *Normative; for the Principal.*
- *Recorded only:* the bounded-context set keeps drifting across S0159, S0160 and S0162 (where lifecycle lives; the order of Evidence and Governance). The advisers' proposals carry no act.
- *Recorded only (W-14):*
  - S0162 places authorization in Delivery, as an enforcement boundary (S0162 L56, L295–310, in meaning). That is a rival application-level placement to the kernel's CE*.
  - S0162's EvidenceRecord, EvidenceReference and EvidenceBundle (S0162 L203–233, in meaning) relate to CON-0238 and §1.16.6 (a reference must say which version it means).
