## 8.46.1 In plain language

1. **One record says the architect's act has "frozen" the character statement; the record a quarter of an hour later calls that "the earlier reading", superseded, and holds the statement as only proposed.** The Constitution's status also swings between "proposed" and "ratified" across the records. Three readings fit: the architect acted twice; the first record's "act" was the recorder's reading; or the Constitution's own amendment rule required the character to stay proposed, so the second record applies it. The records do not show which.
2. **A separate review checks a plan to move the governance record to a new store.** Several of its rules match the book's: copying records into a new store gives them no authority; the boundary is fixed by the kind of act (the designation and its citations), not by position in a store; a passing check is evidence, never a release. One rule goes further than the book: the plan verifies the copy before the switch, while the book's check comes only before the old store is retired.
3. **Zero is given a formal code, Z-KOS-001, and a "SHALL" statement, but it is placed outside the Constitution, neither a kernel law nor an invariant**, in a new namespace opened after the research was closed. The Quranic lens then calls Zero "the stable truth/reference": a truth the system would hold, which the Zero ruling forbids, or the reality that claims are about, which it does not.
4. **The Quranic lens asks "who says it?"; the Zero lens said "do not ask who said it".** Both agree that the speaker does not decide. They differ on whether the speaker's reliability adds confidence. The book keeps three things apart: who acted (standing), what evidence (the class shown), and how reliable a source is (an estimate the book names but has not built).
5. **Several points agree with the book:** "authority is not truth"; "reasoning plus evidence plus validation gives a candidate", not knowledge; "a rule that everything must follow Zero would make Zero an authority". Others restate the pattern of recent windows: a prevention-only description of KnowledgeOS, and a list of "dimensions" that includes a state and an act.
