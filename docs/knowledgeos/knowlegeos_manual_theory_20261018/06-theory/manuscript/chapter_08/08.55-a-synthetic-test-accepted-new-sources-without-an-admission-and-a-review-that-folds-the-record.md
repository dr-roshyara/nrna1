## 8.55.1 In plain language

1. **The protocol was tested on an invented scenario ("controlled, synthetic", "no migration state touched"), and the product owner then accepted the test: "the mechanism solved the intended coordination problem".** The acceptance was again registered by the protocol's own producer, with the producer bar disclosed. The registration says the result was "demonstrated on the DV-correction chain where the coordination failure originally appeared"; the test record says the scenario was artificial. The registration also lists the agent templates as "integrated" without the Governance acceptance the gate named (§8.52 C4).
2. **The Governance bounded review does what the book asks of a reader of authority:** it "folds the record, not accepting from the prompt", declares that it decides and accepts nothing, and states that its identity as the grant's designee cannot be shown. It also finds that documents survive while the authority record does not, which is the book's distinction between the record and its carrier.
3. **The first grammar record admits a fifth outside source "by the HPA's commission"; the second cites four new web sources and says "no new external source enters, no admission act", because it deepens a family already admitted.** Whether new material inside an admitted family needs an act depends on reading "source" as a family or as a text; the closure's words ("any further external material") read most naturally as the second. The classification is the recorder's.
4. **One record says "the kernel stores the semantic roots and rules, not the generated expressions"; the next says the grammar is "the internal language of the engine, never a kernel law".** The book's kernel has no content value, so "semantic roots" in the kernel would conflict with it; as engine content, they do not. Both records cite context to Article 2, which is about dimensions; context is Article 1.4. "Event time is not knowledge-state time" agrees with the book's two times.
5. **Several points agree with the book:** a token is not its meaning; one word is not one meaning; a rule is applied with situation and judgment, which adds no exception to the kernel. The "case grammar" of relations (who, what, with what, from where, for whom) is a vocabulary for relations between items, which the book requires but has not admitted.
