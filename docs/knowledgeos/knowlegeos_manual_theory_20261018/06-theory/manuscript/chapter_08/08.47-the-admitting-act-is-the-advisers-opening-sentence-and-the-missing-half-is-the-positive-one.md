## 8.47.1 In plain language

1. **Two records say the architect's commission is the act that admits a new source after the research was closed, and quote the commission.** The quoted sentence is, in each case, the opening sentence of an assistant's answer filed earlier. A third record quotes another such opening as the architect's analysis. Whether the architect adopted or commissioned those answers is not visible; the records' sentences are all that shows an act.
2. **The records call the agreement between traditions "independent": a "second tradition family" re-deriving Zero, "two independent derivations" of a provenance chain, "four independent tradition families".** All of them come from one session within the hour, which also proposed the terms they agree on. That is one voice, not several.
3. **The line now says its "missing half" was the negative one: what knowledge is not.** The book finds the opposite. Its eleven laws already say only what must never happen; what is missing is what the system must do, such as accept entries and answer questions. Even the line's "positive" half is a duty to preserve, which an empty system also meets.
4. **Several points agree with the book:** a claim needs who declared it, on what basis and until when; a messenger is not the message; an absence can be recorded with what is absent, where and when; agreement is not correctness; a stored row is not a state of knowledge.
5. **The routing again confirms every lens (eight, fifteen and ten for ten), a frame is again placed with the dimensions, and Zero appears in a further sense ("return to Zero").** One record admits a source by naming an act; another says no act was needed, by classifying its input.
