## 8.47.1 In plain language

1. **Two records name the architect's commission, his bringing of a new lens, as the act that admits a new source after the research was closed; they quote the commission's words.** The quoted words are, in each case, word for word the opening of an answer filed earlier, an answer that reads as an assistant's. A third record does the same for another answer's opening. That a request was made is visible in how the answers open ("Yes. …"; "This is a very valuable next lens"). Whether the architect wrote, adopted or commissioned the words is not shown, and a request in a session is not a recorded act.
2. **The records call the agreement between traditions "independent".** All of it comes from one session within the hour, which also proposed the terms. One "re-derivation" even concerns a different sense of Zero from the one it is said to confirm.
3. **The line adds a negative clause to its character statement, "what knowledge is not", and itself says its laws were already negative.** That agrees with the book: the eleven laws say only what must never happen. By the book's floor, what is still missing is what the system must do: accept entries and answer questions. "Positive" means different things in the two places.
4. **Several points agree with the book in shape:** a claim needs who declared it, on what basis and until when; a messenger is not the message; an absence can be recorded with what is absent, where and when; agreement is not correctness; a stored row is not a state of knowledge. Most of these are about the content of knowledge, so their nearest home is now the evidence entry, not the act that gives standing.
5. **The routing again confirms every lens (eight, fifteen and ten for ten), a frame is again placed with the dimensions, and "return to Zero" adds a reading of Zero.** The closure is handled three ways in this window: by naming an act, by classifying the input, and by admitting a source while saying the closure was not reopened.
