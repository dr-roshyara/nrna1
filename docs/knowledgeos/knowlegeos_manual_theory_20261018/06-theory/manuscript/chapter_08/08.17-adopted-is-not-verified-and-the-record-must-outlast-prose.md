## 8.17.1 In plain language

Adopting a model is not the same as checking that it is right, and deciding a target does not change the state. The estate adopted its establishment levels, together with two reasons the book holds invalid as stated. A summary of a decision never replaces the decision's own text. The record that outranks every document was the one thing not kept in history, and what gets lost first is the amendments, which carry the real constraints. A loss can be made detectable, but a conflict must never be settled by quietly keeping one side. Whether a text is a decision or a recommendation is fixed by its author, not by how it is worded. And the record may point to a human act, but never to its own output as that act.

## 8.17.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. An adoption conditioned on a bounded review: adopted is not verified correct (Src; for §8.16 C6, CON-0229).** `[CON-0231 · provisional]`
- *The authority's words first:* the PO/ARB "adopts KOS-AIP04-C10-E2-RECEIPT-COMPLETENESS.md as the governing definition for C-10 receipt completeness" (S0126 L11, in meaning). The registration's rationale names "the three conditions adoption depends on": the governance review's complete, traceable and preserved, "with 0 BLOCKING findings" (S0126 L34, in meaning). *That last phrase is the registrar's (Governance), reporting the act; the authority's own act text is not quoted in the source.*
- So the adoption rests on a review that, by its own bound, did not evaluate correctness (§8.16 C6). **Adopted is not verified correct.** The adopted model governs as a definition, while its correctness has been checked only by the window reviews, which are outside the estate.
- **The decision fixes content and supersedes the artifact on that point.** The adoption fixes the counts at "7 / 3 / 1 / 1", so the artifact's sentence claiming four supporting fields "is therefore superseded by this decision on the point" (S0126 L46, in meaning). A decided text can correct its own object, one point at a time.
- "Adopting a completeness model is not evidence of capability existence" (S0126 L73, in meaning): §8.15 C1's Class C again.
- **Int (§8.16 C6; §9.13).** CON-0229 said a review is citable only for what it checked. S0126 shows the consequence at the next step. An authority may adopt on such a review, and the adoption then binds as a definition, not as a verified truth. For the kernel the parallel is an adoption entry (kind adopt) in CE*. It makes an item current by authority. It does not assert that the item's content is correct, and nothing in Φ reads correctness. *Adversarial check:* does the adoption claim correctness after all? Its non-decisions list no correctness claim, and the rationale cites only the bounded verdicts. So no. But a reader may take "adopted" for "verified"; that is the risk, recorded.

**C2. The estate adopts the level model, including two grounds the book holds invalid as stated (Src; bears on §8.15 C5, CON-0224).** *(No new concept: a recorded tension between an adopted text and the book.)*
- *The authority's act, as registered:* the criteria model "is adopted as the governing method for determining when the C-10 capability may be considered established" (S0127 L11, in meaning). It adopts the assurance levels, with "D2 flips at L2, not L1" (S0127 L29, in meaning). Among the recommendations adopted is "reject Option A as unsatisfiable" (S0127 L76, in meaning).
- **First ground.** The registration repeats the source's reason: the full conjunction "would make C-10 permanently unestablishable" (S0127 L33, in meaning). The book holds this invalid as stated (§8.15 C5): it needs a snapshot premise.
  - The adopted model itself shows the tension. L3 is "L2 + E3(exercised) + E10", and the PO/ARB's own resolution requires L3 to be meaningful, as "AUTHORITATIVE capability establishment" (S0127 L114–127, in meaning). So the estate holds that a level containing E10 can be reached, while it rejects a test containing E10 as unsatisfiable. Both cannot hold as stated. The consistent reading is the book's: E10 cannot be met at first realization, but can be met later.
  - *Recorded:* the adopted outcome (Option C) does not depend on the defective ground, since the levels are justified on their own.
- **Second ground.** The adopted text keeps E6 as "what shows the outcome is owned rather than falling out of another mechanism" (S0127 L30, in meaning). The book holds that repetition is not the discriminator, because CAP-01's bootstrap delivery recurs and is incidental (§8.15 C5). The tension stands; it is the estate's adopted text against the book's reading.
- **Who verifies the first slice is open.** "When L1 or L2 is claimed, who verifies it?" The first satisfaction judgement "needs an eligible, disclosed process" (S0127 L86, in meaning), and the producer is barred from it (S0127 L86, in meaning). This is §8.16 C6 at the next step: the criteria are adopted, and no one is yet eligible to judge them met.

**C3. A summary never silently replaces the governing artifact (Src; for CON-0219).** `[CON-0232 · provisional]`
- The adoption act's summary restated L3 as "operational maturity", while the adopted object, the proposal, has L3 as authoritative establishment with the claim-authority limb E3 exercised. "The summary is a restatement, not the object." (S0127 L39, in meaning).
- The PO/ARB resolved it in its own words: "The adopted model is: L3 = L2 + E3(exercised) + E10, not merely: operational maturity." (S0127 L114, in meaning). And: "A summary must never silently replace the governing artifact." [S0127 L119].
- The distinction protected: "Capability existence ≠ Capability authority ≠ Operational maturity" (S0127 L132, in meaning). Read as maturity only, L3 "would have meant it kept working rather than it may be relied upon" (S0127 L135, in meaning).
- Further distinctions in the act:
  - "Adopting the criteria is not satisfying them." (S0127 L94, in meaning);
  - "existence precedes categorization" (S0127 L147, in meaning);
  - "C-10 is a DESIGNED capability, not an ESTABLISHED one" (S0127 L155, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.15 C6, CON-0219; §9.16 R2).** CON-0219 identified a decision by its proposition, not its label. S0127 adds that a decision's object is the named artifact, not any summary of it. A restatement that diverges is not the decision. For the kernel, an entry's content is its required fields, not a description of them. A Q1 answer must be computed from the entries, never from a summary of the record (§9.13's citation rule, applied to reading). *Adversarial check:* is "never silently" too strong, given that summaries are useful? The rule bars **replacing** the object, not summarising it. So it holds as a rule about which text governs.

**C4. Authority and durability inverted: the record that outranks prose is the least durable (Src; evidence for §9.15 K1).** `[CON-0233 · provisional]`
- *Measured, not argued (S0128 is a PROPOSED ADR, by a process with a disclosed interest; S0128 L22–25, in meaning).* The records "store NO derived state": owner, session states and item state "are computed by the fold and never persisted" (S0128 L45, in meaning). So "there is no runtime state in the runtime directory" (S0128 L66, in meaning): the file "is governance evidence that happens to be stored in a runtime location" (S0128 L68, in meaning).
- The inversion, labelled INFERRED by the source: the record outranks prose (the engine's rule I-10), yet "The outranking artifact is the only untracked one; its derivatives are all durable." (S0128 L81, in meaning).
- **What is lost first.** Session lineage is 100% reconstructable from tracked documents and grants 83%. The uncited 16 grants "are overwhelmingly -AMD* amendments" (S0128 L51, in meaning), which carry the load-bearing constraints. So after a loss, who acted is knowable and "what they were authorized to do after amendment is not" (S0128 L121, in meaning). Then "prose becomes authoritative by default — authority drift by attrition, with no decision ever taken to that effect" (S0128 L123, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 K1; §9.13).** K1 already states the exclusive write path, including durability: an entry removed without trace leaves a record in D whose answers change (§9.15 K1, citing S0341). S0128 adds which part is lost first, the amendment layer, and what loss does to standing. CE* then answers on the remaining entries. A lost amendment, such as a narrowing of a grant, silently restores the wider earlier standing. That is an inference from absence (§8.14 C5, CON-0212) that K1 must exclude. So K1's evidence is now this estate's measured case, not only S0341's. *No change to §9.15 (the Principal's rule); evidence recorded.*
- **The source's own balance.** "83% of grants and 100% of session lineage survive today with no change at all. Option C is not catastrophic" (S0128 L124, in meaning). The inversion is a risk under failure scenarios, not a loss that has occurred (S0128 L83, in meaning).

**C5. Loss detectable, not prevented; a conflict is never resolved by choosing a side (Src; for §9.16 R2's merge).** `[CON-0234 · provisional]`
- A dense, monotonic sequence makes "a dropped transition mechanically detectable as a seq gap" (S0128 L48, in meaning). So "Silent loss is therefore detectable, though not prevented" (S0128 L96, in meaning).
- The danger in concurrent appends "is not the conflict; it is the resolution: a naive take-ours/take-theirs silently DROPS transitions" (S0128 L95, in meaning). Hence the proposed rule: "a conflict in the authority record is never resolved by choosing a side … a resolution that leaves a seq gap is invalid by construction" (S0128 L161, in meaning).
- A second representation must be "a projection, never a source", or "there are two truths, and the estate has no mechanism to detect divergence between them" (S0128 L104, in meaning). And a snapshot "must be MECHANICAL, not an act", or Governance would be preserving its own authority record (S0128 L108, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R2; §9.13 D).** The book's merge already meets the rule by construction. The union of records joins copies by content identity, refuses conflicting copies instead of picking one, and keeps every entry (§9.16 R2). Nothing is dropped and no side is chosen. What the book lacks is S0128's loss **detector**: D has no density check, since entries are identified by content, not by a dense sequence. So a dropped entry is not detectable within D, which is again K1's "not from the record alone". *Adversarial check:* does a content-identified record admit any loss detector without an external anchor? Only if each entry cites its predecessor (a chain, as R4a's use chain does for one grant). That is a design option, recorded under K1, not proposed.

**C6. Import time is not act time (Src; for §9.15 L6).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- "Only 2 of 210 transitions carry any date field … ordering is by seq alone" (S0128 L49, in meaning). If the log is imported into git, "commit times become the only time evidence — and they would be IMPORT times, not act times" (S0128 L171, in meaning). "Do not let an import be mistaken for a chronology" (S0128 L171, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 L6; §9.13).** CE* orders acts by act time and treats ties as unordered. L6 assumes the recorded act times are true. S0128 shows the estate's records have almost no act times, so in this estate L6 would rest on a time field that is mostly absent. An import would supply timestamps that are not act times. This is evidence that L6 is unmet in the estate as it stands. *Recorded, not a kernel change.*

**C7. The force of an act is fixed by its author, not by its wording (Src; answers part of §8.15.3's question on force).** `[CON-0235 · provisional]`
- A message in the authority's own voice said "My recommendation — I would select: Option B′ — Relocation" (S0129 L25, in meaning), while routing the decision onward. The registrar registered it "as a POSITION pending the PO/ARB's confirmation, which is the weaker and safer of the two" readings (S0129 L34, in meaning).
- The author then fixed its force, in its own words: "my previous message was a recommendation, not a PO/ARB decision … I should not let the sentence 'I would select Option B′' be interpreted as 'Option B′ is now adopted.'" (S0129 L87, in meaning).
- The chain the author states: "Architecture proposal → Principal Architect recommendation → PO/ARB decision → Governance registration" (S0129 L94, in meaning). And in the second act: a selection delivered as an instruction is registered as decided, while blank fields are registered as not selected (S0129 L157–166, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.15 C6, open question on force; CON-0207).** §8.15 asked whether an act's force should be a field of D apart from its content. S0129 shows that the force is not readable from the words ("I would select") and that only the author can fix it. When it is unclear, the safer reading is registered until the author confirms. For D this argues that force must be **recorded explicitly**, as a kind or a field, not inferred from content. The book's kinds already do this: an adoption, an annulment or a grant is a recorded act of a stated kind, and a recommendation is no kind of D. *Adversarial check:* could force be inferred from the act's authority (an authority's text is a decision)? No: here the authority's own text was a recommendation.

**C8. The record never manufactures authority: the human act is referenced, and the recorder may not reference its own output (Src; for §9.15 L5, roots).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- The engine "refuses any grant without a humanActRef": "a grant registers a recorded human act by reference — the record never manufactures authority" (S0129 L147, in meaning). "If one process both decides and registers, the humanActRef points at its own output." The precondition exists to prevent that (S0129 L149, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 L5; §9.13 roots).** CE*'s roots are authorized by stipulation, and L5 assumes the root designation norm is followed. S0129 is the estate's mechanism for the same separation: every grant points at an act outside the record's own production, and the one who records may not also be the one whose act is referenced. This is a mechanical form of L5 for grants. It does not attest the human act (L1a, self-report), only that a reference to one is required.
- **The rule against choosing a side, strengthened and grounded in practice.** The authority restates it: a resolution must preserve "sequence integrity · provenance · reconstruction ability" (S0129 L45, in meaning). And "An aggregate cannot depend on accidental reconstruction" (S0129 L44, in meaning); "History ≠ Reconstruction Guess" (S0129 L102, in meaning). Governance grounds the rule in four practices already followed on the work item (S0129 L188, in meaning):
  - a state clarified, not "fixed";
  - an erratum kept;
  - a superseded proposition retained;
  - findings kept when only assurance was refused.

  So CON-0234 and CON-0214 (b) are one practice of additive correction. *(The rule is recommended, not adopted: S0129 L158, in meaning.)*

**C9. The no-side rule adopted; it binds its own implementation; a decision changes the target, not yet the state (Src; for CON-0234 and §9.15 K1).** *(Extends CON-0234.)*
- *The authority's decision, as registered verbatim:* "A governance evidence conflict MUST NOT be resolved by: silently selecting one side · deleting historical evidence · rewriting sequence history. Resolution MUST preserve: provenance · sequence integrity · reconstruction capability." (S0130 L28–29, in meaning). And for durability: "Governance evidence becomes a first-class durable artifact. Runtime remains execution-only. Authority records do not belong to runtime storage." (S0130 L23, in meaning).
- **Reflexive application.** "D2 constrains how D1 is implemented": "the relocation is itself an operation on governance evidence, so R-CONFLICT binds it" (S0130 L58–59, in meaning). The rule governs the act that applies the other decision.
- **Decided is not realized.** "Until relocation happens, the estate remains in the C condition the decision rejects … The decision changes the target, not yet the state." (S0130 L70, in meaning). That is CON-0231's "adopted is not verified" at the level of state. A decision fixes what should hold; the record shows what does.
- **Int (§9.15 K1; §9.16 R2).** CON-0234 is now an adopted rule of the estate (S0130 L16, in meaning). The book's merge already satisfies it (C5). For K1, the decision moves the authority record into versioned, tracked history. That is a step towards an external anchor: commit history makes later removal detectable relative to what was committed, though not deletion before commit. K1's "not from the record alone; with an external anchor, yes" (§9.15 K1) is the frame. The estate's choice is an anchor of that kind, decided and not yet in place. *Recorded; no kernel change.*
