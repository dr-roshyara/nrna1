## 8.17.1 In plain language

Adopting a model is not the same as checking that it is right, and deciding a target does not change the state. The estate adopted its establishment levels, together with two reasons the book holds invalid as stated. A summary of a decision never replaces the decision's own text. The record that outranks every document was the one thing not kept in history, and what gets lost first is the amendments, which carry the real constraints. A loss can be made detectable, but a conflict must never be settled by quietly keeping one side. Whether a text is a decision or a recommendation is fixed by its author, not by how it is worded. And the record may point to a human act, but never to its own output as that act.

**C1. An adoption conditioned on a bounded review: adopted is not verified correct (Src; for §8.16 C6, CON-0229).** `[CON-0231 · provisional]`
- *The registrar's text (Governance), reporting the act; the act itself is not quoted in the source (W-06):* the PO/ARB "adopts KOS-AIP04-C10-E2-RECEIPT-COMPLETENESS.md as the governing definition for C-10 receipt completeness" (S0126 L11, in meaning). The registration's rationale names "the three conditions adoption depends on": the governance review's complete, traceable and preserved, "with 0 BLOCKING findings" (S0126 L34, in meaning). *That last phrase is the registrar's (Governance), reporting the act; the authority's own act text is not quoted in the source.*
- So the adoption rests on a review that, by its own bound, did not evaluate correctness (§8.16 C6). **Adopted is not verified correct.** The adopted model governs as a definition. *(Version 1 added that its correctness was "checked only by the window reviews", a universal claim drawn from a search; withdrawn, W-14.)* A rival basis is in the act's rationale: none of the findings "has architectural impact" (S0126 L41, in meaning), which is the authority's own judgement on the findings, not a correctness verification.
- **The counts (restated at review, W-06).** The adoption fixes the counts at "7 / 3 / 1 / 1", which are "the §5.1 table's counts, which are correct", so the artifact's §1 summary sentence is superseded on that point (S0126 L46, in meaning; the registrar's text). This is **not** a decision correcting its object. It chooses the artifact's own body over the artifact's own summary, which is CON-0232's rule (C3) inside one artifact. *(Version 1 read it as "a decided text can correct its own object"; withdrawn.)*
- "Adopting a completeness model is not evidence of capability existence" (S0126 L73, in meaning): §8.15 C1's Class C again.
- **Int (§8.16 C6; §9.13).** CON-0229 said a review is citable only for what it checked. S0126 shows the consequence at the next step. An authority may adopt on such a review, and the adoption then binds as a definition, not as a verified truth. For the kernel the parallel is an adoption entry (kind adopt) in CE*. It makes an item current by authority. It does not assert that the item's content is correct, and nothing in Φ reads correctness. *Adversarial check:* does the adoption claim correctness after all? Its non-decisions list no correctness claim, and the rationale cites only the bounded verdicts. So no. But a reader may take "adopted" for "verified"; that is the risk, recorded.

**C3. A summary never silently replaces the governing artifact (Src; for CON-0219).** `[CON-0232 · validated]`
- The adoption act's summary restated L3 as "operational maturity", while the adopted object, the proposal, has L3 as authoritative establishment with the claim-authority limb E3 exercised. "The summary is a restatement, not the object." (S0127 L39, in meaning).
- The PO/ARB resolved it in its own words: "The adopted model is: L3 = L2 + E3(exercised) + E10, not merely: operational maturity." (S0127 L114, in meaning). And: "A summary must never silently replace the governing artifact." [S0127 L119].
- The distinction protected: "Capability existence ≠ Capability authority ≠ Operational maturity" (S0127 L132, in meaning). Read as maturity only, L3 "would have meant it kept working rather than it may be relied upon" (S0127 L135, in meaning).
- Further distinctions in the act:
  - "Adopting the criteria is not satisfying them." (S0127 L94, in meaning);
  - "existence precedes categorization" (S0127 L147, in meaning);
  - "C-10 is a DESIGNED capability, not an ESTABLISHED one" (S0127 L155, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.15 C6, CON-0219; §9.16 R2).** CON-0219 identified a decision by its proposition, not its label. S0127 adds that a decision's object is the named artifact, not any summary of it. A restatement that diverges is not the decision. For the kernel, an entry's content is its required fields, not a description of them. A Q1 answer must be computed from the entries, never from a summary of the record (§9.13's citation rule, applied to reading). *Adversarial check:* is "never silently" too strong, given that summaries are useful? The rule bars **replacing** the object, not summarising it. So it holds as a rule about which text governs.

**C4. Authority and durability inverted: the record that outranks prose is the least durable (Src; evidence for §9.15 K1).** `[CON-0233 · provisional]`
- *Reported by the source as measured, and not reproduced here (W-08). S0128 is a PROPOSED ADR, by a process with a disclosed interest (S0128 L22–25, in meaning).* The records "store NO derived state": owner, session states and item state "are computed by the fold and never persisted" (S0128 L45, in meaning). So "there is no runtime state in the runtime directory" (S0128 L66, in meaning): the file "is governance evidence that happens to be stored in a runtime location" (S0128 L68, in meaning).
- The inversion, labelled INFERRED by the source: the record outranks prose (the engine's rule I-10), yet "The outranking artifact is the only untracked one; its derivatives are all durable." (S0128 L81, in meaning).
- **What cannot be reconstructed** *("lost first" withdrawn: the source names a layer, not an order of loss, W-08)*. Session lineage is 100% reconstructable from tracked documents and grants 83%. The uncited 16 grants "are overwhelmingly -AMD* amendments" (S0128 L51, in meaning), which carry the load-bearing constraints. So after a loss, who acted is knowable and "what they were authorized to do after amendment is not" (S0128 L121, in meaning). Then "prose becomes authoritative by default — authority drift by attrition, with no decision ever taken to that effect" (S0128 L123, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 K1; §9.13).** K1 already states the exclusive write path, including durability: an entry removed without trace leaves a record in D whose answers change (§9.15 K1, citing S0341). S0128 adds which part could not be reconstructed, the amendment layer, and what loss does to standing. CE* then answers on the remaining entries. After a lost amendment, standing **reverts to what the remaining entries give, in either direction**: a lost narrowing widens it, and a lost widening narrows it (W-08). That is an inference from absence (§8.14 C5, CON-0212) that K1 must exclude. So K1's evidence is now this estate's measured case, not only S0341's. *No change to §9.15 (the Principal's rule); evidence recorded.*
- **The source's own balance.** "83% of grants and 100% of session lineage survive today with no change at all. Option C is not catastrophic" (S0128 L124, in meaning). The inversion is a risk under failure scenarios, not a loss that has occurred (S0128 L83, in meaning).
- **The evidence grows stronger with each source (W-13, recorded).** S0128 asserts no loss (L83). S0129 calls the untracked commit "C's failure mode, dated and reproducible" (S0129 L187, in meaning). S0130 says "authority lineage cannot be reconstructed" (S0130 L54, in meaning), while S0128 reported 100% and 83% as reconstructable (S0128 L51, in meaning). What was shown is that the record was **untracked**, not that it was lost. The reason given for rejecting option B also changes between S0128 (L104–108) and S0130 (L53).

**C5. Loss detectable, not prevented; a conflict is never resolved by choosing a side (Src; for §9.16 R2's merge).** `[CON-0237 · provisional]` `[CON-0234 · invalid as stated]`
- The source: a dense, monotonic sequence makes "a dropped transition mechanically detectable as a seq gap" (S0128 L48, in meaning). So "Silent loss is therefore detectable, though not prevented" (S0128 L96, in meaning).
- **That detector fails in the case it is offered for (W-01, a high finding).** Two writers each append the next number. Taking one side leaves the sequence dense, so there is no gap. A truncated tail also leaves no gap. The reviewer's small-scope check found 40 of 40 take-one-side losses undetected. **So density detects interior deletion only, and only with numbers unique across writers.** A lost suffix or a dropped concurrent branch is detectable only against an external commitment (§9.15 K1). *Source defect (S0128 L48, L96), listed separately.*
- The danger in concurrent appends "is not the conflict; it is the resolution: a naive take-ours/take-theirs silently DROPS transitions" (S0128 L95, in meaning). Hence the proposed rule: "a conflict in the authority record is never resolved by choosing a side … a resolution that leaves a seq gap is invalid by construction" (S0128 L161, in meaning).
- A second representation must be "a projection, never a source", or "there are two truths, and the estate has no mechanism to detect divergence between them" (S0128 L104, in meaning). And a snapshot "must be MECHANICAL, not an act", or Governance would be preserving its own authority record (S0128 L108, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.16 R2; §9.13 D).** The book's merge already meets the rule by construction. The union of records joins copies by content identity, refuses conflicting copies instead of picking one, and keeps every entry (§9.16 R2). Nothing is dropped and no side is chosen. What the book lacks is S0128's loss **detector**: D has no density check, since entries are identified by content, not by a dense sequence. So a dropped entry is not detectable within D, which is again K1's "not from the record alone". *Corrected at review (W-01):* a predecessor chain, which version 1 offered as the alternative, fails the same two ways: a truncated chain is still consistent, and one branch of a fork is a valid chain. So no detector within the record alone catches these losses, which is K1's "not from the record alone; with an external anchor, yes".

**C6. Import time is not act time (Src; for §9.15 L6).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- "Only 2 of 210 transitions carry any date field … ordering is by seq alone" (S0128 L49, in meaning). If the log is imported into git, "commit times become the only time evidence — and they would be IMPORT times, not act times" (S0128 L171, in meaning). "Do not let an import be mistaken for a chronology" (S0128 L171, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 L6; §9.13).** CE* orders acts by act time and treats ties as unordered. L6 assumes the recorded act times are true. S0128 shows the estate's records have almost no act times, so in this estate L6 would rest on a time field that is mostly absent. An import would supply timestamps that are not act times. So in this estate L6 is **vacuous**, not false: the input is absent, and CE* cannot order acts without it. *(Version 1 said "unmet", W-09.)* *Recorded, not a kernel change.*

**C7. Unambiguous form fixes an act's force; ambiguous wording is fixed only by its author (Src; restated at review, W-04).** `[CON-0236 · provisional]` `[CON-0235 · invalid as stated]` *("not by its wording" withdrawn: the same source reads force from form.)*
- A message in the authority's own voice said "My recommendation — I would select: Option B′ — Relocation" (S0129 L25, in meaning), while routing the decision onward. The registrar registered it "as a POSITION pending the PO/ARB's confirmation, which is the weaker and safer of the two" readings (S0129 L34, in meaning).
- The author then fixed its force, in its own words: "my previous message was a recommendation, not a PO/ARB decision … I should not let the sentence 'I would select Option B′' be interpreted as 'Option B′ is now adopted.'" (S0129 L87, in meaning).
- The chain the author states: "Architecture proposal → Principal Architect recommendation → PO/ARB decision → Governance registration" (S0129 L94, in meaning). And in the second act: a selection delivered as an instruction is registered as decided, while blank fields are registered as not selected (S0129 L157–166, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.15 C6, open question on force; CON-0207).** §8.15 asked whether an act's force should be a field of D apart from its content. S0129 shows both sides. A selection delivered as an instruction is registered as decided, and blank fields as not selected (S0129 L157–166, in meaning): unambiguous form fixes force. A conditional ("I would select"), routed onward, was fixed only by its author, and the weaker reading was registered until then. *(Version 1 said force is "not readable from wording", against these same lines.)* For D this argues that force must be **recorded explicitly**, as a kind or a field, not inferred from content. The book's kinds already do this: an adoption, an annulment or a grant is a recorded act of a stated kind, and a recommendation is no kind of D. *Adversarial check:* could force be inferred from the act's authority (an authority's text is a decision)? No: here the authority's own text was a recommendation.

**C8. The record never manufactures authority: the human act is referenced, and the recorder may not reference its own output (Src; for §9.15 L5, roots).** *(No new concept: evidence.)*
- The engine "refuses any grant without a humanActRef": "a grant registers a recorded human act by reference — the record never manufactures authority" (S0129 L147, in meaning). "If one process both decides and registers, the humanActRef points at its own output." The precondition exists to prevent that (S0129 L149, in meaning).
- **Int (§9.15 L5; §9.13 roots).** CE*'s roots are authorized by stipulation, and L5 assumes the root designation norm is followed. S0129 is the estate's mechanism for the same separation: every grant points at an act outside the record's own production, and the one who records may not also be the one whose act is referenced. The engine enforces only that a reference is **present**. That it is not a reference to the recorder's own output is a norm, not a check (W-12). So it is a **mechanical necessary condition** for L5 on grants, not a mechanical form of it. It does not attest the human act (L1a, self-report).
- **The rule against choosing a side, strengthened and grounded in practice.** The authority restates it: a resolution must preserve "sequence integrity · provenance · reconstruction ability" (S0129 L45, in meaning). And "An aggregate cannot depend on accidental reconstruction" (S0129 L44, in meaning); "History ≠ Reconstruction Guess" (S0129 L102, in meaning). Governance grounds the rule in four practices already followed on the work item (S0129 L188, in meaning):
  - a state clarified, not "fixed";
  - an erratum kept;
  - a superseded proposition retained;
  - findings kept when only assurance was refused.

  These map onto both parts of CON-0214: the erratum kept is (a), correcting an error; the others are (b), withdrawing reliance or retaining history (W-12). *(The rule is recommended, not adopted: S0129 L158, in meaning.)*

**C9. The no-side rule adopted; it binds its own implementation; a decision changes the target, not yet the state (Src; for CON-0234 and §9.15 K1).** *(Bears on CON-0237.)*
- **Lineage gap (W-03).** S0130 records both selections as "supplied by the human PO/ARB" (S0130 L5, in meaning). But the only registered second act left both fields blank (S0129 L157–158, L209–215, in meaning), and S0130 cites only "both acts" (S0130 L86, in meaning). So the decision's basis for D1 and D2 is **not on the record**. Either a third, unregistered act exists, or the registrar supplied the selection (CON-0207). This is a fifth instance of §8.16 C6's "basis outside the record".
- *The decision's text, as registered:* "A governance evidence conflict MUST NOT be resolved by: silently selecting one side · deleting historical evidence · rewriting sequence history. Resolution MUST preserve: provenance · sequence integrity · reconstruction capability." (S0130 L28–29, in meaning). And for durability: "Governance evidence becomes a first-class durable artifact. Runtime remains execution-only. Authority records do not belong to runtime storage." (S0130 L23, in meaning).
- **Reflexive application.** "D2 constrains how D1 is implemented": "the relocation is itself an operation on governance evidence, so R-CONFLICT binds it" (S0130 L58–59, in meaning). The rule governs the act that applies the other decision.
- **Decided is not realized.** "Until relocation happens, the estate remains in the C condition the decision rejects … The decision changes the target, not yet the state." (S0130 L70, in meaning). That is CON-0231's "adopted is not verified" at the level of state. A decision fixes what should hold; the record shows what does.
- **Int (§9.15 K1; §9.16 R2).** The adopted rule ("R-CONFLICT" as decided, S0130 L28–29) is **not the proposed one** (S0128 L161), although both carry one label (W-11). The adopted text has no gap clause and adds "reconstruction capability". So CON-0234's no-side limb is adopted in the estate; its detector limb is not part of the adoption. The book's merge already satisfies it (C5). For K1, the decision makes the authority record a first-class durable artifact, and it reserves location and technology (S0130 L82–84, in meaning). *If* that means versioned history, it is a step towards an anchor. But history in the same repository can be rewritten, so it is not an **external** anchor without a commitment held elsewhere (W-10). K1's "not from the record alone; with an external anchor, yes" (§9.15 K1) is the frame. The estate's choice is an anchor of that kind, decided and not yet in place. *Recorded; no kernel change.*

## 8.17.3 Open questions

- Where does E10's history begin, before or after establishment (C2's fork)? Under (ii) the estate's "permanently" holds and CON-0224 must say so.
- E6 ≠ E10 as a condition for L2 and L3 to be distinct, and criteria graded by strength (identified vs exercised) (S0127 L25–27, L88, L143; coverage).
- Does D need an explicit force for acts, beyond its kinds, for texts that are neither decisions nor nothing (C7)?
- No detector within the record catches a lost suffix or a dropped concurrent branch (C5). Does K1 then need an external commitment, and of what kind (C5, C9)?
- *Coverage, not yet theory:*
  - evidence REPRODUCIBLE only while it exists (S0128 L135);
  - a corroboration that tests a different query from the escalated one (S0129 L177 vs S0128 L10–11), an instance of CON-0229;
  - relocation, not duplication: one representation (S0130 L67);
  - refusal credited by the refuser (S0129 L117–119);
  - "independent" in three senses (E6's acts; "independently reconstructable"; the excluded INDEPENDENT dimension).

## 8.17.4 What does not hold

*Version 1, refuted at review C24:*
- **CON-0234's detector limb** (C5): a dense sequence does not detect a dropped concurrent branch or a truncated tail (W-01). Restated as CON-0237.
- **C2's "both cannot hold as stated"**: a fork with a consistent reading (W-02).
- **CON-0235, "force not readable from wording"** (C7): the source reads force from form (W-04). Restated as CON-0236.
- **CON-0231's supersession clause** (C1): body over summary within one artifact, not a decision correcting its object (W-06).
- **C9's "verbatim" decision, without its lineage gap** (W-03); **C6's "unmet"** (W-09); **C4's "lost first" and one-directional restoring** (W-08).
