## 8.27.1 In plain language

1. **Who may review is read from the record, not from the instructions.** Twice, the list of barred reviewers handed to a process was shorter than the list the record holds. A process that checked only the handed names would have passed a gate it should have failed.
2. **An act may be recorded only if it happened, and an act that happened but is not recorded counts as missing** (I-08). A process refused to record a reviewer's START before an eligible reviewer existed. The record would have said that an independent reviewer was at work when none was. *(The source calls such a record "false"; the comparison "worse than not recording it" is the source's, not the book's: C34 W-05.)*
3. **Before authority moves to the new store, the new store itself is checked, but not again before the old one is removed.** The estate added the first check after a review found that the old check compared only the old store with its own list (C34 W-02).

## 8.27.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Eligibility is read from the registered record, not from a relay of it (Src; evidence for the C23 rule; restated at the C34 review, W-03).** *(No new concept.)*
- The barred process reads its bar from a registered grant, quoted: "bc1b47ef  -  AUTHORING ELIGIBLE,  REVIEWING BARRED,  ACCEPTING BARRED." (S0184 L29, in meaning).
- The list handed to it in the chat commission (a relay) was shorter. "A reviewer checking only the two names it was handed would have PASSED this gate" (S0184 L47, in meaning).
- The fresh process found the same gap: the handed list of eight names lacked a ninth that the record carries, `5e1dd9ee` (S0185 L59–61, in meaning). Its prompt had warned that the list "may be incomplete", so the second occurrence was anticipated.
- *Withdrawn at the consolidation S0182–S0200 (I-07):* the claim that a relay also errs broad. S0183 L294 concerns the AMD6 review and S0185 L38 the DV-correction review: different subjects, so that a relay errs broad is not shown. That it errs narrow stands (two occurrences).
- **Int (the C23 rule).** The record (the grants, which are acts) is read first, and the relay (a prompt, a summary) is a gloss that can be too narrow or too broad.
- **For T8-03 (corrected, W-03).** The instance is not the prompt against the record. It is inside grant 22: its "fresh" requirement is derived from the record (no prior participation), while its bars name processes (set by acts). So both kinds of eligibility rule occur in one gate (S0185 §2, in meaning).
- *n: two occurrences, one line, one chain, one estate.* T8-03 stays open.

**C2. A registered act that is not live is a false record (Src; Int for §1.13, §1.14.2-1 and K3).** *(No new concept.)*
- "A REGISTERED act that is not live is a FALSE RECORD" (S0184 L96, in meaning; S0185 L108, in meaning).
- The case: recording START for a reviewer lane would make the mechanism report the lane "ACTIVE · operable" while no independent reviewer existed (S0184 L88–94, in meaning).
- **The two rules are the two halves of one claim (corrected, W-05):** an act is registered if and only if it is live. "A live but unregistered act still counts as missing" is one half; S0184's sentence is the other half. They are not converses of each other.
- **Int.** In the book an entry is correct when it equals what the kernel's steps accepted (K3, §9.13), and the mechanism records authority but never grants it (ARCH-INV-1, §1.14.2-1). A START recorded without its human act would be a well-formed entry that asserts something false. What D can see of this depends on the falsehood (split at W-05):
  - **false attribution** (an entry recorded in another's name) partly meets the authentication status, where entries are signed (the signed-as-other mark, v9);
  - **a false premise** (a human act that did not happen) is K3's "legitimacy matter": D does not decide it.

  So refusal belongs **before** the record is written (§8.19 C13: "a refusal downstream of this mkdir is not a refusal"). The estate's processes refused at that point. *Earlier home (W-05):* §5.7, "a COMPLETE on a lane that never ran would be a false record".
- *A narrowing (W-05):* the mechanism already refuses START without REGISTER and HANDOFF (S0184 L88, in meaning). The risk therefore lies in false REGISTER and HANDOFF appends, and in a START whose human act is false.
- *Also recorded:* each process refused to register its own appointment ("must not appoint my own judge", S0184 L98–107; "the reviewer must not be its own registrar", S0185 L108, in meaning). That agrees with §1.14.3-2's role laws **by extension**: the source says the bar does not literally cover appointing ("appointing is neither reviewing nor accepting", S0184 L100, in meaning; C34 coverage). The narrowing of the eligible pool is CON-0151's concern (§8.09 C2).

**C3. "Fresh" cannot mean "no knowledge of the estate" (Src; evidence for §8.06 C1).** *(No new concept.)*
- The fresh process discloses that it read the project's memory and context files at start-up through a session hook. So "fresh" is satisfied only "in the sense the record can test — no prior participation" (S0185 L67, in meaning).
- **Int (§8.06 C1).** Working context is one of §8.06's factors of independence. The record can show prior participation; it cannot show the absence of shared context. Agreement; one estate.
- **But the source overstates independence (C34 W-04; CON-0127, §8.06 C3: independence can be refuted on the record, not established).** S0185's headline reads "INDEPENDENCE IS SATISFIED" (S0185 L69, in meaning). It rests on a search that found the identity zero times and on a self-declared identity. The source scopes "fresh"; it does not scope "independent". Its control, falsifiability (S0185 L23, in meaning), works only if some channel could expose a false identity, and none is named. *PROVISIONAL.*

**C4. The new store is verified before authority moves, not before the old store is removed (Src; partial conformance with CON-0259 and CON-0265; restated at the C34 review, W-02).** *(No new concept.)*
- S0183 adds a mandatory step before the switch: the final durable copy is compared against the already-closed enumeration, all or nothing; on failure there is "NO AUTHORITY TRANSFER" (S0183 L143–157, in meaning). The reason given is that the durable copy "was re-verified nowhere before Phase 7 removed the source — and neither operand of Phase 7's gate is the durable copy" (S0183 L159, in meaning).
- **But Phase 7's gate itself is unchanged:** it still compares the source's final-state enumeration (S0183 L164, in meaning). **V (Professor):** read at S0183 L159–164.
- **Int (corrected, W-02).** CON-0259 and CON-0265 (G2) require the survivor to be checked **at the irreversible removal**. The estate checks it at the transfer of authority. A loss in the survivor between that check and Phase 7's removal is not guarded (compare §8.19 C11: guard the destructive act). So this is **partial conformance**: the operand is right, and it is checked at the wrong act. **The estate's design diverges from CON-0265 here**, which is recorded under CON-0265. CON-0265's own verdict is unaffected.
- The correction came from the review the book read as S0142 (§8.20), so it is **one line**, not a second instance.
- *Also recorded:* the enumeration is closed "inside" the old store's history and carried by the switch record, by a termination argument (S0183 L132, in meaning; §8.20).

**C5. Record existence is not authority establishment; storage order is not event order (Src; evidence for §9.17).** *(No new concept.)*
- "RECORD EXISTENCE ≠ AUTHORITY ESTABLISHMENT; PHYSICAL STORAGE ORDER ≠ DOMAIN EVENT ORDER" (S0183 L163, in meaning). The occasion: the switch record had been called the new store's "first record", which is false, since the store already held the copied records.
- **Int.** In the book a designation is the new carrier's first **append**, not its first entry (§9.17 R1), and order across stores comes from citation, not position (§9.17 R3 (c)). S0186 also lists "Artifact existence ≠ Authority" and "Record existence ≠ Authority establishment" (S0186 L617–631, in meaning). Agreement within the estate's lineage.

**C6. The repository named as system of record; processes that see different states; projections (Src; evidence for §9.17 R1, CON-0239 and §8.17).** *(No new concept.)*
- **System of record.** S0186 quotes PKS material it labels "provisionally certified" (S0186 L435, in meaning; C34 W-06; its citations are stripped, so whether it quotes an act cannot be read): "The repository is the system of record: YAML + Markdown under Git." (S0186 L433, in meaning). Later proposals named PostgreSQL instead, and the database decision is deferred (S0186 L443–455, in meaning). *Analogy (relabelled, W-06), not an instance:* in §9.17 R1, which store carries the record is set by a statement in the record, not by what the stores contain. The PKS "system of record" is not ρ. *Recorded (W-06):* S0186 itself says executable evidence outranks prose (L6, in meaning), while its system of record rests on a prose statement (L433).
- **Different states.** "multiple AI processes can see different repository states and can produce provenance/coordination inconsistencies" (S0186 L876, in meaning, reported from "EKS-07" and not shown here). *Int, with rivals (W-06):* this may be the hazard CON-0239 names, and K2-r forbids: readers resolving "the record" differently. Rivals not excluded: separate worktrees (different carriers), uncommitted changes, stale reads.
- **Projections.** "graph, search, and vector representations as potentially rebuildable projections rather than independent semantic authority" (S0186 L471, in meaning). This is §8.17's "a projection, never a source" and CON-0260 (regenerable).
- *A contradiction recorded:* the certified PKS material says the PKS "does not require PHP, Python, or another programming language", while other documents describe PHP/Laravel/PostgreSQL software (S0186 L24, in meaning). S0186 leaves it unresolved; so does the book (T0-03).

**C7. Timestamps are taken from the filesystem, never from a filename (Src; for §8.23 C10).** *(No new concept.)*
- The Review Set states that timestamps are "the strongest available save evidence (filesystem mtime), never inferred from a filename or chat text", and that divergence is noted (S0182 L59–60, in meaning). *Scope (I-20):* those lines concern the 27 renamed corpus entries.
- *For the book's own practice:* §8.23 C10's draft ordered texts by the times in their filenames; the C30 review corrected it (W-09). The estate's rule and the review agree.
