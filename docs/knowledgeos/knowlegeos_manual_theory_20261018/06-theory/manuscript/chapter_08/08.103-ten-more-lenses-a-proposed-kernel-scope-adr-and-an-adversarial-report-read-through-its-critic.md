## 8.103.1 In plain language

1. **Within the estate, ten more lenses are proposed and then counted as confirming the kernel boundary.** The book holds again that lenses from one line counting their own agreement are one voice. One test the adviser draws from them, that two uses of a word with different behaviour must not share one concept, agrees in form with the book's practice. Another, that nothing should be invented when a prerequisite is missing, agrees only partly: within the book's record a refused input creates nothing, but an item with no evidence or no adoption is recorded and shown as such.
2. **Within the estate, an adviser proposes an ADR fixing the kernel's scope.** It separates capabilities from prohibitions, as the book's first result does; but it names only prohibitions, so a kernel that refuses everything would meet it. Its "incapable of" ceiling is stronger than anything the book derives. The ADR is a proposal, not a recorded decision.
3. **Within the estate, a third provider's adversarial report is read by an adviser,** who keeps two distinctions the book also keeps: the record's boundary is one of authority to record, not a claim about what knowledge is; and admission is not final. The question whether checking a contract needs interpretation has, within the book's record, the answer no for the record's rules, and the meanings and relevance it would need are inputs or recorded claims. The adviser's "very strong" convergence of three providers is one adviser's coding of reports this window cannot read, and it disagrees with the adviser's own text.
