## 1.13 Authority as a recorded reference to an external act

*Sources:* AMS, the authority model's business semantics, which is Track B, "exploratory; governs nothing" (path under References); IA, the invariant allocation; the book's own read-only recount of the live authority record; and the corpus sources S0014, S0015, S0023, S0129, with S0095 and S0090 cited as further evidence where they bear on a claim. AMS's rulings AM-1…AM-5 are "approved inside Track B" [AMS L278–288]. Its counts come from the live authority record of 2026-08-16: 9 work items, 20 grants [AMS L19–28]. The recount (1.13.2-1) is the book's later measurement of the same record, so it is not an independent replication (1.13.2-3, point 1). *Status:* the claims keep the status they had; this section's revision history is in `01.99-change-log.md`.

### 1.13.0 In plain language

Authority, as the running system holds it, is not a property of a person but a recorded relation: a human act happens outside the system, and a grant registered by Governance points at that act and permits a named activity within a scope (1.13.1-1). Whether the record *is* the authority or only *represents* it is left to the Principal (1.13.1-2). The book's recount of the live record finds the pattern holding, endings appearing as new records, a new kind of ending (consumed), a worse holder field, and grant records less protected than the acts they point to (1.13.2). AMS's Track B rulings (AM-1…AM-5, "approved inside Track B") on ending, delegation and linkage, and the finding that the register is not the complete record of authorizations, follow (1.13.3).

### 1.13.1 What authority is

**1.13.1-1. Authority, as the system holds it, is a recorded relation to an external human act (Src, AMS L38–51).**
In the running system, authority is **not a property of a person or role**. As the system holds it, it is a **recorded relation**:

> *a human act happens outside the system → governance registers a grant that points at that act → a named activity becomes permitted within a stated scope.*

AMS's proposed definition: *"Authority is a recorded permission for a named activity within a stated scope, whose legitimacy derives entirely from a human act performed outside the system."*

**1.13.1-2. Two valid concepts: the record constitutes authority, or represents it (C1; the sources point both ways; for the Principal).**
AMS defines authority as "a recorded permission … whose legitimacy derives entirely from a human act" [AMS L49] and adds that "it **exists only as a grant**, only for a scope, and only by reference to an act" [AMS L51]. IA, by contrast, notes that "a grant exists but was not recorded" is a real case [IA L99–101]. Two concepts follow, and the sources do not decide between them:
- **C1-a, the record constitutes authority** (AMS L51). Authority is the grant record; where there is no record, there is no authority. *Consequences:* simple and fully auditable; "real but unrecorded authority" is impossible by definition; a missing record yields **no authority**.
- **C1-b, the record represents authority** (IA; and AMS's own clause that legitimacy "derives entirely from a human act"). Authority is a permission for a named activity within a scope, conferred by a valid human act. The grant record is the system's **representation** of it and the **evidence** for it. *Consequences:* closer to how organisations work; a missing record yields **not evidenced** ("cannot determine", §1.17.3, E4); a present record is evidence whose force rests on record integrity (§1.12; 1.13.2-3, point 5).

Under **both** concepts three things must be kept apart: the **human act**, the **grant record**, and the **evidence** of what the act covered. **Recognition** depends on the record under both: I-2 requires standing changes to be explicit, that is, recorded (Ch. 0 §0.5). This is why the axiom's condition "the act is recorded" (§1.6, point 2) is read as the condition of **recognized** standing. The formal model of §1.14.1 expresses both concepts (C1 and the citation parameter κ), and its business text states which answers change.

*Further evidence (pointing both ways; it does not decide the choice).* The corpus later handles an act that was given and not recorded. It is a lifecycle act (a START under a grant already authorized), not a grant, so it bears on act against record (§1.12) more than on whether a grant *is* authority. First the absence was kept: the report was "retained as evidence produced OFF-RECORD", and "that absence is now part of the governed record rather than a gap in it" (S0095 L149, in meaning), a handling compatible with C1-a. Then the PO/ARB stated "I confirm that the human START for `S1-verification-aip04-correction2` was given" and authorized Governance "to transcribe the missing START transition into the workflow record" (S0095 L210, in meaning); the act's occurrence is "the fact only the PO/ARB could provide" (S0095 L216, in meaning). *Int:* the second step treats the act as having occurred before its record, which is C1-b's reading; under C1-a the same events read as the act's standing beginning at the transcription. When the act was given is itself contested in the sources (§8.10 C10). *What this evidence does not show:* which concept the organisation adopts. **That remains a decision for the Principal.**

**1.13.1-3. The mechanism never holds authority: it holds a reference (Src, AMS L214–226; classification Ded).**
The grant *contains no authority*. It contains a **pointer** to the act and a scope: "The mechanism never holds authority because it never contains any. It holds a reference to something it cannot create." Three properties make this work:
1. the act is external and immutable (a committed artifact);
2. the grant is a reference, never a copy;
3. only one role may register grants, and it may only register.

*Classification (Ded):* this updates §1.2 and §1.12, and it is the clearest statement in the sources of *why* "knowledge feeds authority, never holds it" is achievable. The theory's authority axiom (§1.2, §1.6) says standing changes only by recorded acts. AMS shows the **implementation pattern**: the record stores a *reference* to the act, never the act's content as its own. "Never a copy" (Ch. 0 §0.8 (ii)) here protects authority, not only knowledge.

*Further evidence (supporting and challenging; status unchanged).* Supporting: the workflow engine refuses any grant without a human-act reference, in the words "a grant registers a recorded human act by reference — the record never manufactures authority" (S0129 L147, in meaning), and a later verifier states the limit from the other side: "recording an act is not granting one" (S0090 L30, in meaning). Challenging property 3: the same verifier "transcribed the PO/ARB's `START` act into the workflow record myself" (S0090 L30, in meaning), so a non-Governance process recorded a lifecycle act (§8.9 C9). Property 3 is therefore observed for grants and breached for transitions. Both are the same estate and the same engine, so they bear on the pattern without being independent of AMS.

### 1.13.2 What the record shows: an independent check

AMS reported (2026-08-16): 20/20 grants register-by-governance; 20/20 carry a human-act reference; 13/20 cite an immutable commit; 0/20 have validity, delegation or owner fields; status always AUTHORIZED; "two actors, eight spellings" [AMS L57–183].

**1.13.2-1. The recount of the live record (Ind; `.claude/runtime/workflow/*.json`, 22 files; read-only).**

| Property | AMS 08-16 (n = 20) | Recount (144 records = 142 grants + 2 ending records; 18 work items) |
|---|---|---|
| registered only by governance | 20/20 | **144/144 records** (a census of field presence) |
| carries a human-act reference | 20/20 | **144/144 records** (field present; whether each referenced act exists or covers the scope was **not** checked) |
| validity / delegation / owner fields | 0/20 | **0/144** each |
| statuses | AUTHORIZED only | AUTHORIZED 142 · **REVOKED 1 · CONSUMED 1** |
| reference cites a commit or hash | 13/20 (65%) | 32/144 (22%) by a pattern match; **the method is crude** (a reference may name a committed artifact without a hash) |
| distinct "authority" spellings | 8 | **107** |

**1.13.2-2. The limit of the recount: the earlier state cannot be re-verified (a limit).**
The records are **not under version control** (`.gitignore` excludes `.claude/runtime/`). So the 08-16 state could not be reconstructed, and **AMS's original counts cannot be independently re-verified**, only the current state.

**1.13.2-3. What the recount adds (Ded).**
1. **The core invariant holds in the whole current record.** All 144 records name governance as registrar and carry a human-act reference. This is a **census of the current record, not a sample**, so a statistical bound adds nothing. Three limits keep the finding at census strength:
   - the registering tool writes the field, so presence is partly structural, not an independent trial;
   - the 144 very likely include AMS's 20, so this is not an independent replication;
   - only the *presence* of the reference was checked, not that the act exists or covers the scope.

   The finding stays support for the pattern of 1.13.1-1.
2. **Endings now exist, as new records.** A revocation and a consumption are each recorded as a *new* entry referring to the earlier grant, not as an edit of it. This is AM-1 (authority must be able to end) realised in the **append-only** style of I-7 (supersession is a link, never an edit).
3. **A new kind of ending appears in practice: CONSUMED.** The grant was used up by delivering its work. AM-1 lists *starts · expires · revoked · suspended · superseded* [AMS L284]; **consumption is not on that list.** It is a real, distinct business situation: a **single-use authority**, exhausted by use, not by time or by withdrawal. A new idea, from the data.
4. **The holder field got worse.** 107 spellings in 144 grants. Holder and "how the act arrived" are still mixed in one free-text field [AMS L70–74], so "which grants did this authority issue?" is even less answerable than AMS reported.
5. **Record integrity is the weak point (Ded).** AM-3 requires the *act* to be immutably addressed. But the *grant records themselves* are untracked files: a grant can be edited or deleted without trace. By §1.12, the **verifiability of authority rests on record integrity**, and here the records are less protected than the acts they point to. **The theory requires grant records to be at least as immutable as the acts they reference.** A concrete, closable gap.
   - *Dated evidence (supporting):* a commit preserved sixteen narrative review documents "while **all 28 grants and 33 transitions remained outside git**" [S0129 L187]. The narrative survived; the authority record it registers against did not. S0129 states the principle as "An aggregate cannot depend on accidental reconstruction." [S0129 L44], and adds "History ≠ Reconstruction Guess" [S0129 L102], in a rationale that is "still `RECOMMENDATION`, not `DECIDED`" (S0129 L96, in meaning). The same line as AMS (the governance engine), so corroboration, not independent support. Ch. 9 §9.8 states the related conditions: every change to the record an entry (H1′), and a truthful history that also needs soundness and completeness or marked absence (H4, H5; §9.8 result 6).

### 1.13.3 Ending, delegation and linkage (Src, ruled AM-1…AM-5 inside Track B)

- **AM-1. Authority must be able to end; "the end is not one event."** Starts, expiry, revocation, suspension and supersession are different situations [AMS L284]. *Consumption* is a further situation, from the data above.
- **AM-2. Delegated and independent authority must be distinguished.** Today delegation exists only as "re-granting with a narrower scope", with no link between grants [AMS L92–96]. **Not every narrower grant is a delegation** [AMS L285].
- **AM-3. Every grant must reference an immutable human act.** This splits into the **evidence principle** (a source act is always named; 20/20) and the **addressing improvement** (the reference resolves to a fixed artifact) [AMS L286].
- **AM-4. Ownership and authority are separate.** "Who do I contact?" ≠ "who may approve a change?" [AMS L183–194, L287]. This confirms §1.11 with data: 0 grants carry an owner.
- **AM-5. Authority must be linkable to the rule it authorizes.** Today **no grant references a rule and no rule references a grant** [AMS L200–206]. *What exactly* is authorized (one change, a class of changes, an activity) is left **open** [AMS L288].

**1.13.3-1. Delegation, rule authority and maturity (Ded).**
- **Delegation is a derivation relation, not containment.** That containment (child grant scope ⊆ parent grant scope) is **not sufficient** for delegation is the source's own refinement of AM-2: "independent authorization must remain possible" [AMS L285] (**Src**). The book's addition (**Ded**): delegation therefore needs an explicit **derivation edge** child → parent, with these conditions and consequences:
  - (a) scope containment holds **transitively** along derivation edges, and a delegated grant's validity lies within its parent's;
  - (b) the parent must carry a **right to delegate**; otherwise any holder could sub-delegate;
  - (c) with joint authorizations, a grant may have several parents, so the structure is a **directed acyclic graph**, and a forest only if single-parent derivation is ruled. (The recorded "PO/ARB" spellings are **not** an example: they are one holder with a description of how the act arrived [AMS L61–72]; joint authorization is a conceptual case.)
  - (d) **open policy question:** does ending the parent end the child? If legitimacy derives from the parent's act, the theory says *yes, unless independently re-authorized*. The sources do not rule on this.
- **"Authority of a rule" ≠ "authority to change a rule"** [AMS L265–274]. The first is the act that *established* the rule (its standing). The second is a **grant scope covering future acts on it**. Today the first sits in prose headers and the second is "whoever can edit the file". This is precisely where RM-3 (Ch. 2 §2.11) must attach.
- **Subsystems differ in maturity** [AMS L313–318]. The governance engine writes live records; the observation engine has been idle since 5 August. "The platform" is not one operational state. This supports §1.8's finding that the feedback loop has never closed.

**1.13.3-2. The register is not the complete record of authorizations (Src, S0014; with a Ded consequence).**
The corpus's own evidence reconciliation found that real construction (the capability CAP-001) was driven by commissions that **never minted a ruling**. The capability catalogue called it "unauthorized", its README called it "REALIZED", and the register was silent. Its conclusion: "**the register is provably not the complete record of authorizations**" [Src: S0014 L53, L68; E-S0014 Q9]. The finding is a candidate document's, with HIGH confidence, "found blind".
- The corpus offers two remedies, as a pending Decision Authority decision (D-2): **mint** the authorization into the register, **or rule** that the register is not the complete record [Src: S0014 L68].
- **Consequence (Ded):** the authorizations were **unregistered, not unrecorded**. The commissions exist, and the README records "REALIZED" [S0014 L68; S0015 L58]. *(Int:* a README's "REALIZED" records realization, not authorization; the commissions are the authorization records.)
  - Recognition **"as the register stands"** misses these authorizations.
  - Recognition **"as all records stand"** (commissions, READMEs, the register) may not.
  - The register shows such an act as "not found", which is not "not authorized" (§1.17.3).

  Under the two remedies:
  - *mint*: the gap closes prospectively; past acts stay unregistered until minted, with their act time preserved and nothing back-dated;
  - *rule the register incomplete*: recognition must admit the other records of acts.

  This is the act-vs-record question (an act exists independently of its record; 1.13.1-2) observed in the platform itself. *Related evidence:* later verifications in the estate draw the same line for architecture, between "evidence of NON-REGISTRATION" and "evidence of NON-EXISTENCE" (S0089 L81, in meaning; §8.9 C9).
- **The decision is the Decision Authority's** (TODO T1-46).

**1.13.3-3. A forward minting rule stands beside two open options for the past (Int, from 1.13.3-4).**
The two corpus documents that discuss the gap state **a forward rule** (authorizations must be minted from now on) **and** leave **two options open for the existing gap** (mint retroactively, or declare the register incomplete). The forward rule is a record-keeping obligation, not the strict recognition rule "only recorded acts count" of 1.13.1-2's open choice. The evidence is 1.13.3-4.

**1.13.3-4. What the sources say, rule by rule (Src, with Int).**
Both documents carry **both** elements:

| | Forward rule (from now on) | The existing gap (CAP-001) |
|---|---|---|
| S0015 (blind review, 08-02/03) | "a register-completeness rule — authorizations exist only if minted" [L104] | "Mint CAP-001's authorization and acceptance into the register — or record why the register is not the complete authorization record" (IR-1) [L112] |
| S0014 (reconciliation, 08-03) | "commissions that authorize work must mint" (C-2 process fix) [L53] | D-2: mint, **or** rule that the register is not the complete record [L68] |

- **Reading (Int):** "authorizations exist only if minted" is a **forward record-keeping obligation**. It says that authorizations must be minted. It does not say that unminted ones were never authorizations: the same document calls the unminted commissions authorizations missing from the register (S0015 L58).
- It is therefore **not** the strict recognition rule "only recorded acts count".
- For the **existing gap**, both documents leave open whether to mint retroactively or to declare the register incomplete. That is a decision about recognising past acts, and it stays reserved in the corpus (D-2, IR-1) and in this book.
- **The docket's recommendation (S0023).** The decision docket packages this as D-2 for the Decision Authority and recommends **both**: "mint with disclosure + adopt the forward rule" [S0023 L108]. The alternative it names is to rule "that PA commissions are a second lawful authorization channel" [L101]. It is a recommendation; the decision is still reserved.
- **A self-check (Src, with Int).** The minting "must pass PMR-10 — CAP-001 would check the ruling that authorizes CAP-001" [S0023 L103; E-S0023 Q15]. **Int:** this is circular for **verification**, not for **authority**. The ruling's validity comes from the Decision Authority's act (§1.6). CAP-001 can check that the ruling is well-formed; it cannot be the reason the ruling is valid. A failed check would show a defect in the record, not a lack of authority.

### 1.13.98 Open questions

- **C1, constitute or represent:** which concept the organisation adopts is a decision for the Principal (1.13.1-2).
- **Does ending a parent grant end the child?** An open policy question; the sources do not rule (1.13.3-1, point (d)).
- **What exactly a grant authorizes** (one change, a class of changes, an activity): left open by AM-5 (1.13.3, AM-5).
- **The existing authorization gap (CAP-001):** mint retroactively, or declare the register incomplete; reserved in the corpus (D-2, IR-1) and in the book (1.13.3-3, 1.13.3-4; TODO T1-46).

### 1.13.99 What does not hold

- **A "rule of three" bound on the recount.** Presence of the fields is partly structural, the 144 records very likely include AMS's 20, and only presence was checked (1.13.2-3, point 1).
- **"The corpus first proposed the strict rule, then reopened it."** A misreading: both documents carry the forward rule and the open options side by side (1.13.3-3, 1.13.3-4). The withdrawn wording is kept in `BASELINE-REVISIONS.md`.
- **"Unrecorded" for the CAP-001 authorizations.** They were unregistered, not unrecorded (1.13.3-2).
