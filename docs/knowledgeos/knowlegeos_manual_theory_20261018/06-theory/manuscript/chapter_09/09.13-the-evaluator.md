## 9.13.1 The research question

**Can §1.14.1's evaluator be repaired into a function that gives exactly one answer, T, F or U, for every record in a stated domain and every parameter setting in a stated set? If so: on what does its answer rest, and when is the answer T or F rather than U? If not: which defect blocks it?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a record in the domain on which the repaired evaluator gives no answer, or two answers; or a record on which its T or F contradicts A1 recognized. On D every T and F is **forced** (R2, R3): it holds in every fixed point of Φ.

## 9.13.3 The definition

Let the atoms be authorized(x), one per act x. A **three-valued assignment** gives each atom T, F or U. The **information order**: U is below T and below F. Strong Kleene connectives (*Ext:* Kleene 1952).

**The operator Φ.** For a root act, Φ(a)(x) = T. For any other act x:
- F if x cites no basis;
- else (⋁ over cited bases g of basis(g, x)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of x of a(e)), where
  - for a **root** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's actor is x's actor) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for a **grant** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's holder is x's actor) ∧ (t_x in g's window [start, end)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over endings and consumptions e of g, e ≠ x, of (a(e) ∧ before(e, x))), before(e, x) being T, F or U (U at the same act time; v8) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of g, e ≠ x, of a(e)) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for any other entry, or a citation not in the record: F.

A grant is itself an act whose issuance must be authorized (a(g); defect 2). The clauses e ≠ x exclude x's own effects (§1.14.1: "an ending that **is** *x*"); the case "x annuls the very grant it relies on" is not excluded beyond that, and gives U (defect 6).

**The program reading** (for WF, R1 step 8): each basis that passes its record constants (holder, window, covers ≠ F) gives a rule x ← g, [u], not e₁, …, not eₖ, with the endings and annulments negative; a root is a fact; a covers pair with value U adds an atom u with the rule u ← not u. **Φ is Fitting's operator of this program on the act atoms (Ded).** In strong Kleene logic ∧ distributes over ∨, so (⋁_g basis(g, x)) ∧ ¬A_x = ⋁_g (basis(g, x) ∧ ¬A_x), one disjunct per rule. A basis whose record constants fail gives F in Φ and has no rule, which also gives F. A covers constant U behaves as the atom u ← not u, which is U in both readings. So the least fixed point of Φ is the program's Kripke–Kleene model on the acts. The model builds the program separately (`program`) and confirms the identity (check 6).

**current-authority(c, x, t)**, for item c, queried scope x and valid time t: F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute; defect 3); else ⋁ over adoptions y of c with t_y ≤ t of: authorized(y) ∧ t in y's validity ∧ covers(scope(y), x) ∧ ¬(an effective revocation of c ordered after y, with time ≤ t) ∧ every cited grant of y intact at t (its period not ended by t, and no effective **ending** of it ordered after y with time ≤ t; a consumption does not end y's currency, v9). "Ordered after" is three-valued, U at the same act time (v8).

**Two readings of the fixed point:**
- **(KK) the least fixed point of Φ** in the information order: the Kripke–Kleene model (*Ext:* Kripke 1975; Fitting 1985);
- **(WF) the well-founded model of the program reading:** it makes **positive loops**, authority supported only by itself, false (*Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991; equivalently the alternating fixpoint, Van Gelder 1989/1993; the well-founded fixpoint of the approximating operator, Denecker, Marek and Truszczyński 2000).

**CE\*(c, x, t; R≤s)** = current-authority read off the fixed point of the cut R≤s. On D, KK and WF are the same fixed point (R3), so CE* is read off either. It is **undefined**, with the reason, if the **record** R is not in D. *(Corrected at the consolidation S0101–S0130, I-09: since v9 membership of D is checked on the whole record, and the cut R≤s is evaluated on its entries, `ce_star_cut`. A cut need not itself be in D: it may hold a tombstone whose removal act comes later.)*

**Output:** the value, with its **marks** (v6, the Principal after turn 6). An answer is **marked, not refused**, when it rests on **transcribed** (signed-as-other) or **unsigned** entries, or on **removed content** (a tombstone).
- **"Rests on", for the marks (v7, turn-7 settlement, A1-01, A2-01, A1-02, A2-08; the Professor's reading, returned):** the **support** of an answer for (c, t) is the closure, from the adoptions and revocations of c with act time ≤ t, under "reads": an entry reads the entries it cites, the annulments that target it, the endings and consumptions of it, (v8) the removal acts of it (C19, T2-11), and (v10) the transfers of it. It does not depend on which rules are live.
  - **Sound:** every entry the value rests on is in it (Ded: the evaluation of every reached atom reads only cited entries, annulments of it and endings of it; model check 19: 9,443 entries outside the support of 1,500 random records (v9: 9,619), and removing any or changing its holder changed no answer).

  - **F by C1** (no adoption of c by t) has an empty support and no mark; that F rests on the record's completeness, which is legitimacy assumption L4 (§9.15), not on any entry.

For U, the output adds the **refusal reason** (model: `ce_star_explained`; for one act's authorization, `act_explained`; checks 9b, 11b, 15, 15b). The reason names what the U rests on:
- **(i)** every **strongly connected component** of U atoms with a cycle, found by walking what the answer rests on (v5, turn 6, A1-06, A2-04–A2-06).
  - The walk starts only from current-authority's alternatives whose value is U: the U adoption, a U revocation, or a U ending they consult.
  - From there it follows only the U literals of rule bodies that are themselves U; a body that is F supports nothing.
  - By R2 the walk always reaches a component with a cycle, and on D every such component has a negative edge.

  Each component is named once:
  - a **self-annulling act**: an annulment whose target is itself;
  - a **self-cancelling act**: an annulment whose target is a link of its own authorization chain, a grant its authority rests on, directly or through the grants it acts under. The reason names the act and the link;
  - a **mutual annulment** of two acts;
  - an **annulment ring** of three or more;
  - otherwise **a negative cycle** through the named acts, for example through an ending: a grant recorded earlier with a later act time, and an ending citing it.

  A component may hold several cycles; the reason names the component, not every cycle in it. *(Turn 7, A1-13:)* when the component holds a self-cancelling act, the reason names that act and its link, and the other acts of the component may go unnamed.
- **(ii)** every undefined covers pair on a live rule that the walk of (i) reaches (turn 7, A1-14);
- **(iii)** an undefined covers pair between an adoption's scope and the queried scope, **for an alternative whose value is U** (turn 6, A2-05);
- **(iv)** (v8) every pair of acts at the same act time whose order a U alternative reads: "same act time: the order of e and x is undetermined" (checks 10b, 15b). These name a pair, not U acts, so check 15c does not count them (C19, T1-12).

The model checks this (v5):
- **check 15b:** on 3,000 random records in D, all 165 U answers, for items and for acts, had a named reason (v9, with consumptions and unordered ties: all 212; v10: all 208);
- **check 15c:** on 2,000 records in D, mostly rich in annulments, with and without a queried scope, every one of the 68 acts named in a reason was U (v9: 58; order reasons name a pair, not U acts, and are not counted). This is a soundness check of the names. It does not show that the reason is the smallest one. **R2. What T, F, U and "undefined" mean (Ded).**
- **T and F are forced:** every fixed point of Φ lies above KK (KK is least), so a T or F of KK holds in **every** fixed point. It is what every assignment of authorization consistent with Φ gives. On D, WF = KK (R3). current-authority combines the act values with strong Kleene connectives and record constants, so it is monotone in the act assignment, and a T or F read off KK holds in every fixed point too: **every T and F of CE* is forced.** U values are not forced. In the mutual-annulment record (in D), (T, F) and (F, T) are also total fixed points, beside (U, U) (check 3b, under υ = U).
- **Why D needed this (records outside D).** With forward citations, WF can add **grounded** values: they hold only because authority supported by itself is counted as not authorized, and they are not forced. Model check 5 enumerates all 3^k assignments on 300 loop-rich records with forward citations: 331 fixed points, 31 other than KK, all above KK. 31 fixed points are not above WF; 15 of them are other than KK, and the other 16 are KK itself (v4 run, joint count). *(Version 3 said "the same 27", which conflated the two counts; corrected at turn 5.)* On check 7's record (two grants each issued under the other) the all-T assignment is a total fixed point, yet WF gives F. On check 8's record WF gives T to an adoption that another total fixed point makes F (an annulment by an act under a self-supporting loop). The Principal's restriction of D removes this class.
- **U:** the record does not decide. **Its causes, derived (Ded):**
  - **(a) U rests on a U.** At a fixed point of Fitting's operator (KK, and on D also WF), if an atom is U then some rule body is U and none is T, so a body literal is U: every U atom depends on a U atom.
  - **(b) The walk reaches a cycle.** Following such dependencies through finitely many atoms reaches a cycle of U atoms.
  - **(c) On D the cycle has a negative edge.** Positive dependence goes to earlier entries, so the cycle contains a negative edge, or it is a covers atom's u ← not u.
  - **(d) The three causes.** Hence every U on D rests on an undefined covers pair, on a cycle through a negative edge, or (v8) on a **tie**: two acts at the same act time whose order is read (C19, T1-12, T2-09). This is why a refusal reason always exists (§9.13.3).

  **The named cycles:**

The reason in (i) names each (check 15).

**R2a. **R3. Self-supporting authority, and why D allows citations of earlier entries only (the restriction is the Principal's decision; the lemma is Ded).**

- **Lemma (KK = WF without positive cycles; Ded, the version adapted to this model).** If the positive dependencies of the program reading (§9.13.3) form an acyclic graph, the well-founded model of the program equals its Kripke–Kleene model. ## 9.13.6 What follows for the results that depended on CE

- **§9.8 Lemma D** ("if … CE is defined and deterministic for the chosen θ on every cut"):
  - **Inside one record:** a prefix of a record in D is evaluable *(since v9, not necessarily in D itself: a tombstone's removal act may lie after the cut; corrected at the consolidation S0101–S0130, I-09)*. It keeps the order, the fields and the per-entry integrity check, and every citation still names an earlier entry, now inside the cut. So CE* is defined and deterministic on every cut of one record in D, for Θ_f.
  - **A cut across records** (F26) is the merge of several records' prefixes. It is in D only if the merged order keeps every citation pointing to an earlier position (§9.13.2, design limit). So Lemma D's condition on CE is met for a cut across records **only if the merged cut is in D**: a condition on F26 (c), not a consequence of D (turn 5, A1-09, A2-08).
  - An act's value can still change from cut to cut, through endings and annulments recorded later; Lemma D needs definedness on every cut, not monotonicity in the cut. Its other premises (H1′, H2, H3, F26 for the cut across records) are unchanged.
- **§9.9 T1** ("T1 is conditional on CE"): premise (vi)'s adjudicating act counts if **authorized(x)** is T in the fixed point (the act's authorization, not current-authority). T1's condition on CE is met on D for Θ_f, and on a merged record only if the merge is in D.
- **§9.10 Q1 and Q3:** their reference definitions exist **on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute (decided by the Principal)**: Q1 is CE*(k, x, t; R≤s); Q3 is the construction of R5. The present record is not in D.

## 9.13.7 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

**Internally:** on D and for Θ_f, CE* is a function of the record: it always answers, or refuses with a reason. Every U carries a refusal reason (§9.13.3): the self-cancelling act, both acts of a mutual annulment, another named negative cycle, or an undefined covers pair. Its T and F are forced by the record (KK = WF on D, R3; current-authority is monotone, R2); its U values are not.

**About the world:**
- **The integrity check is sound** (key binding, key custody, §9.12.7). This is load-bearing for security, not hygiene: CE* refuses a record with a failing entry, but an entry **removed without trace** leaves a record in D whose answers change. A removed annulment turns its target to T. A removal **by tombstone** keeps the entry's identity, kind, required fields, position and integrity result, so values do not change; the answers are marked "rests on removed content" (v6; under the Professor's reading of the retained fields and the premise that the tombstone's own integrity check is defined, §9.13.2). A removal **without trace** (no tombstone) is a K1 failure (§9.15), which no per-entry check sees.
- **The recorded act times are the ones the organisation recognises** (under A1 recognized they are, by definition; under A1 as fact they must be true times).
- **The root acts are made by whoever the organisation designates.**
- **The recorded acts really occurred.** C1 = constitute makes the record constitutive **within the kernel** only. Whether a recorded act occurred is a separate question about the world (the Principal, 2026-10-20; §9.9's residual trust assumption).

**About the implementation:** steps are serialised (§9.11.5).
