## 9.13.1 The research question

**Can §1.14.1's evaluator be repaired into a function that gives exactly one answer, T, F or U, for every record in a stated domain and every parameter setting in a stated set? If so: on what does its answer rest, and when is the answer T or F rather than U? If not: which defect blocks it?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a record in the domain on which the repaired evaluator gives no answer, or two answers; or a record on which its T or F contradicts A1 recognized. On D every T and F is **forced** (R2, R3): it holds in every fixed point of Φ.

## 9.13.2 The domain and the parameters

**The domain D: well-formed records, by a checkable list** (model: `in_D`).
- A finite sequence of entries, with unique identifiers and one record position each.
- **Order of acts:** the key (t_x, s_x), act time first and position on ties; this is F26 (a), or (c) with its tie rule. F26 (b), a partial order, is outside D.
- **Admitted kinds and required fields:**
  - root act: actor, scope, act time;
  - grant issuance: actor, cited bases, **holder**, scope, act time, start and **end** of period (an open-ended grant records end = ∞);
  - adoption: actor, cited bases, item, scope, act time, validity;
  - revocation: actor, cited bases, item, scope, act time;
  - annulment: actor, cited bases, target, scope, act time;
  - grant ending: actor, cited bases, grant, scope, act time.
- **Not admitted:** supersession, suspension, consumption, the acts that set θ, and O1's other kinds (registration, link, retirement, establishment, declassification; CON-0188). A record holding one of them is outside D until its clause is defined.
- **Integrity:** every entry has passed its integrity check (§9.12 I13). **A record with an entry that fails it is outside D, and Q1 refuses it** with the failing entry named. It does not answer on the remaining entries, because dropping a failing annulment, revocation or ending would turn the acts it targets to T (model check 13): refusing is fail-closed, dropping is not.
- **Citations: of earlier entries only** (the Principal, 2026-10-20).
  - **"Earlier" means an earlier record position.** This, and the clauses below on dangling citations and targets, are the Professor's reading, consistent with the decision. Act times are not compared, so a grant recorded earlier may carry a later act time, and an act may cite it. The decision excludes authorization after the fact **in record order**; in act time, a window starting before the grant's issuance, and backdating, are admitted and open (§9.13.10; turn 6, A1-04, A2-03).
  - An entry that cites an entry at its own or a later position puts the record outside D, and Q1 refuses it, naming that citation (model check 13).
  - **A citation of an entry that is not in the record (dangling) puts the record outside D** (v5, the Professor's reading of "citations of earlier entries only"). Q1 refuses the record and names the citation (model check 13).
    - Version 4 tolerated it as F, "the safe direction". That was **false for negative acts** (turn 6, A1-02): a removed grant makes an annulment, revocation or ending citing it F, so its target turns T. That is the failure the integrity rule refuses.
    - A removal under H1′ (a recorded act with a loss entry) therefore also puts the record outside D, until CE* reads loss entries (I12; open, §9.13.10).
  - **The append guard and the static test now agree** (turn 6, A1-03, A2-01). An entry may be appended iff its citations name entries already present. A record is in D iff every citation names an earlier entry present in it. A citation that names nothing can no longer become a forward citation when the cited entry arrives later.
  - **Targets** (of an annulment, an ending or a revocation) are **not restricted**. A target may be an entry recorded later: an annulment recorded before its target voids the target when it arrives (pre-emptive annulment), and mutual annulment exists in D only through such a forward target. Pre-assigned identifiers may therefore be used for targets, though not for citations. `in_D` does not check a target's kind; a mistyped target has no effect.
  - Under this rule the least fixed point (forced) and the well-founded (grounded) readings coincide (R3). The rule is **sufficient, not necessary**, for that: R3's lemma needs only that the citation graph has no cycle (R3, A1-01 of turn 5).
- **The design limit.** An authority pattern that would need a citation of a later entry cannot be recorded as such: mutual delegation, a chain **recorded** leaf first, and a grant citing a grant still to be recorded. Such patterns **must be written in order**, each grant after the grants it cites. A self-supporting loop of grants therefore cannot be recorded at all. It is not answered F; it is refused (outside D). Consequences:
  - identifiers pre-assigned before recording cannot be used to cite forward;
  - merging records under F26 (c) must keep every citation pointing to an earlier position in the merged order, or the merge leaves D (§9.13.6, Lemma D);
  - **availability:** D is closed under prefixes but **not under appending**. One appended entry that cites an entry not present, repeats an identifier or fails its integrity check puts the whole record outside D, and Q1 then refuses every question on it, although earlier cuts were answered. So does a removal of a cited entry. Refusal is per record, which is fail-closed;
  - **the cost beyond what R3 needs.** Of these patterns only mutual delegation and self-supporting loops are cycles. A chain recorded leaf first, and a citation of a grant recorded later, are acyclic; R3's lemma would hold for them. The rival domain **D_acyclic** (refuse a record iff its citation graph has a cycle; checkable) has the same guarantee, every T and F forced, with the smaller design limit "no citation cycle". **Decided by the Principal (2026-10-20, after the turn-5 settlement; decision record, addendum 2026-10-20T19:03:44Z): keep D.** The reason, in the Principal's words: "Under C1, a grant exists only once recorded, so a citation of a later grant would be authorization after the fact. D is also checkable at each new entry."
    - *The decision record's wording* (addendum 19:03:44Z, the Rule Reviewer's recommendation accepted by the Principal): "under C1 (records constitute authority) a grant exists only once recorded, so citing a grant recorded later would be authorization after the fact; and D is checkable at each new entry (a law on each step), while D_acyclic needs a check of the whole record and admits citations that point at nothing until the cited entry arrives."
    - Under C1 = constitute, an act citing a grant not yet recorded cites authority that did not yet exist **in record order**: authorization after the fact.
    - D's check at each new entry is the append guard above. D_acyclic can also be checked incrementally, by a reachability search from the new entry, so the difference is one of cost, not of kind (turn 6, A1-05). Since v5, D also admits no citation that points at nothing.
    - D_acyclic stays recorded as the rival, with the same mathematical guarantee and a smaller design limit.

## 9.13.3 The definition

Let the atoms be authorized(x), one per act x. A **three-valued assignment** gives each atom T, F or U. The **information order**: U is below T and below F. Strong Kleene connectives (*Ext:* Kleene 1952).

**The operator Φ.** For a root act, Φ(a)(x) = T. For any other act x:
- F if x cites no basis;
- else (⋁ over cited bases g of basis(g, x)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of x of a(e)), where
  - for a **root** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's actor is x's actor) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for a **grant** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's holder is x's actor) ∧ (t_x in g's window [start, end)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over endings e of g, e ≠ x, key(e) < key(x), of a(e)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of g, e ≠ x, of a(e)) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for any other entry, or a citation not in the record: F.

A grant is itself an act whose issuance must be authorized (a(g); defect 2). The clauses e ≠ x exclude x's own effects (§1.14.1: "an ending that **is** *x*"); the case "x annuls the very grant it relies on" is not excluded beyond that, and gives U (defect 6).

**The program reading** (for WF, R1 step 8): each basis that passes its record constants (holder, window, covers ≠ F) gives a rule x ← g, [u], not e₁, …, not eₖ, with the endings and annulments negative; a root is a fact; a covers pair with value U adds an atom u with the rule u ← not u. **Φ is Fitting's operator of this program on the act atoms (Ded).** In strong Kleene logic ∧ distributes over ∨, so (⋁_g basis(g, x)) ∧ ¬A_x = ⋁_g (basis(g, x) ∧ ¬A_x), one disjunct per rule. A basis whose record constants fail gives F in Φ and has no rule, which also gives F. A covers constant U behaves as the atom u ← not u, which is U in both readings. So the least fixed point of Φ is the program's Kripke–Kleene model on the acts. The model builds the program separately (`program`) and confirms the identity (check 6).

**current-authority(c, x, t)**, for item c, queried scope x and valid time t: F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute; defect 3); else ⋁ over adoptions y of c with t_y ≤ t of: authorized(y) ∧ t in y's validity ∧ covers(scope(y), x) ∧ ¬(an effective revocation of c with key above y's and time ≤ t) ∧ every cited grant of y intact at t (its period not ended by t, and no effective ending of it with key above y's and time ≤ t).

**Two readings of the fixed point:**
- **(KK) the least fixed point of Φ** in the information order: the Kripke–Kleene model (*Ext:* Kripke 1975; Fitting 1985);
- **(WF) the well-founded model of the program reading:** it makes **positive loops**, authority supported only by itself, false (*Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991; equivalently the alternating fixpoint, Van Gelder 1989/1993; the well-founded fixpoint of the approximating operator, Denecker, Marek and Truszczyński 2000).

**CE\*(c, x, t; R≤s)** = current-authority read off the fixed point of the cut R≤s. On D, KK and WF are the same fixed point (R3), so CE* is read off either. It is **undefined**, with the reason, if R≤s is not in D.

**Output:** the value. For U it adds the **refusal reason** (model: `ce_star_explained`; for one act's authorization, `act_explained`; checks 9b, 11b, 15, 15b). The reason names what the U rests on:
- **(i)** every **strongly connected component** of U atoms with a cycle, found by walking what the answer rests on (v5, turn 6, A1-06, A2-04–A2-06).
  - The walk starts only from current-authority's alternatives whose value is U: the U adoption, a U revocation, or a U ending they consult.
  - From there it follows only the U literals of rule bodies that are themselves U; a body that is F supports nothing.
  - By R2 the walk always reaches a component with a cycle, and on D every such component has a negative edge.

  Each component is named once:
  - a **self-annulling act**: an annulment whose target is itself;
  - a **self-cancelling act**: an annulment whose target is a link of its own authorization chain, a grant its authority rests on, directly or through the grants it acts under. The reason names the act and the link;
  - a **mutual annulment** of two acts;
  - an **annulment ring** of three or more;
  - otherwise **a negative cycle** through the named acts, for example through an ending: a grant recorded earlier with a later act time, and an ending citing it.

  A component may hold several cycles; the reason names the component, not every cycle in it.
- **(ii)** every undefined covers pair on a live rule;
- **(iii)** an undefined covers pair between an adoption's scope and the queried scope, **for an alternative whose value is U** (turn 6, A2-05).

The model checks this (v5):
- **check 15b:** on 3,000 random records in D, all 165 U answers, for items and for acts, had a named reason;
- **check 15c:** on 2,000 records in D, mostly rich in annulments, with and without a queried scope, every one of the 68 acts named in a reason was U. This is a soundness check of the names. It does not show that the reason is the smallest one. The headline and the reasons for T and F of §1.14.1's answer specification are not yet defined as a function of the fixed point (open, §9.13.10).

## 9.13.4 Results

**R1. Totality and uniqueness (Ded, with proof).** `[CON-0191 · provisional]` For every record in D and the parameters Θ_f: KK and WF exist and are unique. KK is reached from the bottom (all U, roots T) in at most n + 1 applications of Φ, **n the number of non-root acts** (n changing applications and one confirming; turn 6, A1-08, A2-09). WF is reached by the alternating fixpoint in at most n + 1 outer rounds, each two least-model computations of at most n + 1 passes: O(n²) passes.

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Φ is built from strong Kleene ∧, ∨, ¬ over atoms and record constants | §9.13.3: holder identity, windows, keys and covers are constants of the record; covers = U is a constant |
| 2 | strong Kleene connectives are monotone in the information order | **Src:** the corpus uses exactly these tables ("T\land U=U" [S0875 L879]) and its verifier checked them "by exhaustion" to be "monotone in the knowledge order (U ⊑ T, U ⊑ F)" (S1498 L133, in meaning); *Ext benchmark:* Kleene 1952; Kripke 1975 |
| 3 | so Φ is monotone | composition of monotone maps |
| 4 | the assignments over n atoms, ordered by information, have finite height: an ascending chain changes each non-root atom at most once (U to T or to F) | n finite; three values |
| 5 | iterating Φ from the bottom gives an ascending chain | 3, and bottom ≤ Φ(bottom) (roots T in both, others U) |
| 6 | the chain is constant after at most n steps, so it stops within n + 1 applications; its limit is a fixed point, and the least one (it lies below every fixed point by induction along the chain) | 4, 5. **Src, analogous:** the corpus's inference closure for a finite monotonic rule set is the same kind of Kleene iteration on a finite lattice, two-valued and without negation: K_{i+1} = K_i ∪ Infer(K_i, R) until it stops, "we reach a fixed point" [S0875 L1079] |
| 7 | the least fixed point is unique | it is least |
| 8 | WF exists and is unique; the alternating fixpoint reaches it | the program reading (§9.13.3) is a finite ground normal program; *Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991 (definition by unfounded sets); Van Gelder 1993 (the alternating fixpoint equals it). The underestimate of true atoms grows strictly in every outer round but the last, so there are at most n + 1 outer rounds; each least-model computation stops within n + 1 passes |

The model agrees, on the Φ as written: Φ monotone on 800 random pairs, with backward and forward citations (check 1); KK within n + 1 applications on 4,000 random records (worst 0.6 of the bound), and a chain of 8 grants takes 8 applications for n + 1 = 9, both recorded root first (in D) and leaf first (outside D) (check 2); WF within n + 1 outer rounds on 2,000 records (check 2b); the two routes (Φ on the record; the separate program) give the same KK and the same WF on 12,000 records from four generators (check 6).

**Scope.** R1 is proved for Φ as written, that is, for Θ_f. It does not cover the values Θ_f excludes (at-the-time, joint bases, ω on the intersection, σ, other P or ι, C1 = represent): each needs its clause, and then the proof again.

**R2. What T, F, U and "undefined" mean (Ded).**
- **T and F are forced:** every fixed point of Φ lies above KK (KK is least), so a T or F of KK holds in **every** fixed point. It is what every assignment of authorization consistent with Φ gives. On D, WF = KK (R3). current-authority combines the act values with strong Kleene connectives and record constants, so it is monotone in the act assignment, and a T or F read off KK holds in every fixed point too: **every T and F of CE* is forced.** U values are not forced. In the mutual-annulment record (in D), (T, F) and (F, T) are also total fixed points, beside (U, U) (check 3b, under υ = U).
- **Why D needed this (records outside D).** With forward citations, WF can add **grounded** values: they hold only because authority supported by itself is counted as not authorized, and they are not forced. Model check 5 enumerates all 3^k assignments on 300 loop-rich records with forward citations: 331 fixed points, 31 other than KK, all above KK. 31 fixed points are not above WF; 15 of them are other than KK, and the other 16 are KK itself (v4 run, joint count). *(Version 3 said "the same 27", which conflated the two counts; corrected at turn 5.)* On check 7's record (two grants each issued under the other) the all-T assignment is a total fixed point, yet WF gives F. On check 8's record WF gives T to an adoption that another total fixed point makes F (an annulment by an act under a self-supporting loop). The Principal's restriction of D removes this class.
- **U:** the record does not decide. **Its causes, derived (Ded):**
  - **(a) U rests on a U.** At a fixed point of Fitting's operator (KK, and on D also WF), if an atom is U then some rule body is U and none is T, so a body literal is U: every U atom depends on a U atom.
  - **(b) The walk reaches a cycle.** Following such dependencies through finitely many atoms reaches a cycle of U atoms.
  - **(c) On D the cycle has a negative edge.** Positive dependence goes to earlier entries, so the cycle contains a negative edge, or it is a covers atom's u ← not u.
  - **(d) The two causes.** Hence every U on D rests on an undefined covers pair or on a cycle through a negative edge. This is why a refusal reason always exists (§9.13.3).

  **The named cycles:**
  - A **self-cancelling act** (a holder annulling the grant it acts under, check 11) gives **U with a refusal reason** (the Principal, 2026-10-20; check 11b), not a value by causal priority, which the book does not define.
  - **Mutual annulment** (two acts each annulling the other, check 9) gives **U with a refusal reason naming both acts** (the Principal, 2026-10-20, confirming the extension proposed in this section; check 9b).
  - **More negative cycles than these two exist in D** (turn 5, A1-02, A2-03): indirect self-cancelling (an act under a child grant annulling the parent), annulment rings of three or more, and cycles through endings. Version 3, and the decision record's second addendum, called mutual annulment "the other negative cycle"; that premise was false. All of them give U, which the semantics forces. The reason in (i) names each (check 15).
  - **Decided by the Principal (2026-10-20, after the turn-5 settlement; addendum 19:03:44Z): decision 3 covers indirect self-cancelling.** The record's words: "an act that annuls any link of its own authorization chain gives U, with a refusal reason naming the act and the annulled link".
    - **What Φ does (turn 6, A1-01).** Φ gives such an act U **when the cycle through its own link decides it**. It gives **T** when the act has another intact basis, and **F** when the link was already defeated, for example ended earlier (check 15d).
    - **The book applies the decision as:** "**when** such an act's value is U, the refusal reason names the act and the link" (check 15: "self-cancelling act e: it annuls g1, a link of its own authorization chain (through the grants it acts under)").
    - **Returned to the Principal.** Is that the intended reading, or should U be forced by rule? Forcing it would need a new clause in Φ.
    - **Two further readings of "link",** the Professor's (A2-07), are also returned:
      - **a root** is cited directly, but a root cannot be annulled (Θ_f), so an act annulling its root is T;
      - **an unneeded cited basis** (the act is authorized through another) is not counted as a link.
- **Undefined:** the cut is outside D (a failing integrity check, a missing required field, a kind not admitted, a citation of a later entry, no total order), or the parameters are outside Θ_f. Q1 must refuse with the reason (§9.10.2), not answer (check 13).

**R2a. The corpus's four results (Src; Int).** The corpus's reasoning step distinguishes four results, Derived, Refuted, Undetermined and Inapplicable (S0875 L1733–1736, in meaning), and says that when the knowledge is insufficient the kernel "should return" NotDerivable "rather than" False [S0875 L1700] (in meaning). CE*'s values map onto them: T = derived recognition; U = undetermined; undefined = inapplicable; **F = refuted, or not recognized**. The F of Φ arises from: no basis cited; a basis failing its record constants; an effective ending or annulment (refuted); no adoption on record (not recognized: a **closed-world** answer about recognition, nothing recorded, nothing recognized). The corpus's NotDerivable is the **open-world** stance, which would be C1 = represent. **The Principal chose C1 = constitute within the kernel** (2026-10-20). So the closed-world F holds inside the kernel. The open-world question, whether a recorded act really occurred, stays an assumption about the world (§9.9), and is not answered by CE*.

**R3. Self-supporting authority, and why D allows citations of earlier entries only (the restriction is the Principal's decision; the lemma is Ded).**
- **The case.** Take two grants, each issued under the other, with no root in the chain, and an adoption that cites one of them. KK gives U for all three; WF gives F (check 7). With forward citations the two readings differ, and WF's F is grounded, not forced (R2). Version 2 kept WF as a definitional choice (Int) and named acyclic citations as the rival.
- **The decision.** The Principal took the rival (2026-10-20), and confirmed it after the turn-5 settlement with its reason (§9.13.2): D allows citations of earlier entries only. The decision record calls this "the corpus's acyclic recommendation". The corpus recommends an **acyclic** derivation graph (S1486 on S0890), which is weaker than "earlier entries only" (turn 5, A1-01). The loop case cannot then be recorded. It lies outside D and is refused, not answered F (design limit, §9.13.2). On D the readings coincide.
- **Lemma (KK = WF without positive cycles; Ded, the version adapted to this model).** If the positive dependencies of the program reading (§9.13.3) form an acyclic graph, the well-founded model of the program equals its Kripke–Kleene model. On D, record position is such an order, so the lemma holds on D. It also holds on every record without a citation cycle (which makes the program **tight**; tightness itself concerns only the positive cycles of live rules, so citation-acyclic is sufficient, not equivalent; turn 6, A1-08), in or out of D: on 3,228 such records among 6,000 with forward citations, 1,718 of them outside D, WF = KK on all (check 14).

  Only positive dependence is restricted. Targets are free: an annulment recorded after its target is the normal case. A negative dependence may therefore close a cycle, and U remains (check 11). The model agrees:

So the corpus links exogenous authority with acyclicity. It does not state that the readings coincide. Outside:
  - **Apt and Bezem 1991** call a program **acyclic** if some level mapping makes every body literal, positive **and** negative, lower than the head (their Definition 1.4). They show that its immediate consequence operator has a unique fixpoint, which is also the unique fixpoint of Fitting's three-valued operator (their abstract). KK is then two-valued, and WF, lying above it, equals it. That is the **established special case**, under a stronger hypothesis. A negative dependence on a later entry does not by itself break their acyclicity; a **cycle** through a negative edge does. D's programs can contain such cycles: a self-cancelling act, a mutual annulment, and the u ← not u rule of every undefined covers pair. ## 9.13.6 What follows for the results that depended on CE

- **§9.8 Lemma D** ("if … CE is defined and deterministic for the chosen θ on every cut"):
  - **Inside one record:** a prefix of a record in D is in D. It keeps the order, the fields and the per-entry integrity check, and every citation still names an earlier entry, now inside the cut. So CE* is defined and deterministic on every cut of one record in D, for Θ_f.
  - **A cut across records** (F26) is the merge of several records' prefixes. It is in D only if the merged order keeps every citation pointing to an earlier position (§9.13.2, design limit). So Lemma D's condition on CE is met for a cut across records **only if the merged cut is in D**: a condition on F26 (c), not a consequence of D (turn 5, A1-09, A2-08).
  - An act's value can still change from cut to cut, through endings and annulments recorded later; Lemma D needs definedness on every cut, not monotonicity in the cut. Its other premises (H1′, H2, H3, F26 for the cut across records) are unchanged.
- **§9.9 T1** ("T1 is conditional on CE"): premise (vi)'s adjudicating act counts if **authorized(x)** is T in the fixed point (the act's authorization, not current-authority). T1's condition on CE is met on D for Θ_f, and on a merged record only if the merge is in D.
- **§9.10 Q1 and Q3:** their reference definitions exist **on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute (decided by the Principal)**: Q1 is CE*(k, x, t; R≤s); Q3 is the construction of R5. The present record is not in D.

## 9.13.7 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

**Internally:** on D and for Θ_f, CE* is a function of the record: it always answers, or refuses with a reason. Every U carries a refusal reason (§9.13.3): the self-cancelling act, both acts of a mutual annulment, another named negative cycle, or an undefined covers pair. Its T and F are forced by the record (KK = WF on D, R3; current-authority is monotone, R2); its U values are not.

**About the world:**
- **The integrity check is sound** (key binding, key custody, §9.12.7). This is load-bearing for security, not hygiene: CE* refuses a record with a failing entry, but an entry **removed without trace** leaves a record in D whose answers change. A removed annulment turns its target to T. A removed grant leaves a dangling citation, which since v5 puts the record outside D, so Q1 refuses it (fail-closed, §9.13.2). Version 4 called it "F, the safe direction"; that was false for negative acts (turn 6, A1-02).
- **The recorded act times are the ones the organisation recognises** (under A1 recognized they are, by definition; under A1 as fact they must be true times).
- **The root acts are made by whoever the organisation designates.**
- **The recorded acts really occurred.** C1 = constitute makes the record constitutive **within the kernel** only. Whether a recorded act occurred is a separate question about the world (the Principal, 2026-10-20; §9.9's residual trust assumption).

**About the implementation:** steps are serialised (§9.11.5).
