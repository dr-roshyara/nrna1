# 9.13 The evaluator of current authority, CE*: a total function on a stated domain

*Theory turn 4 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: the evaluator of standing (Q1's reference definition): its inputs, its output, the authority it relies on, and when its answer is valid, unique or undefined (TODO-TRIAGE M2). **Prior work, corpus first (practice 2, revised):** six logged scans of the allowed corpus (fixed point; Kleene and related names; three-valued; the evaluator's own terms; circular or self-authorization; delegation chains and roots); a selection rule fixed before reading (most patterns matched, at least one authority or fixed-point pattern, index order, at most 8); after a relevance check at the hit lines, four sources read whole as topic-full (S0875, S1496, S1498, S1761) and two read at their relevant sections (S1414, S1486); the corpus findings are in §9.13.8. A finite model checks the form (`tmp/tools/ce_model.py`, run `ce_model-run-20261020.txt`). **Status: draft for review by two reviewers.***

## 9.13.1 The research question

**Can §1.14.1's evaluator be repaired into a function that gives exactly one answer, T, F or U, for every record in a stated domain and every parameter setting in a stated set? If so: on what does its answer rest, and when is the answer T or F rather than U? If not: which defect blocks it?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a record in the domain on which the repaired evaluator gives no answer, or two answers; or a record on which its T or F contradicts A1 recognized (a standing change recognized without a recorded, authorized act grounded in a root, or refused although one exists).

## 9.13.2 The domain and the parameters

- **The domain D: well-formed records.** A finite sequence of entries with unique identifiers and one total record order (F26 (a), or (c) with its tie rule; (b) below), whose entries have passed the integrity check (§9.12 I13; an entry that fails it is not in the record CE* reads). Every entry kind is one of: root act, grant issuance, adoption, revocation, supersession, annulment, grant ending. A citation of an entry that is not in the record is allowed: it is simply not a basis.
- **The parameter set Θ_m:**
  - υ = "an act of undetermined authorization counts as undetermined" (§1.14.1's first value). The other value is excluded: it makes the operator non-monotone (§9.13.5, defect 1);
  - C1 = "records constitute authority" and κ = constitutive (§9.12 B2; C1 is returned to the Principal);
  - ρ ∈ {at-the-time, now}; P (the ending policy) any; ι any; χ, ω any;
  - **covers**: a given relation between grant scopes and act scopes. A pair outside the relation's domain gives U (§1.14.1 defect 5: covers was undefined; here it is an input);
  - **the actor–holder link:** every grant names its holder; an act's actor matches a grant's holder iff they are the same identity (§1.14.1 defect 7: a grant naming only an issuer gives U for the link).

## 9.13.3 The definition

Let the atoms be authorized(x), one per act x. A **three-valued assignment** gives each atom T, F or U. The **information order**: U is below T and below F. Strong Kleene connectives (*Ext:* Kleene 1952).

**The operator Φ.** For a root act, authorized(x) = T. For any other act x, Φ(a)(x) is
- F if x cites no basis;
- else (⋁ over cited bases g of ⋀ of: a(g); g's holder is x's actor; x's act time lies in g's window; no effective ending of g with time ≤ t_x; g not annulled; covers(scope(g), scope(x))) ∧ ¬(x annulled),

where "effective" for an ending or annulment e means a(e), under υ = undetermined, and a grant is itself an act whose issuance must be authorized (§1.14.1 defect 2). A grant's window closes at its recorded end (defect 3).

**current-authority(c, t)** = F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute; defect 3); else ⋁ over adoptions x of c with t_x ≤ t of: authorized(x) ∧ t in x's validity ∧ ¬(an effective revocation of c ordered after x with time ≤ t) ∧ every cited grant intact at t (not ended by its period and no effective ending after x, under P).

**Two readings of the fixed point, both established:**
- **(KK) the least fixed point of Φ** in the information order: the Kripke–Kleene model (*Ext:* Kripke 1975; Fitting 1985);
- **(WF) the well-founded model:** the alternating fixed point that makes **positive loops**, authority supported only by itself, false (*Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991). Positive dependence here is authorization through a cited grant; negative dependence is the absence of endings and annulments.

**CE\*** = current-authority read off **WF** (the choice is argued in §9.13.4 R3).

## 9.13.4 Results

**R1. Totality and uniqueness (Ded, with proof).** `[CON-0191 · provisional]` For every record in D and every θ ∈ Θ_m, KK and WF exist, are unique, and are reached from the bottom (all U, roots T) in at most n + 1 rounds, n the number of acts.

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Φ is built from strong Kleene ∧, ∨, ¬ over atoms and constants | the definition; times, positions, holder identities and covers on its domain are constants of the record |
| 2 | strong Kleene connectives are monotone in the information order | **Src:** the corpus uses exactly these tables ("T\land U=U" [S0875 L879]) and its verifier checked them "by exhaustion" to be "monotone in the knowledge order (U ⊑ T, U ⊑ F)" (S1498 L133, in meaning); *Ext benchmark:* Kleene 1952; Kripke 1975 |
| 3 | so Φ is monotone | composition of monotone maps |
| 4 | assignments over n atoms, ordered by information, form a finite partial order in which every chain is bounded | n finite; three values |
| 5 | iterating Φ from the bottom gives an ascending chain (Φ monotone, bottom ≤ Φ(bottom)) | 3 |
| 6 | each atom changes at most once along it (U to T or to F), so the chain is constant after at most n steps, and its limit is the least fixed point | 4, 5. **Src:** the corpus's inference closure is the same construction for a finite monotonic rule set: K_{i+1} = K_i ∪ Infer(K_i, R) until it stops, "we reach a fixed point" [S0875 L1079] |
| 7 | the least fixed point is unique (it is least) | definition |
| 8 | WF exists and is unique: the alternating fixed point of two monotone least-model computations over finite sets terminates | Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991 (Ext) |

The model agrees: Φ monotone on 400 random pairs; the least fixed point reached within n + 1 rounds on 2,000 random records (worst case half the bound) (`ce_model.py` checks 1, 2).

**R2. What T and F mean, and when the answer is U (Ded).** Every fixed point of Φ lies above KK in the information order (KK is least). So a T or F of KK holds in **every** fixed point: it is forced by the record (model check 5: 18,624 total fixed points found, all extending KK). WF extends KK (model check 6). Hence:
- **T or F is valid** for A1 recognized, on records in D: it is what any consistent assignment of authorization to the recorded acts gives;
- **U means the record does not decide**: a missing basis whose coverage is undefined, a negative cycle (two acts each annulling the other: U in KK and WF, check 3, 8), or an undetermined actor–holder link;
- **undefined** happens only **outside D or Θ_m**: an unchecked entry, a record without one order, or υ = "does not count until evidenced". Q1 must refuse such an input with its reason (§9.10.2), not answer it.

**R3. Why WF and not KK: circular self-authorization (Ded; a counterexample).** Two grants, each issued under the other, with no root in the chain, and an adoption citing one of them. KK gives U for all three; WF gives F (check 7). Under A1 recognized with C1 = constitute, a standing change is recognized only if a recorded act **grounded in a root** effects it; a chain that supports itself is not grounded, so the answer is F. **Src:** the corpus states the same rule: a party "granting itself authority without an external basis" [S1414 L1273] "should normally be invalid unless the domain explicitly permits self-constituting authority." [S1414 L1281]; and a later verification finds that the estate's authority is "EXOGENOUS in origin and ENDOGENOUSLY RECORDED" (S1761 L96, in meaning), with "an explicit refusal to self-authorize" [S1761 L150], although "a fixed-point construction over self-referential authority is consistent; nothing forces exogeneity" [S1761 L142]. So the corpus says what this result derives: self-reference is mathematically possible, and the estate excludes it by a rule. S1414's exception ("unless the domain explicitly permits") is this result's falsifier. KK's U would treat the loop as "not decided by the record", which fits C1 = represent (records as evidence of possibly unrecorded acts). **So the choice between KK and WF follows C1:** WF for C1 = constitute (the reading DB-1 implies), KK for C1 = represent. *Rival:* KK for both, on the ground that a loop is an anomaly to be reported, not decided. *Falsifier of the choice:* a self-supporting loop that the organisation recognises as authority.

**R4. The authority it relies on.** CE*'s T answers rest on: (i) the root acts (§9.10 O4), whose standing is stipulated, not derived; (ii) the chain of grant issuances from a root; (iii) the parameter values θ(u), which are themselves set by acts (§9.8 H3) — and the reserved ones (P, ρ, υ) by the Principal's decisions. So the evaluator relies on the organisation's root designation and on the acts that fix its own parameters. This is the regress of F31 (which meta-evaluator fixes the policy in force), now located: it ends at the root acts and at the acts that set θ.

**R2a. U is not "false", and F here is not "false in reality" (Src; Ded).** The corpus's reasoning step distinguishes four results, Derived, Refuted, Undetermined and Inapplicable (S0875 L1733–1735, in meaning), and says that when the knowledge is insufficient the kernel "should return" NotDerivable "rather than" False [S0875 L1700] (in meaning). CE*'s values map onto them: T = derived recognition, F = refuted recognition, U = undetermined, out of D = inapplicable. CE*'s F under C1 = constitute for a record with no adoption is a **closed-world** answer about recognition (nothing recorded, nothing recognized), not a claim about the world; the corpus's NotDerivable is the **open-world** stance, which is C1 = represent. This is the C1 question returned to the Principal (§9.12), now with the corpus's own position on the open-world side.

**R5. Q1's vacuity test, now decided (closing turn 1's obstacle for Q1).** Records exist on which CE* gives T and records on which it gives F (check 4). So a constant-U and a constant-T evaluator each fail Q1. Q3 (authorization) shares Φ: its reference definition is authorized(x) under WF, with covers and the actor–holder link as inputs, so Q3's obstacle is closed **on D** too. Q4's (a grading rule) remains.

## 9.13.5 §1.14.1's declared defects, one by one

| Defect | What CE* does | Status |
|---|---|---|

| 2. grant issuance and delegation not checked | a grant is an act; its issuance must be authorized (a(g) in Φ); delegation is issuance under a held grant | repaired (form) |
| 3. no adoption on record; annulled adoption; expiry | no adoption: F (C1 = constitute); an annulled adoption: F with the annulment as reason; a grant's window closes at its recorded end | repaired; C1 to the Principal |
| 4. act time vs order; recording time; same-moment race | one total order is part of D (F26 (a) or (c) with its tie rule); ρ = at-the-time uses positions | repaired for (a), (c); **(b), a partial order, is not modelled**: "ordered after" would become three-valued (U for incomparable acts), a constant of the record, so Φ stays monotone (Ded, not checked) |
| 5. σ recursing outside the fixed point; covers; ω's scope | supersession is a revocation conditional on the successor's adoption being authorized: one more positive clause inside Φ; covers is an input relation; ω reads the act's scope | **σ argued, not modelled**; covers and ω repaired |
| 6. a revoked actor can create a cycle | a negative cycle gives U in both KK and WF; under ρ = at-the-time with one order, evaluation is by position and has no cycles | stated; the policy choice (U or causal priority) is ρ |
| 7. the live records name an "authority" that may be the issuer, not the holder | D requires a holder field; a grant without one gives U for the link | repaired (by a domain condition) |

**§9.7's result 2 ("§1.14.1's CE is not yet a function for any θ") is withdrawn:** CE* is a function on D for every θ in Θ_m.

## 9.13.6 What follows for the results that depended on CE

- **§9.8 Lemma D** ("if … CE is defined and deterministic for the chosen θ on every cut"): a prefix of a record in D is in D (it keeps the order and the integrity check; citations of later entries are simply not bases), so CE* is defined on every cut for θ in Θ_m. **D's condition on CE is met** for θ ∈ Θ_m; its other premises (H1′, H2, H3, F26 for the cut across records) are unchanged.
- **§9.9 T1** ("T1 is conditional on CE"): premise (vi)'s adjudicating act counts if CE* gives it standing; T1's condition on CE is met on D for θ ∈ Θ_m.
- **§9.10 Q1 and Q3:** their reference definitions now exist on D (R5).
- **§9.7.5 condition 4** (agreement on T, F and U, E6 excepted): now testable against CE*.

## 9.13.7 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

Internally, CE* is a function of the record and θ: on D it always answers, and its T and F are forced by the record. It assumes, about the world: that the integrity check is sound (key binding, key custody, §9.12.7); that the recorded act times are the ones the organisation recognises (under A1 recognized they are, by definition; under A1 as fact they must be true times); that the root acts are made by whoever the organisation designates. About the implementation: that steps are serialised (§9.11.5).
