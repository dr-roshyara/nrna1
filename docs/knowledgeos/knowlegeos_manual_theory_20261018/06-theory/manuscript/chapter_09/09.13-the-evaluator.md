# 9.13 The evaluator of current authority, CE*: a total function on a stated domain

Deliverable: the evaluator of standing (Q1's reference definition): its inputs, its output, the authority it relies on, and when its answer is valid, unique or undefined (TODO-TRIAGE M2). **Prior work, corpus first (practice 2, revised):** six logged scans of the allowed corpus (fixed point; Kleene and related names; three-valued; the evaluator's own terms; circular or self-authorization; delegation chains and roots); a selection rule fixed before reading (most patterns matched, at least one authority or fixed-point pattern, index order, at most 8); after a relevance check at the hit lines, four sources read whole as topic-full (S0875, S1496, S1498, S1761) and two read at their relevant sections (S1414, S1486); the corpus findings are in §9.13.8. A finite model checks the form (`tmp/tools/ce_model.py` v2, run `ce_model-run-20261020-v2.txt`). **Status: version 2, settled after two reviews (`review/TURN4-EVALUATOR/SETTLEMENT.md`).** Version 1 claimed totality for every θ in a parameter set Φ did not implement, and called WF's answers "forced"; both are corrected here.*

## 9.13.1 The research question

**Can §1.14.1's evaluator be repaired into a function that gives exactly one answer, T, F or U, for every record in a stated domain and every parameter setting in a stated set? If so: on what does its answer rest, and when is the answer T or F rather than U? If not: which defect blocks it?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a record in the domain on which the repaired evaluator gives no answer, or two answers; or a record on which its **forced** T or F (§9.13.4 R2) contradicts A1 recognized. A **grounded** value (R2) can be disproved only by rejecting the definition that produces it: an organisation that recognises a self-supporting chain (R3's falsifier).

## 9.13.2 The domain and the parameters

**The domain D: well-formed records, by a checkable list** (model: `in_D`).
- A finite sequence of entries, with unique identifiers and one record position each.
- **Order of acts:** the key (t_x, s_x), act time first and position on ties; this is F26 (a), or (c) with its tie rule. F26 (b), a partial order, is outside D.
- **Admitted kinds and required fields:**
  - root act: actor, scope, act time;
  - grant issuance: actor, cited bases, **holder**, scope, act time, start and **end** of period (an open-ended grant records end = ∞);
  - adoption: actor, cited bases, item, scope, act time, validity;
  - revocation: actor, cited bases, item, scope, act time;
  - annulment: actor, cited bases, target, scope, act time;
  - grant ending: actor, cited bases, grant, scope, act time.
- **Not admitted:** supersession, suspension, consumption, the acts that set θ, and O1's other kinds (registration, link, retirement, establishment, declassification; CON-0188). A record holding one of them is outside D until its clause is defined.
- **Integrity:** every entry has passed its integrity check (§9.12 I13). **A record with an entry that fails it is outside D, and Q1 refuses it** with the failing entry named. It does not answer on the remaining entries, because dropping a failing annulment, revocation or ending would turn the acts it targets to T (model check 13): refusing is fail-closed, dropping is not.
- **Citations:** a citation may name any entry in the record, earlier or later. A citation of an entry that is not in the record is not a basis.
- **Sub-domain D_<:** the records in which every citation and every target names an earlier position. On D_< the choice of R3 disappears (R3).

**The parameter set Θ_f: the values Φ implements, and only those.**

| Parameter (§1.14.1) | Value in Θ_f |

| C1, κ | records constitute authority; κ = constitutive (§9.12 B2). **C1 is returned to the Principal**; under C1 = represent no operator is defined here |
| ρ | **now** (all entries of the cut are read). An ending counts for x only if its key is below x's key; an annulment voids its target from the start, at any position. *At-the-time* is not implemented |

| P | for an adoption: authority lapses when a cited grant's period ends or an effective ending of it comes after the adoption, up to t. Delegated grants: **not** propagated (defect 2, open half) |
| ι | (b): no reason condition |
| C6 (a later conflicting adoption) | fixed: an earlier adoption stays current unless revoked; the conflict itself is Q5's matter |

**The present estate's record is not in D.** It keeps a sequence per record, not one order, and records act times for 2 of 210 transitions (§1.14.1, further evidence; §9.7), and its grant records may name the issuer, not the holder (defect 7). To enter D it would need one order of acts, act times, holder fields and grant ends on every entry.

## 9.13.3 The definition

Let the atoms be authorized(x), one per act x. A **three-valued assignment** gives each atom T, F or U. The **information order**: U is below T and below F. Strong Kleene connectives (*Ext:* Kleene 1952).

**The operator Φ.** For a root act, Φ(a)(x) = T. For any other act x:
- F if x cites no basis;
- else (⋁ over cited bases g of basis(g, x)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of x of a(e)), where
  - for a **root** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's actor is x's actor) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for a **grant** g: basis(g, x) = a(g) ∧ (g's holder is x's actor) ∧ (t_x in g's window [start, end)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over endings e of g, e ≠ x, key(e) < key(x), of a(e)) ∧ ¬(⋁ over annulments e of g, e ≠ x, of a(e)) ∧ covers(g, x);
  - for any other entry, or a citation not in the record: F.

A grant is itself an act whose issuance must be authorized (a(g); defect 2). The clauses e ≠ x exclude x's own effects (§1.14.1: "an ending that **is** *x*"); the case "x annuls the very grant it relies on" is not excluded beyond that, and gives U (defect 6).

**The program reading** (for WF, R1 step 8): each basis that passes its record constants (holder, window, covers ≠ F) gives a rule x ← g, [u], not e₁, …, not eₖ, with the endings and annulments negative; a root is a fact; a covers pair with value U adds an atom u with the rule u ← not u. The model builds this program separately (`program`) and checks that its Kripke–Kleene model equals the least fixed point of Φ (check 6).

**current-authority(c, x, t)**, for item c, queried scope x and valid time t: F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute; defect 3); else ⋁ over adoptions y of c with t_y ≤ t of: authorized(y) ∧ t in y's validity ∧ covers(scope(y), x) ∧ ¬(an effective revocation of c with key above y's and time ≤ t) ∧ every cited grant of y intact at t (its period not ended by t, and no effective ending of it with key above y's and time ≤ t).

**Two readings of the fixed point:**
- **(KK) the least fixed point of Φ** in the information order: the Kripke–Kleene model (*Ext:* Kripke 1975; Fitting 1985);
- **(WF) the well-founded model of the program reading:** it makes **positive loops**, authority supported only by itself, false (*Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991; equivalently the alternating fixpoint, Van Gelder 1989/1993; the well-founded fixpoint of the approximating operator, Denecker, Marek and Truszczyński 2000).

**CE\*(c, x, t; R≤s)** = current-authority read off **WF** of the cut R≤s; **undefined**, with the reason, if R≤s is not in D (the choice of WF is argued in R3). **Output:** the value only. The headline and reasons of §1.14.1's answer specification are not yet defined as a function of the fixed point (open, §9.13.10).

## 9.13.4 Results

**R1. Totality and uniqueness (Ded, with proof).** `[CON-0191 · provisional]` For every record in D and the parameters Θ_f: KK and WF exist and are unique. KK is reached from the bottom (all U, roots T) in at most n + 1 applications of Φ, n the number of acts. WF is reached by the alternating fixpoint in at most n + 1 outer rounds, each two least-model computations of at most n + 1 passes: O(n²) passes.

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Φ is built from strong Kleene ∧, ∨, ¬ over atoms and record constants | §9.13.3: holder identity, windows, keys and covers are constants of the record; covers = U is a constant |
| 2 | strong Kleene connectives are monotone in the information order | **Src:** the corpus uses exactly these tables ("T\land U=U" [S0875 L879]) and its verifier checked them "by exhaustion" to be "monotone in the knowledge order (U ⊑ T, U ⊑ F)" (S1498 L133, in meaning); *Ext benchmark:* Kleene 1952; Kripke 1975 |
| 3 | so Φ is monotone | composition of monotone maps |
| 4 | the assignments over n atoms, ordered by information, have finite height: an ascending chain changes each non-root atom at most once (U to T or to F) | n finite; three values |
| 5 | iterating Φ from the bottom gives an ascending chain | 3, and bottom ≤ Φ(bottom) (roots T in both, others U) |
| 6 | the chain is constant after at most n steps, so it stops within n + 1 applications; its limit is a fixed point, and the least one (it lies below every fixed point by induction along the chain) | 4, 5. **Src, analogous:** the corpus's inference closure for a finite monotonic rule set is the same kind of Kleene iteration on a finite lattice, two-valued and without negation: K_{i+1} = K_i ∪ Infer(K_i, R) until it stops, "we reach a fixed point" [S0875 L1079] |
| 7 | the least fixed point is unique | it is least |
| 8 | WF exists and is unique; the alternating fixpoint reaches it | the program reading (§9.13.3) is a finite ground normal program; *Ext:* Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf 1991 (definition by unfounded sets); Van Gelder 1993 (the alternating fixpoint equals it). The underestimate of true atoms grows strictly in every outer round but the last, so there are at most n + 1 outer rounds; each least-model computation stops within n + 1 passes |

The model agrees, on the Φ as written: Φ monotone on 800 random pairs, with backward and forward citations (check 1); KK within n + 1 applications on 4,000 random records (worst 0.6 of the bound), and a chain of 8 grants recorded leaf first takes 8 applications for n + 1 = 9 (check 2); WF within n + 1 outer rounds on 2,000 records (check 2b); the two routes (Φ on the record; the separate program) give the same KK and the same WF on 9,000 records (check 6).

**Scope.** R1 is proved for Φ as written, that is, for Θ_f. It does not cover the values Θ_f excludes (at-the-time, joint bases, ω on the intersection, σ, other P or ι, C1 = represent): each needs its clause, and then the proof again.

**R2. What T, F, U and "undefined" mean (Ded).** Two classes of decided answers:
- **Forced** (the values of KK): every fixed point of Φ lies above KK (KK is least), so a T or F of KK holds in **every** fixed point. It is what every assignment of authorization consistent with Φ gives. Model check 5 enumerates all 3^k assignments: on 300 loop-rich records it finds 333 fixed points, 33 of them other than KK, all above KK. On 300 random records with forward citations each record has exactly one fixed point, KK itself.
- **Grounded** (the values WF adds to KK): they hold because authority supported only by itself is counted as not authorized. This is **not** forced by Φ: the same 33 fixed points are not above WF (check 5). On check 7's record (two grants each issued under the other) the all-T assignment is a total fixed point, yet WF gives F; on check 8's record WF gives T to an adoption that another total fixed point makes F (an annulment by an act under a self-supporting loop). A grounded value is valid **under the definition** that authority must rest on a root (R3), for C1 = constitute.
- **U:** the record does not decide. Its causes in Φ: a covers pair outside covers' domain; a **negative cycle** (two acts each annulling the other, check 9; a holder annulling the grant it acts under, check 11).
- **Undefined:** the cut is outside D (a failing integrity check, a missing required field, a kind not admitted, no total order), or the parameters are outside Θ_f. Q1 must refuse with the reason (§9.10.2), not answer (check 13).

**R2a. The corpus's four results (Src; Int).** The corpus's reasoning step distinguishes four results, Derived, Refuted, Undetermined and Inapplicable (S0875 L1733–1736, in meaning), and says that when the knowledge is insufficient the kernel "should return" NotDerivable "rather than" False [S0875 L1700] (in meaning). CE*'s values map onto them: T = derived recognition; U = undetermined; undefined = inapplicable; **F = refuted, or not recognized**. The F of Φ arises from: no basis cited; a basis failing its record constants; an effective ending or annulment (refuted); no adoption on record, and a self-supporting loop under WF (not recognized: a **closed-world** answer about recognition, nothing recorded or grounded, nothing recognized). The corpus's NotDerivable is the **open-world** stance, which is C1 = represent. This is the C1 question returned to the Principal (§9.12).

**R3. Why WF and not KK: circular self-authorization (Int: a definitional choice; with a counterexample).** Two grants, each issued under the other, with no root in the chain, and an adoption citing one of them: KK gives U for all three; WF gives F (check 7).
- **The choice.** Under A1 recognized with C1 = constitute, a standing change is recognized only if a recorded act **grounded in a root** effects it. That premise is the definition that selects WF; the counterexample shows only that KK and WF differ, not that WF is right.
- **Scope.** The choice matters only when positive dependence can form a loop, that is, with forward citations. **On D_< (every citation names an earlier entry), WF = KK** (Ded): let I = KK and A be an unfounded set for I; take a ∈ A earliest in record order with I(a) ≠ F; every rule for a has a false literal in I or a positive atom b ∈ A, and b is earlier, so I(b) = F; so every rule for a has a false literal, Φ gives a F, and I(a) = F, a contradiction. So the greatest unfounded set is already false in I, I is a fixed point of the well-founded operator, and WF = I. The model agrees: on 3,000 backward-only records WF = KK on all; with forward citations WF decides more on 1 of 3,000 random records and 180 of 3,000 loop-rich ones (check 6).
- **Rival (from the corpus): require D_<.** Step 025a-5 meets circular derivations and recommends an acyclic graph, "PROPOSED" (S1486 L280–319, second-hand for S0890, in meaning). Under that rival positive loops cannot occur and the choice between WF and KK disappears; what remains are negative cycles (R2, U). *Second rival:* KK, with a loop reported as an anomaly, not decided. *Third:* stable models, which also make positive loops false but may give no model for an odd negative cycle, so the answer would not be total.
- **The corpus, read exactly.** S1414 treats the **one-step** case: "We should also prevent" [S1414 L1267] a party "granting itself authority without an external basis" [S1414 L1273], formally Grant(A,A), which "should normally be invalid unless the domain explicitly permits self-constituting authority." [S1414 L1281]. Reading it as covering loops of two or more grants is an extension (Int). CE* makes Grant(A,A) F only when A holds no covering grant from elsewhere, which agrees with "without an external basis". S1761 finds the estate's authority "EXOGENOUS in origin and ENDOGENOUSLY RECORDED" (S1761 L96, in meaning), says "a fixed-point construction over self-referential authority is consistent; nothing forces exogeneity" [S1761 L142], and classifies the stipulation, "implemented, at 100 % coverage, with a fail-closed enforcement mechanism" [S1761 L149] "and an explicit refusal to self-authorize" [S1761 L150], as "`IMPLEMENTATION OBSERVATION`, not an open choice." [S1761 L150] (quoted without its bold); it calls exogeneity architecturally necessary to avoid a cycle in the dependency graph (S1761 L143, in meaning). So the corpus does **not** derive the exclusion: it observes it and requires it to avoid cycles. That supports the acyclic rival as much as WF.
- **Boundary cases.** Self-support through citation: F (WF). Self-reference through annulment (a holder annulling the grant it acts under): U in both (check 11). A loop with a root path into one member: grounded, T.
- **Falsifier of the choice:** a self-supporting loop that the organisation recognises as authority (S1414's exception, "unless the domain explicitly permits").
- **C1.** Version 1 said "KK for C1 = represent". That is **withdrawn**: under C1 = represent, "no basis → F" and κ = constitutive would also change, so KK of this Φ is not the represent reading, and no represent operator is defined here.

**R4. The authority it relies on (Int).** CE*'s T answers rest on: (i) the root acts (§9.10 O4), whose standing is stipulated, not derived; (ii) the chain of grant issuances from a root; (iii) the parameter values, here fixed to Θ_f. In general θ(u) is set by acts (§9.8 H3), and the reserved values (P, ρ, υ) by the Principal's decisions. If those acts are on the record, θ depends on CE*'s own answers: a fixed point over θ that no result here covers. D excludes θ-setting acts, so the regress of F31 (which meta-evaluator fixes the policy in force) is **located**, at the root acts and at the acts that set θ, **not closed**.

**R5. Q1's vacuity test, decided on D (non-degenerate, not validation).** Records exist on which CE* (read off WF) gives T, F and U (check 4). So a constant-U and a constant-T evaluator each fail Q1. This shows that CE* is not degenerate; it does not show that CE* is the right reference. It holds on D, for Θ_f and C1 = constitute; under C1 = represent R5 must be re-derived, once a represent operator exists.
- **Q3 (authorization), a construction.** Q3 asks (a, v, x, t, s) about a possibly unrecorded act. Its reference is: ⋁ over grants and roots g in R≤s of basis(g, h), read off WF of R≤s, where h is a hypothetical act by a, of activity v, on scope x, at time t, placed after the cut, and covers relates activities and scopes. Appending h changes no other atom, because no entry cites or targets a fresh identifier. T = found; F = not found; U = undetermined. "Not found" is not "unauthorized" (§1.17.3), and separation of duties stays a separate finding. This construction is Int, **not modelled**. So Q3's vacuity is decided on D, conditionally on it.
- **Q4's obstacle** (a grading rule) remains.

## 9.13.6 What follows for the results that depended on CE

- **§9.8 Lemma D** ("if … CE is defined and deterministic for the chosen θ on every cut"): a prefix of a record in D is in D. It keeps the order, the fields and the per-entry integrity check, and a citation of a later entry is not a basis. So CE* is defined and deterministic on every cut **for Θ_f**: Lemma D's condition on CE is met **on D, for Θ_f**. With forward citations, an act can be F on a cut and T on a longer cut (its basis is recorded later); Lemma D needs definedness on every cut, not monotonicity in the cut. Its other premises (H1′, H2, H3, F26 for the cut across records) are unchanged.
- **§9.9 T1** ("T1 is conditional on CE"): premise (vi)'s adjudicating act counts if **authorized(x)** is T under WF (the act's authorization, not current-authority). T1's condition on CE is met on D for Θ_f.
- **§9.10 Q1 and Q3:** their reference definitions exist **on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute (pending)**: Q1 is CE*(k, x, t; R≤s); Q3 is the construction of R5. The present record is not in D.
- **§9.7.5 condition 4** (agreement on T, F and U, E6 excepted): testable against CE* on records in D.

## 9.13.7 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

**Internally:** on D and for Θ_f, CE* is a function of the record: it always answers or refuses with a reason. Its KK values are forced by the record. The values WF adds are grounded, valid under the definition of R3.

**About the world:**
- **The integrity check is sound** (key binding, key custody, §9.12.7). This is load-bearing for security, not hygiene: CE* refuses a record with a failing entry, but an entry **removed without trace** leaves a record in D whose answers change. A removed annulment turns its target to T.
- **The recorded act times are the ones the organisation recognises** (under A1 recognized they are, by definition; under A1 as fact they must be true times).
- **The root acts are made by whoever the organisation designates.**

**About the implementation:** steps are serialised (§9.11.5).
