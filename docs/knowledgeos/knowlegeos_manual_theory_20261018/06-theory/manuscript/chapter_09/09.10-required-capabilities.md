# 9.10 The kernel's required capabilities (Req): the questions it must answer and the operations it must accept

*Theory turn 1 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: Req (Ch. 9 TODO T9-01; TODO-TRIAGE M1). No new corpus source was read: the turn collects what the book, the Principal's act on F22 and RESEARCH-PURPOSE already state, and derives from them. **Status: draft for review by two reviewers; nothing here is reviewed.***

## 9.10.1 The research question

**What must the kernel be able to answer and to do, so that the three judgements of the central question ("which knowledge is valid, who owns it, and how can it be trusted?", RESEARCH-PURPOSE, "The central question of the theory") can be made for any item?** Which of these capabilities are grounded by an act, which follow by derivation, and which are only proposed?

**What would disprove the answer** (falsifiers, fixed before the derivation):
- **(F-ins) insufficiency:** a system that meets every Req item and still cannot make one of the three judgements for some item in Req's scope;
- **(F-min) redundancy:** a Req item whose removal leaves every judgement decidable;
- **(F-vac) vacuity:** a trivial system (∅, refuse-everything, no-op, a constant answer) that meets every item's observable consequences.

## 9.10.2 What Req is (definitions)

- **Req** is a pair (Q, O) (Ded; a proposal of this book, extending §9.1 C2) `[CON-0188 · provisional]`:
  - **Q, the questions:** each q ∈ Q is a function from a record cut and parameters to an answer. A question is met if the kernel answers it **for every input in its scope**, with an answer that its reference definition fixes. "U" is allowed only where the reference definition gives U.
  - **O, the operations:** each o ∈ O is a kind of input the kernel must accept. An operation is met if, from a valid state, the input leads to a state where its effect holds (a reachability property, as in §9.1 C2).
- **Why two kinds.** §9.1 C2 states Req as states reachable from s₀. That fits operations ("a claim with standing is reachable"). It does not fit questions: "answer for every item, correctly" quantifies over all inputs and constrains every answer, which no single reachable state expresses. *Ext:* in branching-time logic a reachability requirement is a possibility property ("along some path, eventually", the EF operator of CTL; Clarke and Emerson 1981), and Lamport (1980) showed that linear and branching time differ on such properties. A question is not of that kind; it is closer to a query over the record (below). **Established:** both notions; **the book's own:** only putting the two side by side as Req.
- **The fields of each item** (as the Principal's instruction asks): **purpose** (which judgement it serves); **inputs**; **scope** (for which inputs it must be met); **identity** (what makes two statements of it the same requirement, and two calls of it the same call); **observable consequences** (what one could observe that shows it met or failed); **assumptions** (internal ones, kept apart from assumptions about the world).
- **Grounds** are labelled: **Act** (a recorded act of the Principal), **Src**, **Ded** (derived here), **Hyp** (proposed).

## 9.10.3 The items

### Questions

**Q1. Standing** (R_s). *Grounds:* **Act** (the Principal, F22, 2026-10-19: "the kernel must answer questions of standing", §9.6.6a).
- **Purpose:** "valid" in the current sense (§0.1's *current*), and "owned": whose recorded act gave the standing.
- **Inputs:** an item k with a governed identity (Q3), a scope x, a valid time t (when the standing is asked about), a record cut s (as the record stood), and a parameter version u (the policy values θ(u), §9.8).
- **Output:** T, F or U, with the recorded acts the answer rests on.
- **Scope:** every k known to the record at s. Not the truth of k's content.
- **Identity:** two calls are the same call iff (k, x, t, s, u) are equal; the requirement is the same whoever asks (K5, §9.7, a stipulation for the Principal).
- **Observable consequences:** (i) the same call always gets the same answer (replay; §1.16 pinning; S0896's Replay, §5.4 C9); (ii) U only where the reference definition gives U, or in the cases E6 of Ch. 2 §2.8.8 (§9.7.5 condition 4); (iii) for a fixed cut s the answer never changes; a later act changes answers only for later cuts.
- **Assumptions:** internal: the reference definition CE (§1.14.1, §9.7); an order of acts (F26); the parameters θ. About the world: that recorded acts occurred, and that every act was recorded (§9.9 T-b; turn 6).
- **Obstacle (documented):** the reference definition is not yet a total function (§9.7 result 2; TODO-TRIAGE M2). So observable consequence (ii) cannot yet be tested, and (F-vac) cannot yet be checked against a constant-U evaluator. Turn 4 supplies the definition.

**Q2. Lineage** (traceability). *Grounds:* **Src/chapter must-dos** I-2 (status changes explicit and reasoned), I-3 (provenance), I-7 (supersession is a link) (BOOK-OUTLINE §3a); **Hyp** the direction's "traceable" (EXT-008 L45, direction, not evidence).
- **Purpose:** "trusted": why one may rely on an item is shown by where it came from and how its standing changed.
- **Inputs:** an item k; a cut s.
- **Output:** the recorded acts, supersession links and derivation links of k up to s, in record order.
- **Scope:** items with a governed identity.
- **Identity:** same call iff (k, s) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** every entry about k in the record up to s is returned; no entry is returned that is not in the record.
- **Assumptions:** internal: the record keeps entries (append-only, H1′); without it, Q2 at an old cut returns less than was recorded (§9.8 M1). About the world: that every act about k was recorded (completeness, H5).
- *Ext:* this is a lineage query in the sense of the W3C provenance model, which relates entities, activities and agents by generation, derivation and attribution (PROV-DM, 2013, §2.1). **Established**; the book restricts it to acts and links.
- *Whether Q2 is a kernel requirement beyond Q1* turns on whether the kernel must keep history (H1′; §9.8; F36).

**Q3. Identity resolution.** *Grounds:* **Src/chapter must-do** I-0 (§1.10: identity needs governed names); **Ded:** Q1, Q2 and Q4 take an item as input, so without a resolved identity they are undefined.
- **Purpose:** precondition of all three judgements ("identifiable" in the direction's terms).
- **Inputs:** a reference (a name or identifier) and a context.
- **Output:** the governed item it denotes, or U with the reason (unknown; several candidates, a homonym).
- **Scope:** every reference used as input to another question.
- **Identity:** same call iff (reference, context) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** one reference in one context never resolves to two items; a homonym is reported, not resolved silently (§1.10).
- **Assumptions:** internal: an identity scheme (assigned or content-derived; K2, §9.6.4). About the world: none beyond the record.

**Q4. Evidential grade.** *Grounds:* **Src/chapter must-do** I-9 (quality evidenced, never asserted; §1.3); **Ded:** §0.1's usable(k, t) = current ∧ well-founded ∧ visible needs well-foundedness, which Q1 does not give (standing is not correctness, P1-7, §1.17.2).
- **Purpose:** "valid" in the well-founded sense.
- **Inputs:** a claim k; a cut s; the grading rule (a parameter).
- **Output:** the grade, with the recorded evidence it rests on.
- **Scope:** claims (the bearer question, F37, applies: under reading (A) the grade is per instance, under (B) per claim).
- **Identity:** same call iff (k, s, grading-rule version) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** no grade without recorded evidence (I-9); the same evidence and rule give the same grade.
- **Assumptions:** internal: a grading rule (policy). About the world: that the recorded evidence is what it says it is (its own provenance).

**Q5. Conflict report.** *Grounds:* **Src/chapter must-do** I-4 (contradictions stay visible); Ch. 2 §2.3 (d)–(e).
- **Purpose:** "valid" for rules in use: a rule set with no compliant outcome cannot be followed.
- **Inputs:** the rules that apply in a region, in the restricted language of §2.3 (a); a cut s.
- **Output:** the minimal conflicting sets, point by point, with their regions (§2.3 (d)); and, for a gate, whether every applicable set is consistent (§2.9).
- **Scope:** rules written in the restricted language; others get U (E6).
- **Identity:** same call iff (rule set, region, s) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** no false "consistent" (soundness, §2.6); every case decidable in the restricted language decided (liveness, §2.3 (e)).
- **Assumptions:** internal: the restricted language; applicability independent of the outcome (§2.3 (d)). About the world: none.
- *Mark:* reporting at rest is a kernel candidate; **resolving** a conflict at use is application (BOOK-OUTLINE §3a, I-4).

**Q6. Read visibility.** *Grounds:* **Src/chapter** §1.7 (vis(d) ⊆ ∩ vis(sᵢ), widened only by an act); OQ-25 (who may read, §1.17) is **open**.
- **Purpose:** that knowledge restricted to some readers stays restricted, including in what is derived from it. *Not* §0.1's "visible", which means that the user can see both values, current and well-founded: that is a presentation obligation at use (I-2's third part), which the kernel serves by returning both values (Q1, Q4) and an application meets by showing them. The two senses of "visible" are kept apart.
- **Inputs:** a reader r, an item k, a cut s.
- **Output:** whether r may see k.
- **Scope, identity, observable consequences:** as for Q1; derivation never widens visibility without an act (§1.7).
- **Assumptions:** internal: a read-access rule, which the book does not have (OQ-25). **So Q6 is a candidate with an open rule.**

**Q7. Contract gap** (Zero). *Grounds:* **Src** §5.4 (the gap operator); **Hyp** that the kernel must answer it (outline row "Zero(EC) reachable", qualified at S0100 as Zero-all or Zero-blocking, which one open).
- **Purpose:** whether a knowledge state meets a declared need: a use-side judgement.
- **Inputs:** a state, a contract EC; Δ's two predicates (§5.4).
- **Assumptions:** internal: ContractDerivation and an owner of R and criticality (§5.4 C9, both open).
- *Mark:* contested. The must-do half is open in the source itself; and a contract is a need of a user, which suggests application (Int). **Kept as a candidate, not as Req.**

### Operations

**O1. Accept an act.** *Grounds:* **Ded** (§9.6.3 Analysis 1: with R_s ∈ Req, recording acts is necessary; definitional necessity); chapter must-dos I-2, I-10, §1.13 (grants by reference), §1.16 (atomic change).
- **Purpose:** "owned" and "governable": standing changes only through acts (A1).
- **Inputs:** an act-carrying input: actor, act time, scope, the items it concerns, its reason (I-2), the reference it carries (§1.13).
- **Effect (observable):** after acceptance the act is in the record at a position, atomically with the transitions it causes (§1.16), and Q1 at later cuts reflects it.
- **Scope:** inputs that carry an act performed outside the kernel (§9.1 C6, input origin).
- **Identity:** an act is accepted once (uniqueness, §9.1 C6 results 4–5; K2, K4).
- **Assumptions:** internal: an act identity scheme. About the world: that the act occurred (the label is trusted; §9.1 C6 result 1).

**O2. Register a claim** with provenance and a governed identity. *Grounds:* chapter must-dos I-0, I-3. **Effect:** the claim is in the record with its provenance; Q3 resolves its identifier.

**O3. Link and retire.** *Grounds:* chapter must-dos I-7 (supersession is a link), I-1(GKC) (retirement only by a recorded act). **Effect:** the link or retirement is in the record; nothing is removed (append-only, H1′).

**O4. Refuse and report.** *Grounds:* INV-A3 (STOP · REPORT · ESCALATE on absence, §1.14.2); §9.1 C1 (refusal must not be the only behaviour); §5.5 C3. **Effect:** an input that would break an invariant is refused **with a recorded reason**; a question whose inputs are missing gets U **with the missing input named**, never a silent default; an act whose occurrence is in doubt is recorded at its weaker reading and returned to its author for confirmation (§5.5 C3).

### Candidates already in the book, not made items

- **Distribution: the current rule reaches the actor** (§9.5 C1, CON-0117; "is distribution a required capability?", §9.5 open questions). Its question part, *which version of rule r is current at t?*, is derivable: the latest adopted version not superseded at t is given by Q1 (standing at t) over Q2's supersession links (Ded). Its **delivery** part, that the actor actually receives and uses it (S0048 L67, as quoted in §9.5), happens at the actor, which is application (Int; §0.7's context assembly is marked A in BOOK-OUTLINE §3a). So distribution is not a separate item: its kernel half is Q1 with Q2; its other half is application. *Falsifier:* a case where an actor cannot obtain the current rule although Q1 and Q2 are met.
- **The generative half** (§9.3: promotion, extraction, improvement from evidence). Promotion is an act (O1). Extraction and improvement are proposals made by applications (AI proposes, EXT-007 L260–278, direction), adopted only by acts. So no further kernel operation is derived (Int).
- **A test records three outcomes per item** (§9.3 C2: shown, refuted, not yet exercised): a Req item not yet exercised is undecided, not met.

## 9.10.4 Derivation: from the central question to Req

**Premises.**
- (P-act) the Principal's act: R_s ∈ Req; the kernel serves several independent applications (K3).
- (P-cq) the central question, read through the book's terms: *valid* = current ∧ well-founded (§0.1, P1-7); *owned* = whose recorded act gives standing (A1); *trusted* = why one may rely on it (§9.9's three senses).
- (P-comb, **Hyp**) RESEARCH-PURPOSE's combined form: the kernel keeps knowledge identifiable, traceable, governable and usable, so that validity, ownership and trustworthiness can be decided. This is a proposal there, not a finding.

**Steps.**
1. *valid, current* needs Q1 (P-act; P-cq). Q1 needs O1 (Analysis 1, §9.6.3: definitional) and Q3 (its input k).
2. *valid, well-founded* needs a grade that Q1 does not give (standing ≠ correctness, §1.17.2): Q4. Q4 needs O2 (provenance of evidence).
3. *owned* needs the act behind the standing: Q1's output includes the acts it rests on; O1.
4. *trusted*: of §9.9's three senses, trust in the record rests on Q2 (what was recorded) plus the world assumptions of turn 6; trust in a source (T-a) is a policy value over outcome records (§9.9 T1) and is **not** made a Req item (it is derivable under T1's premises, and "whether Req includes weighting sources" is open, §9.9.10).
5. *visible* in §0.1's sense (both values shown at use) needs Q1 and Q4 to return their values with their grounds; showing them is application. Read visibility (§1.7) is a different sense: Q6, with its rule open, grounded by §1.7, not by the central question.
6. I-4's must-do (contradictions visible) gives Q5; it is a chapter must-do, not derived from the central question: the central question asks about items, Q5 about rule sets.

**Result (Ded, conditional on P-cq and P-comb):** Req = ({Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6}, {O1, O2, O3, O4}), with Q6's rule open and Q7 a candidate. Grounded by act: Q1. Derived as preconditions: Q3, O1. Grounded by chapter must-dos: Q2, Q4, Q5, O2, O3, O4. **None is reviewed.**

**Dependencies between items:** Q1 ← O1, Q3; Q2 ← O1, O2, O3 (and H1′); Q4 ← O2; Q5 ← the rules' language; Q6 ← a read rule (open). Q1 at a past cut needs either the act record (Q2's entries) or a stored history of standings; §9.8's Hist is Q1 with s and u as inputs.

## 9.10.5 Tests of the answer

**Trivial systems (F-vac).**
- ∅ and refuse-everything fail O1–O3 (no act, claim or link can be accepted): they fail Req. **Checked by argument** (§9.1 C2's reasoning carries over to operations).
- A no-op fails O1–O3 in the same way.
- **A constant-U answerer** meets O1–O4 if it records inputs, and answers every question with U. It fails Q3 (it must resolve a known identifier), Q2 (it must return the recorded entries) and Q5's liveness. **For Q1 it cannot yet be excluded**, because Q1's reference definition is not total (the obstacle in Q1). This is the precise gap of this turn.

**Redundancy (F-min).** Q2 and Q1: is Q2 derivable from Q1? No: Q1 returns an answer per (k, x, t, s, u) with the acts it rests on, not every entry about k (a revoked grant that never decided a standing question is in Q2's output and in no Q1 output). Is Q1 derivable from Q2? Yes, **given CE**: Q1 = CE over Q2's entries (this is §9.8's decomposition D). So Q1 is not a separate primitive requirement **if** CE is in the kernel; whether it is, is F22. *Result:* Q1 and Q2 are not independent; Req's minimal form depends on where CE sits.

**Insufficiency (F-ins).** Strongest counterexample found: **trust in a source** (T-a). A system meeting all items cannot say how much weight a source deserves. The answer here is that T-a is derivable under §9.9 T1's premises (adjudicated outcomes, a policy), so it is not a missing kernel capability **if** T1's premises hold. If adjudication must happen inside the kernel, Req is insufficient. Recorded as open, not refuted.

**Rival formulations.**
- **(R1) Req as reachable states only** (§9.1 C2). It cannot express "answers every question correctly"; under it, a kernel that once reaches a state with a standing claim meets "R2" and may answer nothing afterwards. Rejected as the whole of Req; kept for operations.
- **(R2) Req as the direction's four properties as invariants** (identifiable, traceable, governable, usable; EXT-008 L45). As invariants they are prohibitions (nothing unidentifiable, nothing untraceable), which the empty system meets (§9.1 C1). The four become requirements only as questions and operations, as above (Q3, Q2, O1/O4, Q6).
- **(R3) S0167's five verbs** (creating, identifying, relating, governing, preserving; quoted in RESEARCH-PURPOSE from S0167 L1139, not yet read in order: direction). Mapping: creating → O2; identifying → Q3, O2; relating → O3; governing → O1, O4; preserving → H1′ (an invariant, not a capability). **No verb answers a question**, so under the Principal's act (R_s ∈ Req) the five verbs are incomplete as Req (Int).

## 9.10.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world, kept apart

| Item | What the kernel can guarantee internally | What it must assume about the world |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | the same call gets the same answer; U only where defined | that recorded acts occurred; that every act was recorded |
| Q2 | every recorded entry about k is returned (with H1′) | that every act about k was recorded |
| Q3 | no reference resolves to two items in one context | none |
| Q4 | no grade without recorded evidence | that the evidence is what it claims to be |
| Q5 | no false "consistent"; decidable cases decided | none |
| Q6 | read visibility never widened without an act | none (the read rule is open) |
| O1 | an accepted act is recorded once, atomically | that the act carried by the input occurred |
