# 9.10 Required capabilities (Req): the questions the kernel must answer and the inputs it must accept

*Theory turn 1 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: Req (Ch. 9 TODO T9-01; TODO-TRIAGE M1). No new corpus source was read for the argument; three corpus anchors were checked (S0048 L67, S0067 L73–77, S0129 L72–73, through the sections that quote them). **Status: version 2, rewritten at the turn-1 settlement after two reviews (`review/TURN1-REQ/`); the first version is in git (`c0ab6daab`).***

*Scope of the word "kernel" here.* This section lists what the **system the kernel serves** must answer and accept. Which item belongs to the kernel and which to applications is decided per item by the three boundary questions in turn 7; only Q1's necessity has been argued so far (§9.6.3).

## 9.10.1 The research question

**What must the system be able to answer and to accept, so that the three judgements of the central question ("which knowledge is valid, who owns it, and how can it be trusted?", RESEARCH-PURPOSE, "The central question of the theory") can be made for any item? Which of these are grounded by an act, which follow by derivation, which only by the book's own chapter must-dos, and which are only proposed?**

**What would disprove the answer** (fixed before the derivation):
- **(F-ins) insufficiency:** a system that meets every core item and still cannot make one of the three judgements, in the readings stated in §9.10.4, for some item in scope;
- **(F-min) redundancy:** a core item whose removal leaves every judgement decidable;
- **(F-vac) vacuity:** a trivial system that meets every item's observable consequences.

## 9.10.2 What Req is

- **Req** is a pair (Q, O) `[CON-0188 · provisional]` (a **stipulation** of this book, Int; it extends §9.1 C2):
  - **Q, questions.** Each q has a **reference definition** ref_q, a function of a record cut and inputs. q is **met** iff, for every input in its scope, the system's answer equals ref_q's answer. U is a value ref_q may give; an answer of U where ref_q gives T or F is a failure, except in the E6 cases of Ch. 2 §2.8.8 **when the case is shown**: an E6 answer carries the recorded reason (undecidable fragment, unresolved input, or the resource bound that was reached), and a U without it does not conform.
  - **O, inputs to accept.** Each o is a kind of input that some run from s₀ can present and the system accept, with an effect that then holds (reachability, EF from s₀, as in §9.1 C2). *Rival, stronger (open):* enabledness, acceptance from **every** reachable state in which no guard refuses the input; it changes C3's form (§9.11).
- **Why two kinds, and what they are in the kernel type.** A question constrains every answer, so it is a condition on all states (*Ext:* in branching-time terms an AG condition over an observation; an input to accept is a possibility, EF; Clarke and Emerson 1981; Lamport 1980). §9.1 C6 result 7 gives the questions their place in the kernel type: as **observers** whose correctness is a state invariant. *Rival R4* (below) treats the two kinds as one specification; the split is a presentation, not a necessity.
- **Prior work.** Characterising a knowledge base by what can be **asked** of it and **told** to it is Levesque's functional approach (TELL and ASK; Levesque 1984). Separating value-returning queries from state-changing commands is established in software specification (algebraic specification of abstract data types, Guttag and Horning 1978; command–query separation, Meyer 1988, as reported by Fowler 2005). **Req's (Q, O) form is an instance of that established view, not the book's own.**
- **Fields of each item:** grounds (labelled Act, Src, Int, Ded, Hyp, Ext); purpose; inputs; scope; identity (when two calls are the same call; when two statements are the same requirement); observable consequences (against the reference definition, both directions); assumptions, internal ones apart from those about the world; **reference definition** (exists, partial, or absent).

## 9.10.3 The items

### A. Core questions

**Q1. Current authority** (the Principal's R_s).
- **Grounds:** **Act** for the core: "the kernel must answer questions of standing" (the Principal on F22, 2026-10-19; §9.6.6a), with R_s = (k, x, t) (§9.6.3 N3). **Ded** for the extensions: the cut s and the parameter version u (from §9.8 Hist), and replay (from §1.16 pinning).
- **Naming.** The act says "standing"; what the book can compute is **current authority**, which §1.14.1-12 separates from standing (current authority can lapse with time while standing is untouched). Q1 asks current authority as CE defines it; "standing" in the act is read so (Int).
- **Purpose:** "valid" in the current sense (§0.1); and "owned" in the reading "whose recorded act gives the item its authority" (§9.10.4).
- **Inputs:** item k (a governed identifier), scope x, valid time t (the time **at which** authority is asked about, the time axis of the fact), cut s (the record as it stood: a prefix of the record order, H2 of §9.8), parameter version u (the policy values θ(u)). The three times as in §9.8 Hist; *Ext:* t and s are valid time and transaction time (Snodgrass and Ahn 1986); u is close to, but not the same as, decision time (Nascimento and Eich 1995), which records when a decision about a fact was made, while u selects which policy values apply.
- **Output:** T, F or U, with the acts the answer rests on.
- **Scope:** every k known to the record at s. Not the truth of k's content.
- **Identity:** two calls are the same iff (k, x, t, s, u) are equal. The requirement is one requirement whoever asks (K5, §9.7; a stipulation for the Principal).
- **Observable consequences:** the answer equals CE(k, x, t; θ(u), R≤s) for every input in scope, with E6 shown (§9.10.2). Hence the same call gets the same answer, **unless a recorded loss (H1′ as restated) has since removed an entry the answer rests on**: then the answer either rests on what the loss entry keeps (O3) or is U with the loss named.
- **Assumptions:** internal: CE; an order of acts (F26). About the world: that recorded acts occurred, and that every act was recorded (§9.9 T-b; turn 6).
- **Reference definition:** **exists on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute (pending with the Principal)**: CE*(k, x, t; R≤s) (§9.13). The present record is not in D.

**Q2. Lineage.**
- **Grounds:** **Int**: §0.5's reconstructability (R), which §0.5 calls a weak derivation; **Hyp**: the direction's "traceable" (EXT-008 L45, direction, not evidence); **Ded** from "trusted" in its record reading (§9.10.4 step 4). *Not* I-2, I-3, I-7 themselves: those are recording obligations (they ground inputs, O1), not a question.
- **Purpose:** "trusted" (record): why one may rely on an item is shown by what was recorded about it.
- **Inputs:** item k; cut s; and a reader r **if** a read rule exists (Q6): then only entries r may see are returned.
- **Output:** the entries about k up to s (acts, links, evidence, marked absences, recorded losses), in record order.
- **Scope:** items with a governed identifier.
- **Identity:** same call iff (k, s, r) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** every entry about k in the record up to s that r may see is returned; no entry is returned that is not in the record; an entry removed under H1′ (as restated in §9.8: "only by a recorded act, with a recovery pointer or a recorded loss") appears as its recorded loss.
- **Assumptions:** internal: H1′ as restated. About the world: that every act about k was recorded (H5).
- **Reference definition:** **exists** (the record itself, cut at s).
- *Ext:* PROV-DM is a data model (entities, activities, agents; generation, derivation, attribution); it defines no query, cut or record order. Q2 is closer to a transaction-time history of k (Snodgrass and Ahn 1986) with derivation links. The comparison with PROV-DM is by structure only.

**Q3. Authorization.**
- **Grounds:** **Src**: §1.17.3 (IA's corrected evaluator: "does a recorded grant exist that covers this principal, capability, scope and instant?", answering found or not found; a separation-of-duties violation is a separate, positive finding from the records); S0067's read query `authorized` (S0067 L73–77, as quoted in §6.5 C1, checked). **Int**: I-5's must-do "evaluate scope with each act" (BOOK-OUTLINE §3a).
- **Purpose:** "owned" in the reading "who may change it" (RESEARCH-PURPOSE's comparison: governable covers who may change it); needed to refuse an act whose actor holds no covering grant (O1's guard).
- **Inputs:** principal a, capability v, scope x, instant t, cut s.
- **Output:** found (with the grant) or not found; and, separately, any separation-of-duties violation the records show (§1.17.3, §1.14.3). "Not found" is not "unauthorized" (§1.17.3: the cases E1, E3, E4 of Ch. 2 §2.8.8).
- **Scope:** acts of the kinds O1 accepts.
- **Identity:** same call iff (a, v, x, t, s) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** "found" exactly when a recorded, unended grant covers (a, v, x, t) at s, by a reference definition still to be written.
- **Assumptions:** internal: covers(B, x) and the actor–holder link, both **undefined** in CE (§9.7 result 2); at turn 4 both became inputs of CE* (§9.13.2). About the world: that the actor is who the record says (attribution; §5.7 C9, §9.8 H4).
- **Reference definition:** **a construction on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute (pending)**: the basis clause of CE*'s operator for a hypothetical act (a, v, x, t) placed after the cut, read off the well-founded model of R≤s, with covers relating activities and scopes and the actor–holder link as inputs (§9.13 R5; Int, not modelled). Separation of duties stays a separate finding. The present record is not in D.
- *Rival:* Q1 applied to grants as items covers coverage, but not separation of duties or the actor–holder link; so Q3 is not derivable from Q1 as defined.
- *Ext:* HRU's safety question (Harrison, Ruzzo and Ullman 1976) asks whether a subject **can come to** hold a right, which is undecidable in general; Q3 asks whether a recorded grant **does** cover an act at a cut, a membership check. Different questions; the comparison marks what Q3 is not.

**Q4. Evidential grade.**
- **Grounds:** **Int**: I-9's must-do "grade claims by recorded evidence" (BOOK-OUTLINE §3a; I-9 is Src). **Ded**: §0.1's usable = current ∧ well-founded ∧ visible needs well-foundedness, which Q1 does not give (current authority is not correctness, §1.17.2).
- **Purpose:** "valid" in the well-founded sense.
- **Inputs:** claim k; cut s; the grading rule's version g.
- **Output:** the grade, with the evidence entries it rests on.
- **Scope:** claims (under F37's reading (A), per instance; under (B), per claim).
- **Identity:** same call iff (k, s, g) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** the answer equals the grading rule applied to k's evidence entries at s; no grade without a recorded evidence entry.
- **Assumptions:** internal: a grading rule; evidence entries (O2). About the world: that each evidence entry is what it says it is.
- **Reference definition:** **absent**: evidence status has three values in §3.1 and four in §1.3 (F13), and no grading rule is stated.
- *Ext:* the support of a belief by recorded justifications is the subject of truth maintenance (Doyle 1979); not compared in detail.

### B. Item grounded by chapter must-dos only

**Q5. Conflict report (rules).**
- **Grounds:** **Src**: I-4 (contradictions stay visible); Ch. 2 §2.3 (d)–(e).
- **Purpose:** a rule set with no compliant outcome cannot be followed. It is not derived from the central question, which asks about items (step 5 below).
- **Inputs:** the rules that apply in a region, written in the restricted language L of §2.3 (a); cut s.
- **Output:** the minimal conflicting sets, point by point, with their regions (§2.3 (d)); for a gate, whether every applicable set is consistent (§2.9).
- **Scope:** rules written in L. L is a parameter, and it must at least contain propositional formulas over finitely many attributes (§2.3 (a)'s first example): an empty L would make Q5 vacuous.
- **Identity:** same call iff (rule set, region, s, L) are equal.
- **Observable consequences:** no false "consistent" (§2.6); every case decidable in L decided (§2.3 (e)), with E6 shown.
- **Assumptions:** internal: L; applicability independent of the outcome (§2.3 (d)). About the world: that the rules were faithfully formalised into L (§2.7).
- **Reference definition:** **exists** (§2.3 (d)).

### C. Candidates (not items until the condition named is met)

- **Identity resolution** (from a name to an item). Needed **only if** inputs arrive as names rather than governed identifiers. The book shows names used as identifiers in the corpus, with homonyms (§1.10), but it does not state that the kernel's inputs are names. Condition: a premise that inputs are names. Otherwise identity is a constraint on inputs: O1's registration assigns a governed identifier, unique in its namespace (I-0, §1.10), with a namespace owner as an internal assumption.
- **Read visibility (Q6).** §1.7 (vis(d) ⊆ ∩ vis(sᵢ), widened only by an act). Condition: a read rule (OQ-25, open). Without it, deny-all meets every stated consequence. *Ext:* the rule that derived data takes the most restrictive class of its sources is the lattice model of information flow (Denning 1976).
- **Claim conflicts** (I-4's half for claims; fork F24). Condition: F24 decided (preserve or supersede).
- **Contract gap (Q7)** (§5.4). Conditions: ContractDerivation and an owner of R and criticality (§5.4 C9).
- **Trust in a knowledge item (T-c).** §9.9.6 (Hyp): a combination f of grade (Q4), current authority (Q1) and provenance authenticity weighted by trust in sources. **Premise stated here (Hyp):** the kernel answers the components and the combination f is application policy. *Falsifier:* if applications must agree on f (as on CE), f needs a common, versioned definition, like CE, and T-c becomes a question with f as its reference definition.
- **Trust in a source (T-a).** Derivable under §9.9 T1's premises from recorded outcomes (O2) and a policy; so no separate item **if** adjudication of outcomes is outside the kernel (open, §9.9.10).
- **Ownership in §1.11's sense** (who must maintain the item). §1.11 separates it from authority; BOOK-OUTLINE §3a marks it A. The recorded owner is an attribute set at registration or by an act, so it is returned by Q2; judging maintenance is application.

### D. Inputs to accept

**O1. Accept an act.**
- **Grounds:** **Ded** (§9.6.3 Analysis 1: with Q1 required, recording acts is necessary; definitional necessity, system-level); **Int**: I-2, I-10, §1.13, §1.16 must-dos (BOOK-OUTLINE §3a).
- **Kinds of act** (one operation; each kind changes the record by an act): registering a claim with its provenance and a governed identifier (I-0, I-3); a grant, a revocation, an adoption; a supersession link (I-7); a retirement (I-1(GKC): only by a recorded act); an establishment, the act by which an assurance outcome is established (§9.4 C7); a declassification (§1.7).
- **Purpose:** "owned" and "governable": authority changes only through acts (A1).
- **Inputs:** actor, act time, scope, the items concerned, the kind, the reason (I-2), the reference it carries (§1.13).
- **Scope:** inputs that carry an act performed outside the kernel (§9.1 C6, input origin), **and that no guard refuses**. A record of an act whose actor holds no covering grant (Q3: not found) is either refused (if a guard is in force) or accepted **as a record** that confers no authority (ARCH-INV-1: the record records, never grants; Q1 then gives F or U). Which of the two is a policy choice (INV-A3 is not adopted, §1.14.5; §9.5.4).
- **Identity:** an act is accepted **idempotently** under a content-derived act identity (K2, §9.6.4): presenting the same act again changes nothing. Whether duplicates across applications must be **prevented** or only detected is K4, and stays open.
- **Observable consequences:** after acceptance the act is in the record at one position, atomically with the transitions it causes (§1.16); Q1, Q2 and Q3 at later cuts reflect it; an act not accepted leaves the record unchanged and the caller receives the refusal with its reason.
- **Assumptions:** internal: an act identity scheme; the guards in force. About the world: that the act carried by the input occurred, and that the actor is who the input says (§9.1 C6 result 1).
- **Reference definition:** the effect is defined by the record's rules (§1.13, §1.16); the guard set is policy.

**O2. Accept evidence and outcomes.**
- **Grounds:** **Ded**: Q4 needs evidence entries; §9.9 T1 needs adjudicated outcomes. An evidence entry is not an act in A1's sense: it changes no authority.
- **Purpose:** "valid" (well-founded) and "trusted" (source).
- **Inputs:** an evidence entry (an observation, a verification result, an adjudicated outcome) with its source, time and the claims it bears on.
- **Scope, identity:** entries with a source; idempotent under a content-derived identity, as O1.
- **Observable consequences:** the entry is in the record and Q4 and Q2 at later cuts see it.
- **Assumptions:** internal: an evidence vocabulary (F13). About the world: that the entry is what it says it is.

**O3. Record absence and loss.**
- **Grounds:** **Src** via §9.8: H5, "every act has an entry, or its absence is itself recorded", made the must-do there; H1′ as restated ("only by a recorded act, with a recovery pointer or a recorded loss").
- **Purpose:** "trusted" (record): a gap that is marked is not a silent gap.
- **Inputs:** a marked absence (that an act occurred and is not recorded, or that a record is missing), or a recorded loss (an entry removed under retention or law), with its reason and time.
- **Scope, identity:** as O1.
- **Observable consequences:** Q2 returns the marked absence or the recorded loss in place of the missing entry. **A loss entry keeps the identities of the acts the removed entries consumed** (needed for uniqueness, §9.11 R1); whether a law that requires deletion can require deleting those identities too is open.
- **Assumptions:** internal: H1′ as restated, with the loss entry keeping consumed-act identities. About the world: that the absence or loss is real.

**O4. Accept a root act.**
- **Grounds:** **Ded** from §1.14.1's root acts and BOOK-OUTLINE §3a (CON-0054's partner "a recorded root act"); K5 needs one root (§9.7).
- **Purpose:** without a root, no grant chain ends, every act lacks a covering grant, and Q1 and Q3 can never answer T: the system is refuse-everything in effect (the case of §9.5 C5).
- **Inputs:** the root act: who is the root of authority for which scope, from when.
- **Scope:** once per root; outside A1's grant chain by definition (it is not covered by a grant).
- **Identity:** one root per (estate, scope), conditional on K5.
- **Observable consequences:** after acceptance, Q3 can return "found" for grants issued under the root.
- **Assumptions:** internal: K5. About the world: that the root act was made by whoever the organisation recognises as the root (a world assumption of the same kind as occurrence, §9.9.7).

### E. What is not an input to accept

- **Refusal** is a guard (§9.1 C6: a guard refuses a step; "refusal is not a step"). Its required output is a reply **to the caller** with the reason (O1's observable consequences). It is not a state change. Recording a refusal is possible only as an act of its own kind under O1.
- **"U names the missing input"** is part of every question's observable consequences (§9.10.2), not a separate operation.
- **A grant's end by expiry** is not an act (§1.14.1): it is computed by Q1 from the validity period recorded with the grant. Revocation is an act (O1).
- **Returning a doubtful act to its author** (§5.5 C3): the system records the act at its weaker reading and replies to the caller that confirmation is needed (O1's reply). Delivering that request to the author and acting on it are at the actor: application. **Criterion used throughout:** the system records and replies; delivering, presenting and acting are application.

## 9.10.4 Derivation: from the central question to the core

**Readings of the central question (Int; each a premise):**
- **valid** = current ∧ well-founded (§0.1; P1-7). The third part of usable, visible, means that the user can see both values: a presentation obligation at use (I-2's third part), which the system serves by returning both values with their grounds (Q1, Q4).
- **owned**, three readings, kept apart: (o1) whose recorded act gives the item its authority (A1); (o2) who may change it (RESEARCH-PURPOSE's comparison of the purpose's question with the direction's "governable"); (o3) who must maintain it (§1.11's ownership, marked A).
- **trusted**, §9.9's three senses: in the record (T-b), in a source (T-a), in an item (T-c).

**Steps.**
1. *valid, current* needs Q1 (Act). Q1 needs O1 (§9.6.3, definitional, system-level) and O4 (a root; otherwise Q1 never answers T).
2. *valid, well-founded* needs Q4, which needs O2 (evidence entries). *Not* O1's registration: the provenance of a claim is not evidence for it.
3. *owned*: (o1) is in Q1's output (the acts it rests on); (o2) needs Q3 (Q1 over grants does not give separation of duties or the actor–holder link); (o3) is a recorded attribute returned by Q2; judging it is application.
4. *trusted*: (T-b) needs Q2 and O3, plus the world assumptions of turn 6. (T-a) is derivable under §9.9 T1 from O2's outcomes and a policy, **if** adjudication is outside the kernel. (T-c) is a combination f of Q1, Q4 and provenance; the premise that f is application policy is **Hyp**, with its falsifier (§9.10.3 C).
5. Q5 does not come from the central question: it concerns rule sets, and is grounded by I-4.

**Result (Ded, conditional on the readings above and on the Hyp in step 4):**
- **core:** questions Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4; inputs O1, O2, O3, O4;
- **grounded by chapter must-dos only:** Q5;
- **candidates:** identity resolution, Q6 read visibility, claim conflicts, Q7 contract gap, T-c, T-a, ownership in §1.11's sense.

The derivation shows what the three judgements need; it does not show where each item sits (turn 7).

## 9.10.5 Tests of the answer

**(F-vac) Trivial systems against each item.** A question is met only if its answer equals the reference definition everywhere in scope (§9.10.2). So a trivial answerer fails a question **wherever the reference definition gives an answer the trivial one does not**, and the test can be decided only where the reference definition exists.

| Trivial system | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | O1–O4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ∅; refuse-everything; no-op | — (no record) | — | — | — | — | **fail** (nothing accepted) |
| record-and-dump, answering constant U (or "ungraded", or "not found") | **fails** on D (CE* gives T on some records, §9.13 R5) | **fails** (it must return the recorded entries) | **fails** on D, given R5's construction (authorized(x) is T on some records) | undecided (reference absent) | **fails** liveness on L's propositional part | pass |
| constant T | **fails** (CE* gives F on some records) | n/a | **fails** | n/a | fails soundness (a conflicting set reported consistent) | pass |
| constant U presented as time-outs (E6) | **fails**: an E6 answer must carry its recorded bound or reason (§9.10.2) | — | — | — | fails likewise | — |
| an empty restricted language for Q5 | — | — | — | — | excluded: L has a non-empty minimum | — |

**Obstacle, documented precisely:** for Q4 the vacuity test cannot be decided until a grading rule exists. For Q1 and Q3 it was decided at turn 4 (§9.13 R5) on D, for Θ_f, under C1 = constitute; for Q3 through R5's construction. This shows the references are non-degenerate, not that they are right; under C1 = represent it must be re-derived. For the candidates, Q6 is met by deny-all until a read rule exists.

**(F-min) Removing each core item.**

| Removed | Judgement left undecided | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | valid (current); owned (o1) | needed |
| Q2 | trusted (record): what was recorded, including marked absences | needed (Q1 gives only the acts behind one answer, not every entry; Q1 at a past cut is computable from Q2 with CE, under §9.8 Lemma D's premises H1′, H2, H3 and F26 settled for the cut) |
| Q3 | owned (o2) | needed (not derivable from Q1, rival above) |
| Q4 | valid (well-founded) | needed |
| O1 | every question has nothing to answer about acts | needed |
| O2 | Q4 has no evidence; T-a has no outcomes | needed |
| O3 | Q2 cannot tell a silent gap from a marked one (H5) | needed for the record reading of "trusted" |
| O4 | Q1 and Q3 never answer T | needed, conditional on §1.14.1's root acts |

*Not independent:* Q1 at a cut is CE over Q2's entries (§9.8 D), and Q3 is a reference definition over the same entries. So in terms of mechanisms the minimal form is **a record query plus reference definitions plus inputs**; which reference definitions sit in the kernel decides the rest (turn 7, F22).

**(F-ins) Strongest counterexamples, and where they go.**
- T-c and T-a: placed as candidates with a stated premise and falsifier (C).
- Claim conflicts: a candidate, conditional on F24.
- **Bootstrap a product** (§9.2 C1: "can bootstrap a product" is a required capability). The test there was of the engineering kit (the minimum viable kit; "kernel" in F14's other sense, CON-0057), not of the knowledge kernel. So it is a requirement of the kit, not an item here (Int). *Falsifier:* a reading of §9.2 in which the knowledge kernel itself must bootstrap a product.
- **Establishing a capability** (S0164 D5, E1–E10; BOOK-OUTLINE §5): an establishment is an act (O1, kind "establishment") with evidence (O2); E10 needs history (THEORY-MAP: "Option A is unsatisfiable because E10 needs history"), which Q2 and H1′ provide.
- **The state space** (F13, T9-02): not a capability; it is the type's 𝒮.

**Rival formulations.**
- **(R1) Req as reachable states only** (§9.1 C2). It cannot express "answers correctly for every input". Kept for inputs to accept.
- **(R2) The direction's four properties as invariants** (identifiable, traceable, governable, usable; EXT-008 L45). As invariants they are prohibitions the empty system meets (§9.1 C1). As requirements they become: identifiable → O1's registration (and identity resolution, a candidate); traceable → Q2; governable → O1, Q3; usable → Q1 and Q4, returned with their grounds.
- **(R3) S0167's five verbs** (creating, identifying, relating, governing, preserving; quoted in RESEARCH-PURPOSE from S0167 L1139, not yet read in order: direction). Mapping: creating → O1 (registration); identifying → O1's identifier assignment, and identity resolution if names are inputs; relating → O1 (links); governing → O1, Q3; preserving → H1′ (an invariant) and O3. **Q1, Q2 and Q4 have no verb**: under the Principal's act the five verbs are incomplete as Req (Int).
- **(R4) One branching-time specification:** questions as AG conditions over observers, inputs as EF conditions, in one transition system. Adopted for the kernel type (§9.1 C6 result 7). The two kinds above are a presentation of it.
- **(R5) A TELL/ASK interface** (Levesque 1984). Req is an instance; what Req adds is the record cut, the parameter version, and the act discipline of A1 on what may be told.

## 9.10.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

| Item | What the system can guarantee internally | What it must assume about the world |
|---|---|---|

| Q2 | every visible entry about k up to s is returned; losses appear as recorded | that every act about k was recorded |
| Q3 | "found" only with a recorded, unended, covering grant | that the actor is who the record says |
| Q4 | no grade without a recorded evidence entry | that each evidence entry is what it says it is |
| Q5 | no false "consistent"; decidable cases decided | that the rules were faithfully formalised into L |
| O1 | an accepted act is recorded once (idempotently), atomically | that the act occurred; that its actor is who the input says |
| O2 | an accepted entry is recorded once | that the entry is what it says it is |
| O3 | a marked absence or loss is returned in place of the missing entry | that the absence or loss is real |
| O4 | one root per (estate, scope) | that the root is the one the organisation recognises |
