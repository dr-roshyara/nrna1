# 9.17 The designated record, membership, and detection by an outside reference

*Theory turn 8 (Rulebook v1.4 §12b), 2026-10-24, on the Principal's instruction: "one theory turn on record identity and anchoring (K1/K2), from the kernel questions collected in §8.18–§8.21". The research question was written and committed before any search (`review/TURN8-RECORD-IDENTITY/RESEARCH-QUESTION.md`). Status: draft for review by two reviewers.*

## 9.17.1 In plain language

§9.15's kernel assumptions K1 and K2 both speak of "the record". Windows C25–C28 showed that the record cannot say which store it is: two byte-identical copies look the same to every check that reads their entries.

This section draws four conclusions:
1. **Which record counts is set by a designation**, an act outside the record, not by anything in its entries.
2. **K2 as worded is too weak.** Readers can agree with each other on a store that is not the designated record. A restated K2 asks that every write go to the designated record and every read be a cut of it. The old K2 then follows.
3. **An entry belongs to the record if it was written while that store was the designated record.** Its content cannot show this. A designation change can be decided from the record only if the change is itself recorded, outside the record it closes, with the old record's final content listed.
4. **An outside reference detects a loss only under three conditions:**
   - it covers the entries at stake (it was taken late enough);
   - it does not come from the content being checked;
   - it is compared with the store that will be relied on.

   Each condition is needed. Together they suffice, except for the gap between the check and the act, which only serialisation closes.

## 9.17.2 Definitions

- **Store:** a place where an entry sequence is kept (a file, a database table), with a value at each time. Several stores can hold the same entries.
- **Designation:** an act, outside every record it concerns, that names which store **is** a given record of the kernel from a stated time. With several application records (§9.16 R2 (d); F22), the designation names the record set **Ρ = {ρ₁ … ρₙ}**, one store per record.
- **Resolution:** the store a reader or a writer actually uses when it means "the record". It belongs to a component, not to the record (§8.18 C1, CON-0239).
- **Cut of ρ:** a prefix of ρ's entries, named by its entries (§9.15 K2; §9.16 R2 (d)). A read that returns a cut of ρ answers for ρ at that cut, even if the cut is older than ρ's present state.
- **Membership:** an entry e is an entry **of** ρ if a kernel step appended e while the designation in force named the store it was written to as ρ.
- **Outside reference (anchor):** a list of entries (or a commitment to such a list) taken at a time t_a and kept so that the act it guards cannot rewrite it. "Detection" means that comparing a store with the reference shows that they differ. A **loss** is an entry written before an irreversible act that the store relied on after the act does not hold; a **loss-free** act leaves none.

## 9.17.3 Results

### R1. Which store is the record is not a property of its entries (Ded; Src for the estate)

**Claim.** No predicate on a store's entries can decide whether that store is the designated record ρ.

*Proof.* Let s₁ be the designated store and s₂ a byte-identical copy. Any predicate P on entries gives P(s₁) = P(s₂), because the entries are identical. The designation separates s₁ from s₂. So designation is not a function of the entries. ∎

**What this means for D.** D is a predicate on one record's entries (§9.13.2), so it cannot supply the record's identity. This is the step CON-0239 left open ("K1's 'the record' presupposes it, and D cannot supply it"). The record argument of CE* is an **input**, like the covers relation and the identity link (K3). The corpus states the general form: a needed input is either part of the state or "explicitly defined as an external dependency" (S2055 L745–757, in meaning).

*Corpus evidence (Src):*
- copies are one source, not several: "Three replicas contain identical signed evidence." [S0917 L1322], counted as one independent source (S0917 L1327, in meaning); "Replication is not new evidence." [S0904 L328];
- of three meanings of "single source of truth", only one is defensible: "One system is authoritative for a particular fact." [S1375 L83]. That is a designation per fact, not a storage property;
- the estate's own lesson, from the migration plan (read topic-full for this turn; it will be re-read in index order at S0300): "do not let STORAGE LOCATION accidentally become DOMAIN OWNERSHIP" [S0300 L1286]; "a location does not confer authority" [S0300 L1008].

### R2. K2 as worded does not give the designated record; the restated K2 does, and implies the old one (Ded)

**K2 as it stands** (§9.15): readers agree on the entries of the cut, the cut named by its entries.

**Countermodel M2 (K1 and K2 hold; the answers are not answers for ρ).**
1. The one writer resolves a store s ≠ ρ for one append e. The estate shows this can happen: a wrong resolution "silently MANUFACTURES A NEW EVIDENCE LOCATION" [S0300 L578].
2. Every reader also resolves s.
3. The readers agree on s's entries, so K2 holds.
4. ρ changed only through kernel steps, so K1 holds.
5. Yet s holds e, which is no entry of ρ. So every reader's CE* answer can differ from CE* on any cut of ρ: let e be an annulment of an act that ρ holds as current. ∎

So the identity question in §8.18.3 has this answer: identity is **not part of K2 as worded, and not derivable from K1 and K2**.

**Restated K2: a designated record, with three parts.** `[CON-0261 · provisional]`
- **(K2-d) designation:** a designation fixes the record set Ρ, and every component means Ρ when it means "the record";
- **(K2-w) writes:** every kernel append goes to the store that the designation in force names;
- **(K2-r) reads:** every value a reader takes as the record is a cut of a designated record, one prefix per record.

**The old K2 follows (Ded).** Take two readers asked for the same cut, named by its entries. By K2-r, each value is a prefix of the same designated sequence ρ. Two prefixes of one sequence with the same named entries are identical. So the readers agree on the cut's entries, which is the old K2. Agreement on each tombstone's removal acts follows the same way, since tombstones are entries of ρ. ∎ The converse fails (M2).

**K2-d/w/r and K1 are independent (countermodels).**

| Fails alone | Model |
|---|---|
| K2-w (K1 and K2-r hold) | M2 above with no reader of s: the write is lost to ρ while ρ is untouched out of band. Readers of ρ see a cut of ρ, but the accepted act is missing from it |
| K2-r (K1 and K2-w hold) | the writer appends to ρ, and a reader takes a stale copy that also holds a foreign entry, written by an override as a non-authoritative output (S0300 L523, in meaning). Some readers' values are not cuts of ρ |
| K1 (K2-d/w/r hold) | an entry of ρ removed out of band (§9.15.4). Every read is a cut of the altered ρ |

**Classification by §9.15.2's criterion.**
- **K2-w and K2-r are kernel assumptions.** When they fail, the record a reader evaluates differs from the entries the kernel's steps accepted into ρ.
- **K2-d, that the designation is made by a competent authority, is a legitimacy assumption.** CE* is correct relative to the designated record, whoever designated it. It is recorded as **L11 (record designation)**, beside L5 (root designation).

*Label: the Professor's definitional choice, for review.*

**The three boundary questions, kept apart.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is K2-d/w/r necessary? | **Yes**: by M2, without it the answers are not answers for any cut of ρ |
| Must the kernel provide it? | **K2-w:** only if the resolution of the kernel's own appends is a kernel step; the estate's single resolver for writers is that step, as specified (S0300 L513). **K2-r:** the kernel cannot provide it for readers that resolve nothing: the resolver invariant "cannot bind a reader that needs no resolver" [S0300 L454]. So K2-r stays an assumption, and is refutable only by comparing reads with the designated store. **K2-d:** never; it is an act outside the record (R1) |
| Can an application provide it without breaking kernel invariants? | **Yes for K2-w and K2-r:** a resolver is a component that chooses a store, and the estate's design is such an application: "every reader and writer of governance evidence resolves the authority location through ONE governed mechanism" [S0300 L513]. It reads and writes no standing, so no kernel law is touched |

*Trivial cases.* The empty record satisfies K2-d/w/r trivially, and CE* answers on the empty record. A refuse-all kernel appends nothing, so K2-w holds vacuously. The restated K2 is a "must-never" constraint (no misdirected write, no foreign read). It does not oblige the kernel to do anything, and nothing here claims that it does.

### R3. Membership is decided by the designation in force at the write, not by content (Ded); a designation change is decidable only if recorded with the closed content (Ded)

`[CON-0262 · provisional]`

**(a) Content cannot decide membership.**

*Proof.* Let e be appended to store s at time t₁, while s was ρ. Let e′, byte-identical to e, be appended to s at t₂, after a designation moved ρ to s′. By the definition, e is of ρ and e′ is not. They have the same content, so no content predicate separates them. ∎

This is the estate's "one mismatch, two causes, opposite dispositions" (§8.19 C18; S0138 L119–127, in meaning), stated in general.

**(b) What decides it.** Membership needs two things:
- the designation history (which store was ρ, from when);
- the order of each write relative to each designation change.

**(c) When the order is decidable from the records.** A designation change can be decided for writes to the old store if the change is recorded **with an enumeration of the old store's content at the change**. The enumeration lists the entries, or commits to a list of them.

- **Within the enumeration:** an entry is of ρ, written before the change.
- **Outside it:** an entry was written after the enumeration was taken. It is classed **not of ρ**.

The estate's design states exactly this boundary (S0300 L896–899, in meaning): content inside the recorded enumeration is accounted for; content outside it is post-demotion by construction.

*Proof of (c).* Write E for the enumeration, taken at t_E.
- Every member of the old ρ written before t_E is in E.
- An entry outside E was therefore written after t_E. A write after the switch is not of ρ, by the definition.
- A write in [t_E, switch) is of ρ, but is outside E. So it is misclassified.

That window is irreducible without serialisation, and the misclassification is in the safe direction: the write is preserved and not promoted. The estate records the same window (S0300 L905, in meaning). ∎

**(d) Corollaries for the questions collected in Ch. 8.**
- **The closing record lies outside the record it closes.** A statement that closes ρ by a content commitment cannot commit to its own bytes (CON-0254 (i)). A statement that is itself an entry of ρ written after E would fall outside E. So the closing statement is kept in another record: the estate puts it in the newly designated store, as its first governance append after the transfer (S0300 L1315, in meaning). This is §8.20 C6 / CON-0254 restated as a membership consequence.
- **Order across records (CON-0251).**
  - The "if" half (a citation shows order) holds under K2-r, since the cited entry is in a cut the citing writer read.
  - The "only if" half is **not** a K2 corollary. It needs CON-0251's own premise: no shared order source, physical or logical. R3 (c) uses it: with no common clock (the estate dates "2 of 216" transitions, S0300 L1204, in meaning), the enumeration, a content list, is the only order source.
  - So the answer to §8.20.3 ("as a K2 corollary") is **partly**: the "if" half only.
- **Membership at write time (§8.19.3).** D does not need the resolution in force at each write as a field. It needs the designation changes recorded with their enumerations (R3 (c)). Between designation changes, every kernel write goes to ρ by K2-w.

*Prior work (Ext, not opened in this session).* Deciding a write's validity by the configuration in force when it was written is the epoch, or view, of replicated systems. *Ext:* Oki and Liskov 1988 (viewstamped replication, EXT-096). A write carrying a stale epoch is rejected. **Classification: established (adapted).** The adaptation is that the book has no epoch field on entries, so the enumeration does the epoch's work after the fact.

### R4. Detection by an outside reference: coverage, independence, operand (Ded; model check)

`[CON-0263 · provisional]` (CON-0243, CON-0257 and CON-0259 are its three parts)

**Setting.** A store is relied on after an irreversible act; in the estate, the removal of the source. A reference is taken at t_a and compared with a store at a check before the act.

The three conditions:
- **A1, coverage** (CON-0243): the reference covers every entry written before the act. In particular it is taken **after the last write before the act**, which is the "taken again at the operation it protects" of §8.18.3;
- **A2, independence** (CON-0257): the reference carries information that does not come from the checked content and that the act cannot rewrite;
- **A3, operand** (CON-0259): the comparison's operand is the store that will be relied on after the act (the survivor), not the one the act removes.

**Claim.**
- (i) With a reference that lists entries, A1 ∧ A2 ∧ A3 imply that every loss is detected.
- (ii) A loss-free act passes the check.
- (iii) Each condition is necessary: for each, there is a case where the other two hold, it fails, and a loss goes undetected.

*Proof sketch, then the check.*
- **(i)** By A1 and A2, the reference equals the content that ought to survive. By A3 the survivor is compared with it. A lost entry is in the reference and not in the survivor, so the comparison differs.
- **(ii)** A loss-free survivor equals the reference.
- **(iii)**
  - Without A1, a write after t_a is in neither the reference nor a survivor copied before it.
  - Without A2, a reference taken from the survivor matches the survivor whatever it lost.
  - Without A3, comparing the source shows nothing about a faulty copy.

**Model check:** `tmp/tools/anchor_model.py`, run `anchor_model-run-20261024.txt`. It covers 32,000 scenarios: three entries, write times 0–3, a copy time, one dropped entry or none, the reference time, its origin, and the operand. Results:
- sufficiency and no false alarm hold, with 0 counterexamples;
- each condition has a witness of necessity.

*Its scope:* the check and the act are simultaneous in the model; the reference is a list; the copy is one step.

**A hash in place of a list.** A2 and A3 are unchanged. A1 holds by the hash's binding property (an L8-type assumption). Detection then shows **that** the set differs; only a list shows **what** differs (CON-0243).

**What the conditions do not give.**
- **The interval between the check and the act** is not covered. A write in it is lost undetected. The estate: "cannot be caught without a lock" [S0300 L1041].
- This is §1.16.7's division: detection shrinks the window, and only prevention (one serialisation point) closes it. So A1's "again" is **necessary, not sufficient**.
- **The internal detection** of K1 (a surviving citation or a numbered successor) still works for entries outside the reference. R4 adds to it and does not replace it.

**Answer to §8.21.3 ("one concept or several?").**
- One law with three independent necessary conditions, now CON-0263.
- CON-0243, CON-0257 and CON-0259 remain as its parts, each with its own evidence.
- The corpus reached all three one at a time:
  - S0137 (RC-4) for coverage;
  - S0146 for independence;
  - S0142 (DV-1) for the operand.
- Its last review keeps the claim scope apart: a verification receipt "attests the COMPARISON, not the WRITE." [S0253 L186].

*Prior work.*
- *Src:* the corpus states the commitment mechanism, "A root hash provides a compact commitment to a collection." [S0917 L429]. It treats freeze verification as the provable claim: "No write occurred during the window" "is provable by comparing the manifest's hashes" [S0300 L632].
- *Ext (not opened):* tamper-evident logs (EXT-078, EXT-079) give detection relative to an outside commitment, which is A1. The test-oracle problem (EXT-094) is A2. The gap between check and act is the time-of-check-to-time-of-use race (EXT-097, Bishop and Dilger 1996).
- **Classification: established in its parts; the three-way decomposition with an independence model is the book's (adapted).**

### R5. What changes in §9.15 (Ded)

- **K2** is replaced by the restated K2 (K2-d, K2-w, K2-r; R2). The old wording is a consequence (R2).
- **K1** is unchanged in content. Its "refutable inside?" cell now points to R4: with an outside reference meeting A1–A3, a loss among the covered entries is detected. The interval between check and act is not covered.
- **L11 (record designation)** is added: the designation is made by the competent authority. Under DB-1, CE* is correct relative to the designated record whatever its source.

## 9.17.5 Open questions

- **K2-r's refutation.** Is a periodic comparison of readers' values with the designated store an internal check, or a world observation?
- **Designation as a kind in D.** Should a designation act (with its enumeration) become an admitted kind, so that R3 (c) is computed by CE*? It would change D (a Principal's decision).
- **K5's two levels of identity (CON-0240)** are untouched here.
- **The check–act interval.** Is it a K4-type law: one serialisation point for removal?

## 9.17.6 What does not hold

- **"Record identity is part of K2"** (one reading of §8.18.3): K2 as worded does not imply it (M2).
- **"Cross-record order is a K2 corollary"** (§8.20.3): only the "if" half is (R3 (d)).
- **"Taking the anchor again at the operation it protects suffices"**: it is necessary, not sufficient (R4).
