# 9.17 The designated record, its carriers, and checking a carrier change

*Theory turn 8 (Rulebook v1.4 §12b), 2026-10-24, on the Principal's instruction: "one theory turn on record identity and anchoring (K1/K2), from the kernel questions collected in §8.18–§8.21". The research question was committed before any search (`review/TURN8-RECORD-IDENTITY/RESEARCH-QUESTION.md`). Reviewed by two reviewers on different models and settled (`review/TURN8-RECORD-IDENTITY/SETTLEMENT.md`). Status: provisional, as settled.*

## 9.17.1 In plain language

§9.15's kernel assumptions K1 and K2 both speak of "the record". Windows C25–C28 showed that the record cannot say where it is kept: two byte-identical copies look the same to every check that reads their entries.

This section draws four conclusions.

1. **The record and its carrier are different things.**
   - The record is the sequence of entries the kernel's steps accepted.
   - A carrier is the store that holds it.
   - Which store carries the record is fixed by a designation, an act, not by anything in the entries.
   - A record can change carrier and stay the same record.
2. **K2 as worded leaves its referent implicit.**
   - It asks only that readers agree with one another.
   - Restated, it asks that every write go to the designated carrier and every read be a cut of the record.
   - The old K2 follows from the restatement.
3. **Whether an entry is of the record depends on whether it was accepted into the record.**
   - A carrier change carries the record's entries across; the copies are the same entries, not new ones.
   - Without a trusted clock shared with the designation, an entry's content cannot show which side of a carrier change it was written on.
   - A recorded list of the old carrier's members, taken when it closes, then gives a conservative classification. It errs only by withholding, never by promoting.
4. **Checking a carrier change before the old carrier is retired** (an architecture result, not a kernel one):
   - the new carrier must be checked to contain what must be kept;
   - what must be kept is known from the old carrier read at the check;
   - an outside list taken earlier, not derived from the new carrier, also covers entries the old carrier may since have lost;
   - entries lost before anything read them are out of reach;
   - writes between the check and the act need serialisation.

## 9.17.2 Definitions

- **Record:** the sequence of entries that the kernel's steps accepted into it. With several application records (§9.16 R2 (d); F22), there is a record set Ρ = {ρ₁ … ρₙ}.
- **Carrier:** a store (a file, a table) that holds a record's entries. A store has a value at each time, and several stores can hold the same entries.
- **Designation:** an act that names which carrier holds a given record from a stated time.
  - It need not lie outside every record: the estate records it in the new carrier, as the first governance append after the transfer (S0300 L1315, in meaning).
  - It cannot be read off the carriers' content (R1).
- **Resolution:** what a reader or writer actually uses when it means "the record". It has two parts:
  - the **location** (which store);
  - the **predicate** that selects entries there. In the estate the predicate is exactly `*.json` (S0300 L609, in meaning), and a reader's predicate is part of what it reads (§8.20 C4).
  - A resolution belongs to a component, not to the record (§8.18 C1, CON-0239).
- **Cut of ρ:** a prefix of ρ's entries, named by its entries (§9.15 K2; §9.16 R2 (d)).
- **Member:** an entry e is a member of ρ if a kernel step accepted e into ρ, that is, appended it to the carrier designated for ρ at that time.
  - A carrier change (carry-across) copies members into the new carrier. They stay the same members: entry identity is content-derived (§9.16 R2).
  - A write to a carrier that does not hold ρ at that time is **foreign**: an override write, or a write to a retired carrier.
- **Loss (in a carrier change):** a member of ρ held by the old carrier that the new carrier does not hold after the old one is retired. A foreign entry left behind is not a loss.

## 9.17.3 Results

### R1. Which store carries the record is not a property of its entries (Ded)

**Claim.** No predicate on a store's entries can decide whether that store is the designated carrier of ρ.

*Proof.*
1. Let s₁ be the designated carrier and s₂ a byte-identical copy.
2. Any predicate P on entries gives P(s₁) = P(s₂), because the entries are identical.
3. The designation separates s₁ from s₂.
4. So designation is not a function of the entries. ∎

**Scope:** predicates on entries only. A predicate that may also read the location is a resolution, not a property of the record.

**What this means for D.**
- D is a predicate on one record's entries (§9.13.2), so it cannot supply the carrier. This is the step CON-0239 left open.
- **A change of carrier is an entry of D (the Principal, 2026-10-25: "a designation becomes a recordable kind in D. Order across designations comes from citation (the switch cites the old store's last entry, CON-0251), not from recording time.").** A designation entry names the new carrier and cites the last entry under the old one (§9.13.2, kind designate). It is therefore accepted by a kernel step and has K3's form: correct when it equals what the kernel's steps accepted. Only the **root designation**, the initial carrier, stays outside D; it is located under L11.
- Admitting designation as a kind of D would move it into K3's form. That is open (§9.17.5).

*Corpus (Src), on point:* the estate states the same separation as a design rule: "do not let STORAGE LOCATION accidentally become DOMAIN OWNERSHIP" [S0300 L1286]; "a location does not confer authority" [S0300 L1008]. S0300 is a specification that has never been executed (S0253 L252–258, in meaning).

*Related, not on point (analogies):*
- the corpus counts byte-identical replicas as one evidential source (S0917 L1320–1332; S0904 L328, both in meaning). That concerns the independence of evidence, not the identity of a record;
- of three meanings of "single source of truth" only "One system is authoritative for a particular fact." [S1375 L83] is held defensible. That concerns authority per fact. The likeness to designation is an analogy.

### R2. The restated K2: a designated record (Ded)

**K2 as it stands** (§9.15, before this turn): readers agree on the entries of the cut, the cut named by its entries. It names no referent, so there are two readings of it.
- **Reading 1:** readers agree with one another.
- **Reading 2:** readers agree on cuts of ρ. That is K2-r below.

**Countermodel M2, for reading 1.** K1 and K2 hold, yet the readers do not evaluate the current cut of ρ.
1. The one writer resolves a store s ≠ ρ's carrier for one append e. s holds a copy of ρ's entries, so e's citations resolve and s is in D.
2. Every reader also resolves s.
3. The readers agree on s's entries, so reading 1 holds.
4. ρ's carrier changed only through kernel steps, so K1 holds.
5. s holds e, which is not a member of ρ. Let e annul an act that ρ holds as current. Then every reader's CE* answer differs from CE* on the current cut of ρ. ∎

*The estate's actual misdirection is weaker.* A wrong resolution creates an empty new location (S0300 L578, in meaning), where e's citations dangle. That store is outside D, and Q1 refuses it. So M2 is a model, not an observed case.

**So the restatement makes the referent explicit.** It is a re-axiomatisation, not a derivation. `[CON-0261 · provisional]`
- **(K2-d) presupposition:** the record set Ρ and the carrier of each record are designated: the initial carrier by the root designation, outside D; every later carrier by a designation entry in D. K2-w and K2-r presuppose it, and so does K1's "the record" (CON-0239). It cannot fail alone, so no independence is claimed for it. Whether the root designation was made by a competent authority is **L11**, a legitimacy assumption. A designation entry's authorization is evaluated like any act's.
- **(K2-w) writes:** every kernel append goes to the carrier that the designation in force names.
- **(K2-r) reads:** every value a reader takes as the record is a cut of a designated record, one prefix per record.

**The old K2 follows, within one designation (Ded).**
- Two readers are asked for the same cut, named by its entries.
- By K2-r, each value is a prefix of ρ.
- Under one designation, ρ is one sequence. Across a carrier change it is the same sequence by carry-across, and the gate of R4 checks this.
- Two prefixes of one sequence with the same named entries are identical, since D's identifiers are unique.
- So the readers agree on the cut's entries. Tombstones are entries of ρ, so they agree on each tombstone's removal acts too. ∎
- The converse fails (M2).

**K2-w, K2-r and K1 are independent (countermodels).**

| Fails alone | Model |
|---|---|
| K2-w | M2, with no reader of s: the accepted act is missing from ρ, while every reader reads a cut of ρ |
| K2-r | the writer appends to ρ's carrier; a reader takes a store holding ρ's entries plus a foreign override write (S0300 L523, in meaning) |
| K1 | a member of ρ is removed out of band (§9.15.4); every read is a cut of the altered ρ |

**The rival "identity folded into K1."** Read K1's "no entry is lost" as covering a misdirected accepted write, an entry lost to ρ. That reading needs ρ fixed, so it presupposes K2-d, and it covers K2-w's failure. It does not cover K2-r's failure: a reader can take a foreign store while ρ itself is intact. So K1 cannot absorb K2-r, and the rival is not adopted. K1's referent is now explicit: "the record" in K1 is ρ (R5).

**Classification by §9.15.2's criterion.**
- **K2-w and K2-r are kernel assumptions:** when they fail, the record a reader evaluates differs from the entries the kernel's steps accepted into ρ.
- **L11 (record designation) is a legitimacy assumption**, beside L5 (root designation). The record that holds designations must itself be designated. That regress ends at a **root designation**, an act outside the kernel, located under L11 just as L5 locates the root entries.

*Label: the Professor's definitional choice, for review.*

**The three boundary questions, kept apart.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is it necessary? | **Yes.** Without K2-w and K2-r, M2 is possible |
| Must the kernel provide it? | **K2-w:** only if the resolution of the kernel's own appends is a kernel step. The estate specifies one resolver for this (S0300 L513). It holds for the estate's committed code paths, not as a structural guarantee: a substituted interpreter is itself a write path (S0300 L480–488, L424, in meaning), and the enforcing form is deferred (OPEN-M5). **K2-r:** the kernel cannot provide it for readers that resolve nothing. The resolver "cannot bind a reader that needs no resolver" [S0300 L454]. **K2-d:** no; designation is an act |
| Can an application provide it without breaking kernel invariants? | **Yes for K2-w and K2-r:** a resolver chooses a location and a predicate, and reads or writes no standing |

**Must-do.** The restated K2 is a "must-never" constraint: no misdirected write, no foreign read. A kernel that never designates, never changes a carrier and never appends satisfies it vacuously. The capabilities that make the kernel do something are §9.10's. The only must-do this section adds is that a designation exists (K2-d). The kernel does not own it: the root designation is an outside act (L11).

### R3. Membership: decided by acceptance; classifiable across a carrier change (Ded)

`[CON-0262 · invalid as stated]` `[CON-0264 · provisional]` (the restated form)

**(a) Without a trusted order shared with the designation, the content of entries cannot place an entry on one side of a carrier change.**

*Proof, with two histories.*
- In history H₁, an entry e is accepted into carrier s while s carries ρ.
- In history H₂, the same entry, with identical content, is written to s after ρ moved to s′. In H₂ it is foreign.
- s's final content is the same in H₁ and H₂, so no predicate on that content separates the two histories. ∎

**Scope.** Each entry carries a recording time τ, non-decreasing along positions within a carrier (§9.8 H2). Suppose τ is **trusted** and **shared** with the time recorded for the designation change, that is, a shared order source in CON-0251's sense. Then "τ(e) before the change" is a content predicate, and membership is decidable. This is the epoch rule of replicated systems (*Ext:* EXT-096, not opened). (a) therefore holds **only without** such a source. The estate has none: only "2 of 216" transitions carry any time field (S0300 L1204, in meaning).

**(b) Classification with a closing list.** Let a designation change be recorded together with E, a list of the old carrier's members taken at t_E. The list may instead be a commitment to such a list.

*Premises:*
- E is **exact**: it lists the old carrier's entries at t_E;
- the old carrier lost no member before t_E. A member lost before any read is out of reach of every check (R4 C3).

*Rule:*
- an entry in E is a member written before the change;
- an entry outside E is **classed** foreign.

This is a **conservative classification, not a decision**. A member written between t_E and the switch is classed foreign: it is withheld, not promoted. The window is irreducible without serialisation. The estate's design states the same boundary and window (S0300 L896–899, L905, in meaning).

*Other routes:*
- trusted shared τ, by (a)'s scope;
- **sealing** the old carrier at t_E, so that nothing is written after it (prevention, one serialisation point, §1.16.7).

So a closing list is **one** route, not the only one.

**(c) What is recorded, decided (the Principal, 2026-10-25: "a designation becomes a recordable kind in D. Order across designations comes from citation (the switch cites the old store's last entry, CON-0251), not from recording time.").** D does not need a field per entry naming the carrier in force. Each change of carrier is a **designation entry** (§9.13.2, kind designate). It cites the entry immediately before it in ρ's order, the last entry under the old carrier, and D refuses a designation that does not.
- **Membership across the change is then decided from the record:** the old carrier's members are ρ's entries up to and including the cited entry. Content of the old carrier outside that prefix is foreign.
- **Order across the change comes from citation, not from recording time** (CON-0251's form). R3 (a)'s scope (trusted shared τ) is not used.
- **The window closes for kernel writes.** The designation is a kernel step. With serialised steps (§9.15 K6), no member can be appended between the cited entry and the designation. A write landing in the old carrier after the cited entry was not accepted by a kernel step in ρ's order. It is foreign by definition, and is preserved, not promoted. The window of (b) remains only for writes that bypass the kernel's steps, which is a K1 or K2-w failure.
- *Model:* `tmp/tools/ce_model.py` v16, check 28. A designation that cites the last entry is in D. A gap, or an uncited closing entry, puts the record outside D, and so does a designation that closes nothing. Its value is T on a valid basis. No other value changes, since Φ reads no carrier. Every earlier check's output is unchanged against v15.
- The closing list of (b) is the estate's form of the same information. Sealing remains a route for carriers outside the kernel.

Between changes, K2-w sends every kernel append to ρ's carrier.

**(d) Where the closing statement goes.** CON-0254 (i) states two things: a content commitment (a hash) cannot feasibly cover its own bytes, and a closing **entry** can describe a state that includes itself. So membership does **not** force the closing statement out of the record it closes. A list form can name itself.

The estate's placement has a different reason, a termination argument:
- each mechanical write is evidenced by a governance append (S0300 L801–829, PREMISE 2);
- so a closing statement written into the old carrier would reopen what it closes;
- S0253 RV-1 is the same loop, met again at step (iii) (S0253 L164–191, in meaning).

Applying PREMISE 2 without exemption is a design choice of the estate (CON-0254 (ii); §8.20, A-16). So R3 restates CON-0254 as stated: (i) as a fact about hashes, (ii) as the estate's placement under PREMISE 2. It adds nothing beyond that.

**(e) Order across records (CON-0251).**
- **The "if" half:** a citation shows order. This comes from D's rule that citations name earlier entries only (§9.13.2): a citing entry follows what it cites.
- **The "only if" half** needs CON-0251's premise: no shared order source.
- Neither half is a corollary of K2. The answer to §8.20.3 is: **no**.

### R4. Checking a carrier change before the old carrier is retired (Ded; model check)

`[CON-0263 · invalid as stated]` `[CON-0265 · provisional]` (the restated form). **This is an architecture result:** D has no carriers to retire (§8.20 C1, C6). Its kernel part is the coverage clause that R5 writes into K1.

**Setting.** A record ρ moves from an old carrier OLD to a new carrier NEW. OLD is then retired, an irreversible act. Before the act a check compares stores. R is an outside list, a reference.
- **Exact:** R lists members only.
- **Not survivor-derived:** R does not come from NEW.

**Gates.**

| Gate | Checks | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | the survivor only | R ⊆ NEW |
| **G0** | the casualty against the survivor | OLD ⊆ NEW, with OLD read at the check |
| **G2** | both | R ⊆ NEW and OLD ⊆ R. Any content of OLD outside R blocks the act: by R3 (b) it is classed foreign, preserved, not promoted |

**Claims** (C1–C5):
- **C1.** A member that OLD still holds at the check and NEW lacks is detected by **G0** and by **G2**. **G1** misses it whenever R was taken before a later member was accepted. Coverage then fails, and nothing in G1 can see it.
- **C2.** A reference derived from NEW, under G1, detects no loss. It cannot cover what NEW lacks.
- **C3.** A member lost from OLD before any reference or read is detected by **no** gate. It is out of reach: only K1's internal detection (a surviving citation or a numbered successor) can show it.
- **C4.** A foreign entry in OLD blocks G0 and G2, which is the safe direction. With an exact, covering reference, no loss and no foreign entry, every gate passes: no false alarm.
- **C5.** A member lost from OLD **after** an outside reference covered it is detected by G2 and missed by G0. This is the outside reference's own contribution: coverage that does not depend on OLD staying intact until the check.

*Proofs (each one line).*
- **C1:** a loss l ∈ OLD \ NEW falsifies OLD ⊆ NEW. With R exact and covering, it also falsifies R ⊆ NEW. If R was taken before l was accepted, l ∉ R, and G1 only tests R.
- **C2:** R = NEW makes R ⊆ NEW true.
- **C3:** l is in neither OLD nor NEW, nor in any reference taken after the loss.
- **C4:** follows from the gate definitions.
- **C5:** l ∈ R, l ∉ NEW, and l ∉ OLD.

**Model check** (`tmp/tools/anchor_model.py` v2, run `anchor_model-run-20261024-v2.txt`). It covers 384,000 worlds: three members with acceptance times, a foreign entry, a loss on each carrier (early or late relative to the reference), the copy time, the reference time, and three reference origins. Results:
- C1: undetected losses, G2 0, G0 0, G1 16,792;
- C2: 0 detections;
- C3: 0 detections in 1,287 cases;
- C4: 0 foreign passes, 0 false alarms;
- C5: G2 1,620 of 1,620 cases, G0 0.

*Scope:* no write between the check and the act; a reference is a list. Version 1 of the model is superseded; it modelled only copy loss, so its sufficiency held by construction.

**The independence condition is the means to coverage, not a fourth condition.**
- A reference derived from NEW covers nothing NEW lost (C2).
- So "the reference must not come from the checked content" (CON-0257) is what lets a reference cover. It is not independent of coverage.
- CON-0243 (coverage), CON-0257 (independence as its means) and CON-0259 (compare the survivor, **not only** the casualty) are therefore three aspects of one gate. They are not three independent necessary conditions.

**What the gate does not cover.**
- **The interval between the check and the act.** A write in it is classed foreign at best and lost at worst. The estate: "cannot be caught without a lock" [S0300 L1041]. This is §1.16.7's division: detection shrinks the interval, and only prevention closes it. Re-taking the reference at the act ("taken again at the operation it protects", §8.18.3) narrows the interval; it does not remove it.
- **Members lost before any read** (C3).
- **Equality of entries.** Entries are compared by identity, content-derived (§9.16 R2). A byte comparison also needs one decoder and a byte-preserving medium (CON-0240). A hash in place of a list needs the same algorithm and representation (S0917 L220, in meaning). The binding of the hash secures the comparison; it does not secure coverage.

*Corpus (Src):*
- "A root hash provides a compact commitment to a collection." [S0917 L429];
- the estate checks the casualty against a closed list and the survivor against the same list, "two different operands, two different gates, both required" (S0300 L941–947, in meaning);
- a verification receipt "attests the COMPARISON, not the WRITE." [S0253 L186];
- *lineage:* S0300 and S0253 are one line of support (S0253 reviews S0300, and S0300 as read already contains S0253's repairs). The estate sources behind CON-0243, 0257 and 0259 (S0137, S0142, S0146) are one review chain, not three lines.

*Ext (not opened):* tamper-evident logs (EXT-078, EXT-079); the test-oracle problem (EXT-094); the time-of-check-to-time-of-use interval (EXT-097). **Classification: established in its parts; the gate's decomposition is the book's (adapted).**

### R5. What changes in §9.15 (Ded)

- **K2** is replaced by the restated K2: K2-d presupposition, K2-w, K2-r (R2). The old wording follows within one designation.
- **K1's referent is ρ.** Its refutation cell gains the kernel part of R4: a loss among the entries an outside commitment covers is detected by comparing the carrier with that commitment. The commitment must not be derived from the carrier. Entries lost before any commitment, and writes between a check and an act, are not covered. The cell no longer cites a store-removal act.
- **L11 (record designation)** is added, with the root-designation regress located there.

## 9.17.5 Open questions

- *(Decided, the Principal, 2026-10-25: a designation is a kind in D, and order across designations comes from citation; R3 (c).)*
- **K2-r's refutation.** Is comparing readers' values with the designated carrier an internal check, or a world observation?
- **L11's scope.** Competence of a designation per record, as S1375 bounds authority by domain, proposition and time (S1375 L961–993, in meaning).
- **The check–act interval** as a K4-type law: one serialisation point for retiring a carrier.
- **K5's two levels of identity** (CON-0240) are untouched.

## 9.17.6 What does not hold

- **CON-0262, membership "iff appended while designated"** (INVALID as stated). It classed carried-across entries as non-members, and its "decidable only with a closing list" is false: sealing and trusted shared time also decide or classify. Restated as CON-0264.
- **CON-0263, "detection iff coverage, independence and operand, three independent necessary conditions"** (INVALID as stated). Its loss counted foreign entries. Independence is derivable from coverage. The operand condition dropped "not only". The interval clause contradicted the timing reading of coverage. Version 1 of the model was tautological. Restated as CON-0265.
- **"The closing record lies outside the record it closes"** (draft R3 (d)). This reintroduced CON-0252 (INVALID). A list can name itself; only a hash cannot.
- **"Cross-record order: the 'if' half from K2"**: it comes from D's citation rule.
- **"Record identity is part of K2"**: under reading 1, K2 does not imply it (M2). Under reading 2, K2 is K2-r.
- **"Taking the anchor again at the operation it protects suffices"**: it narrows the interval and does not close it.
