## 9.6.1 In plain language

The question is where the record of authority belongs: in the kernel, or in an application context. Three separate questions decide it:
1. Does the kernel need such a record at all?
2. Must the kernel build it itself?
3. Could applications build it without breaking the kernel's rules?

The analyses find:
- recording an act is needed if the kernel must answer questions of standing;
- the **evaluator** that computes standing from the records is needed as well; it can be left to applications only if they share one versioned evaluation rule and value set, and where that rule lives is open (it is not shown to be a stronger kernel candidate than the recorder);
- whether recording must be in the kernel depends on how many independent applications the kernel serves, and on whether the record has to keep any rule of uniqueness across them;
- the platform as built would fail the test in several places;
- none of those failures, by itself, decides where the record belongs.

## 9.6.2 The capability under test, and the invariants it is tested against

**Definitions.**
- **The machinery (Src):** BC-7's record of work items, session assignments, transitions and grants, with its fold and its read-only queries (§6.5.2; S0067 L33, L73–77).
- **Two capabilities, split by this book (Int; the sources keep one root):**
  - **CR-a, registering human acts:** recording that an external human act occurred, by reference, with what it is about. This covers **both** grants (`humanActRef`) **and** the human act carried by every START: "START 35/35 human" [S0078 L63]. CR-a does not evaluate authority. S0078 L63 puts human-act registration in both parts of the record;
  - **CR-w, orchestrating governed work:** lanes, roles as values, handoffs, mutation ownership.

  **The sources argue for one root, not a split.** The record's two parts have cross-part reads: `authorized` requires a registered session [S0072 L32]; `identity` reads both parts [S0078 L266]. The split is this book's analytical choice, made to test necessity separately. It is not the sources' model. `[CON-0135 · invalid as stated]` `[CON-0169 · provisional]`
- **The evaluator, CE** `[CON-0138 · provisional]`**:** the function that answers "does item k have standing in scope x at time t?" from the records. It needs:
  - the acts' times and validity periods (§1.16.2);
  - scope containment;
  - endings (§1.13).

  The recorder may not evaluate (ARCH-INV-1), so CE is a third capability.

**Kernel invariants used below:**

| Name | Statement | Home · status |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | only an act confers standing | §1.6 · axiom |
| ARCH-INV-1 | the mechanism records authority and never grants it | §1.14.2 · Src (an ARB proposal) |
| RA | a record of an act is not the act; verification shows only records of sufficient integrity | §1.12 · Ded |
| I-1 (book) | knowledge never silently disappears | §0.5 · stated |
| U | a **uniqueness** needs a serialization point, or only detection is possible | §1.16.7 · Ded |
| RL | Governance **records** assignments, grants, handoffs, STARTs, transitions | §1.14.3 roles table · corpus law |

**The form of the test (§9.1; RESEARCH-PURPOSE):**
- Kernel′(M, G) = P_S ∧ P_T ∧ P_R ∧ Req (§9.1 C6; the laws above are transition or trace invariants, which §9.1 C2's Kernel(M) = P ∧ Req cannot express);
- a capability is a kernel candidate **only if it is necessary and not delegable**;
- delegability is tested by **relocation**: "move the capability into several independent applications and check the invariants".

**Req for KnowledgeOS is not defined (T9-01)**, so every result is conditional, and the condition is named.

## 9.6.3 Analysis 1: is the capability necessary?

**Definition.** A capability c is **necessary for a requirement r** if no system, kernel plus applications, built only from preserving mechanisms without c, reaches r. *("Mechanism set" means the whole system's.)* It is **necessary** if it is necessary for some r ∈ Req.

**Assumptions, explicit.**
- (N1) A1 holds.
- (N2) The kernel sees acts only as records (RA).
- (N3) **The candidate requirement R_s:** the kernel can answer "does item k have standing in scope x at time t?" (§1.12 verifiability; §1.14.1).

**Derivation.**
1. By N1, the answer depends on which acts occurred, with their scope and time.
2. By N2, an act is known only through a record. So **CR-a is necessary for R_s.**
3. **The record alone does not answer R_s.** The answer is derived (§1.14.1), with two times (§1.16.2) and endings (§1.13). So **CE is necessary for R_s** too.

*What kind of necessity:* steps 1–3 follow from the definition of R_s together with N1 and N2. The necessity is **definitional**: it shows what R_s means, not something learned about systems that answer it. The relocation test of Analysis 2 has not been validated on a known case, a capability whose placement is settled independently (T9-06).
4. **The present record cannot feed CE.**
   - **No time dimension:** "Only 2 of 218 transitions carry any date" [S0234 L283]; "BC-7 holds no clocks" (S0071 L197, in meaning).
   - **No scope containment:** a grant's scope is "an opaque string. Matched by equality; never parsed" (S0071 L202, in meaning).

   So R_s needs, beyond the record as built: an act time, a validity period, endings, and a scope that can be compared.
5. **CR-w is not reached by R_s.** It would be necessary only for a process requirement such as R_pv, "making role separation recordable and checkable" [S0067 L68].

**Trivial cases and boundary cases.**
- **Req = ∅:** nothing is necessary.
- **R_s ∉ Req:** neither CR-a nor CE is necessary.
- **Endings are not wholly absent:** the book's own recount (§1.13.2) found REVOKED 1 and CONSUMED 1 among 144 records. S0071's "41, all AUTHORIZED" is a census of 2026-08-17.

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it.**
- A way to answer R_s without registered acts. That would contradict N1.
- Or a reason why CE is not needed: standing stored, not derived, against §1.14.1.

## 9.6.4 Analysis 2: must the kernel provide it?

**Definition.** The kernel must provide c if c is necessary **and not delegable** (RESEARCH-PURPOSE). c is **delegable** if it can be relocated into **several independent applications** without breaking a kernel invariant or making a kernel-level answer depend on which application is asked.

**Assumptions, explicit.**
- (K1) **Occurrence is one fact.** An act occurred or did not, whoever asks.
- (K2) **The identity mechanism of an act:** either **content-derived** (computed from the act's own content, so independent recorders compute the same identity) or **assigned** (issued by someone). An assigned global identity is itself a uniqueness across applications, one identity per act, which is K4's own example. So "given global act identity" below presupposes K2 = content-derived, or else a serialization point for issuing identities.
- (K3) **The number of applications:** the kernel serves **one** application, or **several independent** ones. *Not decided by the evidence: a question for the Principal.*
- (K4) **Uniqueness constraints over acts:** whether the authority record must keep any **uniqueness** across applications. Examples: one current grant per (subject, scope), no duplicate registration of one act, one mutation owner per shared work item. *Not shown either way in the sources.* **Sub-fork:** must a violation be **prevented** (K4-p) or only **detected** and reconciled (K4-d)? U allows either (§1.16.7). For the Principal: decision brief DB-3.

**Derivation, for CR-a.**
1. **Occurrence alone does not need a single recorder**. If every act carries a **global identity** that is content-derived (K2), the records of several recorders can be merged by set union, and the union is the same whatever the order. This is "a deterministic aggregate … total and single-valued without uniqueness" (§9.4 C2, F19). The reviewer checked it on 64 small cases; that is form only.
2. **A uniqueness constraint does need one serialization point, or only detection is possible** (U, §1.16.7). §1.16.7 also allows "several write paths that all consult one shared registry or lock". So what is needed is a **shared point per constraint**, not one recorder.
3. **Hence, by the relocation test:**
   - with **one** application (K3), every option is trivially delegable;
   - with **several** applications and **no** uniqueness constraint across them (K4), CR-a is delegable, given a content-derived global act identity (K2);
   - with **several** applications **and** a uniqueness constraint that must be **prevented** (K4-p), the serialization point must be shared by applications that are independent of one another. **Something above them** must provide it. That makes the serialization point a kernel candidate. In the kernel type, this is the shared append-only history variable that uniqueness needs (§9.1 C6 results 4–5). The recording itself still need not be one;
   - with a uniqueness constraint that need only be **detected** (K4-d), a shared check after the fact (a merge that reports violations) suffices. It need not sit above the applications at write time, and whether it is a kernel candidate is open.
4. **Kernel closure (KC)** is not the decider. Applied to A1, KC can never be met: a kernel checks records, not acts (RA, residual trust). Applied to ARCH-INV-1, it is circular: an invariant **of the recorder** is a kernel invariant only if the recorder is in the kernel. KC stays as an **alternative stipulation**, defined as "checkable **on records** by kernel capabilities alone", and it excludes invariants that presuppose a placement. `[CON-0136 · invalid as stated]` `[CON-0170 · provisional]`

**Derivation, for CE.**
5. Standing must be one answer per (item, scope, time), whoever asks; K1 extended to standing, a stipulation. CE computes it.
6. If several applications each evaluate, they agree only if they share the evaluation rule **and** the inputs. Shared inputs need the merged record (step 1). A shared rule is a **contract**. So CE is delegable only under a **common, versioned rule and value set** over a shared or mergeable record (§9.7, Analysis 2 step 4). That the rule's home is the kernel does **not** follow (§9.7.9), so this step does not make CE a stronger kernel candidate than CR-a. The order of acts is a uniqueness only where act times are absent, tied or untrusted (F26).

**Trivial cases and boundary cases.**
- **A plane and a context need not differ in substance** (Int). This interpretation leaves a recorded conflict between two acts of unequal standing and order:
  - V3, an AI-generated Track-B proposal of 2026-08-16, rules "plane" (§1.14.3);
  - ADR-AIP-03, accepted by the PO/ARB, recognizes a context, of which S0065 L28 records the acceptance.

  Supersession between them is not recorded. Their acceptance is **not** evidence of what the machinery is.
- **The plane notion moved in the corpus.** S0234 calls something else a control plane: "C-5 Separation attestation … cross-context control-plane assurance capability … NOT a bounded context" [S0234 L126]. That rests on a T4·G proposal in S0234's own grading.
- **The notation is biased:** the Capability Identity Invariant, "one capability identifier = one semantic owner" [S0068 L12], cannot write a capability shared across contexts. It leans the map toward (C).

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it.**
- A uniqueness constraint that a set union satisfies without a shared point (it would break U);
- **or** a case where several recorders disagree on occurrence despite global act identity;
- **or** a reason why the kernel serves only one application.

## 9.6.5 Analysis 3: can applications provide it without violating the invariants?

**Definition.** Delegation is **safe** if each relocated copy preserves every kernel invariant that c bears on (§6.5 C2), and a breach would be detectable.

**Assumptions, explicit.**
- (D1) BC-7 is the one application instance in the evidence. Its subdomain class, "Supporting", is a strategic class, not an architectural layer, and does not decide placement.
- (D2) The evidence is BC-7 as built and measured, 2026-08-17 to 2026-08-21: dated snapshots.

**Derivation: one invariant at a time, against BC-7 as built.**

| Invariant | Preserved? | Evidence | Fixable inside an application? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARCH-INV-1 (records, never grants) | yes | a grant refuses to exist without `humanActRef` [S0067 L51]; "It can never refuse an *unauthorized* one" [S0071 L293]. *Circularity:* the boundary is read off the same mechanism (S0067 L144; S0071 L282); one line, not a test | — |
| single authority writer (I-5) | **the declared writer role is checked; the actual writer is unattested** | the grant path refuses any writer role but governance [S0078 L206], and it "sets `registeredBy = 'governance'` unconditionally" [S0078 L143]. So 47/47 is structural, not evidence. And "A **second write-capable path** exists" through `KOS_MECHANISM_PATH` [S0234 L20] | partly: one write path can be enforced; the actual writer cannot be attested anywhere (§8.6 C3) |
| append-only, nothing lost (I-1) | **no, as built** | "density is NOT a completeness proof": "23 / 30 concurrent-append trials silently losing a transition while every survivor was dense and monotonic" [S0234 L282]; and the store is gitignored, "Authority state has no provenance" [S0062 L113] | yes: a lock or version check, and a tracked durable store |
| recorder role law (RL: Governance records handoffs and STARTs) | **no, by accepted practice** | **HANDOFFs:** the two `architecture` HANDOFFs [S0071 L237] were recorded by the handing-over lane, the actor DP-3 requires [S0078 L201–204]; by RL as stated they are breaches (a scoped RL that leaves HANDOFFs to the mutation owner would clear them: Hyp). **STARTs:** a producing lane "wrote its own seq-3 START into the work-item record" [S0071 L11], a practice "disclosed and accepted" [S0078 L294]. A second instance, BASELINE-002, is disclosed in S0067 L9 (in meaning). In BASELINE-003 the commission had recorded "START NOT performed" for the Human START [S0070 L7] before the producer wrote it. Two instances, one line of support. And `recordedBy` on STARTs names the origin, not the recorder (OQ-10, S0078 L258), shown in 6 explicit cases of 37 (S0073 L88, L155, in meaning) and not independently reproducible (§8.8) | yes, if the role law is kept; or the law is amended (a Governance act) |
| fold fixity | **no, as built** | "Interpretation authority is environment-swappable" [S0062 L112] | yes |

**Result (Ded, given D1–D2):**
- **As built, BC-7 fails I-1 (lost writes, no history), RL (for STARTs, and for HANDOFFs as RL is stated), and the single-write-path part of I-5.**
- Each failure is fixable inside an application, except the attestation of the actual writer, which is fixable nowhere.
- **So the failures as built do not, by themselves, decide placement.** What decides it is Analysis 2 (K3, K4), and Analysis 3 supplies the **contract** a delegated record must keep `[CON-0137 · invalid as stated]` `[CON-0171 · provisional]`:
  1. registration only by reference to an external act, with no evaluation;
  2. one write path, and a declared writer role;
  3. append-only with **no lost update** (a lock or version check) on a durable, tracked store;
  4. a global identity for each act;
  5. an act time and validity, endings, and a comparable scope (for CE);
  6. a fixed fold;
  7. references never dereferenced (T-2).
- **Does not hold:** the "one recorder per act-space" law. It is neither sufficient (one recorder lost writes) nor necessary (set union, or a shared lock).

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it.**
- An invariant missing from the table;
- or evidence that a failure is intrinsic to delegation.

## 9.6.9 What does not hold (refuted at review TT1; one claim withdrawn after review C15)

- **"CE is delegable only as a kernel-defined function over a shared record; CE is therefore the stronger kernel candidate"** (Analysis 2 step 6, first form). "Kernel-defined" does not follow from a shared rule (§9.7.9); what follows is a common, versioned rule with its home open.
- **"The one-recorder rule, a kernel law (Ded, from U and K1)."** INVALID. It is not sufficient: one recorder lost 23 of 30 concurrent writes [S0234 L282]. It is not necessary: set union with global identity, or several paths consulting one lock (§1.16.7). U governs uniqueness, not occurrence (TT1 critical finding 1).
- **"Must the kernel provide CR-a? Decided by KC."** INVALID as the decider. The purpose's relocation test into several applications decides; KC is never satisfiable for A1 and circular for ARCH-INV-1 (TT1 critical finding 2; finding 7).
- **"Append-only history: yes"** (Analysis 3). It rested on `seq` contiguity, which S0234's erratum shows survives lost writes (finding 3).
- **The CR-a/CR-w split "along I-4" (Ded from the sources).** The registration of human acts occurs in both parts, and the sources argue for one root. The split is restated as the book's choice (finding 5).
- **"I-5 … enforced in full on the authority part"** and **"not a breach after all"** for the STARTs. They overreached (finding 6; §9.6.7).
- **"The move was decided, not executed."** Contested in the corpus (finding 9).
- **The options (C) and (S) as distinct.** They were not (finding 8).
- **R_s needing only a record.** It needs an evaluator, time and scope containment (finding 4).
- **BC-7's subdomain class "Supporting" as deciding placement** (D1, Analysis 3). It is a strategic class, not an architectural layer, and does not decide placement.

*Withdrawn after review C15 (Rulebook step 9, the review of the TT2 settlement):*
- **"So the evaluation rule is a kernel-defined function: a kernel candidate under the purpose's test"** (§9.6.6a item 2; the claim CON-0142 states for §9.7, INVALID as stated; its restated form is CON-0173). TT2 found "the kernel must own" a non sequitur (§9.7.9), and review C15 confirmed it: what follows is a common, versioned rule, with its home open.
