## 9.20.1 In plain language

1. **What the kernel must remember is its record: the entries of the admitted kinds, each with its required fields, in record order.** Everything the core questions ask (current authority, lineage, the evidence class) and everything the guards check can be worked out from that record and a few stated inputs.
2. **A table of attributes per item is not enough.** Two records can give an item the same standing, provenance and grade and still answer differently after the same next entry; a table that forgets the order of entries cannot answer as the record stood at an earlier point; one that forgets act times cannot order acts.
3. **Standing and the evidence class are not stored; they are worked out.** A stored copy would go stale: an annulment voids a grant from the start, so values computed earlier change.
4. **So most of the attributes F13 lists are of three sorts:** fields of entries (who acted, on what scope, when), values worked out from the record (standing, the class, the current owner of a grant), or inputs the kernel does not hold (the policy values, which scope covers which, the identity link).
5. **What stays open:** whether the record is the *smallest* such state; the state for the kinds not yet admitted (item lifecycle, registration); item identity across versions; how the class relates to the older grade vocabularies; and the bearer question F37.

**Outcome: partially resolved** (§9.20.6).

## 9.20.4 Step 3: the alternatives, tested

**Th1. The record is a sufficient state for Req's core (Ded; under stated premises).**
*Premises:* C1 = constitute; Req's core is stated over D (§9.19 L166–167, "conditional on stating Req over D"); the inputs of §9.20.3; the kinds admitted to D and W.

| Step | Content | Justification |

| 2 | Q2's reference is "the record itself, cut at s" | §9.10 Q2 |
| 3 | Q4's reference is g1 at s, from the record's entries and stated inputs | §9.18.2; U2 (a) |
| 4 | Q3's output is defined over the grants recorded at s; its reference definition is "still to be written" | §9.10 Q3 |
| 5 | every guard reads the record and CE*: citations of present earlier entries; the single-use guard; the owner check; transfers; removals (T at append); designations (maximal entries); W's acceptance rule G8 | §9.13.2; §9.18.5 G8 |
| 6 | the effect of each accepted input (O1–O4) is an entry appended, or an earlier entry tombstoned by a recorded removal | §9.10.3; §9.19.4 G-kind; §9.13.2 |
| 7 | so two states that are the same record give the same answer to every core observer, the same guard verdict, and the same effect for every input | 1–6 |

*Scope:* sufficiency holds for Q1, Q2 and Q4 as stated, and for Q3 **only once its reference is written over the recorded grants** (step 4; Int). It does not cover O1's kinds outside D (registration, link, retirement, establishment, declassification; supersession and suspension; §9.13 L52), whose clauses are T9-21's. **DC-1 is not met within this scope.**

**Th2. An attribute vector per item is not sufficient (Ded: a counterexample).** DC-3 asks whether an attribute vector over F13's attributes could be sufficient.
- **Model M1:** r₁ = (root, g₁: root grants A, a: A adopts k citing g₁), r₂ the same with g₂ for g₁. Item k's vector (standing T, provenance A, grade not attested) is equal in both. After the same appended entry, an annulment of g₁ by the root, Q1 for k is F on r₁ and T on r₂ (an annulment voids its target from the start; a target may name an entry not present, §9.13.2).
- **Ded:** a vector that forgot the cited bases cannot answer the next annulment. Adding the bases, then (M2) the order of entries, then (M3) the act times, rebuilds the record's information. *So V is refuted as stated, and any sufficient vector carries the record's distinctions* (Int, for the step "rebuilds": shown for the three distinctions the models exhibit, not for every field).
- **§9.1 C4's toy state** (claims, acts, standing) has standing as a component. Under C1 = constitute (decided after §9.1) standing is derived (Th1 step 1). C4's results concern the **form** of existence and minimality and stand; the toy state is not a candidate for 𝒮.

**Th3. A store of derived values adds no required distinction (Ded, with M4).**
- By Th1, every derived value is a function of the record and stated inputs; no decided rule reads a stored copy (the single-use guard **evaluates** CE* at append, §9.13.2). DC-4 is not met.
- **Model M4:** standing stored when the adoption is appended is T; after a later annulment of its grant, CE* is F, also at the adoption's own act time. A store therefore needs recomputation over the whole history, and adds an obligation (stored = derived) that the record does not have (CON-0141: stored is not true).
- *So R+C is R with a cache.* Whether to cache is an engineering choice (EA), outside the theory's 𝒮.

**Th4. Record order and act time are both required, and neither gives the other (Ded; M2, M3).** A state without positions cannot answer at a cut; a state without act times cannot order acts. §9.13.2 already keeps them independent ("Act times are not compared"; "Positions are no longer read by the evaluator; only D's citation rule reads them").

**The trivial case (DC-7).** A one-point 𝒮 answers every Q2 call with one value and every Q1 call with one value, so it cannot give T for a recorded adoption and F before it: a required, non-default answer is unreachable. The distinctions above are therefore not vacuous; the claim would be empty only for an empty Req.

**Tombstones and append-only.** 𝒮's transitions are appends and removals by tombstone. A tombstone changes an earlier entry in place (content dropped, `removed_by` added; §9.13.2). So "append-only" holds of 𝒮 **modulo removal by a recorded act** (H1′ as restated; §9.15 G3), not literally.

## 9.20.5 What is not established: minimality (DC-2)

- **Model M5:** two records that differ only in the recorder of one adoption give equal Q1 answers in all 322 cases tried (every item, act time 0–6, every cut, after none or one of five appended entries). *So for Q1 alone the record is not a minimal state:* the recorder is a distinction Q1 does not read (except through one mark when the status is signed-as-other, §9.13.2).
- **Q2 restores it for entries about an item.** Q2 returns "every entry about k in the record up to s" (§9.10 Q2), so two records that differ in an entry about some item differ in some Q2 answer (Ded). Whether this makes the record minimal for Req's core depends on Q2's reference returning whole entries, which §9.10 states.
- **Entries about no item** (the root act, grants, designations, transfers): whether every difference among them is observable by Q1, Q3 or a guard after some extension is **not shown**. *So R's minimality is open;* the strongest claim is sufficiency (Th1), with minimality shown only for entries about an item, relative to Q2's reference (Hyp for the rest).

## 9.20.6 Step 4: the conclusion

**Partially resolved.**
- **Established (Ded, within the scope of Th1):** the designated record, entries of the admitted kinds with their required fields, in record order, with act times and removal state, is a sufficient state for Req's core (Q1, Q2, Q4, and Q3 once its reference is written), the guards and O1–O4's effects. An attribute vector per item is not (Th2). A store of derived values adds no required distinction (Th3). Record order and act time are both required (Th4).
- **Classified (§9.20.7):** each F13 attribute as a field, a derived value, an input, or outside the kernel's required distinctions; open rows marked.
- **Open:** minimality of the record (§9.20.5); the state for the kinds outside D (T9-21; U6); item identity across versions (T9-14; G10); the mapping of g1's classes to §1.3's and §3.1's statuses (G7's F13 part); the bearer (F37).
- **Why not "resolved":** sufficiency is shown, minimality is not; and the kinds outside D are part of O1 (U6) with no clauses yet, so 𝒮 for them is not fixed.
- **Why not "unresolved":** sufficiency, the refutation of V, and the place of derived values follow from decided definitions (C1 = constitute; D; W; Req's core references).
