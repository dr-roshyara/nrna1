## 9.18.1 In plain language

Promise (b) says KnowledgeOS shows how well-founded a piece of knowledge is: **refuted**, **not attested**, or **supported by recorded evidence**. Today the record holds only acts of authority, so none of the three can be read from it (gap G1, §10.2.3).

This turn models evidence as **entry kinds of the one designated record**, accepted under the evidence area's own rule, and finds:

1. **Three kinds suffice:**
   - an **evidence entry**, which supports or refutes an item's content and names its producer;
   - a **withdrawal of reliance** for a purpose;
   - a **restoration** of that reliance.

   **Correcting an error is not a new kind.** It is standing's annulment, which the decision on T10-10 keeps with standing for every kind.
2. **The class can then be read from the record at a cut**, by a rule that reads no order of act times. A contradiction stays visible: the class is "refuted", and the supporting entries are listed with it. Where an input is undetermined, the class is **U with its entries named**, never laundered.
3. **"Not attested" needs no completeness rule** if it is read as *not attested in the record at this cut*, which is all promise (b) claims.
4. **Containment is possible in part.**
   - A breach of the evidence area's **own** clauses can be contained: standing's answers stay, and only the evidence answers are refused.
   - A breach of the clauses that decide **what an entry is** (identity, integrity, attestation, citations) cannot be contained, because a failing entry's kind cannot be trusted. That residue is a **stated cost of decision (1)**.
5. **A separate evidence record is not shown to be necessary.** No question about it goes to the Principal.
6. **One normative choice remains:** whose evidence counts. It is prepared as a question, queued behind T10-16.

## 9.18.2 The research question, and the method

**Question (the Principal's):** what must the record hold so that each displayed class (refuted / not attested / supported by recorded evidence) can be read from the record alone?

**Starting point (the Principal's decision):** evidence as entry kinds of the one designated record, accepted under the evidence area's own acceptance rule (§10.3.6 (d1)–(d3); T10-10 (1)). A separate record only if shown necessary.

**For each item:** is it **necessary**? Does it belong to the **kernel** or an **application**? **What would disprove it?**

**Method:** the corpus first (§9.18.3); the book's existing results as premises; the model v21 for every claim that can be checked; two reviewers on different models.

## 9.18.4 The model: three kinds, beside D's

*Modelled in v21 (`W_FIELDS`, `in_D_W`, `append_W`, `wclass`). **Not admitted to D**: v21's `in_D` still refuses them (check 34, last line).*

| Kind | Fields (besides recorder, status, store, attestation) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **evid**, evidence entry | actor (the **producer**), cited bases (the producer's grant, if any), **about** (the item), **polarity** (supports / refutes), scope, act time | a recorded claim that an item's **content** is supported or refuted, by a named producer |
| **ewdr**, withdrawal of reliance | actor, cited bases, **target** (an evidence entry), **purpose**, scope, act time | the evidence stays recorded and true as a record; it is not relied on **for this purpose** (CON-0214 (b)) |
| **erst**, restoration | actor, cited bases **including the withdrawal**, target (the withdrawal), scope, act time | reliance restored; **order by citation**, as for designations |

**Correction is standing's annulment.** An annulment of an evidence entry voids it from the start, at every cut that contains the annulment (CON-0214 (a); §9.13 Θ_f ρ). Under T10-10 (1), "removing, annulling and designating stay with standing for every kind", so W needs no correction kind. *A consequence, stated:* whether a correction takes effect is **standing's answer** (the annulment's authorization, CE*). W reads it. That is R5 of the map (W depends on S), not the reverse.

## 9.18.5 The five premises, G8, and the class rule

*For each: the result; necessary?; kernel or application?; what would disprove it.*

**(i) What an entry supports or refutes.**
- **Result:** every evidence entry has a **polarity** (supports / refutes) and an **object** (`about`: an item, its content). It has **no field for standing**, and no standing entry may take it as a basis (`in_D_S`).
- **Necessary?** **Yes.** Without polarity, "refuted" and "supported" cannot be told apart. Without the object, the class has no subject.
- **Kernel or application?** The fields are part of **W's kind** (W's acceptance rule). The kernel's step **enforces** it for W (§10.3.2: "enforces for another").
- **Disproved by:** a displayed class that needs a third polarity (for example "partially supports") or an object other than an item (a relation, a version). *Open, stated:* the **version** an entry bears on, and the bearer (F37; gaps G3, G10), are not modelled. Nor is S0294's second axis, the defeat of a relation rather than of evidence.

**(ii) Correction against withdrawal.**
- **Result:** two operations with different effects:
  - **correction** = standing's annulment (voids from the start, at every later cut, for every purpose);
  - **withdrawal** = W's `ewdr`, for a purpose, restorable by `erst`.
- **As-of effect** (check 34): before the annulment, the cut still shows "refuted"; after it, "supported". The record at a cut answers for that cut.
- **Necessary?** **Yes, both.** They differ in scope (one purpose, or all), in reversibility, and in what the display must say (false from the start, or not relied on). One kind would collapse CON-0214's distinction.
- **Kernel or application?** Correction: standing's kind, its authority standing's answer. Withdrawal and restoration: W's kinds.
- **Disproved by:** a case where the two always give the same class and display. The model shows they differ: withdrawal for p leaves q refuted.
- *Open, the Professor's reading (Int):* a query names a purpose. **With no purpose named, no withdrawal applies, and the withdrawals are listed.** `purpose = '*'` withdraws for every purpose.

**(iii) Ordering at a cut, and U.**
- **Result:** the class reads **no order of act times** (check 34: `wclass` reads no `before`).
  - Withdrawal and restoration are ordered **by citation**.
  - Correction by the annulment's presence in the cut.
  - So **ties are never read** by the class.
- **U enters** only through standing's answers that the class reads: the producer's authorization, or an annulment's or a withdrawal's authority. A U there gives **class U, with the entries named** (CON-0221). It is never shown as "not attested".
- **The rule is Kleene-monotone:**
  - a surely live refutation gives "refuted", whatever else is U;
  - a surely live support gives "supported" only if no refutation may be live.
- **Necessary?** **Yes.** Without an order rule, a withdrawal and a restoration at one act time would need a tie rule; citation order makes one unnecessary (precedent: the Principal's "order across designations comes from citation", §9.13.2 L48).
- **Kernel or application?** The rule is **W's** (its version g, §9.10 Q4). *Where it runs is not placed* (§9.18.7).
- **Disproved by:** a case in which the display needs act-time order between evidence entries, for example "the latest evidence wins". The rule g0 has no recency.

**(iv) Who may record evidence.**
- **Result, modelled both ways:**
  - **(b) "authorised":** an evidence entry counts only if its producer's authorization is T, through a cited grant whose holder is the producer, like any act (Φ, §9.13.3);
  - **"any":** every accepted entry counts.

  Check 34 shows the difference: B, holding no grant, gives "not attested" under (b) and "refuted" under "any".
- **What the book gives:** CON-0150 makes accountability need "a declared producer and an authorizing act", but allows content to be assessed without its producer when it can be re-derived. So **the book does not decide it. It is a normative choice** (T9-12, queued for the Principal behind T10-16).
- **Options:**
  - (a) any producer's entry counts;
  - (b) only authorised entries are accepted;
  - (c) every declared producer's entry is accepted and shown, but only authorised entries count for the class.
- **Recommendation:** (c).
  - It keeps every recorded claim visible (CON-0212: refusing at append would make an unauthorised refutation absent, not "not counted").
  - It keeps "supported by recorded evidence" accountable (CON-0150).
  - It reads producer authority from standing (R5).
- **Necessary?** **Yes:** gap G4 / premise (iv) cannot be left open if "supported" is to mean anything.
- **Kernel or application?** The authorization is **standing's answer**; the counting rule is **W's**.
- **Disproved by:** a corpus or book rule that evidence of unknown provenance must be refused at entry.

**(v) A completeness rule for evidence.**
- **Result:** **not needed**, if "not attested" is read as **"not attested in the record at this cut"**. Promise (b) claims no more (§10.1.3: "no guarantee"). The answer must carry that wording: CON-0212 ("absence … is not evidence of absence"); S0294 ("Do NOT invent negative evidence").
- **Necessary?** **No**, under that reading. A completeness rule would be needed only to show "no evidence exists anywhere", which is a claim about the world. Like L4 for authority, it would be a **legitimacy assumption**, never part of a value (§9.15 R3).
- **Kernel or application?** Not applicable; the wording is a **display rule** (§10.3.3 C).
- **Disproved by:** a display that must distinguish "never examined" from "examined, nothing found". The record cannot tell them apart without an entry recording an examination. *A candidate kind, not modelled; noted for the Principal's later attention, not a question now.*

**G8. Accepting evidence, with its refusal reasons (assigned to turn 5 by §10.2.3; carried here).**
- **Result:** W's acceptance rule, as the guard and D_W's clause (`append_W`, `in_D_W`, check 35). An entry is refused, with the reason:
  - an evidence entry without a valid polarity;
  - a withdrawal that targets no evidence entry;
  - a restoration that does not cite the withdrawal it restores;
  - any entry failing a **generic** clause (a citation of an entry not present, integrity, identity).
- **Unauthorised producers** are accepted under (c) and (a), refused under (b): T9-12.
- **Necessary?** **Yes:** (d2) requires an acceptance rule per kind (§10.3.6).
- **Kernel or application?** The rule is **W's**; the kernel's append step **enforces** it.
- **Disproved by:** an evidence entry the rule accepts that makes the class rule undefined. In the model none does: every record in D_W gets a class.

**T10-20 / SD1-01, where it touches this turn: the class rule g0.**
- At a cut s, for an item and a purpose:
  - an evidence entry is **live** if (under (c)) its producer is authorised, it is not annulled, and no withdrawal of it for that purpose is in effect;
  - **refuted** if some refutation is surely live;
  - else **U** if one may be;
  - else **supported** if some support is surely live;
  - else **U** if one may be;
  - else **not attested** (in the record at s).
- **The reasons list every evidence entry about the item with its state**, both polarities. So **a contradiction stays visible** (I-4; S0237's forbidden collapse "CONFLICTED → Forced TRUE/FALSE"), and the class does not hide the supporting entries.
- *Rivals, recorded:*
  - a fourth class "contested";
  - "one established assessment per claim" (§9.4 C2);
  - recency ("latest wins").
- **What this turn says about its class (SD1-01):** g0 is **W's rule**, and it is what makes the display honest. So it belongs with **row 2's core** (showing well-foundedness), not with the supporting assessment methods (row 4–13). *Int; the classification itself is left to T10-20's revision, as the Principal routed it.*
- **Disproved by:** a case where g0 shows "supported" while a live refutation is recorded (check 34 shows it does not), or where it shows a class without listing an entry it rests on.

## 9.18.6 Containment (the Lead DDD Architect's SD4-02, routed as input; B4)

**The question:** can a breach of W's acceptance rule be contained to W's entries, instead of taking the record out of D and refusing every standing answer? Within one kernel such an entry is refused at append (check 35); the case arises on **merged records or bypass**.

**Result (Ded, and model check 33):**
1. **Standing's values do not depend on W's entries.**
   - An evidence entry is read by Φ only for **its own** atom: its producer's authorization, and annulments targeting it.
   - No standing entry may take it as a basis (`in_D_S`), and no ending, revocation or adoption reads it.
   - So W's atoms are **sinks** in the dependency graph.
   - **Check 33:** on 600 random records with evidence, annulments of evidence, withdrawals and restorations, every standing value (every act, two items, t = 0–8) was equal with and without the W entries. The W entries took every value (T 320, F 1470, U 9), so the case is not trivial.
2. **A breach of a W clause is containable.** An evidence entry with an invalid polarity, on an entry that passes the generic clauses, leaves standing's part in **D_S**, and only **D_W** fails (600 of 600). Under §10.3.6 (d3) as stated (one D for every kind), the same record is refused whole (600 of 600).
3. **A breach of a generic clause is not containable.** If an entry fails its integrity check, its **kind** cannot be trusted, so the failure cannot be attributed to W. The same holds for a repeated identity, a missing attestation, or a citation of an entry not present. Standing is refused (600 of 600). *The book's own reason for refusing whole records (§9.13.2: "refusing is fail-closed, dropping is not") applies here with full force.*

**What follows (Int, for review):**
- (d3) can be restated as **two levels**:
  - **generic clauses**, which hold for the whole record and refuse every answer;
  - **per-area clauses**, each refusing only that area's answers.
- Standing's answers then need **D_S** only. W's answers need D_S's generic part and D_W, plus standing's answers for producer authority and corrections.
- **R6 of the map shrinks:** a breach of W's own rule no longer reaches standing; a generic breach still does.
- **The stated cost of decision (1):** a generic failure of **any** area's entry refuses **every** area's answers on that record. A separate record would contain it; this turn does not find that necessary (§9.18.8).
- **This changes no kernel law.** D for standing's kinds is unchanged; `in_D` in v21 is v20's. It proposes a restatement of **§10.3.6 (d3)**, which is Ch. 10, S0, for review.
- **Disproved by:** a standing value that changes when a W entry is added or removed. That would need a standing entry reading a W entry; check 33 and `in_D_S` exclude it.
- *Limit of the model:* designations and removals of evidence entries are **not modelled** in v21. A designation must cite every maximal entry of the old store, evidence entries included, so the designation rule's interaction with D_W is open (T9-13).

## 9.18.8 Is a separate evidence record necessary?

**No, on this turn's evidence.**
- Every premise and G8 are met inside the one record.
- Containment holds for W's own clauses.
- The **only** thing a separate record would add is containment of **generic** failures of W entries. No book law requires that.
- So the question does not go to the Principal.

**What would make it necessary:**
- a requirement that a damaged evidence entry must never stop standing's answers;
- or evidence too large or too frequent for one serialized record. That is operational and out of scope.
