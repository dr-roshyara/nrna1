## 9.15.1 The research question

**Given the decisions now in force, which properties does the kernel guarantee by its own steps and definitions, which must hold for its answers to be the answers for the record as it really is, and which must hold for those answers to match what happened? Where do "every recorded act occurred" (§9.8 H4) and "every act was recorded" (H5) sit, and what becomes of §9.9's result 5?**

The decisions in force:
- DB-1: A1 is a norm of recognized standing, and the empirical claim is a separate hypothesis;
- C1: records constitute authority within the kernel;
- D: citations of earlier entries only.

**What would disprove the answer:**
- a CE* answer that changes while the record, the inputs and every kernel assumption (K1–K7) are fixed;
- an internal check that establishes the occurrence of an act whose entry is not signed as its actor's;
- for completeness: §1.6's falsifier of the empirical claim, an outside decision that the organisation treats as binding although it has no entry. That falsifies A1-as-fact, not R5.

## 9.15.2 Definitions and premises

- **The criterion (turn 6, C2-01; reworded at turn 7, C1-11).** Two kinds of assumption, kept apart:
  - a **kernel assumption** is one whose failure makes the record or the inputs differ from the entries the kernel's own steps accepted, or makes the computation differ from its definition (a lost or altered entry, a wrong covers input, a faulty implementation or checker);
  - a **legitimacy assumption** relates the record so accepted to the world: its failure leaves CE*'s answer correct **by the norm** (recognized standing, DB-1), but makes recognized standing differ from what happened (A1-as-fact) or from what the rules required (de jure).
  - *Why reworded:* under the first wording, L4 (an annulment that occurred but was never transcribed) met the kernel clause too. *Dependence:* if the identity link (I11) were defined by key binding, L2 would move to the kernel side (§9.12 B3).
- **Labels (turn 7, C1-13).** K1–K7 here are kernel assumptions. §9.6's K1–K4 are research questions (K2 there is the identity mechanism, K4 uniqueness across applications). §9.16 writes "§9.6 K2" or "§9.15 K2" where both could be meant.
- **Recording modes, by what the record shows (turn 6, C1-01, C2-05, C2-06).** An entry is:
  - **signed-as-actor:** it verifies under a key that the record binds to the named actor;
  - **signed-as-other:** it verifies under a key bound to another party, who records on the actor's behalf. This is a declared transcription;
  - **unsigned:** it verifies under no key, and its authorship is a claim ("attribution is recorded as a claim, never as an attestation" [S0078 L218]).

  "Verifies" is a fact about the entry. Whether the named actor in fact wrote it is a further, world fact.
- **P-perf, a premise stated, not derived from C1.** An act is **performed by recording** if its performance is the actor's own signing of the entry, as with a signed adoption. C1 says only that the recorded act is the standing change. It does not say that an act is the writing of its entry. And §1.6 and §1.12-1 hold that an act exists apart from its record ("A report of an act is not the act"). So P-perf holds only for acts whose performance **is** the recording. Its status is Int: a choice of which acts the kernel treats as performatives.
- **Premises:** DB-1; C1 = constitute within the kernel (§9.13.2); D and Θ_f (§9.13); §9.8's H1′–H5 and §9.9's T2 as stated.

**R2. H4 has three parts; one is internal (Ded, under A1 recognized).** §9.8's H4 reads: "every entry reports an act that occurred and was performed by an actor entitled to it". It is a conjunction:
- **occurrence**, that the act happened;
- **attribution**, that the named actor performed it;
- **entitlement** of the named actor.

Entitlement is G2: internal, relative to the inputs. Occurrence and attribution are legitimacy assumptions (R3). *Under A1-as-fact,* the entitlement that matters is the actual performer's, and it depends on occurrence and attribution of the act and of its grants (turn 6, C1-13, C2-14).

**R3. No CE* answer depends on occurrence, attribution or completeness (Ded, from G1, under DB-1 and C1; definitional under DB-1, turn 7, C1-04).** *Scope:* values and marks. The marks read recorded fields that are **claims** about attribution (the status), not attribution itself; I15's recorder field is read by no clause.
- *Proof.* By G1, CE* is a function of the record and the inputs. Occurrence, attribution and completeness are relations between the record and events outside it; changing them while the record and inputs stay fixed cannot change CE*'s answer. Under DB-1 the norm is recognized standing, which is what CE* computes. ∎
- **What they are, then: legitimacy assumptions.** They decide whether recognized standing matches what happened (A1-as-fact) and whether it is de jure. This is the **recognition/legitimacy distinction**, adopted from the reviews (turn 6, C1-15). Version 1 read the kernel's answers as "needing" occurrence through R3; that was wrong (C1-02, C2-01).

## 9.15.4 The strongest counterexamples, and the limits

- **An entry removed out of band** (an annulment deleted outside the kernel's steps). The answers change; K1 fails; no per-entry check sees it. This is why K1 is a kernel assumption.
- **An honest key signing a false act time.** Legitimacy under A1-as-fact fails at L6; under A1 recognized the recorded time stands.
- **An assistant writing under the actor's key.** The entry is signed-as-actor, but was transcribed in fact: a failure of L3. §1.12-2's own example, "written by the deciding person and not by an assistant on their behalf", is this case or, if the assistant writes under their own name, a declared transcription.
- **A silent late transcription** at the next position, with no self-declaration. It is invisible, because version 1's "visible after the fact" rested on L1b.
- **A mistaken submission, an act under duress, an actor without capacity.** All are recognized under the norm. The remedy is an act, an annulment. They are failures of L7 and of de jure legitimacy. This is the price of C1, stated (§9.13 R2a).

## 9.15.5 Rival formulations

- **Recognition against legitimacy** (C1-15): **adopted**. It predicts R3, R5 and R8 (a) directly.
- **Recording modes by verification status** (C1-15): **adopted** in R4, because the record can decide them.
- **Recorder-as-actor** (C2-11): every entry is the recorder's own act ("Governance recorded that X said Y"). L1b then becomes the truth of the recorder's claim, and a misreport is the recorder's breach. It is compatible with R4, but not adopted: under DB-1 standing attaches to the named actor's act, not to the recording.
- **L1 and L4 as institutional duties** (C2-11): breaches are governance faults, visible from outside. This is compatible with R7; it is a framing of the legitimacy assumptions.
- **C1 = represent:** rejected by the Principal (§9.13.2). Under it every act is in effect transcribed, and completeness would be a kernel assumption.
- **A trusted computing base** (*Ext:* Lampson et al. 1992, EXT-057). K1–K7, and L1b's transcriber, form what must be trusted because it cannot be checked from within. This is a framing; including a human transcriber stretches the term.

## 9.15.11 What did not hold (version 1, reviewed at turn 6, commission C)

- **"Occurrence of a self-recorded act is its authenticity."** The proof assumed that the act is the writing of the entry (P-perf), which C1 does not give. The mode was also defined by the very fact in question (C1-01).
- **"The kernel's standing answers need occurrence only through R3."** They need it not at all (R3 now); occurrence is a legitimacy assumption (C1-02, C2-01).
- **"Occurrence is not a kernel primitive."** No test supports it (C1-02, C2-02).
- **W1–W8 as the world side.** The list was incomplete: an exclusive write path, a single view, the inputs, the shared record and the evaluator version were missing. It also did not separate kernel from legitimacy assumptions (C1-03, C2-04, C2-09).
- **G6 as internal.** Two of its items rested on the transcriber's declaration or on a second source, and "unauthorized act: F" is an authorization result (C1-04, C2-03).
- **The R5 "fail-closed" rule.** It changed no answer (C1-09, C2-08).
- **"Every human act in the estate is transcribed" from S0078.** The source concerns STARTs only (C1-09, C2-05).
