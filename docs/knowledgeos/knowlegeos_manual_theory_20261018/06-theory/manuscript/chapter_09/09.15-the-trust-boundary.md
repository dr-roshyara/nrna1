# 9.15 The trust boundary: what the record guarantees, and what it must assume

*Theory turn 6 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. ## 9.15.1 The research question

**Given the decisions now in force, which properties of the record does the kernel guarantee by its own steps and definitions, and which must it assume about the world? In particular, where do "every recorded act occurred" (§9.8 H4) and "every act was recorded" (H5) sit, and does §9.9's result 5, which left occurrence dependent on A1's reading, now resolve?**

The decisions in force:
- DB-1: A1 is a norm of recognized standing, and the empirical claim is a separate hypothesis;
- C1: records constitute authority within the kernel;
- D: citations of earlier entries only.

**What would disprove the answer:**
- an internal check that establishes the occurrence of a transcribed act;
- a guarantee listed as internal that fails while every listed world assumption holds;
- a standing change that, under A1 recognized and C1 = constitute, must be counted although it has no entry.

## 9.15.2 Definitions and premises

- **The internal side:** properties decided by the kernel's definitions over the record (CE*, §9.13; the conflict test, §9.14), or enforced by its own steps (the transition invariants of §9.11, given the implementation assumption that steps are serialised, §9.11.5).
- **The world side:** properties relating the record to events outside it. No check inside the record establishes them; some failures can be refuted from inside (§9.9 T2.3).
- **Actor and recorder.** An entry's **actor** is who performs the act it records; its **recorder** is who writes the entry. The entry is **self-recorded** if the recorder is the actor and the entry is authenticated as the actor's (signed under the actor's key), and **transcribed** otherwise: another party writes it on the actor's behalf, as §1.12-2's "an assistant on their behalf".
- **Premises:**
  - DB-1 (A1 is the norm of recognized standing; §1.6);
  - C1 = constitute within the kernel (§9.13.2): an authorized, recorded act **is** the standing change, not a report of one made elsewhere;
  - D and Θ_f (§9.13);
  - §9.8's H1′–H5 and §9.9's T2 as stated.

## 9.15.3 Results

**R1. What the kernel guarantees internally (Ded, each from its cited result).** `[CON-0193 · provisional]`

| | Guarantee | From |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | on a record in D, CE* answers exactly once; its T and F are forced; every U names its reason; outside D, Q1 refuses with the reason | §9.13 R1, R2, §9.13.3 |
| G2 | **entitlement**: whether a recorded act was performed by an actor entitled to it is decided by authorized(x), a function of the record | §9.13.3 (Φ) |
| G3 | the record's **form**: every change is an entry, removed only by a recorded act with a loss entry that keeps the consumed identities (H1′, P-loss); two orders kept apart (H2); pinned evaluation inputs (H3) | §9.8 H1′–H3; §9.11 (transition invariants, given serialisation) |
| G4 | no act is consumed twice, provided all act-consuming mechanisms share one record | §9.11 (law 24, K4) |
| G5 | conflict among rules: the set-wise report, reduced to pairs for an h ≤ 2 language | §9.14 R1 |
| G6 | **refutability**: some failures of the world side become visible inside the record (see the list below) | §9.9 T2.3; §9.13; S0095, S0103 |

Not one of these is a statement about the world.

**What G6 makes visible inside the record:**
- an integrity failure: refused (§9.13.2);
- a contradiction: U, with its reason;
- an unauthorized act: F;
- a transcription made after the fact: visible as such, because its sequence number is kept later than the work it authorizes. "The sequence number was deliberately not manipulated" [S0095 L221]; "the START is transcribed after the fact" [S0103 L143];
- work done before its recorded start (S0103 L77, in meaning).

**R2. H4 splits; one part is internal (Ded).** §9.8's H4 reads: "every entry reports an act that occurred and was performed by an actor entitled to it".
- **Entitlement** is G2: internal, decided by CE*.
- **Occurrence** is the world part.

So H4 as a whole is not a world assumption; only its occurrence part is.

**R3. Where occurrence sits depends on how the act was recorded (Ded under the premises; Int in applying them to the estate).** Under A1 recognized and C1 = constitute:

| Recording | What occurrence means | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| **self-recorded** (recorder = actor, authenticated) | the act **is** the writing of the entry by its actor (C1: the recorded act constitutes the standing change). So "the act occurred" is "this entry was written by its actor": its **authenticity** | the anchors of §9.9 T2.1: **key binding** (the key is the actor's) and **key custody** (no one else used it before revocation). Occurrence reduces to them |
| **transcribed** (recorder ≠ actor) | the entry reports an act the actor performed outside the record (an instruction, a confirmation), and the transcription is the constitutive recording of it | the transcriber's **fidelity**: the actor did perform it, and its content is as written. A **world assumption**, located at the transcriber, and not reducible inside the record |

- *Proof (self-recorded).* Under C1 the standing change is the recorded act. For a self-recorded entry the recorded act is the actor's writing of the entry. So the act occurred iff the entry is authentic. Authenticity reduces to key binding and custody (§9.9 T2.1). ∎
- *Proof (transcribed).* The act is the actor's, outside the record. Any evidence of it inside the record is a further entry, written by some recorder, so the regress of §9.9 T2.2 applies. ∎

**The estate's own discipline already holds the transcribed case** (Src):
- "A transcription records **a human act that was given**. No such act exists in this lane's record, and **Governance may not supply one.**" [S0095 L141];
- the transcription "declares itself a transcription" [S0095 L218];
- an affirmation by the actor is "the fact only the PO/ARB could provide" [S0095 L216];
- "the human act must *already exist* in the record before it is lawful to transcribe it; a prompt is not evidence that the PO/ARB acted." [S0336 L56];
- and the boundary exists "so that no machine process can manufacture the human act" [S0336 L73].

**R4. H5 is vacuous for standing changes; completeness moves outside the kernel (Ded under the premises).** H5: "every act that changes standing has an entry, or its absence is itself recorded" (§9.8).
- Under A1 recognized and C1 = constitute, a standing change **is** a recorded act, so an unrecorded act changes no standing: **H5 holds by definition for standing changes.**
- What looks like incompleteness is a decision taken outside the record and never transcribed. It is **not recognized**. That is the norm working, not an error of CE*.
- *The estate shows the cost:* a lane with a delivered report but no recorded START was "exactly ONE HUMAN ACT short" [S0095 L193] until the act was transcribed.

Completeness remains a **world assumption** in three places:
- **(a) A1-as-fact:** the hypothesis that recognized standing matches what happened (DB-1's separate claim);
- **(b) evidence and absences** (§9.10 O2, O3): grades and marked absences rest on what was recorded about the world;
- **(c) transcription:** that every outside decision meant to change standing was transcribed. This is an organisational duty, and its failure is visible only when someone outside raises it, as in S0095's X-2.

**R5. The record needs the recorder, apart from the actor (Ded; a consequence for D, returned to the Principal).** R3 decides the boundary per entry, by the recording mode. D's entries name an **actor** (§9.13.2), not a **recorder** or an authentication status. So R3 cannot be applied from the record as D stands.

In the estate the field that should carry this conflates the two. On a START, `recordedBy` names the act's **origin** (the human), while Governance performs the registration, so a reader asking "who registered this transition?" is answered wrongly "on **37 of 37** STARTs" [S0078 L258, in part]. "attribution is recorded as a claim, never as an attestation" [S0078 L218].

- *Fail-closed rule (the Professor's reading):* until an entry names its recorder and its authentication, treat it as **transcribed**. That is the stronger world assumption. The estate's own rule for unestablished lineage is the same in kind: "NOT ATTESTED — never PASS" (S0103 L35, in meaning).
- **Returned to the Principal:** whether D should require a recorder field and an authentication status (a new input, §9.12's list I1–I14 extended).

**R6. The world assumptions, located, and what each one carries (Ded for the dependency; Int for the estate).**

| | Assumption | What depends on it | Refutable inside? |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **transcription fidelity**: a transcribed act was performed by its actor, with the content written | occurrence of every transcribed act (R3); in the estate every human act is transcribed (S0078 L258) | partly: an anomaly in order (G6), a later denial by the actor (§9.12 B3: U) |
| W2 | **key binding**: a key belongs to the party it names | occurrence of self-recorded acts (R3); integrity and authenticity (§9.9 T2.1) | no |
| W3 | **key custody**: no other party used the key before its revocation | as W2; and timing, since signing times can be backdated after a compromise (§9.9 T2.1) | rarely (a revocation recorded later) |
| W4 | **durability**: the record is not lost or reset outside its own rules | H1′ (G3): the record's form guarantees nothing if the medium is lost. In the estate the authoritative workflow store is git-ignored, "preserved by nothing outside" the working tree (S0341 L22–23, in meaning) | no, after the loss |
| W5 | **transcription completeness**: every outside decision meant to change standing is transcribed | nothing in the kernel (R4); the organisation's effective decisions | only when raised from outside (S0095 X-2) |
| W6 | **root designation**: the root acts are made by whoever the organisation designates | every T of CE* (§9.13 R4) | no |
| W7 | **soundness of the integrity check** (implementation) | G1's refusal of failing entries | by testing the checker |
| W8 | **serialised steps** (implementation) | G3, G4 | by testing the implementation |

**Recorded act times** are not on the list under A1 recognized. The recognized time is the recorded one by definition (§9.13.7), except where a compromised key backdates (W3). Under A1-as-fact they would be a further assumption.

**R7. §9.9's result 5, resolved under the decisions in force (Ded under the premises).** Result 5 left occurrence dependent on A1's reading: under A1-as-form it passed the book's primitive test, but so did every assumption tying the record to the world (§9.9.7). With DB-1 and C1 = constitute:
- **(a)** relative to A1 recognized, the kernel's standing answers need occurrence only through **R3**:
  - for self-recorded acts it reduces to key binding and custody;
  - for transcribed acts it is W1, located at the transcriber;
- **(b)** completeness is not needed for standing (R4), only for A1-as-fact, evidence and transcription;
- **(c)** A1-as-fact, the separate hypothesis, needs occurrence and completeness for **every** act (W1, W5), and is refuted by an occurrence conflict (§9.12 B3);
- **(d)** occurrence is therefore **not a kernel primitive**. It is one of the world assumptions W1–W6, as §9.9.7's over-generation argument predicted, now located by recording mode.

## 9.15.4 The strongest counterexample, and the limits

- **A key used by an assistant** (a self-recorded entry written by someone holding the actor's key). R3's reduction makes occurrence rest on custody (W3), and custody failed, so the act did not occur although the entry is authentic as the actor's. *This is the limit of R3, not a counterexample:* R3 says occurrence **reduces to** binding and custody; a custody failure is a failure of an assumption, which the record cannot see (W3: "rarely" refutable). §1.12-2's own example, "written by the deciding person and not by an assistant on their behalf", is exactly this case. R3 places it: transcribed if the assistant writes under their own name, a custody failure if under the actor's key.
- **An act that needs more than recording** (a decision at a meeting, recorded later). Under C1 the standing change is the recording, so the meeting is evidence, and the recording is a transcription of the decision taken there. R3 calls it transcribed.
- **A self-recorded act the actor did not intend** (a mistaken submission). Under the norm it is recognized: recognition does not read intention. The remedy is an annulment by an act. This is the price of C1, stated (§9.13 R2a).

## 9.15.5 Rival formulations

- **Occurrence uniformly a world assumption** (§9.9 before this section). It is true but coarse: it misses that self-recorded acts reduce to the key anchors.
- **C1 = represent** (records as evidence of acts elsewhere). Under it no act is constitutive, every act is in effect transcribed, and H5 becomes a real assumption for standing. The Principal rejected this (§9.13.2).
- **A trusted computing base** in the security sense. The transcriber, the key store and the integrity checker together form what must be trusted because the system cannot check it (*Ext:* Lampson et al. 1992, EXT-057). W1–W4 and W7–W8 are its parts. This is a framing, not a different result.

## 9.15.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

This section **is** that separation: G1–G6 against W1–W8. One thing it adds is about its own scope: the separation holds only on records in D. The present estate's record is outside D (§9.13.2), so for it, G1 reads "Q1 refuses".

## 9.15.7 What follows for earlier sections (Ded)

- **§1.12-2 ("verifiable is not valid")** stands. R3 locates its residual assumption by recording mode. For a self-recorded act under C1 it narrows to the key anchors; for a transcribed act it is W1.
- **§9.8 H4** splits (R2). **§9.8 H5** is vacuous for standing changes under C1 and recognized standing (R4); M4 (an incomplete record, §9.8) then concerns A1-as-fact and evidence, not standing.
- **§9.9.7** (M-T2's over-generation) is confirmed: occurrence is one of W1–W6.
- **§9.13.7**'s world list is refined by R6. Its "the recorded acts really occurred" is W1 for transcribed acts, and W2–W3 for self-recorded ones.

- **The book's own:** the **application**:

## 9.15.9 What this does not decide

- Whether D requires a recorder field and an authentication status (returned to the Principal, R5).
- How durability is provided (W4: an architecture question; S0341 leaves it open).
- Who may transcribe for whom (a governance rule outside the kernel).

## 9.15.10 Open questions

- Should an entry carry recorder and authentication, and should Q1's reply name the recording mode its answer rests on?
- Can transcription fidelity be raised by design, for example by requiring the actor's own signature on an affirmation? Then R3 would move the act to the self-recorded case.
- Does completeness of evidence (R4 (b)) need its own marked-absence rule beyond O3?
