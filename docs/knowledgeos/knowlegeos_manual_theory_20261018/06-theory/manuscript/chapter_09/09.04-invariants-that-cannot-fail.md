# 9.4 Invariants that cannot fail, and what makes uniqueness hold

*Window C9 (2026-10-19). Sources: S0049 and S0050 (2026-08-16), in index order. **Status: reviewed in C9 (2026-10-19); settled in `review/C9-S0046-S0050/SETTLEMENT.md`.***

*Lineage:* S0049 is a review of a design, and S0050 is the design's repair by its author, on the same day and in the same work item. They are one line of support. S0049's independence is "DECLARED — asserted here, recorded in this artifact, not attestable" [S0049 L25–28].

*Existing homes (book search, step 3):* Ch. 1 §1.16.1 (one owner per invariant, one atomic change); §1.16.7 (a uniqueness needs a serialization point, or only detection is possible); §1.14.1 (current authority is derived, never stored); §1.6 and RC-37a (evidence alone cannot effect an act); §1.14.5 P-E; §1.14.3 (producer ≠ verifier); Ch. 9 §9.1. This section adds instances.

## 9.4.1 In plain language

A rule that says "the outcome is always defined" can be made true by a formula with a default. Then the rule protects nothing. S0049 found that the rule doing the real work in a design was a different one, "at most one current assessment per claim", and that this rule needs a boundary where writes are serialized.

## 9.4.1a Terms of the assurance model (Src)

*The terms below are used here, in Ch. 5 §5.6 and in Ch. 8 §8.6, as the sources use them. They are the sources' design vocabulary (KOS-ATTR-ARCH-001), not adopted kernel concepts.*
- **Governed Act:** an act recorded in the workflow (a transition or a grant) about which assurance is asserted.
- **Assurance Claim (claim):** an assertion about a Governed Act on one **dimension**, e.g. "lane X performed T-123", with its claimant [S0049 L68]. The precise rule is "one **current** claim per (Governed Act, dimension)" [S0049 L76].
- **Evidence:** material for or against a claim; a third party's contrary statement "enters as **Evidence** against the standing claim" [S0049 L74].
- **Assessment:** a judgement on a claim, giving an outcome; at most one is established per claim at a time (C2).
- **Outcome:** "current outcome(claim) := outcome of the currently-established Assessment ELSE dimension's weakest" [S0049 L45]. The outcome values form an order with a weakest value.
- **INV-ATTR-2:** in the form S0049 discusses, "at every read, a claim must yield a defined outcome, defaulting to its dimension's weakest" [S0049 L42]. In S0049 and later sources it is also cited for "separation is DECLARED, not attestable" (§8.6 C3).
- **INV-ATTR-3:** cited for "**the** assurance outcome", the singular outcome per claim [S0049 L54].
- **INV-ATTR-1:** cited in later sources for the rule that no gate reads actor identity (S0113 L44, in meaning).
- **Business Use Category C1–C5:** the classes a Governed Act is classified into, which the Assurance Gap derivation consumes [S0049 L132]. **C4** includes Independence claims, among them "Class-B review claims" [S0049 L106].
- **Declared:** the evidence grade of a claim asserted and recorded but not attestable, e.g. a reviewer's own separation [S0049 L25].

## 9.4.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. A read rule discharged by a total derivation cannot justify a boundary (Src + Ded).** `[CON-0114 · invalid as stated]` (valid core below, `[CON-0167 · provisional]`)
- **Src:** the derived outcome is "outcome of the currently-established Assessment ELSE dimension's weakest". "A derivation with an ELSE branch is **total by construction** — no read can ever observe an undefined outcome, transaction or no transaction." So "INV-ATTR-2 alone is sufficient" is "unsound" [S0049 L42–48].
- **Ded (the valid core):** a read rule that a total derivation discharges constrains neither the stored state nor concurrent writes, so it cannot justify a consistency boundary.
- **Two limits:**
  - the rule still has content in its **default**: "weakest", not any value. A total read defaulting to the strongest outcome would break it. So the rule is not met by every total function. It is the default that makes every unattested independence claim "Declared" [S0049 L25–28];
  - totality **presupposes a functional selector**. The derivation is total only if "the currently-established Assessment" names at most one [S0049 L50]. So totality depends on stored state, through C2.

**C2. Uniqueness, if the outcome is that of *the* established assessment (Src; an instance of Ch. 1 §1.16.1 and §1.16.7).**
- "The invariant doing the work is the uniqueness rule …: **at most one established assessment per claim at a time** — protected only by **serializing establishment per claim**, which is precisely an aggregate boundary's job." [S0049 L50]
- **Premise, stated:** the outcome is the outcome of **the** established assessment. A deterministic aggregate (for example, the weakest outcome among all established assessments) would be total and single-valued without uniqueness and without serialization. The design chose the first; the choice is not forced.
- **Given the premise,** the step to §1.16.7 is valid if "global" there means global over the write set the property ranges over (here, the establishments on one claim), and if no establishment path bypasses the aggregate (S0050 L120). §1.16.1 (one owner per invariant, one atomic change) is the closer home.
- **A derivation is not state:** "A derivation is not state and cannot be a member of a transactional boundary." [S0049 L56]. This is §1.14.1's "derived, never stored", in another domain.
- **The rule was presupposed, not stated:** the model speaks of "**the** current outcome" in the singular throughout [S0049 L54], and the repair records it by a human act: "I interpret the approved Assurance Model as requiring at most one established assessment per Assurance Claim at a time." [S0050 L37]

**C3. One current claim per (act, dimension); disputes are not competing claims (Src).** `[CON-0115 · provisional]`
- The rule is "one **current** claim per (Governed Act, dimension)"; superseded claims stand in history [S0049 L76].
- "**Status-disputes are Assessments.**" "**Content-disputes are Evidence + claim-supersession.**" [S0050 L86–88]
- Rejected: claims per (act, dimension, claimant), because it "yields multiple simultaneous current outcomes"; and putting the claimant on the assessment, because it "conflates who asserts with who judges" [S0050 L90].
- **Int:** the asserter and the judge are separate roles, a case of producer ≠ verifier (§1.14.3) inside one record.
- **No owner for the rule itself (review C9, finding 2):** the aggregate holds one claim and its assessments. Two claims for the same (act, dimension) sit in two aggregates, and nothing serializes claim assertion per (act, dimension). By §1.16.7 the rule is then only detectable, not prevented: stated, not held (CON-0102).

**C4. The input set of every gate is declared closed (Src).** `[CON-0116 · invalid as stated]` (valid as a declared constraint)
- "**the input set of every workflow/authority gate is closed: `{transitions, grants}`. Extending it requires an explicit ARB act.**" [S0050 L106]
- **What it does:** it declares that an assurance outcome cannot become a gate input without an act. That is the rule that evidence alone cannot effect an act (Ch. 1 §1.6, RC-37a), and P-E (§1.14.5), stated for one engine. Its home is there, not §1.17.4: §1.17.4 is about passing input values explicitly, for reproducibility; C4 restricts input kinds, for non-coupling.
- **What it does not do:** the role co-location channel is a governance practice, a refusal "implemented in role behavior, invisible to the dependency structure", held "**by policy only**" [S0049 L126]. No rule about an input set can close a practice. Both channels are declared closed; neither is enforced (CON-0102).

**C5. Who establishes, and who classifies (Src).**
- The establishment of an assessment, "the single most consequential write in the domain", had no named owner [S0049 L112]. The repair names the assessor, and says establishment "confers no authority, constitutes no approval, and changes no permission" [S0050 L120].
- Who classifies an act into a use category is given to governance, and "Explicitly not owned by … the assessed party (self-classification would rig the Gap derivation)" [S0050 L130–132].
- **Int:** both are invariant-owner answers. The second is producer ≠ verifier applied to the input of a gap computation (Ch. 5 §5.4).
- **A tension (review C9):** the assessor that performed an assessment also establishes it, with no other act. That looks like self-acceptance (§1.14.3). It is admissible only on the premise that establishment changes evidence, not standing, which S0050 states [S0050 L120]. Whether an act that changes evidence only may be self-performed is open.

**C6. The regress of the assessor's standing ends in disclosure or a human act (Src).**
- "this assessor has Class-B standing" is itself a claim, assessed like any other, defaulting to Declared; "The regress terminates … at a **disclosed Declared** or at a **human acceptance**." [S0050 L98]
- **Limit:** termination is asserted, not shown; it follows only because records are finite. Since establishment rests on standing that is itself Declared by default, an established assessment is no stronger than that default unless the regress ends in a human act.

**C7. The assurance model's trivial case (Ded).**
- The model's default state, where nothing is ever established and every outcome is the weakest (Declared), satisfies the read derivation, INV-ATTR-1 and the closed gate inputs. R-6 names who establishes, but nothing obliges establishment to happen [S0051 L64].
- So the model, like §9.1's scaffold, has must-never rules and no must-do. Which must-do it needs is open.

## 9.4.3 Open questions

- Which invariants in this book are, like C1, satisfiable by a total function, and so need a uniqueness or a must-do companion?
- Does the closed input set (C4) belong to the kernel?

## 9.4.4 What does not hold

- **CON-0114, as first stated:** "a read invariant met by a total derivation is vacuous". INVALID: the default value ("weakest") has content, and totality presupposes a functional selector (review C9, findings 1 and 3).
- **CON-0116, as first stated:** "the closed input set closes two latent channels". INVALID: the role channel is a practice that no input-set rule can close; both channels are declared closed, not enforced (review C9, finding 7).
