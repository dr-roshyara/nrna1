# 9.1 The kernel scaffold, tested

*Deliverable 5 (RESEARCH-PURPOSE). Sources: the Principal's direction EXT-008, EXT-010, EXT-011 (external, not corpus); the extraction review, finding 1 (`review/EXT-DIRECTION-20261019/REVIEW-REPORT.md`). Written 2026-10-19. **Status: PROVISIONAL, not yet reviewed.***

## 9.1.1 What the direction proposes (Ext)

- The kernel is a tuple (𝒮, M, I, A): a state space, mechanisms, invariants and an interface (EXT-010 L19–52).
- Mechanisms are partial functions m : 𝒮 × X → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}, where ⊥ means the transition is rejected (EXT-011 L224–249).
- **Preservation (A1):** every mechanism keeps a valid state valid, m(s, x) ∈ 𝒮_I for s ∈ 𝒮_I (EXT-010 L61–75; EXT-011 L267–275).
- **Minimality (A2):** no proper subset of the mechanisms satisfies the invariants (EXT-010 L81–89).
- **Existence:** whether such a kernel exists is a research question, and "no" is a permitted answer (EXT-010 L220–224).

## 9.1.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. As written, the scaffold is degenerate (Ded).** `[CON-0099 · provisional]`
- *Scope:* A1 and A2 as stated in 9.1.1, with "M' satisfies I" read as "M' preserves I".
- Preservation is a safety property: it says what may not happen. Every subset of a preserving set also preserves, and ∅ preserves vacuously.
- So no non-empty M satisfies A2, and ∅ satisfies it: the "smallest M" of EXT-012 is ∅, and the existence question is trivially "yes".
- **A1 is also inconsistent with ⊥ as written:** ⊥ ∉ 𝒮_I, so every refusal would break A1 (EXT-011 L241 vs L269).

**C2. The repair: required capabilities, and preservation with refusal (Ded; a proposal of this book).** `[CON-0100 · provisional]`
- **Preservation with refusal (P):** for every m ∈ M, s ∈ 𝒮_I and input x, m(s, x) ∈ 𝒮_I ∪ {⊥}.
- **Required capabilities (Req):** a set R of things the kernel must be able to do, each a state that must be reachable from the initial state using only M. This is a liveness property; the direction already points to it (I-Use, EXT-008 L129; ablation measured on unmet requirements, EXT-014 L229–239).
- **Kernel(M) = P ∧ Req.** **Minimal(M) = Kernel(M) and no proper subset of M is a kernel.**
- Under this definition ∅ is not a kernel whenever R is not already met in the initial state: with no mechanism, nothing is reachable except the initial state (Ded, general).

**C3. A finite test (computation; scope: one toy model).**
- **What was tested:**
  - states (claims, acts, standing), each a subset of {a, b}: 64 states;
  - invariants I1 standing ⊆ acts (no standing without an act, the shape of Ch. 1 A1) and I2 acts ⊆ claims, leaving 16 valid states;
  - eight mechanisms: record, grant, a duplicate grant2, revoke, bad (gives standing without an act), refuse (always ⊥), and noop (returns the state unchanged);
  - two required capabilities: R1, a recorded claim is reachable; R2, a claim with standing is reachable.
- **Result** (every subset of the eight mechanisms checked; the three trivial cases of Rulebook §4 are ∅, refuse and noop):

| M | P | Req | Kernel | Minimal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | yes | **no** | no | no (as written: **yes**, the only minimal set) |
| {record} | yes | no | no | no |
| {record, grant} | yes | yes | yes | **yes** |
| {record, grant2} | yes | yes | yes | **yes** |
| {record, grant, revoke} | yes | yes | yes | no |
| {record, grant, grant2} | yes | yes | yes | no |
| {record, bad} | **no** | yes | no | no |
| {refuse} | yes | **no** | no | no |
| {noop} | yes | **no** | no | no |

- **What the result shows, in this model:**
  - ∅ fails;
  - a kernel exists, and the minimal kernels are {record, grant} and {record, grant2}, so a minimal kernel is **not unique**;
  - a mechanism that breaks an invariant (bad), one that refuses everything (refuse), and one that changes nothing (noop) are in no minimal kernel;
  - all three trivial cases preserve the invariants and fail the required capabilities. That is the point of C2: preservation alone cannot tell a kernel from a system that does nothing.
- **What it does not show:**
  - One toy model tests the **form** of the scaffold, not the **content** of KnowledgeOS. The states, invariants and requirements were invented for the test.
  - It does not show that a KnowledgeOS kernel exists, or what it contains. **Deliverable 5 stays open** (T9-01, T9-02).
  - A passing test is not a proof of a general claim. The general statements in C1 and C2 rest on their deductions; the model only illustrates them.

**C4. Two notions of minimality (Int; an open question).**
- **Mechanism minimality** (C2): no proper subset of M is a kernel.
- **Primitive minimality** (RESEARCH-PURPOSE): a primitive is not derivable from other primitives.
- They differ. In C3, grant and grant2 are interchangeable: each is minimal in the mechanism sense, and each is derivable from the other. The relation between the two notions is open (T9-03).
- **Non-uniqueness has a consequence:** if minimal kernels can differ, something must choose between them, a further requirement or a recorded act (T9-04; compare Ch. 1 A1).
