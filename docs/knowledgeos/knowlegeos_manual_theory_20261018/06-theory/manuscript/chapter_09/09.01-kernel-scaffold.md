# 9.1 The kernel scaffold, tested

*Deliverable 5 (RESEARCH-PURPOSE). Sources: the Principal's direction EXT-008, EXT-010, EXT-011 (external, not corpus); the extraction review, finding 1 (`review/EXT-DIRECTION-20261019/REVIEW-REPORT.md`). Written 2026-10-19. **Status: reviewed in C7 (2026-10-19); settled in `review/C7-S0036-S0040/SETTLEMENT.md`.***

## 9.1.1 What the direction proposes (Ext)

- The kernel is a tuple (𝒮, M, I, A): a state space, mechanisms, invariants and an interface (EXT-010 L19–52).
- Mechanisms are partial functions m : 𝒮 × X → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}, where ⊥ means the transition is rejected (EXT-011 L224–249).
- **Preservation (Pres):** every mechanism keeps a valid state valid, m(s, x) ∈ 𝒮_I for s ∈ 𝒮_I (EXT-010 L61–75; EXT-011 L267–275).
- **Minimality (Min):** no proper subset of the mechanisms satisfies the invariants (EXT-010 L81–89).
- *Notation:* Pres and Min are the direction's two axioms (EXT-010 calls them A1 and A2). In this section, "A1" always means the act axiom of Ch. 1 §1.6.
- **Existence:** whether such a kernel exists is a research question, and "no" is a permitted answer (EXT-010 L220–224).

## 9.1.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. Under the closed reading, the scaffold is degenerate (Ded).** `[CON-0099 · validated]` (scope: the closed reading, and the Pres ∧ Min fragment only)
- *Two readings of "mechanism".* EXT-011 defines mechanisms as "operations that affect or constrain state transitions" [EXT-011 L230], with a Constraint type beside Transition (L253–259).
  - **Closed reading:** mechanisms are the only transitions. Then removing one can only remove transitions.
  - **Guard reading:** some mechanisms constrain transitions made by others. Then removing a guard can break the invariants.
- **Under the closed reading:** preservation is a safety property. Every subset of a preserving set also preserves, and ∅ preserves vacuously. So no non-empty M satisfies Min, and ∅ does. The "smallest M" of EXT-012 is ∅, and the existence question **for Pres ∧ Min** is trivially "yes". EXT-010 asks about all its requirements (L220), so this does not settle existence as the direction poses it.
- **Under the guard reading:** Min is not vacuous, but a different trivial case satisfies P and Min: a single guard that refuses every transition.
- **Pres and ⊥:** Pres as written, m(s, x) ∈ 𝒮_I, does not mention ⊥, while mechanisms are partial and may return ⊥ (EXT-011 L241). So Pres is **under-specified** on refusal, not inconsistent: read with *m is partial*, it holds wherever m is defined.
- **What survives both readings:** conditions on what must never happen (P, Min) are satisfied by a system that does nothing. A must-do requirement is needed beside them.

**C2. The repair: required capabilities, and preservation with refusal (Ded; a proposal of this book).** `[CON-0100 · invalid as the kernel type]` (its results for state invariants stand; the kernel type is C6, CON-0183)
- *Scope:* C2's invariants are **state** invariants only, a subset 𝒮_I of states. The laws the book tests in §9.6–§9.9 are about steps and runs, which 𝒮_I cannot express (C6).
- **Preservation with refusal (P):** for every m ∈ M, s ∈ 𝒮_I and input x, m(s, x) ∈ 𝒮_I ∪ {⊥}.
- **Required capabilities (Req):** a set R of things the kernel must be able to do, each a state that must be **reachable** from the initial state using only M. (A reachability property, *some path reaches it*, not a liveness property, *it eventually happens on every run*.) The direction points to it: I-Use (EXT-008 L129), and ablation measured on unmet requirements (EXT-014 L229–239).
- *"Required" here*: a required **capability**, a reachable state. Ch. 5 §5.4's "requirement" is an epistemic requirement of a contract. The relation of the two is open (T9-01).
- **Kernel(M) = P ∧ Req.** **Minimal(M) = Kernel(M) and no proper subset of M is a kernel.**
- **∅ is not a kernel** whenever some requirement in R is not met in the initial state, since with no mechanism only the initial state is reachable (Ded, general). With R = ∅, ∅ is the kernel again (checked in the model): R must be non-trivial.

**C3. Existence is decided by one check (Ded, with proof).**
- **Claim:** given a pool of candidate mechanisms, a kernel exists iff the set U of all pool mechanisms that individually preserve the invariants satisfies Req.
- **Proof.**
  1. P is a condition on each mechanism separately, so a set satisfies P iff each member does. Hence a set satisfies P iff it is a subset of U.
  2. Req is monotone: adding mechanisms only adds transitions, so every state reachable before stays reachable.
  3. (⇐) If U satisfies Req, U is a kernel.
  4. (⇒) If some M is a kernel, then M ⊆ U by step 1, and U satisfies Req by step 2.
- *Scope:* the closed reading (C1), where mechanisms only add transitions. Under the guard reading, step 2 fails, because a guard removes transitions. For transition and trace invariants, see C6 results 3–5: C3 holds for transition invariants and fails for trace invariants unless they are carried by a history variable that is empty at s₀, never shrinks, and is read and written by every mechanism that consumes acts.

**C4. A finite test (computation; scope: one toy model).**
- **What was tested:**
  - states (claims, acts, standing), each a subset of {a, b}: 64 states;
  - invariants I1 standing ⊆ acts (no standing without an act, the shape of Ch. 1 A1) and I2 acts ⊆ claims, leaving 16 valid states;
  - seven functionally distinct mechanisms (the script asserts it):
    - record;
    - grant;
    - recgrant (record and grant in one step);
    - revoke;
    - bad (gives standing without an act);
    - refuse (always ⊥);
    - noop (returns the state unchanged);
  - two required capabilities: R1, a recorded claim is reachable; R2, a claim with standing is reachable.
- **Result** (all 128 subsets checked; the three trivial cases of Rulebook §4 are ∅, refuse and noop):

| M | P | Req | Kernel | Minimal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | yes | **no** | no | no (as written: **yes**, the only minimal set) |
| {record} | yes | no | no | no |
| {record, grant} | yes | yes | yes | **yes** |
| {recgrant} | yes | yes | yes | **yes** |
| {record, grant, revoke} | yes | yes | yes | no |
| {record, bad} | **no** | yes | no | no |
| {refuse} | yes | **no** | no | no |
| {noop} | yes | **no** | no | no |

- **What the result shows, in this model:**
  - ∅, refuse and noop all preserve the invariants and fail Req. Preservation alone cannot tell a kernel from a system that does nothing;
  - a kernel exists, in agreement with C3: the union of the preserving mechanisms satisfies Req;
  - a mechanism that breaks an invariant (bad) is in no kernel;
  - **which sets are minimal depends on the pool:**
    - without recgrant, the only minimal kernel is {record, grant};
    - with it, {recgrant} is a second minimal kernel, of a different size.

    So minimality is relative to the pool and to how finely its mechanisms are cut. With an unrestricted pool, a single preserving mechanism that reaches every required state is a minimal kernel whenever R is not met initially (its only proper subset is ∅, which fails).
- **What it does not show:**
  - One toy model tests the **form** of the scaffold, not the **content** of KnowledgeOS. The states, invariants and requirements were invented for the test.
  - It does not show that a KnowledgeOS kernel exists, or what it contains. **Deliverable 5 stays open** (T9-01, T9-02).
  - A passing test is not a proof of a general claim. The general statements are those of C1–C3; the model only illustrates them.
- *Correction recorded:* an earlier version of the model had a "second" grant that was the same function under another name, and reported two minimal kernels from it. The C7 review found this (review C7, finding 1).

**C5. "Minimal kernel" needs a fixed interface (Int; an open question).**
- **Mechanism minimality** (C2) is relative to the pool (C4). In the direction, the pool is what the interface A offers (EXT-010 L19–52). So *the minimal kernel* is well posed only once A, and with it the granularity of mechanisms, is fixed (T9-03).
- **Primitive minimality** (RESEARCH-PURPOSE): a primitive is not derivable from other primitives. In C4, recgrant is derivable from record followed by grant, yet {recgrant} is mechanism-minimal. **The two notions differ** (T9-03).
- **Whether minimal kernels are unique** is open. In C4 the minimal kernel is unique for the fine-grained pool and not unique once recgrant is added (T9-04).

**C6. The kernel type, with transition and trace invariants (Ded and computation; a proposal of this book).** `[CON-0183 · provisional]` (restates CON-0100 as the kernel type)
- **Why C2 is not enough.** C2 checks states. The laws the book tests are about steps or runs: append-only (H1′, §9.8), "knowledge never silently disappears" (I-1(GKC), §0.5), the outside origin of an act (Ch. 1 A1; ARCH-INV-1), and uniqueness (U, §1.16.7). A set of states cannot express them. Two models show it. In each, P and Req hold, and a law the book tests fails:
  - **the deleting record:** a record mechanism that also deletes the claims that carry no act. It breaks append-only;
  - **the forge:** a mechanism that records an act itself and grants standing on it. I1 (standing ⊆ acts) holds; the act's outside origin fails.
- **The type.** A kernel candidate is (𝒮, X, M, G, I_S, I_T, I_R, Req):
  - **inputs** X. Each input carries an act performed outside the kernel, or none: act(x) ∈ Acts ∪ {none}. This is the **input-origin condition**: the kernel receives acts; it does not make them;
  - **mechanisms** m : 𝒮 × X → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}, as in C2, and **guards** g : 𝒮 × X × 𝒮 → {pass, ⊥}, which refuse the steps of other mechanisms (C1's guard reading). A **step** of (M, G) is a triple (s, x, m(s, x)) with m(s, x) ≠ ⊥ that every guard in G passes;
  - **state invariants** I_S ⊆ 𝒮 (C2's 𝒮_I);
  - **transition invariants** I_T ⊆ 𝒮 × X × 𝒮. Examples: append-only (the record at s is contained in the record at s′); origin (acts and standing change only on an input that carries an act, and only for what that act names);
  - **trace invariants** I_R, over the runs from s₀. Example: uniqueness (no act is consumed twice in a run). I_R is not restricted to safety properties; C3 needs the restriction (results 4–5, and §9.11);
  - **replies:** for every input, a reply to the caller: the result of the step, or the refusal with its reason (result 8);
  - *premise of the type:* I_S, I_T, I_R and the observers' reference definitions are given independently of (M, G); they are the specification the mechanisms are tested against (C3 needs this; §9.11);
  - **Req** = (Q, O) (§9.10): the inputs to accept as reachability over the steps of (M, G), as in C2; the questions as observers (result 7).
- **Applications.** A system is a kernel's (M, G) together with application mechanisms M_A. The kernel's invariants are required of the system's steps and runs, so an application mechanism is bound by I_T and I_R as a kernel mechanism is. The relocation test (§9.6.2) asks whether the invariants survive when a capability moves into applications. For a trace invariant this cannot be checked one application at a time (result 4); by result 5, every application that consumes acts must share one append-only history variable, which is a shared serialization point (§9.6.4 step 3, K4).
- **Kernel′(M, G) = P_S ∧ P_T ∧ P_R ∧ Req:**
  - P_S: every step from a valid state ends in a valid state (C2's P; refusal is not a step);
  - P_T: every step from a valid state is in I_T;
  - P_R: every run from s₀ is in I_R.

  Minimality is as in C2, over M ∪ G.
- **Results.** The computation is one toy model (`tmp/tools/kernel_type_model.py`, version 2: items {a, b}, two act identities per item, 1,024 states, 10 mechanisms; all assertions passed). Results marked "general" are deductions; the model only illustrates them.
  1. **Both counter-models are excluded, relative to faithful input labels.** {record_del, grant} and {record, forge} are kernels under C2 and not under C6 (P_T fails). {record, grant} is a kernel under both, and so is {record, grant, revoke}, in which a granted-then-revoked state is reachable. The origin condition is checked against the act an input **carries as a label**. A forge fed a falsely labelled input passes. So the exclusion holds relative to faithful labels: A1-as-form, with the residual assumption of §1.12-2 and §9.9 T-b.
  2. **The trivial cases still fail Req:** ∅, refuse and noop (Rulebook §4).
  3. **C3 holds for state and transition invariants, closed reading (general).** P_S and P_T are conditions on single steps, so a set satisfies them iff each member does: C3's step 1 holds. Req is monotone under the closed reading: step 2 holds. *Ext:* this is the standard invariance argument for safety properties of transition systems (Lamport 1977; Alpern and Schneider 1985), applied to sets of mechanisms; **established**, not new.
  4. **C3 fails for trace invariants (a counterexample).** grant_nh and revoke_nh are grant and revoke without a record of consumed acts. Each satisfies uniqueness alone. Together, one run grants on act (a,1), revokes on act (a,2), and grants again on (a,1), consuming (a,1) twice. So P_R is not a condition on each mechanism. The union of the individually preserving mechanisms fails P_R, although kernels exist ({record, grant}). Existence by one check does not hold for trace invariants.
  5. **What restores it (general, with three conditions; a reduction to result 3).** A trace invariant that is a safety property can be stated as a transition invariant over a state that carries the needed history (a **history variable** H; here, the set of consumed acts). Uniqueness then becomes the transition rule T3: a step that consumes an act needs it outside H and adds it to H. C3 holds again for the class of mechanisms that satisfy T3, provided (i) **H is empty at s₀**, (ii) **H never shrinks**: no step of any mechanism removes from it, and (iii) every mechanism that consumes acts reads and writes H. *Proof:* once consumed, an act stays in H by (ii), and a consuming step needs it outside H, so no run consumes it twice; with (i) this holds from the first step. Then P_R follows from P_T, and result 3 applies. **Condition (ii) is necessary:** clear_used, which empties H and consumes nothing, satisfies (iii) and uniqueness alone, yet with record, grant and revoke it lets a run consume one act twice (checked). In the model, the class satisfying T3 is derived (record, grant, revoke, refuse, noop), every union in it keeps uniqueness, and existence in it is decided by the one check. *Ext:* history variables are the established device of Abadi and Lamport (1991) for refinement mappings; the reduction of a safety trace property to an invariant over an augmented state is **established**. What is the book's own is only the application to sets of mechanisms and the three conditions as a premise of C3. Uniqueness, append-only and origin are safety properties. I-1(GKC) is one if "silently" means "without a recorded act". Whether every trace invariant the kernel needs is a safety property is open.
  6. **Guards.** A guard g_origin that refuses the steps breaking origin makes {record, forge} with g_origin a kernel. Adding a guard only removes steps (checked), so Req is not monotone in G, and C3 does not extend to guards (C3's scope). With guards, existence needs a search over (M, G). For a finite pool and a finite state space, that search is decidable by enumeration.
  7. **Questions as observers (Ded, general; from §9.10).** Req is (Q, O) (§9.10.2): inputs to accept, which are reachability as in C2, and questions, which constrain every answer. Add to the type a set of **observers** obs_q : 𝒮 × In_q → Ans_q, one per question, and a reference definition ref_q of the same type. Two conditions are possible:
     - (a) obs_q = ref_q on **every valid state** (a state invariant on the observer). It does not mention M or G at all, so it holds or fails independently of the mechanism set; C3 then applies unchanged to the inputs to accept, and existence is decided by the one check for them.
     - (b) obs_q = ref_q on the **reachable** states only (an AG condition). Adding mechanisms can only enlarge the reachable set, so (b) is preserved under removing mechanisms and can be broken by adding them: it is **antitone** in M, the reverse of Req's monotonicity, and C3's step 2 fails for it.

     So the book takes (a): a question is met by an observer correct on all valid states. It is the stronger condition, and the one that keeps C3. *Ext:* observers in the sense of algebraic specification (Guttag and Horning 1978) and AG conditions (Clarke and Emerson 1981); **established**; the book's own is only the choice of (a) to keep C3.
  8. **Replies (Ded; added at theory turn 2).** Refusal is not a step, so no condition on states, steps or runs can require a refusal's reason, or forbid a guard to refuse on its own judgement. A reply map out : 𝒮 × X → Reply (a Mealy-style output: the step's result, or ⊥ with a reason) makes both expressible as conditions on replies, of the same kind as observer correctness (result 7 (a)): for example, "every refusal names the absent permitting record" (ARCH-INV-1's guard half, §1.14.2) and "a refused act is reported to the caller" (§9.10 O1; INV-A3's REPORT, if adopted). Such conditions are checked on each step or refusal, so for mechanisms C3's step 1 holds for them as for P_T; conditions on guards' refusals fall under the guard reading, where C3 already does not apply (result 6).
- **What it does not show:**
  - the type is a form. Its content stays open: 𝒮, the acts, and Req for KnowledgeOS (T9-01, T9-02, F13);
  - the origin condition is a trusted label on the input. The type does not express that the act occurred (§9.9 T-b); it expresses only that the kernel does not make acts itself;
  - for each of append-only, origin and uniqueness: is it a kernel invariant or an application invariant? That is **F36**, decided per law by the three boundary questions (RESEARCH-PURPOSE). That the type must be able to express them, if the kernel carries them, follows from "Why C2 is not enough" and is not open;
  - §9.6–§9.9 test transition and trace laws (A1's origin, H1′, U, RL). They use this type in prose, and their results are **not derived from the model**: they are informal with respect to C6 (each section says so).
