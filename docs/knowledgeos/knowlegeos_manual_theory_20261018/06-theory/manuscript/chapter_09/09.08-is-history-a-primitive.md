## 9.8.1 In plain language

"History" is on the list of the kernel's candidate primitives. This turn asks whether it is one. It defines history as the ability to answer "what standing did item c have at time t, as the record stood at time s?". It then shows that this answer can be computed, if the record keeps three properties: nothing is removed from it, it carries two orders (when an act happened, when it was recorded), and the rules used to evaluate it are versioned. Each of the three can fail while all the others hold, so none follows from the others. So "history" is not one primitive. It is three independent properties of the record, plus the evaluator. And the record itself is not on the list of primitives.

## 9.8.2 Definitions

- **The record R:** a sequence of entries (e₁, e₂, …), each with an identity, a type and a payload. The book's acts, grants and transitions are entries (§1.13, §6.5).
- **Position:** the index of an entry in R. **Recording time** τ(i): when entry i was written. **Act time** t_x: when the act an entry reports happened (§1.14.1; §1.16.2).
- **Cut:** R≤s, the entries recorded at or before time s (TT2, §9.7 Analysis 3).
- **The evaluator** CE(c, t; θ, R≤s): standing of c at t under parameters θ, against the cut R≤s (§9.7). It is not yet a total function (§9.7.7 result 2); every result below that uses it is **conditional on its repair**.
- **History (the capability under test):** Hist(c, x, t, s) = the standing of c in scope x at time t, as the record stood at s. This is S2775's pair of axes, valid time and knowledge time, and S2782's two questions, what was true for the domain at a time [S2782 L775] and what KnowledgeOS believed or knew at that time [S2782 L779] (in meaning).
- **Primitive (Rulebook §12b (a)):** a concept with a precise definition and an **independence model**, a model in which it alone fails while the other primitives hold. If no such model exists, it is derivable.

## 9.8.3 Three properties of the record

- **H1, prefix preservation:** for every s < s′, R≤s is recoverable from R≤s′. No entry is removed or changed. (Append-only: S2782 L868, in meaning: H_{t+1} = H_t ∪ {e_{t+1}}.)
- **H2, two orders:** each entry carries its recording time τ(i), and each act entry its act time t_x, and the two are kept apart.
- **H3, versioned evaluation inputs:** for every s, the θ and the definition of CE in force at s are recoverable (S2782 L927–958, in meaning: replay under rule version 4 of an event processed under version 3 "may produce K′_t ≠ K_t"; §1.16.6, pinning).

## 9.8.4 Derivation: history from H1–H3 (Ded, conditional)

**Claim D.** If H1, H2 and H3 hold and CE is a total function, then Hist is a function of the record:

  Hist(c, x, t, s) = CE(c, t; θ(s), R≤s),

where θ(s) is the parameter set in force at s.

**Proof.**
1. By H1, R≤s exists as a part of R for every s.
2. By H2, the entries of R≤s can be selected by recording time, and the acts in it can be ordered by act time, with ties broken as F26 decides (§9.7 step 2: a global position, a partial order, or a tie rule). Without H2 the cut cannot be taken at a time s, only at a position, and acts can be ordered only by position.
3. By H3, θ(s) and the definition in force at s are available.
4. CE is a function (premise), so its value on these arguments is determined. ∎

**What D shows:** under its premises, history needs no stored field and no separate mechanism. It is the evaluator applied to past cuts. **What D does not show:** that CE is total. That is TT2's open repair. F26 (the order of acts) enters through step 2; H2 is what makes its option (c) available.

## 9.8.5 Three independence models

The other candidate primitives are taken as in BOOK-OUTLINE: identity (I-0), lifecycle, authority (A1), evidence, relationships, policy. Each model below keeps them, and breaks one of H1–H3 only.

**M1: an overwriting record (H1 fails; H2, H3 hold).**
- Each change of standing is made by an act, so A1 holds at every step. Items keep their identities (I-0). Superseded claims are kept as objects with a link (I-7). Rules are versioned. But each new entry **replaces** the previous entry for the same item, with its recording and act times.
- *Check:* identity, lifecycle, A1, evidence, relationships and policy are all stated over the current state and the current act, so they hold. Hist(c, x, t, s) for s before the last change of c has no input: the earlier cut no longer exists. **So H1 does not follow from the others.**
- *The estate's own case:* commit `9c93e2f0` removed "**73 lines**" from an append-only session log, and the content "is recoverable at `e8b3842b`" [S0095 L38]. H1 failed at the level of the file and held at the level of git. *Int:* H1 is a property of a named record, and which record is meant must be stated (§6.7 C7, a claim declares its layer).

**M2: a record with positions but no times (H2 fails; H1, H3 hold).**
- An append-only record with dense positions and no recording or act times. This is the estate's own record as measured: "Only 2 of 210 transitions carry any `date` field" (§9.7, input table, S0128).
- *Check:* the other primitives hold. Hist can be answered **by position** but not **by time**: "what did we know on 1 June?" has no answer.
- **And one order cannot stand in for the other.** In S0095, a START was transcribed into the record at "seq 16", a position "later than the artifact it authorizes" [S0095 L218]; "A transcription that appeared at seq 15 would read as though the `START` had preceded the report. **It did not, and the record now says so.**" [S0095 L221]. So recording order and act order differ in the estate, and the record keeps both only because the transition says so in prose. **So H2 does not follow from H1.**

**M3: an unversioned evaluator (H3 fails; H1, H2 hold).**
- An append-only record with both times, evaluated under whatever θ and definition are current.
- *Check:* the other primitives hold. Hist(c, x, t, s) is then computed with θ(now), not θ(s): it answers "what would we decide now about then", not "what did we decide then". S2782: a different result of replaying under a later rule "does not necessarily mean the historical state was wrong." [S2782 L947]; it may mean that replay under version 4 differs from replay under version 3 [S2782 L952, in meaning]. **So H3 does not follow from H1 and H2.**

**Result (Ded, from M1–M3):** H1, H2 and H3 are mutually independent, and independent of the six other candidates. Each has a model in which it alone fails.

## 9.8.6 What follows for the list of primitives

1. **"History" is not one primitive.** `[CON-0148 · provisional]` Under D, it is derived from H1–H3 and the evaluator. Its content is three independent properties, which the single word overloads (the model-integrity check: an overloaded dimension, not a new one).
2. **The record is missing from the list.** `[CON-0149 · provisional]` H1–H3 are properties of the record, and no candidate primitive is "the record". *Hyp:* the list should carry **the record** (entries with identity, two orders, prefix preservation) in place of history, and versioned policy under "policy".
3. **§1.2 presupposes history.** §1.2 defines a checkable authority over a claim's history: "a claim's history is valid iff it starts in a non-authoritative state and every change is labelled by a recorded act" (§1.2). So in the book's own chain, H1 is prior to authority's checkability, not derived from it. M1 shows what is lost without it: A1 still holds as a fact about each change, but it can no longer be checked afterwards, which is §1.12's distinction of valid against verifiable.
4. **A history that is reconstructed is not history.** S0129 records the principle "**`History ≠ Reconstruction Guess`**" [S0129 L102]. And S0095: restoring append-only history "is **itself a history edit**", and "**A recorded truncation with a recovery pointer is a truthful history. A silently reconstructed one is not.**" [S0095 L162]. *Int:* where H1 fails, the record can still be truthful about the failure, by recording it as a new entry. That keeps H1 for the new record of the loss. This is §5.7 C3's rule (recognized only when recorded) applied to history.
5. **Conflict persistence is a further, separate question** (F24). S2782 asks that "Epistemic conflicts must remain representable." [S2782 L3604] (XIX-C9). That is a property of the state space, not of the record, so it does not belong to H1–H3.

## 9.8.7 Boundary cases and what a reviewer would need to challenge

- **If "the record" is defined as append-only,** H1 is part of the record's definition, and M1 is not a model of the record. The result then reads: the record is a primitive with axioms H2 and H3; history is still derived.
- **If authority is defined to include checkability over time** (§1.2), M1 breaks authority as well, and H1 is a part of authority. Then history is not independent of authority, but authority is not one primitive either.
- **If CE stays undefined,** D fails, and history cannot be derived. It would then have to be a primitive of its own, defined by its queries.
- **To refute the result:** a model in which H1, H2 or H3 fails and some other candidate primitive fails with it, under the outline's definitions; or a derivation of one of H1–H3 from the others.

## 9.8.8 Results, prepared (nothing reserved is decided)

1. History, defined as Hist(c, x, t, s), is derivable from H1–H3 and the evaluator, **conditional on CE being repaired** (D).
2. H1, H2 and H3 are mutually independent and independent of the other six candidates (M1–M3).
3. So "history" is not a primitive in the §12b sense. The record, with H1–H3, is a candidate primitive missing from the list (*Hyp*).
4. The estate keeps H1 at the level of git, not of each file, and keeps H2 only in prose (S0095).
