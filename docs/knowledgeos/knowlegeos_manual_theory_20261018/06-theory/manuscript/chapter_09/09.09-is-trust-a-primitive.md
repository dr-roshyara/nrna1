## 9.9.1 In plain language

"How can knowledge be trusted?" is half of the central question, and the book has had no concept of trust. The sources use the word in two senses. In the first, trust is an assessment: how much weight one party gives a source, for a domain, in a context, at a time. That kind of trust can be built from things the kernel already has: evidence about the source's past outcomes, identities with a lifecycle, a record, and a policy that turns evidence into weight. It is a derived piece of knowledge, and it obeys some laws: it never gives authority, it never creates evidence, it does not carry over to other domains, it is not transitive, and it must not feed on itself. In the second sense, trust is what the whole system rests on: that the records report the acts faithfully. That kind cannot be derived from the records, because every piece of evidence about the records is itself a record. It is not a concept the kernel computes but an assumption it must state, or a fact an outside act must supply.

## 9.9.2 Definitions

- **Trust as assessment (T-a):** Trust(a, d, c, t), the confidence one party places in source a, for domain d, in context c, at time t (S0913 L134, in meaning; S0973 L367, in meaning: "How much confidence does x place in y, for context C, at time t?").
- **Reliability:** Reliability(a, d, t), an empirical estimate from evaluated outcomes. "Reliability can be empirically estimated." [S0973 L441]. Trust "may additionally incorporate" authority, security, provenance, policy and historical behaviour (S0973 L451, in meaning).
- **Trust as the ground of the record (T-b):** the residual trust assumption of §1.12: that the recording process reports acts faithfully; in §9.8's terms, H4 (soundness) and H5 (completeness or marked absence).
- **Primitive (Rulebook §12b (a)):** a concept with a precise definition and an independence model, a model in which it alone fails while the other primitives hold.

The other candidate primitives are taken as in BOOK-OUTLINE and as restated in §9.8: identity (I-0), lifecycle, authority (A1, with §1.2's checkability), evidence, relationships, policy, and the record (§9.8's H1′, H2, H3).

## 9.9.3 Trust as assessment is derivable (Ded, conditional)

**Claim T1.** `[CON-0160 · provisional]` If (i) evidence about a source's past outcomes is recorded with its provenance, (ii) sources have identities with a lifecycle, (iii) the record keeps the outcomes and their times (H1′, H2), and (iv) a versioned policy f maps that evidence to a weight for a domain and context (H3), then

  Trust(a, d, c, t) = f_u(Evidence about a's outcomes in d up to t, Identity status of a at t, c),

and T-a is a knowledge item: an assessment with evidence, period, domain, method, uncertainty and revision history.

**Proof.** Each argument of f is available under (i)–(iv): the evidence and its provenance by (i) and (iii), the identity status at t by (ii) ("identity has lifecycle", S0973 L600, in meaning; "IdentityValidityAtTime", S0973 L656–698, in meaning), and f by (iv). The value is then determined, as in §9.8's Lemma D. ∎

**The source states the same conclusion as a requirement:** "Now trust is itself an epistemic object." [S0913 L781]; "TrustAssessment" is "a derived knowledge object" (S0913 L793, in meaning), so that "trust" does not become "magic metadata" (S0913 L804, in meaning).

**What T1 rests on, and what it does not show:**
- It **defines** T-a as evidence-based. Trust by fiat, a weight set without evidence, exists in practice and is not covered. Under T1's definition, a fiat weight is a policy value without a basis: a failure of policy (H3a, RM-3), not of trust.
- It needs **evidence about outcomes**, so it needs the outcomes to be evaluated. Who evaluates, and whether that evaluation is independent of the source, is §8.6's question (law L5 below).
- It does not show that the kernel **must** compute T-a. That depends on Req: whether answering "how can it be trusted?" requires weighting sources by their record, or only grading each claim's evidence (§1.3).

## 9.9.4 Laws on any trust assessment (Src; Ded where shown)

- **L1. Trust never confers authority.** "Trust\neq Authority" [S0913 L197]; "Trust\ never\ substitutes\ for\ authority." [S0913 L1399]. *Ded (from A1):* only an act confers standing (§1.6). A trust assessment is an assessment, not an act; if it conferred standing, A1 would fail. So under A1, L1 holds for any T-a. Trust can still inform an act, as evidence does.
- **L2. Trust creates no evidence.** "Trust changes the **assessment of evidence**; it does not create evidence." [S0913 L1421]. *Ded:* T-a is computed from evidence (T1); counting it as further evidence for a claim of the same source would count that evidence twice (§8.6, information lineage).
- **L3. Trust is indexed by domain, context and time, and does not carry over.** A high trust for code analysis does not imply one for legal interpretation (S0973 L379–425, in meaning); "A source may be generally trustworthy but unreliable for a particular measurement." [S0913 L217]. *Ded:* T-a's evidence is about outcomes in d; outcomes in d say nothing, by definition of f, about d′ unless f is told so.
- **L4. Trust is not transitive.** "Trust transitivity is not universally valid." [S0973 L1424]. *Ded (a counterexample):* let A trust B in domain d, from evidence about B's outcomes in d; let B trust C in d, from B's evidence about C. A holds no evidence about C's outcomes; B's assessment of C is B's claim, which A may weigh as a claim of B's (L2), not as A's evidence about C. So Trust(A, C) is not determined by Trust(A, B) and Trust(B, C).
- **L5. Trust must not feed on itself.** "trust must not be allowed to recursively reinforce itself without independent validation." [S0913 L752]. *Ded (with §8.6):* if a source's outputs count as evidence of its own reliability, its trust can rise with no outside check, so the evidence for T-a must be independent of the outputs it assesses. The verifier of the series confirms the risk and records that no damper is proposed: a "self-reinforcing-reputation feedback loop is a genuine identified failure mode with no proposed damper — flagged, unresolved" (S1500 L225, in meaning).

*Status:* L1 and L2 follow from A1 and from T1's definition. L3–L5 are constraints stated by one source line, each with a short derivation from T1's definition; as kernel laws they are *Hyp*.

## 9.9.5 Trust as the ground of the record does not reduce (Ded)

**Claim T2.** `[CON-0161 · provisional]` The residual trust assumption (T-b) cannot be derived from the record.

**Argument.**
1. Any evidence that the recording process reports acts faithfully is itself a record, made by some process.
2. Using it to establish T-b assumes that this further record is faithful, which is T-b again for another record.
3. So within the record, T-b either regresses or is assumed at some root. ∎

**What follows (Int):** T-b is not a concept the kernel computes but an **assumption** it must state, a root of trust in the sense used in security engineering (*Ext:* a component that must be trusted because its trustworthiness cannot be checked from within the system). It can be discharged only from outside the record:
- by an act of a person entitled to state the fact, as when the PO/ARB stated that a START "was given" (S0095 L210, in meaning; §1.12);
- by external attestation, one of S0096's six separations (S0096 L30, in meaning; §8.10 C7), absent by construction in a single-operator estate.
The estate's own discipline is consistent with this: identities are "self-declared and NOT attestable" (S0090 L11, in meaning; §8.9), and authorship of an artifact, though recoverable from the record of the writing act, never shows "a process's separation" (S0092 L350, in meaning; §8.10 C5).

## 9.9.6 Independence models

**M-T1: a kernel with no trust assessments.** Identity, evidence, the record and policy hold; no T-a is computed. Each claim is still graded by its own evidence (§1.3). What fails is weighting sources by their record. *Whether this is a failure depends on Req.* If Req needs source weighting, then T-a is needed, but by T1 it is built from the others, so its absence is a missing policy value (H3a), not a missing primitive. **So no independence model for T-a exists under T1's definition.**

**M-T2: a kernel with a faithful-record assumption removed.** All primitives hold as forms; the records may be unfaithful (§9.8 M5). Hist is well defined and may be false; no check from inside the record detects it (T2). **This is an independence model for T-b:** T-b fails while every other candidate, as a form, holds. But T-b is an assumption about the relation between the record and the world, not a concept with operations; so it is a candidate **axiom** of the kernel, not a candidate primitive in §12b's sense.

## 9.9.7 Results, prepared (nothing reserved is decided)

1. **"Trust" has two senses in the sources and the book:** an assessment of a source (T-a) and the ground of the record (T-b).
2. **T-a is derivable** (T1), conditional on evidence of outcomes, identities with a lifecycle, the record and a versioned policy. It is a knowledge item, not a primitive. Its definition as evidence-based is the source's stipulation.
3. **Five laws constrain any T-a:** L1 (no authority) and L2 (no evidence) follow from A1 and T1; L3 (domain-bound), L4 (not transitive) and L5 (no self-reinforcement) are Hyp as kernel laws, each with a short derivation.
4. **T-b does not reduce** (T2). It is a candidate axiom, the kernel's stated root of trust, dischargeable only by an outside act or attestation.
5. **For F23:** trust as assessment is derivable; trust as ground is an assumption. Neither is a primitive in §12b's sense. *Not decided here:* whether the kernel must compute T-a (Req), and where the policy f lives (kernel or application; cf. §9.7's evaluator).

## 9.9.8 Boundary cases, and what a reviewer would need to challenge

- **Fiat trust.** If the organisation sets trust weights by decision rather than evidence, T-a is a policy value, and an act sets it (A1). It is then not trust in T1's sense; L1 still applies.
- **Trust in a person's word as evidence.** Testimony is evidence of a kind; its weight may depend on T-a. That uses T-a; it does not make T-a a primitive.
- **To refute T1:** a requirement on trust that no function of evidence, identity status, record and policy can meet. **To refute T2:** a check from inside the record that establishes faithfulness without assuming any record faithful.
