## 9.9.1 In plain language

"How can knowledge be trusted?" is half of the central question. The sources and the book use "trust" in at least three senses. **Trust in a source** is an assessment of how much weight to give it, for a domain, in a context, at a time. It can be computed from evidence about the source's past outcomes, its identity and a policy, but only if someone the system accepts says which past outcomes were correct, and that regress ends at a root set by an act. **Trust in a knowledge item** is what the central question asks about; it plausibly combines the item's evidence, its standing and the authenticity of its provenance. **Trust in the record**, that the records report acts faithfully, splits into three parts: that an entry is unchanged and that it came from its claimed author can be reduced to trust in keys and timestamps; that the act it reports really happened cannot be established from inside the record at all, though its failure can sometimes be detected. None of the three is a primitive in this book's sense. The first two are built from other things; the third is an assumption the kernel must state.

## 9.9.2 Definitions and senses

- **T-a, trust in a source:** Trust_x(a, d, c, t), the weight a relying party x gives source a, for domain d, in context c, at time t. The sources write it with and without the truster: "Trust(a,d,c,t)" with agent, domain, context, time (S0913 L134, in meaning), and "How much confidence does x place in y, for context C, at time t?" (S0973 L367, in meaning). Here the truster x is explicit.
- **Reliability:** Reliability(a, d, t), an empirical estimate from evaluated outcomes: "Reliability can be empirically estimated." [S0973 L441]. *The sources disagree on containment:* S0973 makes trust the wider notion, which "may additionally incorporate" authority, security, provenance, policy and historical behaviour (S0973 L451, in meaning); S0913 makes reliability the finer, question-specific notion beside a general trustworthiness: "The architect may be highly trustworthy." [S0913 L223], yet unreliable for an exact network fact (S0913 L217, in meaning). This section follows S0973's direction and narrows it (T1 below).
- **T-c, trust in a knowledge item:** S0973's TrustAssessment(a) takes an **artifact** as argument, with identity, authenticity, integrity, authority, epistemic support, context and time as inputs (S0973 L996–1012, in meaning), and its "SemanticTrustBoundary" separates unverified input from "TrustedKnowledgeState" (S0973 L1775–1799, in meaning). Its "most important distinction" is between "Who said this?" [S0973 L1843] and "Why should we believe this?" [S0973 L1851].
- **T-b, trust in the record:** the residual trust assumption of §1.12: that records report acts faithfully; in §9.8's terms, H4 (soundness) and H5 (completeness or marked absence). It is the **book's** sense; the sources come closest in "CryptographicTrust" depending on key management (S0973 L557, in meaning) and in a provenance graph that is verifiable "if the relevant provenance records are protected" (S0973 L828, in meaning).
- **Further uses, kept apart:** sincerity of an agent (S0913 L223, in meaning); key assurance (S0973 L557); admission to the knowledge state (S0973 L1794). They are noted, not analysed.
- **Primitive (Rulebook §12b (a)):** a concept with a precise definition and an independence model, a model in which it alone fails while the other primitives hold.

## 9.9.3 T-a: a conditional, definitional reduction (Ded)

**Claim T1.** `[CON-0160 · provisional]` Let:
- (i) evidence about source a's past outcomes in d be recorded with its provenance;
- (ii) sources have identities with a lifecycle ("identity has lifecycle", S0973 L600, in meaning; IdentityValidityAtTime, S0973 L656–698, in meaning);
- (iii) the record keep the outcomes and their times (§9.8 H1′, H2);
- (iv) a versioned policy f_u map that evidence to a weight (§9.8 H3);
- (v) the outcome records be faithful (T-b; see 9.9.5);
- (vi) outcomes be **adjudicated**: labelled correct or incorrect either by an act under A1 that says what counts as correct, or by observation graded under §1.3 without using T-a.

Then Trust_x(a, d, c, t) = f_u(adjudicated outcomes of a in d up to t, identity status of a at t, c).

**Proof.** Each argument of f is available under (i)–(vi), and f is a function (iv). ∎

**What T1 is.** A conditional reduction **by definition**: T-a is defined as evidence-based, as the source requires ("Now trust is itself an epistemic object." [S0913 L781]; a "derived knowledge object", S0913 L793, in meaning, so that trust does not become "magic metadata", S0913 L804, in meaning). Its content is the premise list. In particular:
- **Premise (vi) has a regress.** If the adjudicators' labels are weighted by their own T-a, then T-a depends on T-a. It ends only at a **root**: a designated evaluator or a prior set by an act, or observation that uses no T-a. **Fork F34**, beside F31.
- **f is unconstrained** in the sources. A natural constraint (*Hyp*) is that f is monotone in favourable adjudicated outcomes and calibrated. The series once had a probabilistic reliability model and dropped it: the Beta-reliability formalization was dropped "entirely" (S1500 L223, in meaning), so in this line f has no formal model.
- **T1 narrows S0973's trust.** It keeps reliability and identity and leaves out authority, security and policy as inputs. Authority stays outside by L1; security ("will the source be compromised?") is not a function of past outcomes without a threat model, and none exists in the series (S1500 L285, in meaning).
- **Fiat trust** (a weight set by decision) is then a policy value set by an act, not trust in T1's sense.

## 9.9.4 Constraints on any T-a (Src; Ded where shown)

- **L1. Trust is not an act, though it may be the condition of one.** "Trust\neq Authority" [S0913 L197]; "Trust\ never\ substitutes\ for\ authority." [S0913 L1399]. *Ded (from A1):* only an act confers standing (§1.6), and an assessment is not an act. A standing rule that grants a permission when trust exceeds a threshold does not refute this: the rule's act confers, and trust is its condition. S0973's direction, authority as an input to trust, is the converse and does not conflict.
- **L2. A trust assessment adds no evidence beyond its basis.** "Trust changes the **assessment of evidence**; it does not create evidence." [S0913 L1421]. *Ded (scoped):* T-a is computed from evidence about a's outcomes (T1); counting it as further evidence for a claim **of the same source** counts that evidence twice (§8.6, information lineage). For claims of other sources (as in L4), a trust assessment is testimony and is weighed as such.
- **L3. Trust in one domain does not carry over to another unless a policy says so** (*Hyp*). A high trust for code analysis does not give one for legal interpretation (S0973 L379–425, in meaning). Under T1 this holds only if f is per domain; and identity compromise (S0973 L568–596, in meaning) crosses domains. S0913's "generally trustworthy" (L223) is a cross-domain notion in tension with this.
- **L4. Trust is not determined by a chain of trust** (*Ded, for non-determination*; *Hyp* as a law). "Trust transitivity is not universally valid." [S0973 L1424]. *Two models:* (a) A's policy gives Low to any source A holds no adjudicated outcomes for, and does not count B's testimony: then Trust_A(B), Trust_B(C) high and Trust_A(C) low. (b) A's policy counts B's testimony about C with a discount: then Trust_A(C) is positive but lower. So Trust_A(C) is not a function of Trust_A(B) and Trust_B(C) alone; transitivity holds only by an explicit referral policy. Referral with discounting is a legitimate policy, not a defect.
- **L5. The evaluator of a source's outcomes must be independent of the source and of trust-weighted acceptance** (*Int, Hyp* as a law). The source states: "trust must not be allowed to recursively reinforce itself without independent validation." [S0913 L752]. The loop is: trust → weight → acceptance → validation → trust. Reliability is measured **on** a source's outputs, so what must be independent is the judgement of their correctness, premise (vi). By §8.6 C1 that independence can be refuted from the record, never shown. The verifier of the series records that no damper is proposed: a "self-reinforcing-reputation feedback loop is a genuine identified failure mode with no proposed damper — flagged, unresolved" (S1500 L225, in meaning).
- **The trivial case.** A constant f (every source Low) satisfies T1 and L1–L5. All five are must-never constraints. *Must-do candidates (Hyp):* revise an assessment when new adjudicated outcomes arrive, and version it ("But reputation should itself be evidence-based and versioned." [S0913 L732]); withdraw the weight of artifacts signed by a revoked identity ("must consider revocation/status of the identity key", S0973 L590, in meaning); state the basis of every assessment ("A trust assessment should itself have:" evidence and more, S0913 L758, in meaning). No owner of these operations is named in the sources.

## 9.9.5 T-b: what reduces, and what does not

Split T-b into three parts:
- **integrity:** the entry is unchanged since it was written;
- **authenticity:** it came from its claimed author;
- **occurrence:** the act it reports happened (and, for H5, no act happened without an entry).

**Claim T2.** `[CON-0161 · invalid as stated]` (restated here, `[CON-0182 · provisional]`)
1. *Integrity and authenticity reduce to anchors.* With hashes and signatures they reduce to trust in keys, key custody and timestamps: a verification holds "subject to the key-management assumptions" (S0973 L538, in meaning), and cryptography "does not manufacture truth" (S0973 L297, in meaning). The anchors are smaller than T-b, but not empty: a key compromised before it is revoked yields valid signatures by an attacker (S0973 L542–598, in meaning), and signing times can be backdated after a compromise.
2. *Occurrence cannot be established from inside the record (Ded).* Any evidence inside the record that an act occurred is an entry, written by some process. Three ways out:
   - assume some entry faithful: then T-b is assumed at a root;
   - regress to a further entry: then the question repeats;
   - **corroborate** by independent entries: but independence on the record can be refuted, not established (§8.6 C1), and in a single-operator estate organisational independence and external attestation are absent by construction (§8.10 C7).
   So in this estate occurrence rests on an assumption. ∎ *(Given §8.6 C1 and the single-operator setting.)*
3. *Unfaithfulness can be refuted from inside the record:* a hash mismatch, a contradiction between entries, an act whose author was not entitled to it. T-b is refutable, never establishable from within, the same asymmetry as independence (§8.6 C1).
4. *An outside act names the root; it does not discharge T-b.* When the PO/ARB stated that a START "was given" (S0095 L210, in meaning), the statement entered the system as a record transcribed by Governance. It fixes who vouches for the fact, not the fact's time: the time of that START is contested in the sources (§8.10 C10).

**What follows (Int):** occurrence is a **candidate axiom** of the kernel: a root of trust in the sense used in security engineering (*Ext:* a component that must be trusted because its trustworthiness cannot be checked from within the system). The estate's own discipline is consistent with it: identities are "self-declared and NOT attestable" (S0090 L11, in meaning; §8.9), and the provenance of the writing act shows an artifact's author only under the assumption of intact transcripts, itself a case of T-b (§8.10 C5). *Link to F33:* a workflow state that only a fact from outside the record can leave (§8.10 C10) is the operational face of T2 point 2; for facts about the world, the kernel can make their absence visible (§1.16.8), not supply them.

## 9.9.6 T-c: trust in a knowledge item (Hyp)

**Hyp.** Trust in a knowledge item k combines k's evidential grade (§1.3), k's standing (§1.6, §9.7's evaluator), and the authenticity of k's provenance, the last weighted by the T-a of k's sources. Under that reading T-c is built from T-a, evidence, standing and provenance, and is conditional on T-b. S0973's TrustAssessment(a) over an artifact is a sketch of this kind, with an undefined f (S0973 L996–1012, in meaning), and it overlaps the evidence assessment of the same source (S0973 L1559–1577, in meaning). *Not shown:* which combination, and whether standing and trust stay separate in it (L1).

## 9.9.7 Independence models

**M-T1: a kernel with no source assessments.** Identity, evidence, the record and policy hold; no T-a is computed. Each claim is still graded by its own evidence (§1.3). What fails is weighting sources by their record. Under T1's definition, its absence is a missing policy value, not a missing primitive. *This is trivial given T1's definition, so it is no evidence beyond the definition.*

**M-T2: a kernel whose records may be unfaithful.** All primitives hold as forms; occurrence may fail. Hist (§9.8) is well defined and may be false; no check inside the record establishes faithfulness, though it may refute it (T2). **This is an independence model for T-b's occurrence part:** it fails while every other candidate, as a form, holds. Being an assumption about the record's relation to the world, it is a candidate **axiom**, not a candidate primitive in §12b's sense.

## 9.9.8 What the source line says about the kernel (Src)

S0973 asks whether identity belongs in the kernel and answers that "Identity" "should now be considered a **kernel-level concern**, because trustworthy provenance requires attribution." [S0973 L1727], while PKI and key vaults stay infrastructure; its updated kernel lists identity and not trust (S0973 L1747–1773, in meaning). The source thus treats trust as derived, not as a kernel concept. *One line.* Its stated reason holds only if provenance must be authentic (S0973 L47–67, in meaning).

## 9.9.9 Results, prepared (nothing reserved is decided)

1. **"Trust" has at least three senses here:** in a source (T-a), in a knowledge item (T-c), in the record (T-b), with further uses (sincerity, key assurance, admission) noted.
2. **T-a reduces conditionally and by definition** (T1): its content is six premises, of which (v) is T-b and (vi) is adjudication, whose regress ends at a root set by an act (F34).
3. **Five constraints, rescoped:** L1 (trust is not an act; it may be a condition of one) and L2 (no evidence beyond its basis, for the same source) follow from A1 and T1; L3–L5 are Hyp as kernel laws; all five are must-never, and must-do candidates are Hyp.
4. **T-b splits** (T2): integrity and authenticity reduce to key and timestamp anchors; occurrence cannot be established from inside the record in this estate and is a candidate axiom; unfaithfulness is refutable.
5. **For F23:** none of the three senses is a primitive in §12b's sense; T-a and T-c are built from other candidates under stated premises, and T-b's occurrence part is an axiom. *Not decided:* whether the kernel must compute T-a or T-c (Req); where f lives; F34.

## 9.9.10 Open questions

- Should the kernel state occurrence (T-b) as an explicit axiom beside A1?

## 9.9.11 What does not hold (first version, refuted at review TT4)

- **"'Trust' has two senses in the sources and the book."** Trust in a knowledge item, key assurance, sincerity and admission are further uses, and T-b is the book's sense, not the sources' (A finding 1; B findings 4, 8).
- **"Trust as assessment is derivable", labelled Ded without its premises.** It is a conditional reduction by definition; it hides T-b for outcome records and the adjudication of outcomes, whose regress needs a root (A 2, 3; B 2, 3).
- **"The residual trust assumption does not reduce" and "dischargeable only by an outside act".** Integrity and authenticity reduce to anchors; the argument missed the corroboration case; an outside act names a root and does not discharge it (A 4; B 1).
- **L1 as "trust never confers authority"** without the threshold case; **L2** unscoped; **L3** "by definition of f"; **L4**'s counterexample, which was not a model; **L5** as independence of evidence from the outputs it assesses, which cannot be met (A 5, 6, 8, 9, 10; B 6, 7).
- **No trivial-case test** for L1–L5 (A 7; B 5).
- **T1's formula without a truster**, and the silent dropping of S0973's authority input (A 8; B 4).
