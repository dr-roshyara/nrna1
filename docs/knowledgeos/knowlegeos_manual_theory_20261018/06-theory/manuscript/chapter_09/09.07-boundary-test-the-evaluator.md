## 9.7.1 In plain language

To say whether a piece of knowledge counts today, something has to compute it from the record of acts. The three questions get these answers:
- **Necessary?** Yes, the Principal has decided the kernel must answer such questions.
- **Must the kernel provide it?** The rule of the computation, and the policy choices inside it, must be the same for every application. If two applications use different policies, they disagree about what is valid. The computation itself can run anywhere, because anyone can repeat it and check the result.
- **Can applications provide it safely?** Yes for the computation, provided its result is never stored as if it were authority.

But the computation needs inputs the present record does not have: when each act happened, how long each grant was valid, and how far its scope reached.

## 9.7.2 Definitions

- **CE(c, t; θ, R):** the value of currently-authoritative(c, t) of §1.14.1, in {T, F, U}, with:
  - the record R as input;
  - the parameter vector θ = (ρ, κ, χ, ω, σ, π, υ, P, ι).

  Some parameters are policy: P (the ending policy, reserved decision 1), ρ (the record view, reserved decision 2) and υ (reserved decision 6).
- **Agreement:** two applications agree on standing if, for every item c, time t and scope, they return the same headline.
- **Three temporal questions (Src, S2775):** "What contracts were active on 1 June?" is ValidAt; "What contracts did we believe were active on 1 June?" is KnownStateAsOf; "What do we know today about which contracts were active on 1 June?" is CurrentKnowledgeAbout. "These are three different queries." [S2775 L1121–1149]

## 9.7.3 Analysis 1: is CE necessary?

**Assumptions:** the Principal's act, R_s ∈ Req (§9.6.6a); N1 and N2 of §9.6.3.

**Derivation:** by §9.6.3 steps 2–3, R_s needs a registration of acts (CR-a) **and** a derivation of standing from them (§1.14.1, current authority is derived). The derivation is CE. **So CE is necessary** (Ded, given the act).

**What CE needs as input, and what the record lacks (Src; Ded):**

| Input CE uses (§1.14.1) | In the estate's record? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| act time t_x | **no:** "Only 2 of 210 transitions carry any `date` field" | [S0128 L49] |
| record position s_x | yes, `seq` | [S0128 L48] |
| a grant's act window (start, end, endings) | partly: validity fields 0/144; endings rare (REVOKED 1, CONSUMED 1) | §1.13.2 recount |
| coverage of scope | **no:** the scope is an opaque string matched by equality | §9.6.3 step 4 |
| who issued a grant, and under which authority (delegation) | **no:** delegation fields 0/144 | §1.13.2; formal defect 2 of §1.14.1 |
| the time an assertion was accepted as knowledge | not modelled in §1.14.1 | S0916 (C3 below) |

**Result:** CE is necessary, and **it is not computable from the record as it stands**, because act times, validity periods, scope coverage and delegation are missing. S0128 warns against the obvious repair: on import, "commit times become the only time evidence — and they would be IMPORT times, not act times … Do not let an import be mistaken for a chronology" [S0128 L171].

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it:** a reading of R_s that needs no derivation (e.g. standing stored per item), which §1.14.1 rejects; or an input in the table that is in fact recorded.

## 9.7.4 Analysis 2: must the kernel provide CE?

**Assumptions:**
- K3: several independent applications.
- **K5 (stated here): standing has one answer per (item, scope, time) across applications.** It is the reading of the central question "which knowledge is valid", under which validity is not relative to the application that asks. *A stipulation, for the Principal to confirm.*

**Derivation:**
1. **Deterministic, and defined where it is defined.** For fixed R and θ, CE returns one value wherever the least fixed point exists (§1.14.1, well-definedness). So two applications that use the same R and the same θ agree.
2. **Different θ breaks agreement, shown by the book's own cases:**
   - case 10 of §1.14.1, a late-recorded cancellation, gives authorized under ρ = at-the-time and not authorized under ρ = now;
   - case 4, a consumed grant, depends on P.

   So two applications that differ in ρ or P disagree on some item. Under K5 that is not allowed.
3. **Hence the parameters that are policy (P, ρ, υ, and the alternatives C1–C8) must be fixed once for all applications,** per kind of item. They are governed choices, acts in the sense of A1, not settings of each application. The **definition** of CE must also be one, or the applications compute different functions.
4. **Execution is delegable.** CE is a deterministic function of shared inputs and fixed parameters, so any application may compute it, and any other may recompute it to check. That is the mechanical check class of §8.6 C5.
5. **Therefore: the kernel must own CE's definition and its policy parameters, as governed objects. It need not run CE.** The record input must be shared or mergeable, as in §9.6.4 step 1.

**Trivial cases and boundary cases:**
- **One application:** K5 holds trivially and nothing needs to be shared. This is the case the Principal's act excludes.
- **Where CE is not yet defined:** under υ = "does not count until evidenced", the operator "is **not monotone**, so a least fixed point need not exist" (§1.14.1, formal defect 1). There, step 1 fails, and the kernel **cannot** adopt CE for that parameter value until the defect is repaired.
- **A per-application policy is coherent only without K5.** If the organisation accepts that standing may differ by application, the parameters need not be shared. The Principal's central question reads against that, but K5 is stated, not proved.

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it:**
- a case where different θ always agree (it would refute step 2), against cases 4 and 10;
- or an argument that K5 is not implied by the central question.

## 9.7.5 Analysis 3: can applications compute CE without violating the invariants?

**Derivation, invariant by invariant:**

| Invariant | Holds if an application computes CE? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A1 (only an act confers standing) | yes | CE reads acts; it performs none |
| ARCH-INV-1 (the recorder never grants) | yes, if the evaluator is not the recorder | §9.6.2: the recorder may not evaluate; a separate evaluator may |
| P-8 and CON-0134 (a derived representation never gains authority; derived state is never stored) | **only if** CE's output is not stored as standing | a stored result is "a second truth able to drift" [S0071 L208]; the stored value must be inert (§8.8 C1) |
| producer ≠ verifier (§1.14.3) | yes, by recomputation | a deterministic result is checked by re-running it, by any other party |
| revocation is not retroactive (S1414's I53; §1.14.1's premise) | it depends on P | S1414: "Authority changes affect future authorization but do not retroactively invalidate historically authorized transitions unless an explicit domain rule requires it" [S1414 L1162–1165]. In CE that is P not containing revocation for that kind |

**Result (Ded):** applications may compute CE, provided that:
1. its definition and policy parameters are the kernel's (Analysis 2);
2. its output is a **derived, inert** value, never stored as standing;
3. its inputs come from the shared record.

The one failure that delegation itself would cause is a stored result read as authority. S0128 names the failure from the other side: if the record is lost, "prose becomes authoritative by default — authority drift by attrition" [S0128 L123].

**What a reviewer would need to challenge it:** an invariant broken by an application that computes CE under these three conditions.

## 9.7.6 Agreement with the brainstorm line (Src; Int; a second line)

- **C1. Authority is scoped and temporal:** authority is modelled as Authority(a, C, O, t) (S1414 L411, in meaning), it "can expire" [S1414 L463], and a grant is "not merely an RBAC table" [S1414 L506] but "a governed fact" [S1414 L508]. This is §1.6's act × scope × time and §1.14.1's act window.
- **C2. Authority is necessary, not sufficient:** a valid transition needs authorization, evidence, preconditions and invariant preservation; "Authority is necessary but not sufficient." [S1414 L340–375]. This is §1.17.2 (valid is not well-founded) as a transition rule.
- **C3. A third time:** valid time, transaction time and knowledge time (S0916 L77–87, in meaning), where an assertion "may exist in the system but not yet be accepted as knowledge" [S0916 L216]. Also: "Historical decisions must be evaluated against the historical epistemic state." [S0916 L1699]. **Extends §1.14.1:** its two axes (act time, record position) do not separate **recorded** from **accepted**. The question "what did we believe on 1 June?" needs the acceptance time.
- **C4. Delegation cannot exceed its source:** I52, "Delegated authority must not exceed the scope, duration, or nature of the authority from which it derives." [S1414 L614–616]. This is §1.13.3(d), and it bears on formal defect 2 (issuance and delegation unchecked).
- **C5. Reconstruction needs preserved times:** S2775's reconstruction theorem assumes "each relevant event has preserved temporal semantics" [S2775 L1602] and "transaction/knowledge time is preserved" [S2775 L1603]. The estate's record does not meet the first assumption (Analysis 1). **So historical standing in this estate cannot be reconstructed from the record alone.**

## 9.7.7 Results, prepared (nothing reserved is decided)

1. **CE is necessary** (given the Principal's act).
2. **The kernel must own CE's definition and its policy parameters (P, ρ, υ, C1–C8),** as governed objects, under K5 (stated, for the Principal to confirm).
3. **CE's execution is delegable,** provided its output is inert and its inputs are shared.
4. **CE cannot be computed from the present record.** It needs act times, validity, endings, comparable scope and delegation, and possibly an acceptance time. That is a contract for the record (§9.6.5).
5. **CE is not yet well-defined for every parameter value** (formal defect 1). The kernel cannot adopt it until that is repaired.

**For the Principal:**
- K5: is standing one answer across applications?
- The values of the reserved decisions 1, 2 and 6, which this turn shows must be **one** value per kind of item, not one per application.
