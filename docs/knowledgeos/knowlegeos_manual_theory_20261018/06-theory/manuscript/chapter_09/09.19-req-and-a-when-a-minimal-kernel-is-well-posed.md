## 9.19.1 In plain language

- §9.10 already answers most of what BOOK-OUTLINE L179 calls missing. Five of the six deferring claims are answered: each requirement part maps to a Req item, and what remains (delivery to an actor, or an actor's duty to act) is outside Req for a stated reason. One, §9.5 C1, is answered only in part: the "latest adopted version" of a rule has no reference definition, and detecting an act taken under an older version needs a field Req does not yet name.
- "A minimal kernel" means nothing until two things are fixed: **Req**, and an **interface assumption A**. A says where the system ends and how finely its mechanisms are cut. Fix both, and a minimal kernel exists whenever a kernel exists.
- **Uniqueness is a different matter.** It holds exactly when the kernels of the fixed pool are closed under intersection (T3, proved here under two stated hypotheses). Whether KnowledgeOS's pool meets this is **not known**, because the pool is not fixed. It cannot be fixed without the state space (T9-02).
- Coarser mechanisms always break uniqueness when the fine minimal kernel has two or more mechanisms (T5). Guards counted as separate components break the hypotheses themselves (T6). So **no unique minimal kernel is claimed**, and none can be claimed from Req alone.
- What does hold for every pool under the stated boundary is necessity **by kind**: every kernel must be able to append each kind of entry Req asks to accept (T4). Necessity of a **named mechanism** fails as soon as composite mechanisms are allowed.

## 9.19.4 A: the system boundary and the mechanism granularity, stated separately

**A = (B, G).** The two parts are independent choices. A result can hold for one B and fail for another G, and the reverse (§9.19.6).

**B, the system boundary (from settled sections; Int where marked).**
- **Inside:** the designated record (§9.17 R1) and the kernel's steps that write it. K1 is the exclusive write path: the record changes only through the kernel's steps (§9.15; restated §9.17). The reference definitions the kernel answers by: CE* for Q1 (§9.13), Q3's (R5), Q2's return of entries, and Q4's if G7 places it.
- **Outside:**
  - the world and its occurrences (R7: world assumptions);
  - agents and applications, which propose, execute and deliver (§10.2; T10-01 (b));
  - evidence producers (O2 accepts their entries);
  - the carrier of the record (K2-d, a presupposition, §9.17);
  - the kit (F14).
- **On the line:** the guards. Per law, some are kernel guards and some are application guards (R6; F22 (C + O): an owner application per grant).
- **What B fixes:** what counts as a state change (only kernel steps, K1), and therefore what a mechanism is a mechanism **of**.

**G, the mechanism granularity (not fixed by the book; stated as alternatives).** A **pool** 𝓜 is a finite set of mechanisms m : 𝒮 × In → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}. Mechanisms are individuated as **functions**, so two labels for one function are one mechanism (§9.1 C4's revision). A **composition reading** says what a set's transitions are:
- **G-kind:** one **appender** per input kind of Req (O1–O4; an appender of kind i is a mechanism that adds an entry of kind i to the record), each guarded inside itself; closed reading (a set's transitions are the union of its members').
- **G-act:** one mechanism per act kind (grant, revocation, annulment, removal, establishment, adoption, recusal, declassification, and the others §9.10.7 names); closed reading. It refines G-kind's O1.
- **G-coarse:** a pool that also contains composites, mechanisms equal to a sequence of others in one step; closed reading. **EA-1** (an engineering assumption, labelled): implementations commonly expose writes through a few coarse operations, so G-coarse is a live possibility for an implementation's pool. It is not evidence about KnowledgeOS.
- **G-guard:** guards are separate pool components; a set's transitions are its appenders' steps, minus those blocked by a guard in the set. This is §9.1 C3's "guard reading".

**What the book does not fix.** The book fixes none of G-kind, G-act, G-coarse or G-guard. The pool for KnowledgeOS needs 𝒮 (the state space, T9-02, F13), since mechanisms are functions on it. **T9-02 is not absorbed here.** Req does not need it (Req's items are stated over the record and reference definitions). A's G does need it for any statement about KnowledgeOS's own pool. *Escalated, §9.19.9 U1.*

**Two minimality notions (T9-03), related under A.**
- **Mechanism minimality:** Minimal(M) over the pool, §9.1.
- **Primitive minimality:** no generator is derivable from the others.
- **They agree when the pool contains no composite** (every mechanism is underivable from the others in 𝓜). Then a mechanism-minimal kernel consists of primitives by construction (Ded, from the definitions).
- **They come apart under G-coarse.** {c_all} is mechanism-minimal and derivable (model, COARSE), as recgrant was in §9.1 C4.
- **Partial answer to T9-03:** the relation is stated; the pool is not fixed.

**Observers are a separate axis.** §9.10.8 states that the questions' condition does not depend on the mechanism set. So Kernel splits into:
- the **transition part**: P, and O1–O4 as reachability;
- the **observer part**: Q1–Q4 met by reference definitions.

Mechanism minimality concerns the transition part only. On the observer part, §9.10.5 F-min shows each core question is needed for some judgement (requirement-level). §9.10.5 also notes "Q1 at a cut is CE over Q2's entries", so at the level of reference definitions Q1 is not an independent observer. **Requirement-level and observer-level minimality therefore differ on Q1 (Ded, from §9.10.5's two statements).** Neither is preferred here: choosing one is a selection criterion, which the commission excludes.

## 9.19.6 Results, with proofs

**Setting (for all results).**
- 𝓜 is a finite pool over a finite 𝒮.
- I is the invariant set; S_I is the valid states; s₀ is the initial state.
- P(M): every transition of M from S_I lands in S_I ∪ {⊥}.
- Req_O(M): the input part, each O_i as EF(an entry of kind i), §9.10.5 R1/R4.
- 𝒦 = {M ⊆ 𝓜 : P(M) ∧ Req_O(M)}.
- The observer part is fixed separately (§9.19.4).
- **H-down:** P(M) ∧ N ⊆ M ⇒ P(N). **H-mono:** Req_O(M) ∧ M ⊆ N ⇒ Req_O(N).

**T1. Well-posedness (Ded).** For fixed (B, G, Req), Minimal is defined on 𝒦. A minimal kernel exists iff 𝒦 ≠ ∅. Without a fixed G, "a minimal kernel" has no fixed referent: the minimal sets differ between pools of one model (model: FINE vs COARSE; §9.1 C4).

**T2. Existence by one check (§9.1 C3, cited, not redone).** Under the closed reading with H-down, H-mono and P closed under union, 𝒦 ≠ ∅ iff M* = {m : P({m})} satisfies Req_O. *Scope as in §9.1 C3: it fails under the guard reading.*

**T3. A structure theorem for uniqueness (Ded).** Let 𝒦 ≠ ∅.
- (a) If 𝒦 is closed under pairwise intersection, 𝒦 has exactly one minimal element.
- (b) Under H-down and H-mono, the converse holds: if 𝒦 has exactly one minimal element, 𝒦 is closed under pairwise intersection.

**T4. Necessity by kind (Ded, under B's K1 and s₀ with no entry of kind i).** Every M ∈ 𝒦 contains, for each kind i that Req_O requires, a mechanism that appends an entry of kind i at some state reachable under M. **Necessity of a named mechanism fails under G-coarse:** in the model's COARSE pool, no mechanism lies in every kernel.

**T5. Coarsening breaks uniqueness (Ded, with stated conditions).** Let E ∈ 𝒦 be minimal with |E| ≥ 2. Let c_E be a mechanism that, from s₀, performs in one step a sequence of E's transitions reaching a state that meets every requirement of Req_O, and that preserves I from every state of S_I. In 𝓜′ = 𝓜 ∪ {c_E}:
- {c_E} ∈ 𝒦′ and is minimal;
- E is still minimal in 𝒦′ (minimality of E concerns subsets of E only, which are unchanged);
- so 𝒦′ has two distinct minimal elements.

**T6. Guards as components break the hypotheses (model; one counterexample).** Under G-guard, H-down and H-mono both fail. Witnesses:
- P({u_act, g_root, a_evid, a_loss, a_root}) holds; without g_root it fails;
- Req_O holds for that set and fails once g_block is added.

So T2 and T3 (b) do not apply under G-guard, and a superset of a kernel need not be a kernel. *(In this one model the minimal kernel happens to be unique anyway. T6 refutes the hypotheses, not uniqueness.)*
