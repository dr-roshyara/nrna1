## 9.19.1 In plain language

- §9.10 maps all six deferring claims of BOOK-OUTLINE L179 to Req items, but it **fully answers none of them**. Each is answered in its record part. Each leaves a residue that is undecided, has no reference definition, or rests on a reading of Req that is still open. The residues are:
  - the EF reading of the inputs;
  - O1's act kinds that the evaluator's domain D does not admit;
  - an open law;
  - a missing reference definition.

  None of the six needs a new capability **under the EF reading**. §9.5 C5 needs a stronger reading if the Principal adopts one.
- "A minimal kernel" means nothing until two things are fixed: **Req** (including how its inputs are read and cut) and an **interface assumption A** (where the system ends, and how finely its mechanisms are cut). Fix both, with a finite pool, and a minimal kernel exists whenever a kernel exists.
- **Uniqueness is a different matter.** It holds exactly when the kernels of the fixed pool are closed under intersection (T3). A sufficient condition is that each required input has one minimal set of mechanisms that enables it (T10). Uniqueness fails in three situations:
  - when a mechanism offers an alternative route;
  - when a composite is added under T5's conditions;
  - when an input requirement is cut coarsely, for example "some act entry".

  It returns when every input carries one entry, or when the requirement is cut per act kind (the model, version 2). Whether KnowledgeOS's pool is unique is **not known**, because the pool is not fixed. Fixing it needs the state space (T9-02).
- So **no unique minimal kernel is claimed**, and none can be claimed from Req alone.
- What does hold under the stated boundary is **necessity by kind, as Req individuates kinds** (T4). The mechanisms necessary in every kernel are exactly the intersection of all kernels (T9). Where substitutes exist, that intersection can be empty, so a per-capability removal test can find nothing necessary even though the empty system fails.

## 9.19.4 A: the system boundary and the mechanism granularity, stated separately

**A = (B, G).** The two are independent choices. **Req's own cut** (how its input requirements are individuated, for example O1 as one requirement or one per act kind) is a third choice, on the requirement side. It matters for uniqueness and necessity (C-05, B F7).

**B, the system boundary.**
- **Inside:**
  - **one** designated record (§9.17 R1);
  - the kernel's steps that write it, with K1 the exclusive write path;
  - the reference definitions the kernel answers by: CE* for Q1, Q3's (R5), Q2's return of entries, and Q4's if G7 places it.
- **Outside:**
  - the world (R7's world assumptions);
  - agents and applications (§10.2; T10-01 (b));
  - evidence producers;
  - the carrier (K2-d);
  - the kit (F14).
- **On the line:** the guards, per law (R6; F22 (C + O)).
- **An alternative boundary, stated so the scope is visible (A-04).** The book also has a record set Ρ (§9.17), owner-application records that carry uses (§9.16 R4d), and several records as an open question (T9-07 (c)). If application records count toward Req_O, a kernel with **no mechanisms** can meet it. **T1, T4, T7 and T9 are stated for B with K1 and one designated record.**

**G, the mechanism granularity (not fixed by the book).** A pool 𝓜 is a finite set of functions m : 𝒮 × In → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}. In G-kind, "guarded" means the mechanism's own partiality (⊥), not a separate guard in C6's sense.
- **G-kind:** one **appender** per input kind of Req (O1–O4). An appender of kind i is a mechanism that adds an entry of kind i to the record. The reading is closed.
- **G-act:** one mechanism per act kind; this refines G-kind's O1.
- **G-coarse:** composites (a sequence of other mechanisms in one step). **EA-1** (labelled): implementations commonly expose writes through a few coarse operations. It is not evidence about KnowledgeOS.
- **G-guard:** guards are separate components (§9.1 C3's guard reading).
- **Batching (C-04):** whether one input may carry several entries. Under C6's input-origin condition, one input carries one act. A transition invariant "at most one kind added per step" then **excludes** the composites, and in the model uniqueness returns (COARSE-T). Batching is undecided (§1.16.1).

**What the book does not fix:** G, batching, and Req's cut. The pool needs 𝒮 (T9-02, F13).
- **Req's core** (Q1–Q3, Q5, O1–O4) is stated over the record and its reference definitions, without T9-02. Even this is conditional on stating Req over D (B F9).
- **Q4's reference** needs F13 under G7 (A-09, B F9, C-08).

The commission's limit, "if Req cannot be stated without it, stop and escalate", therefore applies to Q4's reference. Q4's reference was already absent in §9.10, so the core is stated and **Q4's reference is escalated** (U1, U2). T9-02 is not absorbed.

**Two minimality notions (T9-03).** Mechanism minimality (Minimal over the pool) and primitive minimality (no generator derivable from the others).
- **Definition.** A mechanism is *derivable* in 𝓜 if it equals, as a function, a sequence of other members applied in one step. A *composite* is a derivable mechanism.
- **One direction (Ded):** in a pool with no composite, every mechanism-minimal kernel consists of underivable mechanisms.
- **Not the converse.** A set of underivable mechanisms need not be minimal (model ALT: {a_act, a_evid, a_evid2, a_loss, a_root}) (B F6, A-10).
- **They come apart under G-coarse:** {c_all} is minimal and derivable.
- **A homonym.** "Primitive" here means a mechanism. The candidate primitives of §9.19.5 and RESEARCH-PURPOSE are concepts (B F6).

**Observers are a separate axis.** §9.10.8: the questions' condition does not depend on the mechanism set. So Kernel splits into a transition part (P; O1–O4 as EF) and an observer part (Q1–Q4 met by reference definitions).
- **Q contributes nothing to mechanism minimality** (A, B, C at step 1; Ded from §9.1 C6 result 7 (a)). Which questions sit in the kernel is decided by the boundary questions, not by minimality.
- On the observer side, requirement-level minimality (§9.10.5 F-min) and observer-level minimality differ on Q1, which is CE over Q2's entries (Ded). Neither is preferred: choosing one is a selection criterion.

## 9.19.6 Results, with proofs

**Setting (for all results).**
- 𝓜 is a finite pool over a finite 𝒮; I is the invariant set; S_I is the valid states; s₀ is the initial state.
- P(M): every transition of M from S_I lands in S_I ∪ {⊥}. In the model, the invariants are **state invariants**. A transition invariant is added only in COARSE-T.
- Req_O(M): each input requirement O_i, **as Req cuts it**, read as EF(an entry of kind i) from s₀.
- 𝒦 = {M ⊆ 𝓜 : P(M) ∧ Req_O(M)}.
- B is as in §9.19.4, with K1 and one designated record.
- **H-down:** P(M) ∧ N ⊆ M ⇒ P(N). **H-mono:** Req_O(M) ∧ M ⊆ N ⇒ Req_O(N). Both hold under the closed reading with EF. They need not hold under G-guard (T6) or under enabledness.

**T1. Well-posedness (Ded).** For fixed (B, G, Req) with 𝓜 finite, a minimal kernel exists iff 𝒦 ≠ ∅. Without a fixed G and a fixed cut of Req, "a minimal kernel" has no fixed referent (model: FINE, COARSE, ALT; GACT under two cuts).

**T2. Existence by one check (§9.1 C3, cited).** Under the closed reading, EF, H-down, H-mono and P closed under union: 𝒦 ≠ ∅ iff M* = {m : P({m})} meets Req_O.

**T3. A structure theorem for uniqueness (Ded).** Let 𝒦 ≠ ∅.
- (a) If 𝒦 is closed under pairwise intersection, 𝒦 has exactly one minimal element.
- (b) Under H-down and H-mono, the converse holds.

**T4. Necessity by kind (Ded; B with K1; s₀ without kind i; EF).** Every M ∈ 𝒦 contains, for each kind i **as Req individuates kinds**, a mechanism that appends kind i at a state reachable under M. How many appenders T4 demands depends on Req's cut (B F7). Necessity of a **named** mechanism fails when substitutes exist (T9).

**T5. Adding a composite breaks uniqueness (Ded, with stated conditions).** Premises:
- s₀ does not meet Req_O (A-06);
- E ∈ 𝒦 is minimal with |E| ≥ 2;
- c_E is a mechanism that, from s₀, reaches in one step a state meeting every requirement of Req_O, and preserves I from every state of S_I.

Then in 𝓜′ = 𝓜 ∪ {c_E}, both {c_E} and E are minimal, and they are distinct. **Scope (C-04, A-05):**
- **augmentation only:** replacing E by c_E leaves one minimal kernel (A's probes P1a, P1b);
- **state invariants with batching:** c_E must be allowed to add several kinds in one step. Under the one-entry-per-input invariant no such c_E preserves I, and uniqueness returns (COARSE-T);
- c_E needs a state meeting every requirement. Where no such state is valid, T5 does not apply. A partial composite can still break uniqueness (A's P2b). A generalisation T5′ (replacing a sub-sequence) is **not proved**.

**T6. Guards as components: the hypotheses need not hold (model; one counterexample).** Under G-guard the model has:
- P({u_act, g_root, a_evid, a_loss, a_root}), and not P without g_root;
- Req_O for that set, and not Req_O once g_block is added.

So H-down and H-mono **need not hold** (C-16, A-11), and T2 and T3 (b) do not apply. T6 refutes the hypotheses, not uniqueness.

**T7. The trivial cases (Ded; B with K1 and one record).** If s₀ does not meet Req_O, then ∅, refuse-all and noop each satisfy P and fail Req_O, under every G. **Not excluded beyond s₀:** the one-witness system (accept each kind once, then refuse). EF does not exclude it (§9.19.3; U5).

**T8. Zero's two predicates follow from Q7 (Ded).** Given Q7's typed gap Δ with criticality: Zero-all = [Δ = ∅]; Zero-blocking = [no blocking requirement in Δ]; Zero-all ⇒ Zero-blocking.

**T9. The necessary mechanisms are the intersection of all kernels (Ded; C-14).** A mechanism m lies in every kernel iff m ∈ ⋂𝒦. So:
- (i) with substitutes, ⋂𝒦 can be empty while ∅ ∉ 𝒦 (model COARSE: "in every kernel: []"). A per-capability removal test (RESEARCH-PURPOSE's test 1) then finds **nothing** necessary, though the empty system fails. The test is informative only where substitutes are absent.
- (ii) Under T3's hypotheses, the minimal kernel is unique iff ⋂𝒦 ∈ 𝒦.

*This bears on §9.6.3's use of the removal test; recorded, not re-derived there.*

**T10. A sufficient condition for uniqueness (Ded).** Suppose there are sets E_1, …, E_n ⊆ 𝓜 with Req_O(M) ⟺ E_r ⊆ M for every r (each requirement has one minimal enabling set, and no alternative route). Assume H-down, and let E = E_1 ∪ … ∪ E_n with P(E). Then 𝒦 = {M : E ⊆ M, P(M)}, and E is the unique minimal kernel. ALT breaks the condition, because evid has two enabling sets. GACT with O1 as "an act entry" breaks it the same way. C's random check of the Horn form found uniqueness in all 644 cases (form only; reported, not re-run).
