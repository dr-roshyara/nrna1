## 9.14.1 The research question

§2.3 (d) defines conflict on **sets** of rules, point by point, and shows by a counterexample that checking pairs is not enough. **For which rule languages does checking single rules and pairs find every conflict that the set-wise test finds, with the same regions? And which of the book's rule classes are such languages?**

**What would disprove the answer:**
- a rule base over a language said to need only pairs, on which the set-wise test finds a minimal conflicting set of three or more rules;
- or a language said to need more than pairs, every one of whose bases has all its conflicts caught by pairs (the realisability premise of R1 (c) would then be false for it).

## 9.14.2 Definitions and assumptions

- **Context, outcome, effect set** (§2.3 (d)).
  - At a context c, rule r's mandatory effect φᵣ(c, x) constrains the outcome x ∈ X. Its **effect set** is Eᵣ(c) = {x ∈ X : φᵣ(c, x)}.
  - **X is the outcome space** the effects are evaluated in. If X is restricted by a constraint between attributes (a coupling, such as "exactly one of three"), that constraint is part of X, or, equivalently, a rule of its own in every checked set.
  - A set S of rules is **minimal in conflict at (c, t)** iff all of S apply at (c, t), the **indexed** family (Eᵣ(c))_{r∈S} has empty intersection, and every proper subfamily has a common point (§2.3 (d)). Two rules may have the same effect set; a minimal family never repeats a member.
- **The admitted language L.** The effect forms an analyser accepts (§2.3 (a): the restricted language). Its **effect class** 𝓔(L) is the set of all effect sets that rules written in L can have, at any context, over X.
- **Helly number** (*Ext:* Helly 1923; Danzer, Grünbaum and Klee 1963). h(𝓔) is the largest size of a minimal **finite** subfamily of 𝓔 with empty intersection, and 0 if no finite subfamily has empty intersection. Equivalently, every finite subfamily with empty intersection has a subfamily of at most h(𝓔) members that already has empty intersection.
  - The case h ≤ 2 is the **Helly property** of a set system.
  - For a single base ℛ, **m(ℛ)** is the largest size of a pointwise-minimal conflicting set; m(ℛ) ≤ h(𝓔(L)) (R1 (a)).
- **Scope.** Mandatory effects only (MUST, MUST_NOT). Permissions against rules and SHOULD tensions keep their own finding classes (§2.3 (b), (d)).
  - Exceptions narrow **applicability** (§2.3 (b), effective applicability A′). Allow-lists are such exceptions (§2.9, §2.11). They do not change effect sets, so they do not change h.
  - Placement rules that **compete for one output** are resolved by precedence and are not checked for conflict (§2.8.4a).
  - **Enforcement thresholds** are not effects: the design checks' phase targets are enforcement, the obligation is fixed at zero violations (§2.9). They are outside this section.
- **Assumption for R1 (c) (realisability):** any finite family of effect sets from 𝓔(L) can be carried by rules of L that all apply at one point. Applicability "true" is in the language (§2.2), and effects may be the same at every context.

## 9.14.3 Results

**R1. Pairs suffice for a language exactly when its effect class has Helly number at most 2 (Ded).** `[CON-0192 · provisional]` Let L be an admitted language and h = h(𝓔(L)).
- **(a) Bound.** For every rule base over L, at every point, every set minimal in conflict has at most h members.
- **(b) Reduction.** The set-wise report (§2.3 (d): each minimal conflicting set with its region) is obtained by testing, point by point, every subset of at most h rules. If h ≤ 2, testing single rules and pairs point by point gives exactly the set-wise report, regions included.
- **(c) Converse, for the language.** If h > 2 and 𝓔(L) is realisable, some base over L has a conflict that no pair and no single rule shows.
- **(d) For one base.** Pairs-only checking is sound for a base ℛ iff m(ℛ) ≤ 2. h(𝓔(L)) ≤ 2 is sufficient for every base over L. It is not necessary for a given base (§9.14.4).

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | S is minimal in conflict at (c, t) iff all of S apply at (c, t) and the indexed family (Eᵣ(c))_{r∈S} is minimal with empty intersection | §2.3 (d); effective applicability enters only through which rules apply at (c, t) |
| 2 | a minimal indexed family has no repeated member and at most h members | a repeated member could be dropped; the definition of h over finite subfamilies |
| 3 | so \|S\| ≤ h: (a) | 1, 2 |
| 4 | the minimal conflicting sets at (c, t) are exactly the subsets of at most h applying rules that are minimal in conflict there; each set's region is the set of points where it is minimal | 3; the region is computed per set (§2.3 (d)) |
| 5 | so testing all subsets of at most h rules, point by point, reproduces the report: (b) | 4 |
| 6 | if h > 2, there is a minimal finite family F ⊆ 𝓔(L) with empty intersection and \|F\| ≥ 3. Realise it by rules applying at one point. Their set is in conflict there, and every proper subset, every pair included, is satisfiable there: (c) | definition of h; realisability |
| 7 | for one base, the minimal sets have at most m(ℛ) members, so pairs suffice iff m(ℛ) ≤ 2: (d) | 4, with m(ℛ) for h |

Applicability plays no part in the bound. A pairwise test run **point by point** ("is there a point where both apply and their effects clash?") is all that h ≤ 2 needs.

The same bound holds for the UNKNOWN report under three-valued applicability (§2.8), when the context is fully described: a set possibly in conflict at a point has an effect family with empty intersection, and step 2 bounds its minimal subfamilies. An unknown context is open (§9.14.9).

**R2. The product lemma (Ded).** **Premise: X is the full product ∏ₐ Xₐ of the attribute domains, with no coupling constraint, and every effect set is a product ∏ₐ Eₐ** (each rule constrains each attribute separately). Then h(𝓔) ≤ maxₐ h(𝓔ₐ), where 𝓔ₐ is the class of attribute-a factors.

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⋂ᵢ ∏ₐ Eᵢₐ = ∏ₐ ⋂ᵢ Eᵢₐ | set algebra on a full product |
| 2 | F minimal with empty intersection ⇒ for some a, ⋂_{i∈F} Eᵢₐ = ∅ | a product is empty iff a factor is |
| 3 | some G ⊆ F with \|G\| ≤ h(𝓔ₐ) has ⋂_{i∈G} Eᵢₐ = ∅ | definition of h(𝓔ₐ); empty factors are allowed |
| 4 | then ⋂_{i∈G} ∏ Eᵢ = ∅, so G = F, by the minimality of F | 1, 3 |
| 5 | \|F\| ≤ maxₐ h(𝓔ₐ) ∎ | 4 |

- **The premise is needed.** On X = "exactly one of p, q, r" (a coupled space), the three Boolean literals MUST_NOT p, MUST_NOT q, MUST_NOT r are pairwise satisfiable and jointly not, so h ≥ 3, although each factor class has h = 2 (check 6).
- *Model:* on 60 random pairs of factor classes over a full product, h(products) ≤ max h(factors) in every case (check 2; products searched up to 4 members).

**R3. The book's rule classes (Ded for each h; Int for each example's place in the book).**

**h belongs to the union of the forms admitted on an attribute.** The rows cannot be combined freely: two h = 2 forms on one attribute can together have unbounded h (the row "threshold with forbidden value").

| Form on one attribute (example in the book) | Effect sets | h | Pairs suffice? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **required value** ("use TLS 1.3" vs "use TLS 1.2 only", §2.3 (b), read as the version in use) | single values {a} | **2** | **yes** |
| **Boolean obligation or prohibition** (a required trace link per document kind, §2.0, SD L103–108; a design check's prohibition of a pattern, obligation zero violations, §2.9; both Int) | {true} or {false}, on **independent** Booleans | **2** | **yes** |
| **threshold** (a count with an obligation "≤ m", "≥ m", or between) | intervals of an ordered domain | **2** (Helly in dimension 1; proof below) | **yes** |
| **required value with threshold** | singletons are intervals, so the union is the interval class | **2** | **yes** |
| **forbidden value** ("internal production APIs must not [use OAuth2]", §2.3, RM L154–162) | complements of single values, on a k-valued attribute | **k** (the k complements form a minimal family); on an unbounded domain no finite family is empty (h = 0) | only if k ≤ 2 or the domain is unbounded |
| **required or forbidden value** (the OAuth2 pair is one of each) | {a} or its complement | **max(2, k)**; **2** on an unbounded domain (singletons and cofinite sets) | only if k ≤ 2 or unbounded |
| **threshold with forbidden value** on one ordered attribute (e.g. "violations ≤ 3" and "violations ≠ 1"; also one rule's conjunction "x ≤ 3 ∧ x ≠ 1") | intervals and point complements | **n** on n values; **unbounded** on an unbounded discrete domain: x ≤ m − 1, x ≠ 0, …, x ≠ m − 1 is minimal with m + 1 members, for every m (check 6) | **no** |

| Form across attributes | Effect sets | h | Pairs suffice? |
|---|---|---|---|

If the count is itself the only outcome attribute, it is a threshold (h = 2) |

**How an example is placed depends on its representation (Int).**
- **The OAuth2 pair:** if the attribute is the Boolean "uses OAuth2", the language has h = 2. If it is "authentication method" with k ≥ 3 values and the language admits forbidding each, h = k.
- **"TLS 1.2 only":** read as a required value of the version in use, or as Boolean literals over the versions in use on a full product. Either reading gives h = 2.

*Proofs of the entries.*
- **Required value:** two different single values are disjoint. Any family containing one value a and otherwise only supersets of {a} has the common point a. So an empty family contains two different singletons: h = 2.
- **Forbidden value:** a family of complements of values is empty iff it forbids every value of the domain. That needs all k members, and drops no member: h = k. On an unbounded domain it never empties.
- **Required or forbidden:** an empty family contains two different singletons, {a} together with its complement, or all k complements.
- **Interval** (finite families in a total order): if every two intervals meet, choose pᵢⱼ = pⱼᵢ ∈ Iᵢ ∩ Iⱼ (and pᵢᵢ ∈ Iᵢ). Let lᵢ = minⱼ pᵢⱼ ∈ Iᵢ and x = maxᵢ lᵢ = l_k. Then lᵢ ≤ x ≤ p_kᵢ for every i, with lᵢ, p_kᵢ ∈ Iᵢ, so x ∈ Iᵢ by convexity: h = 2.
- **Threshold with forbidden value:** {x ≤ m − 1, x ≠ 0, …, x ≠ m − 1} has empty intersection. Dropping the threshold leaves m, m + 1, …; dropping x ≠ j leaves j. So it is minimal, with m + 1 members.
- **Allowed set:** contains every complement of a value: h = k by R4.
- **Cardinality:** the owner constraint and any three of the four rules leave a common point: drop MUST_NOT owner(a) and a is the owner; drop the owner constraint and no one owns. All four together leave none.
- **Linear, over the reals:** Helly's theorem gives d + 1. Tightness for d = 2: x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, x + y ≤ −1 are satisfiable two at a time, with witnesses (0, 0), (0, −1) and (−1, 0); the three sum to 0 ≤ −1, a contradiction (check 5).

*Model (check 1; exhaustive on small domains unless a cap is given, in which case the value is a lower bound and the cited theorem the upper bound):*
- required value h = 2 for k = 2, 3, 4; forbidden value h = k; both together h = max(2, k); allowed set h = k;
- interval h = 2 on 0..5; forbidden range h = 6 on 0..5;
- boxes h = 2 on a 4 × 4 grid (cap 4); partial assignments h = 2 on 3 Boolean attributes (cap 4);
- clauses h = 4 = |X| on 2 Boolean attributes; lattice half-planes h = 4 = 2² on a 4 × 4 grid (cap 5).

*Check 6:* required values with thresholds h = 2; the threshold-with-forbidden-value family minimal for m = 1..5; the coupled and cardinality examples minimal.

*End to end (check 3):* on 300 random rule sets per class, with context-dependent applicability, an **independent pairs-only analyser** (single rules and pairs tested directly at each point) was compared with the set-wise report.
- It agrees for required values and threshold intervals (h = 2).
- It misses a conflict on 136 and 52 rule sets for forbidden values on 3 values (h = 3) and for clauses (h = 4).
- No minimal conflicting set exceeded h.

**R4. A finite outcome space bounds h by its size, and the bound is tight (Ded).** If X is finite, h(𝓔) ≤ |X|.
- *Proof.* Let F be minimal with empty intersection. For each A ∈ F, the other members have a common point x_A, and x_A ∉ A. For A ≠ B, x_A ∈ B but x_B ∉ B, so x_A ≠ x_B. Hence |F| ≤ |X|.
- *Tight:* the complements of the single points of X form a minimal family of size |X|. On n Boolean attributes each is a clause of length n, so general propositional effects reach h = 2ⁿ.
- *Model:* check 4 (random classes; tightness for |X| = 2..5).

**R5. What follows for the book (Ded).**
- **(a) Cost (§2.3 (d)).** For a language with Helly number h, the set-wise report needs at most C(n, 1) + … + C(n, min(h, n)) subset tests. Each test is §2.3 (d)'s ∃(c, t) ∀x question on at most h rules. The **number** of tests is polynomial in n when h is a fixed constant small against n. For h = 2 it is that of pairs.
- **(b) Partitioning (§2.6).** "Comparing every pair whose keys intersect is not enough" (§2.6) holds in general. **For a language with h ≤ 2 it is enough**, provided single rules and pairs are tested point by point (R1 (b)).
- **(c) h belongs to the language, attribute by attribute, on a full product.**
  - Admitting the **forbidden-value form** on a k-valued attribute, with k ≥ 3, raises that attribute's h to k. A single forbidden-value rule does not raise the language's h by itself: it takes all k complements, or the form admitted for every value.
  - Admitting thresholds and forbidden values together on one attribute makes h unbounded on an unbounded domain.
  - *Example:* three placement roots, as one 3-valued attribute, and three independent obligations "must not be placed under root i". They are pairwise satisfiable and jointly not. Placement by precedence is a different mechanism (§2.8.4a).
- **(d) A test the analyser can run.** Pairs-only checking is sound **for every base over L iff h(𝓔(L)) ≤ 2** (the converse under realisability), and sound for a given base iff m(ℛ) ≤ 2 (R1 (d)).
  - **Languages with h = 2, on a full product X:**
    - on each attribute, either required values with thresholds, or required and forbidden values when the attribute is Boolean or unbounded;
    - Boolean literals on independent Booleans;
    - across attributes, products of these.
  - **Not h = 2:**
    - thresholds with forbidden values on one attribute;
    - forbidden values or allowed sets on three or more values;
    - cardinality over members;
    - linear inequalities across attributes;
    - disjunctions;
    - any coupling between attributes.

## 9.14.4 The limit of the converse, and the strongest counterexamples

**The limit of the converse.** R1 (c) is about languages. A base can be pair-sound although its language has h > 2. For example, its forbidden-value rules on one attribute may never apply at one point, because each lies in a different region. For a base the exact quantity is m(ℛ) (R1 (d)). This is a limit of the converse, not a counterexample to R1.

**The strongest counterexamples, to version 1's classification** (R1 (a) itself was not refuted):
- **union on one attribute:** thresholds with forbidden values, minimal sets of every size (turn 5, B1-01, B2-01);
- **a coupled outcome space:** three Boolean literals under "exactly one" (B1-02, B2-02);
- **cardinality over members:** a minimal set of four (B1-05).

Each one refuted a version-1 sentence, now corrected in R3 and R5.

## 9.14.5 Rival formulations

- **Relational consistency** (*Ext:* Dechter and van Beek 1997). This is the closest established notion, a relation-based local consistency on subsets of constraints rather than on variables. It characterises, for several constraint properties, when a given level of local consistency guarantees global consistency (their abstract). R1 instead bounds, from the effect class alone, the size of the subsets that need to be tested.
- **Local consistency in constraint networks** (k-consistency: every consistent assignment to k − 1 variables extends to any k-th; *Ext:* Freuder 1978, 1982; Mackworth 1977). It concerns extending partial assignments of **variables**; R1 concerns **subsets of constraints** over all variables. The two properties are defined on different objects, and neither is claimed here to imply the other. §2.3 (d) cites the local–global gap as the established fact behind its counterexample.
- **Algebraic characterisation.** Jeavons, Cohen and Cooper 1998: their abstract states that a simple algebraic property characterises the constraint types for which strong k-consistency ensures global consistency, for k > 2. It is a benchmark for the kind of result; its property is not cited here because it was not read.
- **Minimal unsatisfiable subsets** (*Ext:* Liffiton and Sakallah 2008) enumerate the minimal conflicting sets of a given base. R1 bounds their size in advance, from the language alone.

## 9.14.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

**Internally:**
- R1–R4 are mathematics about effect sets.
- R1 (b) guarantees that a pairs-only analyser misses nothing **if** the admitted language has h ≤ 2 on the outcome space it is evaluated in.

**About the world, or the implementation:**
- **The outcome space is what the analyser assumes.** R2 and every "product" row need a full product with no coupling. A constraint between attributes (schema, background logic, "exactly one owner") must be in the checked set as a rule, or part of X, and it counts toward h (S1539's MCS-2 has three members only because of such a bridge, §9.14.7).
- **The representation is fixed.** Whether an example is a k-valued attribute or k Booleans, a count or per-member memberships, changes h (R3).
- **Each rule's effect is correctly formalised** into its form; a free-text effect is in no form (§2.3 (a); E6).
- **Domains are what the analyser assumes.** A value added later (§2.8, "adding a value") changes h for forbidden values: with k values h = k, adding a value raises it to k + 1. An unbounded domain gives different answers from a finite one (R3).
- **An UNKNOWN effect** (undecidable, time-out) is reported, not dropped (§2.3 (e)).
