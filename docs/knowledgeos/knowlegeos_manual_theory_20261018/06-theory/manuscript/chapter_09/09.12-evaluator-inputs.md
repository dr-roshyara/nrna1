# 9.12 The evaluator's inputs: the bearer fork (F37) and the readings of A1

*Theory turn 3 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: what Q1's reference evaluator (CE, §9.10) takes as input under each reading of F37 and each reading of A1, and what its answer then means. One corpus source checked at its anchor: S0053 L16–48 (logged). **Status: version 2, rewritten at the turn-3 settlement after two reviews (`review/TURN3-INPUTS/`); the first version is in git (`dc07032fd`).***

## 9.12.1 The research question

**What must CE take as input? Does the answer change with the bearer of the dimensions (F37: (A) one bearer, the instance; (B) a bearer per dimension), with the granularity of what acts name (documents or claims), or with the reading of A1 (de jure, recognized, as form, as fact)? Where the inputs stay the same, do the parameters, the meaning or the falsifier change?**

**What would disprove the answer:** two settings claimed to need the same inputs, and a record on which a correct evaluator under one must answer differently from the other on those inputs; or a setting claimed to need an extra input, and a correct evaluator under it that never consults it.

**Words.** CE computes **current authority**: derived from **standing** together with validity and the ending policy P, and able to lapse with time while standing is untouched (§1.14.1-12; §9.10 Q1). A1's readings speak of standing (§1.6). The bearer question (F37) is a question about standing's bearer (F6); current authority follows from it.

*Why I14 (Src).* The estate's one examined approval act adopts its object **item by item** ("In force as the approved Stage-1 design", nine items), states what it does **not** adopt ("INV-ATTR-4 / 5 / 6 remain CANDIDATE / NOT ADOPTED … not adopted by this act"), and names its object across **four commits** (rev 3, rev 2, rev 1, "as repaired by" a fourth) [S0053 L16–48]. §5.6 already drew the consequence: an act's negative scope is part of what it covers. So "an adoption of d adopts every claim d carries at one version v" would **over-adopt** here.

## 9.12.3 The bearer fork (F37) and the granularity of acts

**Act-object granularity is its own parameter, not a consequence of F37.** §1.14.1 lets an act's object be a claim, a grant or an act. Whether the estate's acts name **documents** or **claims** is a matter of practice. It is **Hyp** that they name documents (§5.6 C3 is one case; S0053 lists items, so it names claims inside documents). When acts name claims directly, neither reading needs a carry relation inside CE. When acts name documents, **both** readings need it.

**Inputs common to both readings, when acts name documents:** claim identity independent of carriers (§1.10); carrier@version; the carry relation Carries(d, c, v); and the act's coverage (I14), which **overrides** the carry relation where the act lists or excludes items. The pinned version may be replaced by detection or prohibition of change (§1.16.6: a reference that carries meaning is pinned, live with detection, or live with prohibition).

**Where the readings really differ: endings.** Adoption is mapped from carriers to claims by the carry relation and I14. Endings (revocation, supersession, annulment of an act on a carrier) need a further rule under each reading.
- **Under (B) (a bearer per dimension; standing belongs to the claim, given F6 splits governance status):** a **lifting rule** saying how an ending on a carrier reaches the claims it carries. Without it, CE cannot apply condition 3 ("no effective revocation … of c") to a revocation of a document.
- **Under (A) (the instance):** either authority is per instance, or an **aggregation rule** combines instance answers into a claim's.

**Result B1, restated (Ded, given the premises named).** `[CON-0190 · provisional]` The two readings are **not** the same information in different form. *Counterexample (two records, REPORT-A A-01):*
- R1: x1 adopts d1; x2 adopts d2; both carry c; then y revokes d2.
- R2: x2 adopts d2; y revokes d2; then x1 adopts d1.

Per instance, both records give the same answers: (c, d1) authoritative, (c, d2) revoked. Under (B), with the lifting rule "a revocation of a carrier revokes the claims it carries", condition 3 gives c = F in R1 (y comes after x1) and T in R2 (x1 comes after y). No function of the instance answers separates R1 from R2, so no aggregation rule under (A) reproduces (B) with that lifting rule. (B)'s answer depends on the order of acts across carriers, which per-instance answers discard. They coincide on records with adoption acts only, and on claims with one carrier.

**A claim with no carrier.** (B) can answer for it if the act cites it directly (it needs an identifier). (A) cannot, unless it admits a null carrier (c, ⊥), the repair §3.1 names.

**Neither reading supplies covers(B, x)** (I10): under (B) canon scope has no bearer yet (§3.1); under (A) it is the instance's; in both the mapping between grant scope and canon scope is undefined (§1.6 refinement 3).

## 9.12.4 The readings of A1: inputs, parameters, meaning

The readings (§1.6): **de jure**, a norm the organisation adopts (standing *ought* to change only by recorded acts); **recognized**, a stipulated definition (a change of standing is *recognized* only if a recorded act effects it); **as form**, a property of the record (every standing change in the record is labelled by a recorded act); **as fact**, an empirical claim (standing changes only by acts that occurred). The Principal's decision (DB-1, 2026-10-20), accepted as the brief recommended: **"A norm (a stipulated definition of recognized standing), with named readings; the empirical claim kept apart as a hypothesis."** The brief's implication: "CE evaluates A1-recognized from the record" (DECISION-BRIEFS-S0100, DB-1).

**Result B2, restated: the readings fix parameters (Ded; definitional).**
- **Record R0: no adoption act for c.** Under A1 recognized, nothing recorded effects a standing change, so c is **not recognized: F**. §1.14.1's answer specification under C1 = "records represent authority" gives **"cannot determine" (U)**: "missing evidence is never reported as missing authority". So one function cannot be recognized authority under both values of C1.
- So **recognized and de jure fix C1 = "records constitute authority"**, and with it κ = constitutive (an unknown citation is not a recorded basis: F). **As fact** is compatible with C1 = "represent" and κ = evidential, which treat a record as evidence of a possibly unrecorded act.
- **As form** is a condition on the input domain (every standing change labelled) and, to be **checked**, needs an input CE does not otherwise read: the stored statuses, compared with the acts. CE derives standing and never reads a stored status.
- **Same kinds of records, different parameters:** de jure, recognized and as form read I1–I14; the readings differ in C1 and κ, in meaning, and in what can falsify them (§9.12.5).

**Result B3, restated: what A1 as fact adds (Ded).**
- **Integrity is common to every reading** (I13). A failed signature or a hash mismatch shows that an entry is not authentic or intact (§9.9 T2 points 1, 3); it does not show that the act did not occur. Under every reading, such an entry is not a recorded act (§1.12-2).
- **Occurrence evidence is read only under as fact.** *Counterexample:* an adoption act x, intact and authorized, and a further intact entry by x's named actor stating that the actor did not perform x (a denial recorded as evidence, not as an annulment act). No annulment follows. CE under A1 recognized answers **T**: no act ended x. An evaluator under A1 as fact must answer **U** (x is not shown to have occurred), **not F**: the conflict shows that some entry is unfaithful, not which one. Under as fact, the **marked absences** of O3 are also read: a marked absence of a revocation of c moves the answer off T.
- **Two parameters as fact needs and CE lacks:** a rule for occurrence evidence whose own reliability is undetermined (without it, anyone can make any act U by recording a conflicting entry; the analogue of υ and of §1.14.1's safeguard 6); and the choice between reading **recorded** refutations and reading the **verification material** itself (keys, key binding, key revocation; §9.9 T2 point 1). The two differ on an unflagged forgery.
- *Removed from the first version:* "an act whose author was not entitled" is an authorization failure, which CE already computes, not an occurrence refutation.
- *Rival framing:* occurrence refutations as a **domain exclusion** (records containing them are outside CE's domain), as with as form. Not taken: Q1 must answer for every record in its scope (§9.10.2), and a domain exclusion would make it partial.
- *Depends on I11:* if the actor–holder link is defined by key binding, some integrity and authenticity failures fall into authorized(x) inside CE (turn 4).

**The consequence of DB-1, restated.** By DB-1 and its brief's implication, **Q1's reference evaluator is CE read under A1 recognized**, with C1 = "records constitute authority" and κ = constitutive (B2). Whether the kernel provides CE is turn 7's question (§9.7.4; §9.10). Since de jure and recognized give the same function (B2), the choice between them does not change CE; de jure adds that a deviation in practice is a breach. **C1 is an alternative §1.14.1 kept open; DB-1 fixes it by implication.** Returned to the Principal at turn 3, it was **decided at turn 4 (2026-10-20): records constitute authority within the kernel**; whether a recorded act really occurred stays an assumption about the world (§9.9). Decision record, addendum 2026-10-20T17:25:51Z.

**Rival: standing conferred by practice** (§1.6's rival). An evaluator for it would read usage evidence instead of adoption acts. Excluded for Q1 by DB-1; kept as the rival the empirical claim must beat.

## 9.12.6 Strongest counterexamples, rival, falsifiers

- **Against "the bearer readings are the same information":** R1/R2 (B1).
- **Against "the readings never change anything":** R0 (B2); the conflicting-entries record (B3).
- **Rival structure (adopted here, from REPORT-A):** inputs in four groups: (i) the act's coverage, (ii) the rule from carriers to claims for endings, (iii) the integrity of entries, for every reading, (iv) occurrence evidence, for as fact only. F37 changes (ii); A1 as fact changes (iv); the readings fix C1.
- **Falsifier of B1 as restated:** a lifting rule under (B) for which some aggregation rule under (A) gives the same answers on every record. *Searched:* R1/R2 refute it for the lifting rule above; other lifting rules (for example "a revocation of a carrier revokes only the adoptions of that carrier") make (B) agree with (A) under disjunction, at the cost that a revoked document no longer ends a claim it carries elsewhere. So the readings coincide exactly when endings act per carrier, not per claim.
