# 9.12 The evaluator's inputs: the bearer fork (F37) and the readings of A1

*Theory turn 3 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: what the evaluator of current authority (CE, Q1's reference definition, §9.10) takes as input under each reading of F37 and each reading of A1, and what its answer then means. No corpus source was read; the turn works from §1.14.1's definition, §9.7's input table, §3.1 (F37) and §1.6 (A1's readings). **Status: draft for review by two reviewers.***

## 9.12.1 The research question

**What must CE take as input, and does the answer to that change with the bearer of the dimensions (F37: (A) one bearer, the instance; (B) a bearer per dimension) or with the reading of A1 (de jure, recognized, as form, as fact)? Where it does not change the inputs, does it change what the answer means, or what can falsify it?**

**What would disprove the answer:** two readings claimed to need the same inputs, and a record on which the two give different answers from those inputs; or a reading claimed to need an extra input, and a correct evaluator under that reading that never consults it.

## 9.12.2 CE's inputs, as §1.14.1 defines them

currently-authoritative(c, t) is a disjunction over **adoption acts x of c** with t_x ≤ t and over bases B of x, of four conditions: x authorized through B and not annulled by t; t inside the validity c has by x; no effective revocation or supersession of c by a later act; B intact at t under the ending policy P (§1.14.1). authorized(x) is T for a root act, and otherwise depends on the grants x cites, their act windows, covers(B, x) and the reason condition.

| # | Input | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | the item c | the disjunction over adoption acts of c |
| I2 | acts: kind, actor, act time t_x, record position s_x, cited bases B, reason, the items concerned | authorized; the four conditions |
| I3 | grants: holder, issuer, scope, period, endings, delegation parent | act windows; covers(B, x) |
| I4 | revocations, supersessions, annulments, and their own authorization | conditions 1, 3, 4 |
| I5 | root acts (O4) | authorized(x) = T for a root act |
| I6 | the parameters θ = (ρ, κ, χ, ω, σ, π, υ, P, ι) at version u | throughout |
| I7 | the cut s and the valid time t | ρ; conditions 2–4 |
| I8 | an order of acts (F26) | conditions 3, 4; ρ |
| I9 | covers(B, x): whether a grant's scope covers the act's subject | condition (ii) of authorized — **undefined** (§1.14.1 defect 5) |
| I10 | the actor–holder link | authorized — **undefined** (defect 5, 7) |

## 9.12.3 The bearer fork (F37): what c is, and what each reading adds

**What an adoption act targets.** In the estate's practice, acts accept or adopt **documents and deliverables** (§5.6 C3: an acceptance covers "the delivered version only, pinned"; S0053 lists the approved items by commit). A document is "a bundle of claims, governed claim by claim" (Ch. 0 §0.3).

**Under (B), a bearer per dimension:** authority (the standing part of governance status, if F6 splits it) belongs to the **claim**. So c is a claim, and an act that adopts a document must be mapped to the claims it bears on.
- *Extra input (Ded):* **the carry relation** Carries(d, c, v): carrier d, at version v, carries claim c, with **the version pinned by the act** (§1.16.6). Without it, CE cannot tell which claims an adoption of d adopted. Without the pinned version, a later edit of d would change what an earlier act adopted, against §5.6 C3.
- *A claim with no carrier* is still an item; it has authority only through an act that cites it directly.

**Under (A), one bearer, the instance (claim, carrier):** c is an instance; an act that adopts d adopts the instances (c, d) for the claims d carries at the pinned version.
- *Extra input (Ded):* **instance identity** and the claim-of relation (which claim an instance is of). And a rule for one claim with several instances: (A) is well-typed only if a claim's own values are equal across its instances, its authority among them if F6 splits governance status (§3.1). CE computed per instance can give two instances of one claim different answers, for example when one carrier is adopted and another is not. So (A) needs either **an aggregation rule** (how instance answers combine into the claim's), or the statement that authority is per instance and the equality constraint does not cover it.
- *A claim with no carrier* has no instance, so CE has no item to answer about.

**Result B1 (Ded).** `[CON-0190 · provisional]` On carried claims the two readings need the same information in different form: (B)'s carry relation with pinned versions yields (A)'s instances, and (A)'s instances with the claim-of relation yield (B)'s claims. They differ in two places: (A) needs an aggregation rule (or per-instance authority); (B) can answer for a claim no carrier carries, (A) cannot. **Neither reading supplies covers(B, x)** (I9): under (B) canon scope has no bearer yet (§3.1), under (A) it belongs to the instance, and in both the mapping between grant scope and canon scope is undefined (§1.6 refinement 3). So F37 does not close CE's scope gap.

## 9.12.4 The readings of A1: same function, different meaning — with one exception

The four readings (§1.6): **de jure**, a norm (authority *ought* to change only by recorded acts); **recognized**, a stipulated definition (a change is *recognized* only if a recorded act effects it); **as form**, a property of the record (every authority change in the record is labelled by an act); **as fact**, an empirical claim (authority changes only by acts that occurred). The Principal decided (DB-1, 2026-10-20): **A1 is a norm, with named readings; the empirical claim is kept as a separate hypothesis.**

**Result B2 (Ded).** CE as §1.14.1 defines it reads only I1–I10: acts, grants, endings, roots, parameters, the cut, the order. None of the readings de jure, recognized and as form changes these inputs:
- **recognized:** CE's answer *is* recognized authority as of the cut. The definition is this reading written out.
- **de jure:** the same function; the reading adds that a practice which changes authority without an act is a **breach** of the norm. CE does not see the breach (no act is recorded); a breach is shown by evidence outside CE.
- **as form:** a condition on CE's **input domain**: records in which every authority change is labelled. Under it, CE never meets an unlabelled change; C6's origin rule (§9.1) keeps the condition.

So under these three, the readings differ in **what the answer means and what can falsify it**, not in what CE reads.

**Result B3 (Ded): A1 as fact changes the inputs.** Under A1 as fact, an act that did not occur gives no authority. The record can refute an act's occurrence from inside: a hash mismatch, a contradiction between entries, an act whose author was not entitled to it (§9.9 T2 point 3). Such refutations are **evidence entries** (§9.10 O2), not acts.
- *Counterexample to "same inputs":* an adoption act x, authorized, with a recorded refutation e of x's occurrence (its signature fails) and no annulment act. CE as defined (reading recognized) answers **T**: no act has annulled x. An evaluator under A1 as fact must answer **F or U**: x is shown not to have occurred. So CE_fact takes the refutation entries as an input that CE does not read.
- *What it does not give:* CE_fact still cannot establish that an unrefuted act occurred (§9.9 T2 point 2). It differs from CE only where the record refutes an act.

**Consequence of DB-1.** The kernel's evaluator is **CE under A1 recognized (as a norm)**. CE_fact belongs to the empirical claim kept apart: it is what one would compute to test A1 as fact against the record. The two are kept apart: a refutation entry is a reason **for an act** (an annulment) under the norm, and a direct input under the empirical reading.

**Rival: authority conferred by practice** (§1.6's rival). An evaluator for it would read usage evidence (what the organisation acts on) instead of adoption acts. Excluded for the kernel by DB-1 (A1 is the norm); kept as the rival the empirical claim must beat.

## 9.12.5 The combined table

| | inputs beyond I1–I10 | what the answer means | what can falsify a T |
|---|---|---|---|
| (B) × recognized (the kernel's choice under DB-1, if F37 is decided for (B)) | the carry relation with pinned versions | recognized authority of a claim, as of the cut | a recorded act CE missed or misread |
| (A) × recognized | instance identity, claim-of, an aggregation rule | recognized authority of an instance (and of a claim, by the rule) | as above |
| either × de jure | as recognized | as recognized, plus: a deviation in practice is a breach | as above; a breach does not falsify the answer |
| either × as form | as recognized; domain restricted to fully labelled records | as recognized | an unlabelled change in the record (excluded by C6) |
| either × as fact | as recognized, **plus the refutation entries** (O2) | authority that rests on acts not shown not to have occurred | a refutation of an act the answer rests on; an unrecorded act (W: completeness) |

## 9.12.6 Strongest counterexample, rival, falsifier

- **Strongest counterexample** to "the A1 readings never change CE's inputs": the refuted-act record of B3.
- **Rival formulation:** CE_fact as the kernel's evaluator, with refutations as automatic voiders. It is stronger against forged records, but it makes evidence entries change authority without an act, against A1 as the norm the Principal adopted, and against §1.6's "two keys" (evidence earns, governance grants).
- **Falsifier of B1:** a carried claim whose authority (B) and (A) answer differently from the same records, with an aggregation rule that is the identity on single-instance claims. *Not found:* where a claim has one carrier, the readings coincide.

## 9.12.7 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

Internally, CE under A1 recognized reads only the record and the parameters; its answer is a property of the record. Under A1 as fact, the answer is about the world and rests on occurrence and completeness (turn 6); refutations give it a partial check from inside. Under both, the carry relation (B) or instance identity (A) is an internal input that **someone must record**: the act that adopts a document must pin the version and, under (B), the claims it bears on.
