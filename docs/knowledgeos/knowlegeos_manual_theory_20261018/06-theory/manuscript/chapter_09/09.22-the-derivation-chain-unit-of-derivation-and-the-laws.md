## 9.22.1 In plain language

1. **Where the laws come from: three sources, not one.**
   - Laws about what any **state** may be follow from the record's type (its kinds, fields and D's clauses).
   - Laws about **answers** follow from Req's reference definitions over that record.
   - Laws that forbid **steps** (append only; nothing silently removed; nothing the kernel makes up itself) follow from **neither**. They are axioms the kernel type takes as given. Req checks answers state by state, and adding a mechanism never makes Req fail. So Req, read as the book formalises it, cannot forbid any step between two valid states.
   - That is the commission's "not derived from any candidate unit" result, for the step laws.
2. **One law, end to end.** "No authority without a recorded act" follows from the definition of current authority. A T answer always rests on a recorded adoption and on **some** recorded chain of grants back to a recorded root. Read so, it is a law about answers, forced by Req, with no use of the origin premise.
3. **"Primitive" has five senses:** a concept, a field, a mechanism, an axiom and a requirement. Each has its own test.
4. **A question for the Principal.** Req's own text says "the same call gets the same answer", which read across states would also forbid silent removal. The book's formal reading checks each state alone. Which reading Req has decides whether the append-only laws are forced or are axioms.

## 9.22.4 One law, end to end (deliverable 2)

**Law 1 (§9.11 row 1, I1): no authority without a recorded act, in answer form** *(restated: A-03, A-04, A-17, B F1, F13, C-03, C-17)*.

**Statement (Ded, definitional, as §9.20 Th1 is):** for every record R in D, item c, scope x, valid time t and cut s, if CE*(c, x, t; R≤s) = T, then R≤s contains:
- (i) an adoption y of c with t_y ≤ t and authorized(y) = T; and
- (ii) **some** basis b cited by y with authorized(b) = T, from which a chain of recorded bases, each cited by the next and each with value T, ends at a recorded root.

| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CE*(c, x, t; R≤s) is current-authority read off the least fixed point of R≤s | §9.13 L135 |
| 2 | current-authority is F if c has no adoption act on record | §9.13 L129: "F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute; defect 3)" |
| 3 | otherwise it is a disjunction over adoptions y of c with t_y ≤ t, each conjunct requiring authorized(y) (and every cited grant **intact** at t, not authorized) | §9.13 L129; L87 |
| 4 | so T needs some adoption y of c, t_y ≤ t, in R≤s, with authorized(y) = T | 2, 3; under strong Kleene a disjunction is T only through a T disjunct |
| 5 | **Lemma (stage induction):** KK is reached from ⊥, where roots are T and every other atom is U, in finitely many applications of Φ. At stage 0 only roots are T. A non-root becomes T only through basis(g, x) = T for some cited g that is a root or a grant with a(g) = T one stage earlier. A basis not in the evaluated cut is F | §9.13 L118–123 (L123: absent from the cut gives F); R1 (L170) |
| 6 | so authorized(y) = T has a chain of T bases, each cited and in R≤s, of strictly decreasing stage, ending at a root | 5 |
| 7 | the statement (i)–(ii) | 4, 6 |

- **Premises:** C1 = constitute; CE* on D for Θ_f; the operator Φ. **No use of the origin premise.** D's earlier-only citation rule is not needed either, since the stage induction terminates by itself (C).
- **What it does not give:** that the recorded entries came from outside (the forge still satisfies it, §9.1 L91), which is law 2's origin part; or that the acts occurred (law 3, W).
- **The universal form is false:** "for **each** grant y cites, a chain" fails. A, B and C each exhibit a record in D where y cites a second grant g2 with no basis, a(g2) = F, and still CE* = T. Authorization is disjunctive over bases (χ, §9.13 L83, L120), and currency needs cited grants intact, not authorized (L129). C's random probe found 119 universal-form failures and 0 existential-form failures in 2,464 T answers (form only).
- **Falsifier:** a record in D with CE* = T and no adoption of c at or before t, or with an adoption none of whose T bases has a chain of T cited bases to a root.
- **A check, form only:** A ran 3,000 random records (2,096 T answers) and found no violation of the corrected form.
- **Forced by Req (Ded):** Q1's reference is CE* (§9.10 Q1), and observer correctness holds on every valid state. Outside D, Q1 refuses with a reason, so it gives no T.
- **Its class (consequence, A-16):** in this form law 1 is an **O law** (observer correctness), not §9.11's S law. This answers §9.20.8 L156's open implication: under 𝒮 = R the S form holds by construction, and the origin share is law 2's.

## 9.22.6 Consequences and the question (deliverable 4; stated, not settled)

- **A question for the Principal (A-01, A step 1 "Corollary C").** Req's call identity is read two ways in the book: state-free in Req's text ("same call iff (k, x, t, s, u) are equal"; "the same call gets the same answer", §9.10 L36–37, L47), and state by state in the type (C6 result 7 (a)).
  > *Is Req's call identity (a) **state-free**, so that stability across states is part of Req, or (b) **per state**, as C6 result 7 (a) formalises it?*

  - **No recommendation:** the evidence fixes what follows from each reading.
