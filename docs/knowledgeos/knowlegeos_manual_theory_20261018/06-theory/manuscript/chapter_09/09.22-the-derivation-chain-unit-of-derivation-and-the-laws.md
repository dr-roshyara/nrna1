## 9.22.1 In plain language

1. **What the laws are derived from.** Not from the list of candidate primitive concepts, whose definitions are mostly open. Not from mechanisms either: they change no answer and no step (§9.21). The book's laws follow from **the record's type**, its entry kinds, fields and D's and W's clauses, together with **Req's reference definitions over that record**. The first gives the laws about states and steps; the second gives the laws about answers.
2. **"Primitive" has four senses:** a candidate concept, a field or kind of the record, a mechanism, and an axiom (a law not derivable from the others). Each has its own test, and they should not be mixed.
3. **One law, end to end.** "No authority without a recorded act" follows from the definition of current authority. A T answer always rests on a recorded adoption and a recorded chain of grants back to a recorded root, in the cut asked about.
4. **The laws marked.**
   - Some laws are **forced by Req**: any system that answers Req's questions correctly must keep them.
   - Some are **compatible**: kernel laws Req does not force, several of them kept only because D's decided clauses require them.
   - The rest are **independent**: assumptions about the world, about the implementation, or rules for applications.

## 9.22.4 One law, end to end (deliverable 2)

**Law 1 (§9.11 row 1, I1; the shape of A1): no authority without a recorded act.**
**Statement over the unit (Ded):** for every record R in D, item c, scope x, valid time t and cut s: if CE*(c, x, t; R≤s) = T, then R≤s contains (i) an adoption y of c with t_y ≤ t whose authorization is T, and (ii) for each grant y cites, a chain of recorded grants each based on the one before, ending in a recorded root, all in R≤s.

| Step | Claim | Justification | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CE*(c, x, t; R≤s) is current-authority read off the fixed point of R≤s | §9.13 L135 | Src (definition) |
| 2 | current-authority is **F** if c has no adoption act on record | §9.13 L129 ("F if c has no adoption act on record (C1 = constitute)") | Src (definition; C1 decided) |
| 3 | otherwise it is a disjunction over adoptions y of c with t_y ≤ t, each conjunct requiring authorized(y) and every cited grant of y intact at t | §9.13 L129 | Src |
| 4 | so T needs some adoption y of c, t_y ≤ t, in R≤s, with authorized(y) = T | 2, 3; strong Kleene: a disjunction is T only if a disjunct is T | Ded |
| 5 | authorized(y) = T in the least fixed point is reached from the bottom (all U, roots T) by finitely many applications of Φ | §9.13.4 R1 (L170); R2 (L188: "every T and F of CE* is forced") | Ded (cited) |
| 6 | Φ makes an atom T only through a basis clause that cites a recorded grant or root whose own atom is T (basis(g, x), §9.13 L122) | §9.13 L120–125 | Src |
| 7 | so authorized(y) = T has a finite chain of recorded grants to a recorded root, each cited, all in R≤s | 5, 6; citations name earlier entries present (§9.13 L63) | Ded |
| 8 | the conclusion (i)–(ii) | 4, 7 | Ded |

- **Premises:** C1 = constitute; D (citations of earlier entries only); CE* on D for Θ_f (§9.13.4 R1). **No use of the origin premise.** The law says the act is **recorded**; that it **occurred** is law 3, a world assumption (A1-as-fact).
- **Falsifier (DC-4):** a record in D with CE*(c, x, t; R≤s) = T and no adoption of c at or before t in R≤s, or with an adoption whose authorization chain leaves R≤s. *Model check (form only):* to be run on `ce_model.py`'s random records at review if a reviewer wishes; not run for this draft.
- **Not trivial (DC-8):** with an empty set of kinds there is no adoption, so every CE* is F, and the law is vacuous there. It says something only because D admits adoptions and grants.
- **Forced by Req (Ded):** Q1's reference is CE* (§9.10 Q1). So any system whose Q1 observer equals its reference on every valid state answers T only under (i)–(ii).

## 9.22.6 Consequences (deliverable 4; stated, not settled)

- **Deliverable 1 (the primitives).** The derivation's base is the record theory (U-k, U-r), not the concept list. A concept survives as a **name for the kinds and fields it covers** (P-concept over P-field), with the removal models of §9.19.5 as its test. *Not decided:* whether deliverable 1 is restated as "the record's kinds and fields, with the concept each realises"; the primitive set stays not finalised.
- **T9-03 (primitive minimality).** Three minimality notions are now apart:
  - mechanism minimality (P-mechanism; G inert, §9.21);
  - field minimality (P-field: a field is needed iff a removal model exists);
  - axiom minimality (P-axiom: a law independent of the others).

  Primitive minimality in RESEARCH-PURPOSE's sense ("not derivable from other primitives") is the P-axiom or P-field notion, not the mechanism one. No independence model is given here.
- **The chain itself.** One law is derived end to end (law 1). The other F laws have their grounds named, not proof tables; writing them out is further work, not done here.
- **Not changed:**
  - RESEARCH-PURPOSE.md;
  - the primitive set;
  - T9-21 and Ch. 10;
  - §9.11's classes, which this turn's marks add to and do not replace.
