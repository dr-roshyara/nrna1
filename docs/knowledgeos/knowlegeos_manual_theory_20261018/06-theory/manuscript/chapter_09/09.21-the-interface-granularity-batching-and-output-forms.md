## 9.21.1 In plain language

1. **What a mechanism is, at the grain the book can derive.** Since the Principal cut O1 per act kind (U6), "one mechanism per input kind" and "one mechanism per act kind" are the same pool.
2. **Composites and batching.** The kernel type says that each input carries one act or none (§9.1 C6). A mechanism that appends several acts in one step from one input breaks that condition, so composites that do so are excluded, and so is batching of several acts into one input. Even if batching were allowed, no answer and no reachable record would change: guards are checked entry by entry, and every prefix of a record can be evaluated.
3. **Guards.** Whether a guard is counted as a separate component or as part of its mechanism changes no answer. It matters only for how mechanism minimality is counted, which is T9-03's question.
4. **The outputs.**
   - Q2's output is fixed by its own text: it returns the entries themselves. Its scope is fixed by the record's kinds: only adoptions and revocations (and evidence entries) name an item, so grants and roots are never "about" an item.
   - The text does not fix Q1's "acts the answer rests on" or Q3's "with the grant". They may be the acts' identities or the whole entries. The two forms differ in exactly two fields, the recorder and the authentication status, because an entry's identity is everything else. That difference decides whether the record is a minimal state for Req's core. It is one question for the Principal.

## 9.21.3 Step 2: the five objects

**(1) G-kind and G-act coincide under U6 (Ded).**
| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | G-kind has one appender per input kind of Req | §9.19.4 L160 |
| 2 | Req's input kinds for O1 are the act kinds | U6 (a): "O1 is one requirement per act kind" (§9.19.9) |
| 3 | so G-kind's appenders for O1 are one per act kind, which is G-act | 1, 2; §9.19.4 L161 ("G-act … refines G-kind's O1") |
| 4 | for O2–O4 the two agree already | §9.19.4 L160–161: G-act refines only O1 |

*Scope:* the act kinds **admitted to D** and W's kinds; O1's five kinds outside D (T9-21) have no clauses, so their appenders are not defined. DC-2 is not met: G-kind and G-act are the same pool.

**(2) Composites that append several acts in one step are excluded by the kernel type (Ded, from §9.1 C6 as written).**
| Step | Claim | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | each input carries an act performed outside the kernel, or none | §9.1 C6 L93, the input-origin condition: "the kernel receives acts; it does not make them" |
| 2 | each step consumes one input | §9.1 C6 (m : 𝒮 × In → 𝒮 ∪ {⊥}; §9.19.4 L159) |
| 3 | a composite appending act entries of two kinds in one step appends at least one act its input did not carry | a composite is "a sequence of other mechanisms in one step" (§9.19.4 L162); 1, 2 |
| 4 | so it breaks the input-origin condition | 1, 3 |

*Not excluded:* a step that appends one act entry and changes an earlier entry in place (a removal act and its tombstone, §9.13.2) is one act. *Scope:* C6 as written. **DC-3:** met only by a mechanism whose single input carries several acts, which contradicts step 1's wording; if "an act" were read as "acts", (2) fails. That reading is not in the book (Int).

**(3) Batching: excluded as written, and unobservable (Ded).**
- **Excluded:** batching is "whether one input may carry several entries" (§9.19.4 L164). Several act entries in one input contradict step 1 of (2), so batching of acts is excluded by C6 as written (Ded, same premise).
- **Unobservable, whatever is decided (Ded):**
  1. the append guard is checked at each new entry against the record before it (§9.13 L63, L73);
  2. every prefix of a record in D is evaluable (§9.13 L69);
  3. Q1–Q4 at a cut read a prefix of the record order (§9.10 Q1, Q2; H2);
  4. so a record reached by a batch that is accepted entry by entry is reached by the same entries appended singly, and every core observer gives the same answer at every cut. An all-or-nothing batch can only refuse more, so its reachable records are a subset.
  - **DC-4 is not met.** Batching bears only on the **pool** (T5's composite c_E), not on Req's observers or the reachable records.

**(4) G-guard against guarded mechanisms: no observer separates them (Int); the choice belongs to mechanism minimality (T9-03).**
- A guarded mechanism is a mechanism made partial by its guard (§9.19.4 L159); G-guard counts the guard as a separate member of the pool. On the same guards in force, both reach the same records and give the same replies (§9.1 C6 result 8: replies are conditions on steps or refusals, not on the pool's partition).
- They differ only in what a minimal kernel counts: under G-guard, H-down and H-mono need not hold (T6). Which guards are kernel and which are applications' is decided per law (F22 (C + O); §9.16 R6), not by granularity.
- **Narrowed:** the choice changes no answer; it is needed only if mechanism minimality is to be stated (T9-03).

**(5) The output forms.**

| Output | Form | Class | Grounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q2's output** | **whole entries** | **derived (Src)** | §9.10 Q2: "the entries about k up to s (acts, links, evidence, marked absences, recorded losses), in record order"; reference definition "the record itself, cut at s" |
| **Q2's scope** | **items, as named by an entry's item field; grants and roots are not items** | **derived (Src; Ded) under D's admitted kinds** | §9.10 Q2: "items with a governed identifier"; of D's kinds only `adopt` and `revoke` carry an item field (`ce_model.py` FIELDS; §9.13.2), and an evidence entry its object (§9.18.4). A grant or a root names a scope, a holder, a window, not an item; its identity is content-derived (§9.16 L93), not governed. **DC-6:** met if registration (outside D, T9-21) gives grants or roots governed identifiers; so the result is conditional on T9-21 |
| **Q1's "acts the answer rests on"** | identities **or** whole entries | **narrowed** | §9.10 L34 says "acts"; neither the purpose (§9.10 Q1: "valid"; "whose recorded act gives the item its authority", which the actor answers, a field of identity) nor the reference definition fixes the form. **R2 does not decide it** (Ded, checked): the merge joins copies with one identity into one entry whose recorder is the set of recorders and whose status is the weakest (§9.16 R2), so a whole-entry output of a merged record is a function of the copies, not of the merge order |
| **Q3's "found (with the grant)"** | the grant's identity **or** the whole entry | **narrowed** | §9.10 Q3; the observable consequence names "a recorded, unended grant", not its fields. As for Q1 |

**What separates the two forms of Q1 and Q3 (Ded, from §9.16 L93).** An entry's identity is "kind and D's required fields other than the recorder and the status" (plus, for grants since v7, the owner and recording application, §9.16 L93 N1). So the two forms differ in exactly: (i) the **recorder** and the **authentication status**; (ii) **content beyond the required fields**. Act times are required fields, so they are in the identity: identity outputs **do** separate §9.20.5's M6 pair (act times of roots and grants), **if** the root and the grant are among the acts Q1's answer rests on (Int: CE*'s support of a value runs through the cited grant to the root, §9.13).

## 9.21.4 Minimality (§9.20.5), restated per form

Let **F_id** be (Q1 and Q3 as identities; Q2 whole entries, items only) and **F_e** be (Q1 and Q3 as whole entries; Q2 as derived).

| Pair of records (differing only in …) | Separated under F_id? | Under F_e? |
|---|---|---|
| act time of a root or of a grant (M6) | **yes**, through Q1's act list (Int, as above) | yes |
| recorder or status of an adoption or revocation | **yes**, through Q2 (whole entries about k); and under signed-as-other through the mark (M5b) | yes |
| recorder or status of a grant, root, annulment, ending, consumption, transfer, designation or removal | **no** core output returns it; no clause reads it outside the signed-as-other mark (§9.20.5; B P2–P4, C P2 at the T9-02 turn) | **yes**, through Q1's or Q3's whole entries, **if** the entry is among the acts returned |
| content beyond the required fields of an item entry | **yes**, through Q2 | yes |

- **Under F_id (Ded):** the record is **not** a minimal state for Req's core. The coarsest output-respecting quotient identifies records that differ only in the recorder and status of entries of the non-item kinds, outside the signed-as-other mark. D requires those fields by decision (the Principal's v6 decision on recorder and status), not by Req.
- **Under F_e (Ded, for the tested fields; no general minimality claim):** for each tested field, a record pair that differs only in it is separated, **provided** the entry is among the acts some output returns. An entry no output ever returns (for example a grant never cited by any adoption) still leaves a pair unseparated (Int; DC-7 to be checked at review).
- *So the deciding question is exactly (i): must Req's core outputs convey who recorded an act and with what authentication status, for the acts that are not about an item?*

## 9.21.5 T9-22, and the consequences

- **T9-22: reduced to one decision (Q1's and Q3's form).** Q2's form and scope are derived. The question:
  > *Do Q1's "acts the answer rests on" and Q3's "with the grant" return (a) the acts' identities, or (b) the whole entries, including recorder and authentication status?* Options as above. Under (a), the record is not a minimal state for Req's core, and the recorder and status of non-item entries are required by D's decision only. Under (b), §9.20.5's tested pairs are separated where the entries are returned. **No recommendation:** the evidence fixes what follows from each option, not the choice (as for U2).
- **T9-03 (consequence, not settled).** Over §9.20's 𝒮 with G = G-kind = G-act and composites excluded (C6), §9.19.4's one direction applies: mechanism-minimal kernels consist of underivable mechanisms. The two notions can still come apart through guards counted as components (G-guard), which this turn leaves to T9-03.
- **T9-04 (consequence, not settled).** T5's route to non-uniqueness (adding a composite under batching) is closed by C6. The remaining routes are alternative appenders of one kind (ALT) and guards as components (T6). If a pool has exactly one appender per admitted kind and no guard counted separately, T10's condition holds and the minimal kernel is unique **within that pool**. Whether KnowledgeOS's pool has alternative appenders is not fixed.
- **Not changed:** U3 stays decided (b) for whatever this turn does not derive. No primitive is fixed, and no minimal kernel of KnowledgeOS is stated. RESEARCH-PURPOSE.md is not edited. T9-21 is not started.
