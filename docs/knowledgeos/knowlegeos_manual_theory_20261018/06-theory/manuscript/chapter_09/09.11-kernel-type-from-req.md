# 9.11 The kernel type, derived from Req: which laws need states, steps, histories, or more

*Theory turn 2 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: whether the corrected kernel type (§9.1 C6, CON-0183) is enough for Req (§9.10, CON-0188) and for the laws the book tests, and which laws need steps or whole histories. No corpus source was read; the turn classifies laws already in the book. **Status: draft for review by two reviewers.***

## 9.11.1 The research question

**Given Req, can the type (𝒮, X, M, G, I_S, I_T, I_R, observers, Req) of §9.1 C6 express every law the book states for kernel candidates, and every Req item's observable consequence? For each law: does it constrain states, single steps, whole histories, something that must eventually happen, several runs at once, or the world outside the record?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a law the book requires of the kernel that falls in a class the type cannot express, while the text says the type is enough; or a law placed in a class it does not belong to (shown by a counterexample run or pair of runs).

## 9.11.2 The classes (definitions; Ext where marked)

A run is a sequence of states s₀ x₁ s₁ x₂ s₂ …; a property is a set of runs; a hyperproperty is a set of sets of runs.
- **S, state invariant:** a condition on every reachable state (C6's I_S).
- **T, transition invariant:** a condition on every step (s, x, s′) (C6's I_T).
- **H, history safety:** a condition on finite prefixes of runs, violated by some finite prefix (C6's I_R). *Ext:* a safety property, "nothing bad happens" (Lamport 1977; Alpern and Schneider 1985).
- **O, observer correctness:** obs_q = ref_q on every valid state (C6 result 7 (a)). It is an S condition on the observers.
- **R, reachability:** some run reaches a state where p holds (EF p; Clarke and Emerson 1981). Req's inputs to accept are of this class.
- **L, liveness:** a condition no finite prefix can violate; something must eventually happen, for example "whenever p, eventually q" (*Ext:* Alpern and Schneider 1985: every property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property).
- **AE, recoverability:** from every reachable state some continuation reaches p (AG EF p, a branching-time condition, Clarke and Emerson 1981): neither a safety nor a liveness property of single runs.
- **HP, hyperproperty:** a condition on several runs at once, for example that two runs that agree on their inputs agree on their answers (*Ext:* Clarkson and Schneider 2010; noninterference is their standard example).
- **W, world assumption:** a relation between the record and events outside it; no condition on the system's own runs expresses it.

**What C6 expresses, by construction:** S, T, H (directly as I_R, and as T over a history variable when the conditions of C6 result 5 hold), O (result 7), R (Req's inputs). **What it does not:** L, AE, HP, W. This is read off the type's definition (Ded); the question is which laws fall in the last four classes.

## 9.11.4 Results

**R1. Whole histories: what needs them, and how C6 carries them (Ded).** `[CON-0189 · provisional]` Laws 7, 8, 9 and 16 refer to what happened before the current state: removal only by a recorded act, no act consumed twice, the answer at a past cut, every entry about an item. Each is expressible in C6 **if the state contains the record**, an append-only sequence of entries (H1′ as restated: removal only by a recorded entry). *Proof sketch:* (i) with the record in the state, a past cut is a prefix of the current record, so Q1 at a past cut and Q2 are observers of the current state (class O); (ii) uniqueness is the transition rule "a consuming step needs its act absent from the record's consumed acts and adds it", and the record never shrinks except by a recorded entry, which keeps the act's earlier consumption visible, so C6 result 5's three conditions hold with the record as the history variable: empty at s₀ (or holding only the root act, O4), never losing a consumption, read and written by every act-consuming mechanism (O1 and O4, the inputs that carry acts). So **"whole histories" are needed, and they are carried by making the record part of 𝒮**, not by a new kind of invariant. *Ext:* this is the history-variable construction of Abadi and Lamport (1991) with the record as the variable; established.

**R2. C6 is not enough for four classes of law (Ded, read off the type).**
- **L, liveness.** Law 18 (§5.6) and the ESCALATE part of law 19. *Counterexample:* a system that accepts the held act and never applies or returns it meets every S, T, H and O condition, and meets Req's inputs (it **can** apply it: EF holds); it violates "eventually applied or returned". So C6 cannot require it.
- **AE, recoverability.** Law 20 (F33). *Counterexample:* a system with one workflow state reachable only by an act and left only by a fact from outside the record meets Req (that state is reachable from s₀) and violates F33. C6's Req is EF from s₀; F33 needs EF from every reachable state.
- **HP, hyperproperties.** Laws 21, 22, 24. *Counterexample for 21:* two applications, each with its own record and the same observer, receive the same acts in different orders under F26(b) (a partial order); each run is correct for its own record, and the two answers differ. No condition on single runs excludes that; K5 is a condition on the pair. **But:** if every application reads **one** record (or a merge with a fixed order) through the same observer, agreement follows from law 15 (Ded): one state, one observer, one answer. So K5 is an HP **only if** records are separate; with a shared record it is O. That is exactly F22 and K4's question (§9.6.6a), now stated in the type's terms.
- **W, the world.** Laws 3, 14 (its W part), 25: outside every internal type (turn 6).

**R3. What the type must add, or what must leave the kernel (a fork, not a decision).** For each law in L, AE or HP, one of two:
- (a) **extend the type:** a liveness component I_L under a fairness assumption (for L), a recoverability condition AG EF (for AE), a condition over pairs of runs (for HP); or
- (b) **place the law in applications:** the kernel only makes the outcome reachable (R) and correct when asked (O); that it actually happens, or that separate systems agree, is the applications' obligation.

This is the per-law question F36 already asks ("which laws are kernel invariants?"). **F36 is extended here** to the laws of R2: §5.6's held act, ESCALATE, F33, K5 across separate records, non-disclosure, U across applications. *Rival:* treat every L, AE and HP law as application by stipulation, so that the kernel is a safety-plus-observer system only. *Falsifier of the rival:* a law of these classes that applications cannot keep without the kernel (for K5: separate records with no shared order, which F26 shows cannot agree without one).

**R4. C3 under the extended view (Ded).** C3's existence by one check holds for S, T, the H laws carried by the record (R1), O (C6 result 7 (a)) and R. It does not extend to L, AE or HP: a liveness or recoverability obligation is not preserved by adding mechanisms (a new mechanism can open a path that avoids the obligation, or reaches a state from which the required state is no longer reachable), and an HP compares systems, not mechanism sets. If R3 (a) is chosen for any law, existence for that law needs its own argument.

## 9.11.5 Counterexamples, rival and falsifier, in one place

- **Strongest counterexample to "C6 is enough":** §5.6's held act (law 18): every safety and reachability condition holds, and the act is never applied. It shows the class L is needed, or the law leaves the kernel.
- **Rival formulation:** the kernel as a safety-plus-observer system (S, T, H via the record, O, R), with every L, AE and HP law placed in applications (R3 (b) for all). It is consistent and simpler. Its cost is K5 across separate records, which applications cannot keep by themselves (R2).
- **Falsifier of R1:** a law that needs the past and cannot be stated over a record that keeps every entry or its recorded loss. None was found among the 25; laws that need the world (W) are not such laws, since no record carries them.

## 9.11.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

The type guarantees S, T, H (via the record), O and R internally. L needs a fairness assumption **about the system's scheduler**, which is internal to the system but not to the type as defined; AE is a property of the system's reachable graph; HP needs either a shared record or a condition across systems. W stays outside: occurrence, completeness (H5), key binding, timing (turn 6).

## 9.11.8 What this does not decide

Which laws of L, AE and HP the kernel must keep (F36, extended; turn 7). Whether records are shared or separate (F22, K4). Fairness assumptions. The content of 𝒮 beyond "contains the record" (F13, T9-02).
