# 9.11 The kernel type, derived from Req: which laws need states, steps, histories, or more

*Theory turn 2 of the critical-path chain (Rulebook v1.3 §12b, entry 59), 2026-10-20. Deliverable: whether the corrected kernel type (§9.1 C6, CON-0183) is enough for Req (§9.10, CON-0188) and for the laws the book tests, and which laws need steps or whole histories. No corpus source was read; the turn classifies laws already in the book. **Status: version 2, rewritten at the turn-2 settlement after two reviews (`review/TURN2-TYPE/`); the first version is in git (`5ebf1659a`).***

## 9.11.1 The research question

**Given Req, can the type of §9.1 C6 express every law the book states for kernel candidates, and every Req item's observable consequence? For each law: does it constrain states, single steps, whole histories, something that must eventually happen, a branching structure, or the world outside the record? And for which of them does C3's existence by one check still hold?**

**What would disprove the answer:** a law placed in a class it does not belong to (shown by a run, or a structure, that the class admits and the law excludes, or the reverse); a claim that C6 cannot express a law that C6's definition admits.

**Selection of laws.** The laws the book states, in the sections that Req (§9.10) and the kernel tests (§9.1–§9.9) rest on: §0.5, §1.6, §1.7, §1.13, §1.14.2, §1.16, §5.5 C3, §5.6, §8.10 C10, §9.4, §9.5, §9.6, §9.7, §9.8, §9.9, §9.10; found by reading those sections and by a search for "eventually", "always able", "across applications", "whoever asks", "must reach" over the chapters. A law outside these sections is not classified here.

## 9.11.2 The classes

A run is a sequence s₀ x₁ s₁ x₂ s₂ … (every step is on an input: C6 has no input-free step). A property is a set of runs.
- **S, state invariant:** every reachable state satisfies it (C6's I_S).
- **T, transition invariant:** every step (s, x, s′) satisfies it (C6's I_T). *Homonym:* "transition invariant" in the termination literature (Podelski and Rybalchenko 2004: a relation containing the transitive closure of the transition relation) is a different object; here it is a condition on single steps.
- **H, history safety:** a property violated by some finite prefix (*Ext:* safety, Lamport 1977; Alpern and Schneider 1985).
- **L, liveness:** a property no finite prefix violates; "whenever p, eventually q" is the common form (Alpern and Schneider 1985).
- **O, observer and reply correctness:** an observer, or the reply to an input, equals its reference definition on every valid state (C6 results 7 (a), 8).
- **R, reachability:** some run from s₀ reaches p (EF p; Clarke and Emerson 1981). Req's inputs.
- **AE, recoverability:** from every reachable state some continuation reaches p (AG EF p). **Not a property of runs:** no set of runs characterises it; it is a property of the branching structure.
- **HP, hyperproperty:** a condition on several **alternative runs of one system** at once, for example noninterference (*Ext:* Clarkson and Schneider 2010). Not a property of runs either. A condition across **components of one composed system** is not an HP: it is a property of the composed system's runs.
- **W, world assumption:** a relation between the record and events outside it.
- **E, environment assumption:** a condition on which inputs arrive and when. Because every step of C6 is on an input, "eventually q" for the system requires some input to arrive: an assumption about the environment (*Ext:* assumption–guarantee specification, Abadi and Lamport 1993). E is internal to no type of the system alone.

**What C6 expresses (Ded, from its definition).** S, T, O directly; H and L as I_R, since C6 does not restrict I_R to safety (§9.1 C6); R as Req's inputs. **It does not express** AE and HP, which are not sets of runs, nor W and E, which are about what lies outside the system. **Where C3 holds:** for S, T, O and R; for H only inside C6 result 5's class (a history variable empty at s₀, never shrinking, read and written by every act-consuming mechanism); not with guards (result 6); not for L, which is not preserved under adding mechanisms (R4).

## 9.11.4 Results

**R1. Whole histories: what needs them, and the premises under which the record carries them (Ded).** `[CON-0189 · provisional]` Laws 7, 8, 9b, 16 and 24 refer to what happened before. They are expressible over a state that contains the record. *Proof, step by step:*
1. Let 𝒮 contain the record ρ (a sequence of entries).
2. Law 7 and 8 are T conditions on how ρ changes. Law 9a is a T condition on presenting an act already in ρ.
3. For law 9b take as history variable the set H(ρ) of act identities consumed by ρ's entries. C6 result 5 needs three conditions:
   - (i) H empty at s₀: holds if ρ is empty at s₀. The root act (O4) is an input, not part of s₀.
   - (ii) H never shrinks: **does not follow from H1′ as restated.** A recorded loss that drops a consumed act's identity lets the same act be consumed again (a run: consume a, remove its entry with a loss, consume a again; H1′ holds, uniqueness fails; the same case as clear_used, C6 result 5). **Premise added (P-loss):** a loss entry keeps the identities of the acts the removed entries consumed (§9.10 O3). With P-loss, H(ρ) never shrinks.
   - (iii) H read and written by every act-consuming mechanism: holds for the kernel's own mechanisms; for laws 9b **and 24** it holds only if **one** record is read and written by every act-consuming mechanism, kernel and application. That is the shared serialization point of §9.6.4 step 3, open under K4 and F22.
4. With (i)–(iii), P_R for uniqueness follows from P_T (C6 result 5).
5. For law 16: a past cut is **not** a prefix of the current record after a recorded loss (§9.8: recoverable "except for losses that are themselves on record"). So "Q1 at a past cut" is an observer of the current state only up to recorded losses; §9.10 Q1 now says what the answer is when a loss intervened. Replay also needs §9.8 Lemma D's premises: H2, H3, F26 settled for the cut, and CE defined.

*Result:* the laws that refer to the past are expressible over a state that contains the record, **if** P-loss holds and, for uniqueness across applications, one record is shared. *Ext:* auxiliary variables in proofs (Owicki and Gries 1976) and history variables for refinement (Abadi and Lamport 1991); **established**. What is the book's own is only the record as the variable, with P-loss.

**R2. What C6 cannot express, and what it can (Ded, with counterexamples).**
- **AE: law 20a.** *Structure:* a state w reachable from s₀ with no enabled step on any input; EF from s₀ of Req's effects still holds; AG EF fails. Not a set of runs, so not in C6.
- **HP: the second part of law 22.** Two runs that differ only in what a reader may not see must give that reader the same replies. Not a set of runs; reducible by self-composition to a safety property of a product system (Terauchi and Aiken 2005), which a type over the product can express.
- **W and E:** laws 3, 10b, 11b, 14, 20b, 25, 28, and the "eventually" of law 18 (ii).
- **Expressible after all** (corrections of the first version): liveness (law 18 (ii)) is expressible as I_R; laws 21 and 24 are properties of the composed system's states and runs, not hyperproperties. What fails for them is **C3** (L) or **enforceability without a shared point** (24), not expressibility.
- **Expressible only with replies:** laws 4b, 19 (REPORT), 31: C6 result 8, added at this turn.

**R3. For each law outside C3's reach, three options (a fork per law; F36).**
- (a) **extend the type or the method:** liveness kept as I_R with its own existence argument; AE as a condition on the structure; HP by self-composition;
- (b) **place the law in applications or in the environment:** the kernel makes the outcome reachable (R) and answers correctly when asked (O); that inputs arrive, or that a delivery reaches someone, is the environment's or the applications' part;
- (c) **impose a sufficient condition the type can carry:** for law 18, the T reading (i) (bounded response 0); for law 22, unwinding-style state and step conditions that imply non-disclosure; for law 20a, an enabledness condition on Req's inputs. Its cost: it is stronger than the law and may reject correct systems.

**F36 is restated accordingly:** for each of laws 9b/24 (uniqueness, kernel or across applications), 18, 20a, 22: (a), (b) or (c). *Rival:* the composed-system, input-driven reading, in which every law needing "eventually" is an environment assumption and only law 22's second part is a hyperproperty; the kernel then needs no liveness extension. *Falsifier of the rival:* a law the book requires of the kernel that needs eventual action by the kernel **without any input** (a timer step). None was found in the selected sections; §1.16.8's time-triggered conditions are evaluated at read time (O).

**R4. C3's reach (Ded, with counterexamples).**
- **S, T, O, R:** C3 holds (C6 results 3, 7 (a), 8), under the closed reading.
- **H:** only inside result 5's class, with R1's premises.
- **Guards:** C3 does not apply (result 6).
- **L:** two mechanisms each satisfying "eventually done" alone can loop together (m₁: a → b, b → done; m₂: b → a, a → done; their union admits a → b → a → …). So L is not closed under adding mechanisms, without a fairness assumption; under a fairness assumption the case changes. C3 does not hold for L in general.
- **AE:** adding a trap mechanism can break it; adding an exit can repair it. It is neither monotone nor antitone in M, so neither direction of C3's argument applies.

## 9.11.5 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

The type guarantees, internally: S, T, O (observers and replies), R, and H inside result 5's class with P-loss. **Assumptions about the environment (E):** that inputs arrive (for every "eventually"); for law 20b, that the outside act arrives. **Assumptions about the world (W):** occurrence, completeness (H5), soundness (H4), timing, key binding, that the recorded evaluator version is the one that ran, K1. **Assumptions about the implementation:** that each step of the type is executed atomically and serially (laws 12, 26; §1.16.7).

## 9.11.7 What this does not decide

Which option (a), (b) or (c) each law of R3 takes (F36; turn 7). Whether records are shared (F22, K4). Whether Req's inputs carry the enabledness reading (§9.10.2). The content of 𝒮 beyond "contains the record" (F13, T9-02). Whether a law requiring deletion can require deleting consumed-act identities (P-loss).
