**R2. Records merged without a serialization point (Ded; model checks 18–18d).** `[CON-0194 · provisional]`

*Setting (corrected at turn 7, D1-01, D2-01, D1-02, D2-02).*
- R₁, …, Rₙ are the records of several applications.
- **Each application's record is in D.** Each holds its own entries and **copies of every entry they cite**; otherwise its own Q1 refuses it (a dangling citation, §9.13.2).
- **Identity:** an act's identity is derived from the fields CE* reads: kind and D's required fields other than the recorder and the status, which are not part of it (§9.6 K2, content-derived).
- **The merge rule:** the union of the records, joining copies with one identity.
  - the fields CE* reads must agree, or the merge is **refused** (conflicting copies);
  - a tombstone wins over a full copy;
  - the status is the **weakest** of the copies (unsigned < signed-as-other < signed-as-actor), so a mark is never lost by merging;
  - the recorder is the set of recorders.
- R is the merged entries in some order in which every citation points backward: a **linear extension** of the citation graph.

*v3 (the Principal after turn 7: "acts at the same time are unordered; the answer is U").* The evaluator no longer reads positions (§9.13 v8), so no merge order can change any value. For marks this needed one repair (C19, T1-06, T2-04): two removals of one entry in two applications made the "removal not authorized" mark depend on the merge order. removed_by is now a set, and the mark needs every removal act of the entry to be non-T (§9.13 v9; check 18e: both orders give the same marks). Crowded act times: 0 of 400 histories differ by merge order under unordered ties (model check 18c, v9). The race below gives **U in both orders**, with the reason "same act time: the order of x and e is undetermined" (check 18, v9). The claims (b)–(c) and the costs below are kept as the analysis of the tie rules that were rejected.

*Claims.*
- **(a)** Each record in D is closed under citation, so the union is closed under citation. Its citation graph is acyclic: each citation points to an entry that already existed, which is a sub-relation of happened-before, a strict partial order (EXT-084). That an application cites only what it has received is the premise. So a linear extension exists, and the merged record is in D unless the merge is refused.
- **(b)** If ties of act time are broken by a function of the entries' content (F26 (c)), CE*'s **values and marks** are the same for every linear extension.
- **(c)** If ties are broken by position, as §9.13.2 does now, two linear extensions can give different values.
- **(d)** A cut across records takes one prefix per record. Because each record is in D and D is closed under prefixes, every such cut is in D: **the cut condition is automatic**. It is the citation analogue of a consistent cut (EXT-085): every consistent cut is closed under citation, not conversely (D1-10).
  - *Version 1* stated (d) as "in D iff closed under citation". That has content only for **fragments**, records that hold only an application's own entries and are not themselves in D (model check 18: 128 of 600 such cuts refused). Version 1's claim that the causal premise is "checkable" holds only for records in D, where each application's own append guard checks it.
  - Under a merge, Q1's cut parameter must name the entries, one prefix per record, not a position (R1, the H2 row).

*Proof of (b).*
1. CE* reads the order of entries only through the key of each time test and D's citation rule (§9.13.2–3; model `Record.key`, `in_D`; confirmed independently at turn 7, A2, D1, D2). The marks read the support (§9.13.3 v7), which reads no order.
2. With ties broken by content, the key is a function of the entries.
3. Under the merge rule, identities are unique in the union, and every linear extension passes D's citation rule.
4. So Φ, its least fixed point (= WF on D, §9.13 R1, R3), current-authority and the support are the same on every linear extension. ∎

*The race (c), a countermodel.*
- Application 1 records A's grant x to B, under A's grant g, at act time 3.
- Application 2 records P's ending of g, also at act time 3.
- Neither entry cites the other. B later adopts c under x.
- With ties by position, CE*(c, 6) is T in one merge order and F in the other.
- With ties by identity it is **the same in both**. Which value it takes depends on whether x's identity sorts below the ending's (corrected, D2-05, D1-08: version 1 said "F in both").

*What the condition costs (version 2; moot under v3's unordered ties, kept for the record).*
- A content tie rule decides a same-time race by what the entries are, not by who recorded first.
  - Ties by identity are deterministic but arbitrary.
  - Whoever controls a free content field can steer a content-derived identity and so win a race (D1-08).
  - A recorder who asserts act times can backdate (law 10b is W; backdating is admitted in act time, §9.13.10; D2-05).
- "Endings and annulments first at equal act time" is a content rule and the conservative one. But it is a preorder: two endings can still tie, so it needs a completion, e.g. by identity (D1-08).
- **The corpus's own rival.** S0969's "false global ordering" experiment, inside the range cited for the partial order: "Two distributed events have no causal ordering." **R3. Which laws need coordination across applications (Ded sketch; corrected at turn 7, D2-03, D1-05).**
- **The test (Ext: invariant confluence, Bailis et al. 2014, EXT-087).** The paper's framework "determines whether an application requires coordination" (its abstract). Its definition is about merging valid states that are reachable from a common valid ancestor under the given transactions. That definition is recalled by a C19 reviewer from the paper, not checked here (T1-19); version 2's gloss, "any two states", was stronger than it. Version 1's "only laws that bound a count" was a special case, not shown complete.
- **Merge-safe (no coordination), by class:**
  - laws stated per entry or over an entry and what it cites: laws 1, 2, 4a, 5, 6, 13 and D's per-entry rules;
  - O1's idempotence (9a), under the identity and merge rule;
  - per-record transition laws (7, 8, 10a), which hold within each record.

  The union keeps every entry with its citations, so these are preserved (Ded sketch; not checked law by law).
- **Not merge-safe** (merging two valid states can violate them):
  - **count bounds:**
    - single use, law 9b/24 (consumption admitted to D after turn 7);
    - law 26, at most one established assessment per claim;
    - **role exclusivity**, "the invariant must be role-specific" [S0991 L1145], against "For others, multiple authorized decision-makers are intentional." [S0991 L1143];
    - §9.6 K4's "one mutation owner per shared work item";
  - **once-only handling:** law 18 and law 31 ("applied once", "returned to its author") when several applications hold the held act;
  - **acting under an ended authority**, the race of R2 (c). After the merge, CE* gives F (detection). Preventing the act needs the ending to reach every writer first (S0991's authorization freshness).
  - **D's unique identifiers.** These are kept by the identity derivation: two copies of one act are one entry, and copies that disagree are refused. That is detection, not prevention.
- **Law 21 (§9.7 K5)** is a uniqueness of **answers**, not of entries. It holds for a given cut with equal entries (R2 (b)). Answering "now" across applications without coordination or a sealed cut is not possible in general. CE* is **not monotone** in the record: an annulment recorded later turns T into F. *Ext:* CALM (Hellerstein and Alvaro 2020, EXT-088): only monotone programs have coordination-free consistent outcomes. So "evaluation needs none" holds for a **given cut** only (D1-05).
- **Recording needs none** for merge-safe laws. **Plural bases are no violation**: in CE*, authorized(x) is a disjunction over x's bases.
- **For each law that is not merge-safe:**
  - *detection (K4-d)* needs no shared point. A deterministic aggregate decides the conflict on the merged record, independently of the merge order by R2 (b). Examples: §9.4 C2's weakest outcome, or the earliest consumption by key. Its limit is that an act on the losing side may already have happened in the world. With recorder-asserted act times, "earliest" can also be manipulated by backdating (D2-05);
  - *prevention (K4-p)* needs coordination. Either one serialization point per constraint, shared by its writers (§9.6.4 step 2; §1.16.7), or **ownership**: one owner of the right per key, with coordination only to transfer it (escrow, bounded counters; *Ext:* Balegas et al. 2015, EXT-089; D1-04).
- **What the corpus gives here: the question's form, not its answer.**
  - It prefers prevention for exclusive authority: "Where governance defines exclusive authority, the distributed system must prevent simultaneous conflicting authoritative states." [S0991 L2058–2063].
  - On the bound itself, the corpus's verification says: "Serializability (§92.10), idempotency (§92.31), and the authority-uniqueness cardinality bound (§92.45) are correctly stated." [S1519 L234]. The same line grades the invariants themselves as names: "0/11 invariants are definitions" [S1519 L234] (quoted whole at turn 7, D1-12, D2-09).
  - S0915 leaves high-impact decisions under partition to policy, "This should be governed by policy." [S0915 L1315]. That is an analogue for K4-p against K4-d, not a statement about it (D2-09).

- **The line that carries the classification (Int; the Professor's reading, which reproduces the Principal's list; restated at C19, T1-09, T2-07).** For the first two laws, the effect the law protects lies **outside the record** (delivered work, a delivery to the author). For the last three it is **standing**. Acts done under an exclusive role or an ended authority can also have effects in the world; under this reading those are legitimacy matters (§9.15), not effects the law protects. **This line is not derived from the criterion.** Under a uniform reading (count every world effect, or only standing effects) the five laws do not split two and three. So the split rests on the Principal's reading, which this table records, with the reasons above as the Professor's gloss. S0991 is a counter-source for role exclusivity.
- **Consequence for the guards (Int, from the decision; not Ded, C19, T2-07).**
  - Single use: the append guard refuses a consumption while another consumption of the grant is not F (§9.13 v9, `append_guard`). It prevents the entry; it prevents the use only under §9.15 L9 (record before use). Across applications, this needs one coordination point per grant (§9.15 K4).
  - Held acts: the same, per held act; not modelled, since §5.6's held act is not a kind of D.
  - The three detected laws need no shared point. Each needs its deterministic aggregate in the contract (R4 (iii)).

**R4. What K4 decides for F22 (Ded; a sufficient condition, not an "iff"; corrected at turn 7, D1-04, D2-04).**
- **Where K4 belongs.**
  - In Req, as a property of the guards of R3's laws that are not merge-safe (§9.10 O1);
  - typed in §9.11 (law 24: H over the composed run, enforceable only with coordination);
  - at the trust boundary as §9.15 K4, needed only where such a law is a kernel law under prevention.
  - Not in the evaluator for a given cut.
- **(i) Sufficient for a kernel coordination point.** If some kernel law is not merge-safe and its violations must be prevented, the kernel must provide coordination for it: a shared serialization point, or ownership with transfer. No application can provide it for writers independent of it (§9.6.4 step 3, with R3's test).
- **(ii) Positions.** *(v2:)* Θ_f broke ties by position, and under F26 (a) positions are themselves a uniqueness across applications: "K4 = yes for positions" (§9.6.6a). *(v3:)* ties are unordered (decided after turn 7), so the order needs no shared point.
- **(iii) What (C) then needs.** If no kernel law requires prevention and ties are by content, recording and evaluation at a given cut are delegable under a contract. The contract holds:
  - D and Θ_f;
  - the pinned CE* version;
  - the tie rule;
  - the identity function and the merge rule;
  - under K4-d, the deterministic aggregates;
  - a rule for cuts (sealed cuts, or answers marked "as of the entries received").
- **Not an iff with F22 as §9.6.6 defines (P).** §9.6's (P) places "the serialization point **and the evaluator CE**" in the kernel. (i) forces only the coordination point; CE's placement stays open (§9.7 step 4). And (iii) makes (C) possible, not necessary.

*Applied (§9.13 v12; model v13, checks 24d, 24h, 24l):*
- **Single-use is a mark of the grant** (field `single_use`). Every act under such a grant (every act that cites it as a basis, except a transfer of it) is a **use**. Each use is checked in three ways:
  - **owner-checked**;
  - **single use is prevented**: no other use of the grant may be not F;
  - **chained**: the use names the previous use of the grant (`prev_use`).

  A grant not marked single-use gets no owner check (check 24d). Its consumptions keep the in-record guard of §9.13, and across applications a second consumption is detected, not prevented. *(The last sentence is the Professor's reading of "need no owner check", for review.)*
- **Who may transfer.** The grant's issuer (the issuance act's actor), or an actor whose T basis is the root or a grant whose scope strictly covers the grant's scope. Never the holder, even with a basis that covers the scope. *(How "above" is tested is the Professor's technical choice, for review.)*
- **Carrying the record of uses: how (the Professor's design, for review).**
  - A transfer names the grant's **last use** (`last_use`), and the kernel checks it against the uses it has accepted. A wrong one is refused.
  - Each use names the **previous** use. So the uses form a chain, and the citation closure of the transfer reaches every earlier use of the grant. The new owner's record, holding the transfer and its closure, therefore holds the record of uses its single-use guard needs.
  - D checks both pointers: each names an earlier use of the same grant. The marks' support follows them.
  - **Premise:** the kernel sees each use (it owner-checks every one), so it knows the last use.

**R7. A test telling a kernel primitive from a world assumption (a definition; revised at turn 7, D2-08, D1-14; adopted provisionally as Int by the Principal after turn 7, for C19's review).** The turn-6 settlement left this test to turn 7 (§9.15 R8 (c); §9.9.7).
- **The test.** A candidate c is a kernel primitive iff both hold:
  - (i) c has an independence model among the candidates (§12b (a), as in §9.9.2);
  - (ii) c is **read**, that is consulted by a clause, of some **existing** reference definition of Req: the evaluator (§9.13.3, D), the marks, or O1–O4. Merely being listed as an input does not count: I2's recording time, I12 and I14 are listed and not read.
- **What (ii) does, stated plainly.** It **stipulates** a line between a **recorded field or input** (which can be read) and **what that field is about** (which cannot). A world assumption is a claim about the second, so it fails (ii) by definition. The test's content is therefore that line, and the claim that the line is the right one is the definitional choice returned to the Principal.
- **What it gives.**
  - Occurrence and completeness are read by no clause, so they are world assumptions.
  - **Key binding and timing:** the evaluator reads the **integrity result** (I13, through D) and the **recorded act time** (the key). These are evidence, read, and primitive candidates. The binding's truth (L2) and the true time (L6) are not read; they are world assumptions. So version 1's "none of them is read" was false as worded: their evidence is read.
  - Under A1-as-fact, occurrence **evidence** would be read, and so a candidate; occurrence itself stays a world assumption.
- **Boundary cases and limits.**
  - The test is **partial**: undefined for a candidate read only by Q4, which has no reference definition yet. Such a candidate is undecided, not failing.
  - It depends on the current CE* and θ.
  - Candidates are **individuated** as recorded fields and inputs, so "act time" is a candidate and "timing" is not.
  - Every candidate of RESEARCH-PURPOSE passes (ii), so the test decides nothing among them. It separates them only from world assumptions.
- **Rivals.**
  - (1) an independence model alone (§9.9.2 as it stands), under which world assumptions can pass (§9.9.7);
  - (2) "**needed by**" a reference definition instead of "read" (D1-14).
- **Falsifier.** A concept the book treats as a kernel primitive that no existing Req definition reads; or a world assumption that some Req definition must read.
- **Decision.** Adopted provisionally as Int (the Principal after turn 7). Under it, occurrence is a world assumption, not a kernel primitive (§9.15 R8 (c), provisional).

## 9.16.4 The strongest counterexamples, and the limits

- **The race (R2 (c))** refutes order-independence under ties by position. That is why R2 (b)'s condition is needed.
- **Records not in D (fragments)** can be merged into a record in D or not. The cut condition matters only for them (R2 (d)).
- **Copies of one act that disagree** on a field CE* reads are refused by the merge. That is detection of a conflict between applications, not a choice of one copy.
- **Answering "now"** across applications: non-monotone (R3, law 21). An answer given before a later annulment arrives is withdrawn by it.
- **K4-d's limit:** an act on a losing consumption may already have happened in the world, and "earliest" can be backdated.
- **The limits of the model checks:** form only, two applications, the generator's kinds, one item.

## 9.16.5 Rival formulations

- **One global sequencer** (F26 (a) across applications): simpler, and the order is one fact. But it is K4 = yes for positions, and makes the order a kernel coordination point by construction (R4 (ii)).
- **Evaluation per application, with no merge:** fails law 21 whenever the entry sets differ.
- **A partial order** (F26 (b)): U where acts are incomparable. It is the corpus's own expectation ("OrderingUncertain", S0969). **Adopted at ties after turn 7** (§9.13 v8): CE* stays total (checks 1, 6).
- **Ownership instead of a shared point** for prevention (R3; EXT-089): one owner per key, with coordination only to transfer rights.
- **The composed-system reading of §9.11 R3**, under which every "eventually" is an environment assumption: compatible with R6's recommendations for laws 18 and 20a.

## 9.16.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

- **Internal**, given the identity and merge rule and each application's record in D: R2's order-independence of values and marks, at a given cut. It is checkable on the records: dangling or forward citations are refused, and so are conflicting copies.
- **Kernel assumptions:** §9.15 K2 is weakened under unordered ties; §9.15 K4 applies only under R3's laws with prevention; §9.15 K5 covers tombstones and the status (§9.15 v4).
- **About the world:**
  - that an application cites only what it has received (R2 (a));
  - that an act performed on a consumption that loses at the merge can be reconciled (R3), a legitimacy and governance matter;
  - true act times (L6), against backdating.
