**R2. Records merged without a serialization point (Ded; model check 18).** `[CON-0194 · provisional]`

*Setting.*
- R₁, …, Rₙ are the records of several applications;
- every entry carries a content-derived identity (K2);
- **the causal premise:** every citation is of an entry already made (in any of the records) when the citing entry was made;
- R is the union of their entries, listed in some order in which every citation points backward: a **linear extension** of the citation graph.

*Claims.*
- **(a)** Under the causal premise the citation graph is acyclic, so a linear extension exists: it is a sub-relation of happened-before, a strict partial order (EXT-084). R is in D iff its entries meet D's per-entry rules and the union is **closed under citation**. A union of records each in D need not be: an entry of R₁ may cite an entry that only R₂ holds.
- **(b)** If ties of act time are broken by a function of the entries' content, CE*(R) is the same for every linear extension. This is F26 (c), for example ties by identity, or "endings and annulments first".
- **(c)** If ties are broken by position, as §9.13.2 does now, two linear extensions can give different answers.
- **(d)** A cut across records takes one prefix per record. It is in D iff it is closed under citation: the consistent-cut condition (EXT-085). Lemma D's condition for a cut across records is then exactly this.

*Proof of (b).*
1. CE* reads the order of entries in two places only. The first is the key of each time test, (t, s) on ties (§9.13.2; in the model, `Record.key`). The second is D's rule that citations point to earlier entries (`in_D`). Everywhere else Φ and the currency rule read the record as a set of entries (§9.13.3).
2. With ties broken by content, the key is a function of the entries alone.
3. Every linear extension passes D's citation rule, by its definition.
4. So Φ is the same operator on every linear extension. Its least fixed point, which equals the well-founded model on D (§9.13 R1), is the same, and so is current-authority read off it. ∎

*Proof of (c), a countermodel (the race).*
- Application 1 records A's grant x to B, under A's grant g, at act time 3.
- Application 2 records P's ending of g, also at act time 3.
- Neither entry cites the other. B later adopts c under x.
- With ties by position, CE*(c, 6) is T in one merge order and F in the other.
- With ties by identity it is F in both.

*Model check 18 (form only; `tmp/tools/ce_model.py` v7, run `ce_model-run-20261020-v7.txt`).*
- 600 random causal histories, split at random between two applications, each merged in 6 random linear extensions. With ties by identity, all 600 gave the same answers in every order (item c, times 0–6, with and without a queried scope).
- With ties by position, **none** of the 600 differed: the difference needs a same-time race of the kind above, which the generator rarely builds. Claim (c) therefore rests on the constructed race, not on the random sample.
- Cuts across records: 472 were closed under citation and in D; 128 were not, and all 128 were refused.

*What the condition costs.*
- A content tie rule decides a same-time race by what the entries are, not by who recorded first. Ties by identity are deterministic but arbitrary.
- "Endings and annulments first at equal act time" is also a content rule, and it is the conservative one.
- Which rule to use is a value of θ (F26), so the choice is reserved for the Principal.

*Src.* The corpus states the pattern informally: "Merge raw evidence; recompute derived knowledge." [S0915 L668–669]; "It must not blindly assume arrival order equals causal order." [S0991 L657]; and events form a partial order without a global sequence (S0915 L167–201, S0969 L1215–1264, in meaning). What is the book's own: that CE* on D has the property, under which tie rule, and the consistent-cut form of Lemma D's condition. *Ext:* union of a grow-only set converges without coordination (EXT-086); **established**.

**R3. Where a uniqueness across applications arises (Ded from §9.13.2 and the laws of §9.11).**
- **Recording needs none.** With content-derived identities, a duplicate of an act is the same entry, and the union absorbs it (§9.6.4 step 1; O1's idempotence).
- **Evaluation needs none**, by R2 (b).
- **Plural bases are no violation.** In CE*, authorized(x) is a disjunction over x's bases (§9.13.3), so "one current grant per (subject, scope)", an example in §9.6's K4, is not needed by CE*. It would be a further law.
- **So K4 arises only from laws that bound a count across applications.** In the book's selection (§9.11.1):
  - law 9b/24: single use (§1.13's CONSUMED; consumption is not an admitted kind of D);
  - law 26: at most one established assessment per claim at a time (§9.4 C2).
  - Outside that selection, the corpus has the exclusivity of a role: "Where governance defines exclusive authority, the distributed system must prevent simultaneous conflicting authoritative states." [S0991 L2058–2063], and "Therefore the invariant must be role-specific." [S0991 L1145].
- **For each such law, detection (K4-d) needs no shared point, and prevention (K4-p) does.**
  - *Detection:* a deterministic aggregate decides the conflict on the merged record, order-independently by R2 (b). For law 26 this is §9.4 C2's rival, the weakest outcome. For single use, the earliest consumption by key counts and a later one does not.
  - What detection cannot do: an application may already have acted on the consumption that loses. That act happened in the world, and the record can only show it after the merge.
  - *Prevention:* needs one serialization point per constraint, shared by every application that writes it (§9.6.4 step 2; §1.16.7). It does not need one recorder.
- **What the corpus gives here: the question's form, not its answer.** It prefers prevention for exclusive authority (S0991 above) and leaves decisions under partition to policy, "This should be governed by policy." [S0915 L1315]. Its own verification grades these invariants as names, not definitions: "0/11 invariants are definitions" [S1519 L234]. They are Src for the shape of K4, not evidence for either answer.

**R5. The history result after tombstones (Ded; model check 17).**
- **A tombstone changes no CE* answer.**
  1. Φ and the currency rule read only kind, citations and the fields D requires (§9.13.2–3).
  2. A tombstone keeps exactly these.
  3. So Φ is unchanged, its fixed point is unchanged, and so is every answer at every cut. Only the marks change ("rests on removed content").
  - *Premise:* the integrity check is defined on the tombstone itself (K5): what remains must still be checkable once the content is gone.
- **P-loss follows for kinds whose required fields carry what they consumed.** §9.11 R1's P-loss says a loss keeps the identities of the acts the removed entries consumed. If consumption is admitted with the consumed act as a required field, a tombstone keeps that field, so P-loss holds for removals by tombstone. It does not hold for a loss that drops identities, which the tombstone rule does not make.
- **F30, partial:** removal of content is answered; dropping identities, and how long tombstones are kept, are open.

**R7. A test telling a kernel primitive from a world assumption (a definition, proposed; Hyp until reviewed; returned).** The turn-6 settlement left this test to turn 7 (§9.15 R8 (c); §9.9.7).
- **The test.** A candidate c is a kernel primitive iff both hold:
  - (i) c has an independence model among the candidates (§12b (a), as in §9.9.2);
  - (ii) c is **read** by some reference definition of Req: some field or value of c is among the evaluator's inputs (§9.12 I1–I15, θ) or among the inputs of O1–O4.

  An assumption that relates what is read to events outside the record is a **world assumption**: occurrence, attribution, completeness, key binding, timing. It is never read; only evidence about it can be.
- **What it gives (Ded from the definition and §9.15 R3).**
  - Under DB-1, occurrence is read by no reference definition (§9.15 R3), so it is a world assumption, not a primitive.
  - Under A1-as-fact, the evaluator reads **occurrence evidence** (§9.12.4). Then the evidence is the input and a primitive candidate, and occurrence itself stays a world assumption.
  - The over-generation of §9.9.7 (key binding, timing and completeness passing M-T2's construction) is removed by (ii): none of them is read.
- **Boundary cases.**
  - Every candidate of RESEARCH-PURPOSE is read: identity (I1), authority acts and grants (I2, I4), evidence (O2), policy (θ), history (the record, the cut). So (ii) excludes no candidate.
  - Lifecycle is read only through act kinds (I2).
  - Relationships are read only through citations and coverage (I2, I14).
  - A candidate read only by an application, not by Req, fails (ii). That is the intended consequence: it is then an application concept.
- **Rival.** Primitive = an independence model alone (§9.9.2 as it stands), under which world assumptions can pass (§9.9.7).
- **Falsifier.** A concept the book treats as a kernel primitive that no Req definition reads; or a world assumption that some Req definition must read.
- **Decision.** The test is a definitional choice, so it is the Principal's. §9.15 R8 (c) stays "not decided" until it is adopted.

## 9.16.4 The strongest counterexamples, and the limits

- **The race (R2 (c)).** It refutes order-independence under ties by position. That is why the condition of R2 (b) is needed, not a convenience.
- **A cut that is not closed under citation** is refused, not answered wrongly. The cost is availability: such a cut must wait for the cited entries.
- **A breach of the causal premise:** an entry that cites one made later. It is outside D, and D's append guard refuses it at the entry (§9.13.2). So the premise is checkable, not assumed.
- **K4-d's limit (R3):** a consumption that loses at the merge may already have caused an act in the world. Detection gives the record the right answer afterwards. It does not undo the act.
- **The limits of model check 18:** form only, two applications, the generator's kinds, one item. The random sample gave no instance of (c).

## 9.16.5 Rival formulations

- **One global sequencer** (F26 (a) across applications): simpler; the order is one fact. But it is K4 = yes for positions, and makes F22 a plane by construction. R2 shows that evaluation does not require it.
- **Evaluation per application, with no merge:** fails law 21 whenever the entry sets differ.
- **A partial order** (F26 (b)): U where acts are incomparable. Outside D now, and §9.13.10 asks whether CE* stays total under it.
- **The composed-system reading of §9.11 R3**, under which every "eventually" is an environment assumption: compatible with R6's recommendations for laws 18 and 20a.

## 9.16.6 Internal guarantees and assumptions about the world

- **Internal**, given content-derived identities and the causal premise: R2's order-independence and the consistent-cut rule. Both are checkable on records: a dangling or forward citation is refused.
- **Kernel assumptions:** K2 is weakened to "the same entries at the cut" (R1); K4 is needed only under R3's laws.
- **About the world:** that an act performed on a consumption that loses at the merge can be reconciled (R3). This is a legitimacy and governance matter, not a property of the record.
