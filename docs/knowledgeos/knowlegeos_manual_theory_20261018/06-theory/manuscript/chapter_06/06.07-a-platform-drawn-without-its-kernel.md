## 6.7.1 In plain language

The architecture diagrams of 2026-08-18 draw the whole KnowledgeOS / AI Engineering Platform as one box, with the AI runtime outside it. They call themselves a proposal. They draw no line between a kernel and its applications. The same capability, recording grants and handoffs, is placed in different boxes in different views, and the human authorizer points at a different box in each view. The arrows mix data flow, control and authority, so the book's direction law cannot be tested on them. One diagram, the contract-neutrality fact model, states a precise point: a final metric alone cannot show that the facts behind it are right.

## 6.7.2 Claims and derivations

**C1. The set declares itself a proposal (Src; an instance of §1.14.5 and P-8).**
- "They should be treated as an architecture proposal until the corresponding PO/ARB decisions are accepted into the authoritative model." [S0081 L3].
- **Int:** the set claims no standing for itself and names what would give it standing: acts accepted "into the authoritative model". That is §1.14.5's order, decision before enforcement, applied to a model. Which acts these are, and whether any exist, the five files do not say.

**C2. No kernel/application line is drawn (Src; Int for the reading).**
- Every capability sits inside one boundary, "KnowledgeOS / AI Engineering Platform" [S0083 L12; S0086 L8], or "KnowledgeOS Platform" [S0085 L7]. Outside it are the repository and the AI runtime: "AI / Agent Runtime", "Claude/Codex or other AI execution terminals" [S0083 L14; S0085 L20].
- Inside, the same boundary holds a "Governance Engine" with the workflow acts "REGISTER, GRANT, HANDOFF, START, ACCEPT, COMPLETE, CANCEL" [S0086 L9], and an "Architecture Workbench" and an "Implementation Workbench" [S0086 L12–13], and in the component view a "Drafting Studio" that "Produces bounded proposals and reports" [S0085 L14].
- **Int (RESEARCH-PURPOSE, the test for every concept):** the diagrams answer "what is the platform?", not "what is the kernel?". Under the purpose's stability test, the workbenches and the drafting studio are candidates for applications; the governance engine and knowledge engineering are candidates for the kernel. *Hyp:* that split is the book's reading; the diagrams do not draw it.
- **A tension with §0.2:** the statement of 07-27 that "AI is a capability inside the system. It is not the system." (§0.2, DevConst L24) puts AI inside. These views put the AI runtime outside, as an external system. *Int:* the two may not conflict, since a capability that uses AI and an AI runtime are different things, but the diagrams do not say which is meant.

**C3. One capability, different homes in different views (Src).**
- Container view: the "Governance Engine" carries "GRANT, HANDOFF" [S0086 L9], and "Session Orchestration" carries "Assignment, grants, handoffs, mutation ownership" [S0086 L15]. Both write to the estate: the governance engine "Persists lifecycle records" [S0086 L30], and session orchestration "Reads / writes workflow state" [S0086 L35].
- Component view: one "Workflow Engine" carries "Grants, assignments, handoffs, mutation ownership and transitions" [S0085 L10], beside a separate "Session Manager" that "Registers and activates work sessions" [S0085 L9].
- **Int (§6.6 C1, §9.6):** in the container view, grants and handoffs have two homes and two writers. In the component view they have one. The book's measured machinery, BC-7, keeps sessions, grants and transitions in one record with one fold (§6.5, §9.6), which matches the component view. A component view is meant to refine its container, not to move a capability between containers. Which view is authoritative the set does not say (C1: the set calls itself a proposal).

**C4. The human authorizer points at a different box in each view (Src; Int).**
- Context view: "PO / ARB" → "Governance Engineer", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0083 L16]; the Governance Engineer is a person, "Authority, lifecycle, grants, acceptance" [S0083 L6].
- Container view: "PO / ARB" → "Governance Engine", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0086 L20].
- Component view: "PO / ARB" → "Workflow Engine", "Authorizes / accepts" [S0085 L22].
- **Int (§1.13, §1.12):** in the book's model, the human act happens outside the mechanism, and Governance registers a grant that points at it. Only the context view draws that: a person between the act and the record. The other two draw the act as an input to a mechanism. Read literally, the record would then be where the act happens, which §1.12 rules out. *Not shown:* that the authors meant this. The arrows' meaning is not defined (C5), so this is an ambiguity, not a contradiction.

**C5. The arrows have no declared meaning, so the direction law cannot be tested here (Ded, from the labels).** `[CON-0147 · provisional]`
- The relation labels mix four kinds: data flow ("Persists state", "Reads evidence") [S0085 L32, L28]; control ("Bootstraps and constrains AI sessions") [S0083 L23]; authority ("Authorizes / accepts") [S0083 L16]; and use ("Consumes approved architecture") [S0086 L26].
- **Ded:** §6.6 C8's direction law is about **dependency**: applications depend on the knowledge system, never the reverse. A diagram whose arrows mix dependency with data flow and control gives no dependency graph, so the law can be neither confirmed nor refuted on it. For example, "Bootstraps and constrains AI sessions" (platform → AI) and "Executes assigned work under grants" (AI → platform) [S0083 L23–24] run both ways, but neither is declared a dependency.
- *Consequence for the boundary question:* a diagram can test the direction law only if its dependency relations are marked as such, for example by typing each relation (depends on, writes, controls, authorizes).

**C6. "Supplies authoritative state": the homonym again (Src; a second instance of §6.6's homonym, same commit).**
- "Workflow Engine" → "Communication Capability": "Supplies authoritative state" [S0085 L26].
- **Int (§1.14, state ≠ authority):** "authoritative" here means the canonical source of a state, as in S0066's "establish engineering authority" (§6.6, S0066 L286–287). It does not mean standing conferred by an act. *Not independent of §6.6:* S0066 and this set were first committed together (lineage).

**C7. A final metric alone cannot verify the facts behind it (Src; Ded; Ext for the metric's definition).** `[CON-0146 · provisional]`
- The fact model's layers: source text → "L0-L2 extraction into language-neutral facts" → "L3 Fact Model" → "L4 Cohesion Semantics" → "L5 Metric", "LCOM4", "Derived cohesion metric" [S0084 L7–10]. The conformance evidence is "Node set + edge set + final metric" [S0084 L11]. Two constraints: "Qualifier kind is preserved, not inferred from final metric", and "Metric alone is insufficient" [S0084 L21–22].
- *Ext (definition, standard in the software-metrics literature):* LCOM4 counts the connected components of the graph of a class's methods and fields, with edges where a method uses a field or calls another method.
- **Ded (a non-injective map):** let f map a fact graph G to LCOM4(G). Two different graphs G ≠ G′ that are both connected give f(G) = f(G′) = 1. A check that compares only f(G) with the expected value accepts G′ where G was expected. So agreement of the final metric cannot show that the extracted facts are right. **A check must compare the intermediate levels it is meant to verify**, here the node and edge sets. The same holds for any derivation that is not injective.
- **Ded (no back-inference):** for the same reason, a property of the inputs, such as the qualifier kind, cannot be recovered from the output. It must be carried forward ("preserved") from the level where it is a fact.
- **Int:** this is §8.8 C2's rule, a count needs its method, put as a theorem about derivations. It also states the layer each claim holds at, the point the map records as missing from the book (a neutrality claim that "never stated its layer", S0164). For the kernel: evidence for a derived claim must carry the derivation's intermediate facts, not only its result. *Scope:* one diagram states the rule for one metric; the Ded part holds for any non-injective derivation.
