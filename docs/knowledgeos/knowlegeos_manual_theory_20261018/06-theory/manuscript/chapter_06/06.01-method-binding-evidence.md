# 6.1 Method, binding and evidence: where the platform ends

## 6.1.1 In plain language

A reusable piece of engineering knowledge usually arrives mixed with things that belong to one product. One example is a method for finding the boundaries of a business domain, which may also:
- name the product's own certified knowledge release as its only permitted input;
- carry worked examples from the product's election rules.

The corpus's Architecture Baseline (S0028, 2026-08-02, a generated candidate) says that making the platform reusable means **separating** these parts:
- the **method**, which is reusable;
- the **binding**, which is what the method needs from a particular product;
- the **evidence**, which is what happened when it was used there.

This section makes the separation precise. Its main result: **from one product, the separation is not determined, unless the method declares its interface.**

## 6.1.2 Definitions

- **Method, binding, evidence (Src).** "Every reusable artifact must explicitly separate (1) the METHOD — domain-free, reusable · (2) the BINDING — the product-specific input contract · (3) the EVIDENCE — product-specific case law." [S0028 L104] `[CON-0073 · invalid]`. It is not exhaustive: the source's own later note says it "lacks … an explicit `Ambiguous` class" [L101] (T6-01).
- **Product binding (Src).** "the product-specific input contract a reusable method requires." [L29] `[CON-0072 · validated]`.
- **Three properties of an artifact (Src):** *d* domain-free · *b* binding-free · *e* free of product case law [L114–121]. "Evidence-free" in the source means free of **one product's case law**, not unsupported by evidence.
- **Formal reading (Ded).** A method *M* maps a binding β to a practice. A text written inside one product is a representation of that practice together with the product's case law *E*:

  > *A* = *r*(*M*(β_PD), *E*_PD)

  Here *r* maps a practice and its case law to a text. **The extraction hypothesis:** the product enters *M* only through β. If the method itself was shaped by the product's case, then *M* = *M*_PD and no such parameter exists. That is what extraction must test.

## 6.1.3 Claims and derivations

**C1. One product does not fix the split, unless the interface is declared (Ded).**
- Given *A* only, many pairs (*M*′, β′) produce the same text. Any constant in *A* can be counted either as part of the method or as part of the binding. So, without further information, the split is **not identifiable** from one product.
- **Two ways to identify it:**
  1. **A declared interface:** the method names its binding explicitly, as an input contract. This is the source's own remedy for SD-1, "replaced by a generic input contract" [L83]. It works from one product.
  2. **A second, sufficiently different product:** what the two artifacts share constrains the method; what differs constrains the binding. Two products whose bindings coincide (for example two election products) do **not** suffice: shared product-specific constants would be misread as method.
- **Bearing on the component rule (Ch. 1 §1.8, CON-0076):** this grounds only its third condition, "a repeatable pattern extracted from ≥ 2 instances", and only where no interface is declared. It does not ground the second condition ("exercised by someone other than the originator"), which concerns independence of use, not identifiability.

**C2. SD-1 admits several splits (Ded).**
- The baseline asks: "Is SD-1 a PRODUCT BINDING or a PLATFORM RULE?" [L190]. SD-1 reads "consumes only the Certified Domain Knowledge Release v1.0" [L83].
- By the definitions, every clause can be split into a rule part and a binding part, so the either/or form of the question is not well posed. But by C1, **which** split is right is not determined by this one product. Candidates include:
  - "consume only a **certified release** of the product's domain knowledge" + "which release: v1.0";
  - "consume only **certified** domain knowledge" + "certification means this release";
  - "consume only an **authoritative** input" + all the rest.
- **Choosing among them is part of the ARB's question,** not a result of this book (fork F10).

**C3. Portability is a lattice, not a ladder (Ded).** The source's four tiers [L116–121]:
1. all three properties hold: ready;
2. domain-free but bound to a product: blocked;
3. domain-free but diluted with case law: blocked;
4. not domain-free: product-specific.

For domain-free artifacts, the pair (*b*, *e*) ∈ {0,1}², ordered component by component, forms a lattice:

```
              (1,1)  ready
             /     \
   (0,1) bound      (1,0) diluted
             \     /
              (0,0)  bound and diluted
```

- Tiers 2 and 3 are **incomparable**. Removing a binding and separating case law are independent operations.
- The bottom point has no tier: the source marks one artifact "2/3" [L131].
- **The full space is {0,1}³.** The source's tiers form a quotient that treats "not domain-free" as dominating whatever *b* and *e* are. That assumption is the source's, and it is stated here.
- The source's values are **graded** ("~90% case law", L132–133), so [0,1]² under the same order is the better model; it is still a lattice.
- Counting blocking properties gives an **ordinal** distance to ready, not a cost.

`[CON-0074 · invalid]` names the ladder as stated; `[CON-0075 · validated]` names this lattice.

**C4. The kernel test is necessary, not sufficient.** `[CON-0057 · validated]` (as a necessary condition)
- "Only Tier-1 items" may enter the kernel (§3.6).
- *Counterexamples to sufficiency:*
  - a tier-1 artifact can be untested or unadopted; the source itself adds "once ADOPTED";
  - portability is a property of the **set**, not of its elements (Ch. 1 §1.8: every element survived; the set still failed).
- *A refinement:* a method that **declares** its binding as a named parameter is not binding-free in form, yet portable. So the better condition is "binding only through a declared interface", not "binding-free".

**C5. A responsibility can exist without a component (Src).**
- The three conditions for a component are "(1) exercised at least once · (2) exercised by someone other than the originator · (3) a repeatable pattern extracted from ≥ 2 instances" [L142].
- "PKS generation" exists as a responsibility, but not as a component: "The port exists; the adapter is a person" [L150].
- A person here is **assigned** to a responsibility, as a role-holder in the sense of Ch. 1 §1.14.3. That is not the "realization" of §3.8, whose nodes do not include persons.

**C6. Where evidence lives: the corpus does not follow its own placement rule (Src, with Ded).**
- **The rule:** case law belongs to the product's knowledge space [L74–75]. If followed, the evidence that a **platform** method works in a product sits in that product's space. The platform could then justify its methods only by reading across a space boundary (§3.6, CON-0041).
- **Corpus practice differs:**
  - the platform's own evidence register (S0027) lives with the platform documents while recording one product's events;
  - the AI-workflow log (S0030) names a **third** home, "`engineering/observations/` or `engineering/evidence/`" [S0030 L8], which is neither the platform nor a product space;
  - so there are at least three homes for evidence.
- The conditional result stands. Its premise is not what the corpus does (T6-02).

**C7. The baseline's headline claims, against C1 (Ded).**
- "method has been demonstrated portable" [L257] is **not** supported: by C1, portability cannot be shown on one product unless the interface is declared, and no second product exists [G-3, L211].
- "The programme does not need more evidence to define its architecture" [L259] conflicts with "All five principles … rest on n=1" [L257].
- **The counter-practice exists in the corpus (S0031):** a companion document restates a prior verdict as "the current strategic model is sufficient FOR THE PRESENT MATURITY LEVEL" [S0031 L13], not as "sufficient". That is the scoping C7 asks for: a claim stated relative to the evidence available.
- **But the scoping is confined to that one claim.** The same file closes on unscoped absolutes: "KnowledgeOS does not need to be designed" [S0031 L240], and that it is the last document "that should be written before the first of them fires" [S0031 L241]. Its companions do the same: "The strategic model is stable. Stop consolidating it." [S0033 L263], and "nothing at the strategic level" is missing (S0035). So the corpus shows the scoped form once, beside four unscoped ones, and its own count claims in the same file do not reproduce (9 of 16 rows needing no move, against 7 in its table; S0031 L151, L132–149).

**C8. The relation "creates" is a hypothesis (Src).** "KnowledgeOS → creates → PKS → guides → Product" [L32]. But generating a knowledge space is n=0: "every PKS to date was hand-built" [L25]; L-2 is a "HYPOTHESIS" [L178]. So "creates" is a **hypothesized** edge, outside the evidenced orders of §3.8. "Guides" is a relation outside those orders.
