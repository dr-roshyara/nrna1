## 0. In business language

The document asks whether a **meta-model** (the basic kinds of thing the platform is made of) has emerged from the earlier work.

It first checks what already exists. Three of four "remaining discoveries" proposed by a review were already done:
- the invariant set;
- the owners of each kind of change;
- the kernel, which was tested and failed.

Five earlier classification schemes exist. So the real question is whether they **agree**.

**Answer: yes, but not in the shape proposed.** The proposal was a flat list of seven categories (domains, processes, capabilities, knowledge assets, governance assets, runtime assets, product assets). The evidence supports a **dimensional model with archetypes**: every element has a **kind** plus a position on the knowledge dimensions (nature, representation, governance status, authority scope).

**Nine kinds are found:** domain, process, capability, knowledge, artifact, runtime asset, container (knowledge space), projection, purpose. Three proposed categories are not kinds:
- **"Governance asset"** is a *nature* value (normative force);
- **"Product asset"** is a *scope* value;
- **"Knowledge asset"** wrongly fuses knowledge with its carrier.

**One proposed relationship is refuted:** domains do **not** own capabilities; capabilities are anchored to design policies (DP-n). Four other relationships hold.

**Still open:** whether "kind" is its own axis or just a nature value; who owns the meta-model (nobody); whether purpose is a kind; and the warning that this must not become a **fourth unreconciled ontology**.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1]** This is the corpus's strongest application of the **model-integrity rule**: before adding a category, ask whether it is a new dimension, a value of an existing dimension, or a fusion of two. Governance → nature value; product → scope value; knowledge asset → fusion.
- **[I-2]** "Domains own Capabilities" refuted, with capabilities anchored to DP-n, is consistent with S0008's normative strand (principle → design policy → capability) and with the book's ownership model (Ch. 1 §1.11).
