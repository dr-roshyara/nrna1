## 0. In business language

The commission asked which decisions KnowledgeOS should help people make. The author first checked and found the **Engineering Decision Model already exists as canon**: DRAFT, accepted by the ARB on 2026-07-11, owned by the Decision Authority, with eight engineer decisions, a stopping rule and the stance "the AI evaluates and recommends — authority remains with governance". "Discovering" it again would have been model amnesia occurrence #12. So the work became a **reconciliation**: the declared catalogue against the decisions actually taken.

**Findings:**
1. Engineer decisions have a **catalogue**, made in advance; governance decisions have only a **log** (77 rulings, read after the fact). Whether that is a defect or correct is left open.
2. Exactly two recurring decisions extend nothing in the catalogue:
   - **FREEZE**, used at least three times, "each time by declaration, under no named authority, procedure, or reversal condition";
   - **RETIRE**, the open question of when knowledge is retired.
3. Each ontology element is bound to the decision it makes easier. The deletion test ("can this be deleted without loss of governed knowledge?") cannot even be asked unless knowledge and its file are different things. **Domain** and **purpose** currently decide nothing.

**The answer to the commission:** KnowledgeOS helps engineers make the eight engineer decisions. For **governance** decisions it **prepares but never makes** them: "for authority decisions, the platform's product is DECISION-READINESS, not decisions". 77 rulings were made by humans; the platform shaped them (evidence graded, options separated, unknowns carried).

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1] Decision-readiness is the corpus's own statement of this lane's business objective:**
  - the system decides what is within its procedure (engineer decisions);
  - for authority decisions it investigates, grades the evidence, separates the options and carries the unknowns;
  - the human decides.

  This is the AI-recommends / human-decides boundary expressed as a **product** definition.
- **[I-2] FREEZE "by declaration, under no named authority"** is a case for the book's §1.14.5 (an authorized emergency freeze requires an act within a grant) and for the authority theory's freeze question: freezes occurred as **acts without a recorded authority, procedure or reversal**.
- **[I-3]** The catalogue/log asymmetry is a design question for the authority theory: are authority acts **enumerable in advance** (a catalogue of decision types and who decides them), or only **recordable after the fact**?
