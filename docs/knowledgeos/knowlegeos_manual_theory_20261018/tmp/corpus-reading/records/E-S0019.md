## 0. In business language

The document asks one question, "What is KnowledgeOS?", and it **corrects the order of the earlier work**. Mission must come first, then domains, ontology, taxonomy, indexing and automation; the track had run it backwards.

**Body (first version):**
- Two statements in the canon. **M-A**, adopted: the platform "exists solely to improve delivery of PublicDigit". **M-B**, unapproved: a market hypothesis for an "AI-native Knowledge Operating System".
- They seem to conflict ("solely PublicDigit" vs "independent of project"). They do not: **M-A constrains *why* work is done, the Reference Architecture constrains *what* is built.** "Build portable things — but only when doing so advances this product." AIP-14 is "a cost control, not a boundary".
- Eight **responsibilities**, each traced to an artifact: govern the engineering lifecycle; hold the authority model; hold the method; define the shape of a capability; make knowledge retrievable and enforceable; harvest reuse from evidence; keep runtime vocabulary out of governance; **refuse to approve its own work**.
- Ten **non-responsibilities**: business knowledge (N-1, **breached**); product evidence; register contents; runtime vocabulary; its own approval; governance defined by automation; duplicated knowledge; materialized copies; a product's PKS; orchestration machinery.
- **Three authority systems**, not one ownership list: **policy** (Decision Authority / ARB), **method and implementation** (sponsor + ARB; the knowledge machinery: one individual), **domain knowledge** (the product team). "No authority spans all three, and none is designated to reconcile them."
- The knowledge-platform domain splits: the **machinery** is generic (replaceable); the **knowledge constitution** is supporting.
- Lifecycle links graded: 1 of 4 observed.
- **The finding:** "Almost every architectural difficulty in this track is a symptom of designing under M-B while governed by M-A."

**Annotation (later the same day):** "two missions" is **withdrawn**. AIP-14 is a **constraint**, not a mission: it ships with a per-iteration metric and a review trigger, and "a mission does not; a control does". The mission is "implicit but evidenced, not vacant": it is **enacted in machinery**, through eight artifacts none of which is PublicDigit-specific. "Vacant" was the author's third instance of one error: **artifact absence ≠ concept absence**.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1] Three authority systems** is a direct contribution to the book's authority theory: authority is **plural by subject** (policy, method, domain knowledge), each with its own holder, and **no meta-authority** reconciles them. In the book's terms, grant scopes are partitioned by subject matter, and a question spanning two scopes has no designated decider (MQ-2).
- **[I-2] Mission vs constraint** gives a test for the *kind* of a normative statement: one that ships with a metric and a trigger is a control; a mission states purpose without a measure.
- **[I-3] Why vs what** is a clean resolution pattern for apparent contradictions between norms: check whether the two constrain **different objects** (purpose vs product). It is an instance of the book's contradiction procedure, step 2 (scope).
