## 0. In business language

A reviewer summarized an external article, "OKF", about knowledge layers for AI. This document checks what the article adds to KnowledgeOS. It **declares up front that the article itself was not read**: every OKF claim is second-hand, from the reviewer's summary, and marked as such.

**What the repository independently confirms:**
- knowledge is an architectural layer, not just documentation;
- markdown plus structured metadata is a workable substrate;
- business meaning must come from domain owners;
- "AI proposes, the human approves". This is the most heavily evidenced principle in the repository: every generated artifact says it is never authoritative without human review, and the engineering protocol says automation "does not approve itself".

**What is not confirmed:** that the **feedback loop** works. It has never run. The sharper diagnosis is that the loop's *entry event*, a retrospective, has never taken place. The trigger never fired; it is not that nobody participated.

**What is gained is one name: "Metadata ≠ Knowledge".**
- Metadata says *what exists*.
- Knowledge says *how it should be used*: owner, allowed use, trust, review dates.
- The repository already does this. Its `authority`, `owner`, `audience` and `next_review` fields are knowledge; `knowledge_type` is metadata.
- A reviewer's addendum had mapped this to the method/binding/evidence split. That is wrong: those are different axes.

**Two further findings:**
- There are **two knowledge bodies**, the governed documentation corpus and the operational registers the checks actually read, and no single "knowledge layer" spans both.
- The five "knowledge kinds" proposed reduce to **three** (engineering/platform, product, operational).

**Corrections:** KnowledgeOS is not a Core Domain (a 2026-07-27 ruling says Supporting Subdomain), and PKS is not a generated projection (existing ones are authored).
