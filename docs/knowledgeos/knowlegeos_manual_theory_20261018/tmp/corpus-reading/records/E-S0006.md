## 0. In business language

A reviewer suggested that KnowledgeOS might need a new "decision model". This document checks whether the **existing Engineering Decision Model** already does the job, and finds that it does.

The existing model was created on 2026-07-11 to list **which decisions an engineer makes** with the rules, without becoming a second copy of the rules ("a decision index, never a second rulebook"). It covers eight everyday engineering decisions:
- where something belongs;
- what type it is, and whether it stays;
- whether it is worth reusing or promoting;
- which standards apply, and how it is checked.

It **deliberately excludes** governance acts: ADRs, approvals, authority acts, promotion rulings. "Promotion is a human governance act and is never automated"; "the AI evaluates and recommends — authority remains with governance". The absence of governance decisions is therefore **by design**.

**Knowledge-governance decisions** (what knowledge is needed, its source, representation, qualification and promotion) are **reserved as a future catalogue**. It arrives only with pilot evidence, and this programme's work is that evidence.

**A second decision model would break three existing rules:** the model's own charter, a lint-enforced rule ("one authoritative artifact per topic"), and "one rule → one home".

**The four "viewpoints"** (meaning, element types, decision resolution, enactment) are only partly supported:
- the ARB had already called the Reference Architecture and the Decision Model "complementary siblings, not a hierarchy", so the viewpoints get no arrows;
- the structural slot is contested.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1]** The model draws a clean three-way separation:
  - **rules** live once, in the standards;
  - **decisions** are indexed, and a decision is a *specification*, not executable code;
  - **authority acts** are outside the catalogue and human.

  This is the corpus's own form of "AI evaluates and recommends; governance decides". Its structure matches the business objective of this lane: decide within authority, recommend otherwise.
- **[I-2]** "a decision is a specification, not a service" is a typing statement: a decision names *what must be resolved and by what rule*, and it does not *run*. It parallels the book's "knowledge does not execute" (Ch. 1 §1.1).
