## 0. In business language

The document derives **one meta-model** that all future artifacts must fit, built from three existing models (RQ-002, the platform metamodel, the executable schema) rather than invented.

**Dimensions.** Each candidate dimension is tested with the repository's own rule (a new dimension must be **orthogonal, necessary and sufficient**). The result is **six dimensions:**
- four that already exist: nature (what a thing is), representation (how it is encoded), governance status (lifecycle position), authority scope (how far it reaches);
- two **new** ones: **evidence status** (observed / inferred / hypothesized) and **operational state** (enforced / advisory / unexercised).

**Rejected on the same rule:**
- **"intent status"** (proposed / intended / ratified / operational) is rejected: it is not orthogonal, because "ratified" and "approved" are the same axis. Intent is **derived**: intent = governance status × owner;
- the author also **withdraws** its own "INTENDED" evidence grade, because "intent is not evidence".

**Six concepts:** mission, engineering knowledge, engineering capability, engineering artifact, product knowledge space, product. The most important edge: **engineering knowledge ≠ engineering artifact**. This makes a recurring error unstatable: one can no longer say "X does not exist" when one means "no artifact carries X".

**Test:** six real artifacts are classified without changing the model. The test shows governance status and operational state vary independently:
- a constitution is *frozen and enforced*;
- a capability is *realized yet only advisory*.

Corrections the same day: level mixing (the six concepts are not siblings); PKS is a **container**, not a layer; the chain gains **design policy (DP-n)**.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1]** The intent derivation is a clean instance of the book's method: **reduce before adding**. A proposed dimension that is a function of existing ones (plus OWNER) is not a dimension.
- **[I-2]** M2: intent depends on OWNER, which is not a dimension. So either OWNER is an attribute outside the dimensional space (like provenance), or the model is incomplete. The book's §1.2 has a similar structure: authority = (provenance, standing), with standing changed by **acts of owners/authorities**.
- **[I-3]** GOVERNANCE-STATUS ⟂ OPERATIONAL-STATE (frozen yet enforced; realized yet advisory) is the corpus's demonstration of Ch. 2 §2.9–2.10: **how strongly a rule binds ≠ what enforces it**.
