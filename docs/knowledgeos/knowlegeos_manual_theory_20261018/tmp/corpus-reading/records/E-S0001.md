## 0. In business language

The document asks one question: who owns each architectural "viewpoint" of the platform? The five viewpoints are semantic, structural, behavioural, governance and operational. The commission's premise is that ownership is a strong signal of where a separate business domain ("bounded context") lies.

To answer it, the author reads the owner field from each carrying document's header rather than guessing. Three findings follow.

**1. All platform-side viewpoints have the same owner, the Decision Authority.**
- So viewpoints are different views of one thing, not separate domains.
- The author first said this *proves* there is one context, then weakened it in a revision: shared ownership is only evidence *against* separate contexts.

**2. Ownership changes between "spaces", not between viewpoints.**
- The spaces are platform, knowledge platform (EKP) and product.
- The space map may therefore be the real ownership structure. This is held as a hypothesis, seen on one platform only.

**3. There are two kinds of "unowned".**
- A draft nobody has adopted yet has no owner. That is correct: ownership comes with adoption.
- Something running in production with no owner is a real defect. Exactly one case was found, the Runtime Adapter.

The document also corrects an earlier claim of its author's, and decides nothing: everything is submitted to the Decision Authority.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- **[I-1]** Ownership is treated as **evidence, not proof** of boundaries. The document revises its own inference from "one owner ⇒ one context" to a weaker evidential claim. This is the same move as "proposed ≠ validated": the author polices how strong an inference may be.
- **[I-2]** "Candidate-unowned" vs "operating-unowned" is a precise theoretical distinction:
  - ownership is a consequence of an adoption act (a grant), not a property of the artifact;
  - so a missing owner is a defect only when the artifact already has operational effect.

  This links ownership to the authority lifecycle (act → adoption → operation).
- **[I-3]** Hypothesis, not established: KnowledgeOS ownership boundaries = spaces. Evidence: n = 1, as the source says.
