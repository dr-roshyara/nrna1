## 0. In business language

The question: **can today's architecture support a command `knowledgeos init`** that would set up the platform for a new product? The assessment scores eleven platform responsibilities (from product discovery to capability evolution) on method, binding to one product, evidence of use, implementation, and a fifth dimension it adds: **enforcement**.

- **Its main contribution:** three kinds of gap that had been mixed up, distinguished by **what closes them**:
  - an **architectural** gap is closed by **modelling** (the architecture has no place for a responsibility);
  - a **governance** gap is closed by **a ruling** (a clause couples a reusable thing to one product);
  - an **evidence** gap is closed **only by doing it** (the thing exists but was never exercised).
- **Answer:** architecturally almost (one gap); evidentially no. `init` is premature: it would automate a process that has been done once, by a person.
- **The one architectural gap is "genesis":** the platform governs **change**, but has no route from an **idea** to the **first decision**, because its own rules require operational evidence that does not exist at the start. The gap "is invisible from inside a mature project, because genesis happened before the governance existed". A one-clause fix is offered; whether genesis is the platform's job at all is open.
- **The highest-leverage act** is a ruling: is SD-1 a product binding or a platform rule?
- **Maturity ≠ enforcement.** No responsibility is enforced by the platform; all its automation is advisory ("adoptable by habit, never by mechanism"). **Corrected:** the runtime does enforce 41 controls (19 "deny", 22 "ask"). So enforcement exists, but in the **runtime adapter**, not in the platform's capabilities: "Enforcement exists exactly where governance does not, and is absent exactly where it does."
- **Where things belong:** what goes into the kernel (only items that are domain-free, binding-free and evidence-free), into a product's PKS (bindings and evidence), and into the business product; what must never become a separate product.
- **Verdict:** "The architecture is not underdesigned. It is under-exercised — and nowhere enforcing." Do not design more.

## 11. [I] Interpretation (kept apart)

- [I-1] **Gap kinds and the two engines of change.** A governance gap is closed by a **ruling** (a governance act: granted). An evidence gap is closed **only by doing** (evidence: earned). This is the book's distinction between governance-act and evidential progression (Ch. 1 §1.6, RC-37a) applied to gaps. The third kind (closed by **modelling**) is neither an act nor evidence; it is design work.
- [I-2] **Genesis is the root-act problem at architecture level.** Ch. 1's authority model stops the regress of grants at **root acts**, which need no grant. The architecture has no equivalent: every route to a decision requires prior evidence. The proposed clause (the stakeholder's stated need as admissible evidence; the first decision Provisional/Proposed) would introduce exactly such a root. This bears on TODO T1-17 (Genesis), which this source names as OQ-S3.
- [I-3] **Enforcement and governance are disjoint** in this platform, by the author's corrected finding. This is a sharper form of Ch. 2 §2.10 ("the rules with meaning and the rules with enforcement are different sets"): here the two sets sit in **different components** (platform vs runtime adapter).
- [I-4] **Decomposition without materialization** is a classification that creates no new artifact. In Ch. 3 §3.4's terms, it changes what is known about knowledge without producing a new carrier.
