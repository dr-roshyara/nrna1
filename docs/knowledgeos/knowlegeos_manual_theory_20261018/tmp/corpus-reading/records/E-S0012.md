## 0. In business language

A cross-product test had seemed to "falsify" invariant I-4 (a derived projection never becomes authoritative), because in a hospital example a derived discharge summary *does* become authoritative. This document **withdraws that claim**:
- the summary became authoritative through **review and attestation**, not through being derived;
- "Governance created the authority, not the derivation";
- the model's mistake was to **conflate derivation with attestation**.

Before analysing gaps, it checks what exists:
- an authoritative lifecycle document with a state machine, role table and machine-checkable transition guards;
- **eight further lifecycles** elsewhere.

So the gap is **not** "no lifecycle".

**The precise gap, found by comparing two configuration files:**
- **document status** has a governed state machine (order, terminal marker, who may move it, guards);
- **authority** has none;
- so nothing governs who may move an item from "derived" to "authoritative", and the model cannot express "attested".

**The gap's open question is then withdrawn as ill-posed.** "authority" is really **two dimensions in one field**:
- immutable **provenance** (generated, derived);
- act-changed **standing** (authoritative, provisional, historical).

"Provenance CANNOT have a state machine; standing changes by governance ACTS."

**Classifying the gaps:**
- only **one of four** is genuinely about the ontology (a missing "specializes" relation);
- one is lifecycle (attestation);
- one is governance: **external obligations have no home in an ownership model**;
- one dissolves: a **deployment is a nested knowledge space**.

**Also found:**
- the authoritative lifecycle document lists "idea" and "research" as statuses, while the executable enum defines them as **types**, the very conflation the document warns against;
- some lifecycles may be duplicates.
