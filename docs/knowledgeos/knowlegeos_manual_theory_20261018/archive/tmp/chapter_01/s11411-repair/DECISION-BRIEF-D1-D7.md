## D-1: no adoption on record

- **Question.** A procedure is in use, but no record shows it was ever adopted. Is it "not current" or "cannot tell"?
- **Why it is a decision.** It follows from the open choice whether a **record creates authority** (C1-a) or only **evidences** it (C1-b).
  - Under C1-a the answer is plainly "never adopted".
  - Under C1-b the honest answer is "cannot tell". Pushed to its end, though, C1-b would make **every** "not current" doubtful, since an unrecorded re-adoption can never be ruled out.
- **Options:**
  - **H2 (minimal):** "cannot tell" only when **no** adoption is on record at all. Once any adoption is on record, the records are taken as complete for that claim. This is the original text's intended answer.
  - **H1 (uniform):** every answer is explicitly "on the record". "Not current" then always means "not shown to be current".
- **Consequences.** H2 keeps answers useful but makes an unstated completeness assumption, which it must now state. H1 is fully consistent, but no answer ever says more than "not shown".
- **Evidence.** AMS L49–51 leans to C1-a ("exists only as a grant"). IA L99–101 leans to C1-b ("a grant exists but was not recorded" is a real case). **No rule decides.**
- **Recommendation (AI).** H2 now, with its completeness assumption written down. Revisit together with C1.
- **Approval needed.** Choose H2 or H1, or defer it to the C1 decision.

## D-2: a record that does not say which permission was used

- **Question.** An approval is on record, but nothing says under which permission. Is that "no permission" or "cannot tell"?
- **Why it is almost not a decision.** The book promises that missing evidence is never reported as missing authority unless records **create** authority (C1-a). So under C1-b the answer **must** be "cannot tell". That is **derived**, not chosen.
- **Options:** tie this to C1 as derived · keep it as a free choice, which breaks the promise under C1-b.
- **Evidence.** The book's own promise (§1.14.1, "Five things"). **No source rule.**
- **Recommendation (AI).** Tie it to C1.
- **Approval needed.** Confirm only.

## D-3: delegation (three parts)

- **Question.**
  - **(c)** If the Chair's permission ends (expired, revoked, annulled), does the Secretary's delegated permission end with it?
  - **(a)** Can a delegated permission be **wider** in area or **longer** in time than the Chair's own?
  - **(b)** May anyone holding a permission pass it on, or only someone expressly allowed to?
- **Options and consequences:**
  - (c) **yes**: authority stays a tree rooted in a live permission, and a removed delegator cannot leave active delegates behind. The cost: when a term ends, delegated work in progress stops unless it is re-authorized.
  - (c) **no**: delegates survive, which is stable but means a removed person's delegates keep acting.
  - (c) **per kind of permission**: flexible, but needs a table the organisation maintains.
  - (a) **containment required**: no delegate exceeds the delegator. **Not required**: a delegation can enlarge authority, which is effectively issuing new authority under a delegation label.
  - (b) **right to delegate required**: prevents chains of sub-delegation nobody approved. **Not required**: any holder can create holders.
- **Evidence.** The source rules **only** that delegated and independent authority must be distinguished, and that "not every narrower grant is a delegation" (AMS L285). The book's §1.13.3 derives (a) and (b) and leans **yes** for (c), noting that "the sources do not rule on this". **No source rule for (a), (b) or (c).** Today's records have no delegation link at all.
- **Recommendation (AI).** (a) yes · (b) yes · (c) yes for delegated permissions, with an independent re-authorization route to keep work going.
- **Approval needed.** Adopt (a) and (b) as governance rules (today they are the book's derivations), and choose (c).

## D-4: the exact instant of expiry

- **Question.** The Chair's permission runs "until 31 March 12:00". Is an approval made at exactly 12:00 valid?
- **Options and consequences:**
  - **Half-open, valid up to but not at 12:00.** Consecutive periods (until 12:00, then from 12:00) neither overlap nor leave a gap, so exactly one permission holds at every instant.
  - **Closed, valid at 12:00.** Matches everyday "until and including", but two consecutive periods both hold at 12:00, so a handover instant has two holders.
- **Evidence.** IM requires only start < end (L131) and evaluates expiry against the moment of the question (L211–219). It does not say whether the end instant counts. AMS lists expiry as its own ending (L284) and says nothing on the boundary. **No source rule.** The original text's "half-open" was the author's convention, not a sourced rule.
- **Recommendation (AI).** Half-open, and make **date-only** periods explicit: "until 31 March" means up to the start of 1 April, so the everyday meaning is kept.
- **Approval needed.** Choose.

## D-5: can someone without authority use up a single-use permission?

- **Question.** A permission may be used once. An unauthorized person "uses" it first. Is the permission now spent?
- **Options and consequences:**
  - **Spent only by an authorized use:** nobody can exhaust someone else's permission by misusing it (the "nobody can freeze a decision" safeguard).
  - **Spent by any use:** anyone could block a single-use permission by an improper attempt.
  - **Consumption treated as a separate act needing its own authorization:** this conflicts with the platform record, which says consumption is "recorded, not newly authorized".
- **Evidence.** The one live CONSUMED record (book §1.13.2). **No source rule.**
- **Recommendation (AI).** Spent only by an authorized use.
- **Approval needed.** Choose. This is the open alternative C13.

## D-6: a revocation recorded late

- **Question.** The Chair's permission is revoked on Monday. The revocation is **recorded** on Wednesday. The Chair approves a procedure on Tuesday, recorded Tuesday. When judging "by the records as they stood at the time", from when does the revocation count against that procedure?
- **Options and consequences:**
  - **From when it was recorded (Wednesday):** Tuesday's approval was properly made on what was known, and it stops counting from Wednesday if the policy says revocations carry over. This protects good-faith acts and still lets the revocation take effect.
  - **From its own date, as the original text says:** for a revocation dated **before** the approval, this text never reaches the approval at all. It stays current for ever with no note. **That is a gap, not a policy.**
  - **Judge everything by today's records** (the other record view): Tuesday's approval was never valid. This is strict, but it undoes acts that were properly made on the information available.
- **Evidence.** The book's safeguard 3: late records exist, and the choice "is a policy the organisation must decide". **No source rule.**
- **Recommendation (AI).** From when it was recorded. This needs records to carry a **recording time**, which today's do not.
- **Approval needed.** Part of reserved decision 2 (record view). Choose this timing rule within it.

## D-7: two records that undo each other

- **Question.** X revokes Y's permission. Y then, with a record backdated to before X's act, annuls the permission X used. Judged by today's records, each act depends on the other.
- **Options and consequences:**
  - **"Cannot tell" for both:** honest, but the revoked person has frozen the revoker.
  - **No backdated effect against acts already on record:** no loops, but "void from the start" no longer reaches those acts.
  - **Judge "as the records stood at the time":** no loops can arise at all (proved for M1, pending review). X stands, Y's annulment fails.
- **Evidence.** None in the sources. This is a property of the model, found in review.
- **Recommendation (AI).**
  - make "as the records stood at the time" the default view;
  - where today's-records judgements are needed, report a loop as its own reason ("cycle"), never as a silent "cannot tell";
  - this ties to D-6 and reserved decision 2.
- **Approval needed.** Choose. This is the open alternative C10.
