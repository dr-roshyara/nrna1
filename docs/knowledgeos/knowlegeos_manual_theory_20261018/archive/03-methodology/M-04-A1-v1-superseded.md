*Not part of the approved method until the Principal approves it. The approved text above is unchanged. Rationale and the change record are in the methodology review of 2026-10-18 (`M-04-review-20261018.md`). Hypothetical simulations motivated A-1(b) and (c) only as stress tests; they are not KnowledgeOS findings.*

**(a) Conclusions no stronger than the evidence.** Refines step 4. For every important claim I ask five questions:
1. what kind of claim is it?
2. what evidence would support or refute it?
3. what evidence did I actually examine?
4. what conclusion does that evidence justify?
5. what remains assumed or uncertain?

The conclusion written into the book may not be stronger than the answer to question 4.

| Kind of claim | What it needs | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| source claim | the source passage | **only what the source states**, not that it is true |
| decision | the governing record | a decision **within its actual authority and scope** (proposal ≠ exploratory ruling ≠ adopted decision) |
| logical or mathematical claim | premises, derivation and a counterexample attempt; a proof for theorem-level claims | a conclusion **from stated premises** |
| implementation claim | code, configuration, records or tests | behaviour **for the paths, versions and conditions examined** |
| empirical claim | observations with a stated population, period and coverage | a finding **within that coverage**. *Absence of a record is not absence of the event* until coverage is known |
| researcher deduction | named premises and an explicit inference | a deduction, labelled as mine |
| hypothesis | a stated test | nothing yet; stays provisional |

**(b) Split compound statements.** One sentence or paragraph can hold several claims needing different checks; each is recorded and checked separately. *Covers a gap shown in simulation A: step 3 labels "each idea" but does not say compound statements must be split.*

**(c) Contradictions are not settled by date or by majority.** A newer document may be exploratory or outside its author's authority, and an independent reviewer's disagreement is settled by evidence and reasoning, not by vote. I keep both claims with their provenance, authority, scope and evidence, and revise the book only when the reasoning justifies it. *Clarifies step 3's "Contradiction" row; motivated by simulation B.*

**(d) A glossary of the book's terms.** One meaning per term, kept as an appendix and checked at every five-file review. *Evidence (confirmed, not hypothetical): both pilots found real term clashes. Pilot 1: "authority scope" meant two things. Pilot 2: "standing" meant two things.*

**(e) The completeness re-read covers the whole source**, including footnotes, tables, examples, code and qualifications, not only the passages already extracted. *States explicitly what step 6a implies; adds no new step.*

**(f) Audits are measured, not declared.** Each independent audit records:
- omissions (foundational and other, separately);
- interpretation errors;
- claims stated more strongly than their evidence;
- defects still present after correction;
- open conflicts with their reasons.

There is no zero-defect target. Severity decides the response, and a significant miss on a foundational idea can justify a pause.

**Limits, stated plainly:** completeness is a goal to be tested, not a status the register can certify; a coherent book is not yet a validated theory.
