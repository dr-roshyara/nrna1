# M-04 — How I work: the research method in business language

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-10-18 |
| Replaces | nothing. M-00 described the method *as planned*. This page describes the method *as actually used* after two pilots and the Principal's instructions |
| Status | **Approved as the working method by the Principal (2026-10-18)**, with three corrections incorporated: separate reviewer authorization · narrowed V-2 claim · source-to-register-to-book completeness check |

## The goal in one sentence

Turn about 3,000 scattered documents into **one well-reasoned book** that explains the KnowledgeOS theory. The book must say clearly, for every statement, whether the documents say it, whether we checked it, or whether it is our own reasoning.

## The working loop: one file at a time

**1. Check permission first.**
- Before touching a document I confirm I am allowed to read it.
- The main corpus is still locked until the approvals are recorded, so I work only on permitted background documents.
- Locked files, test material and password files are never opened, not even indirectly.

**2. Read to understand, not to copy.**
- I read the whole document and ask: what problem is it solving, what does it propose, what does it assume, what does it leave open?
- Ideas hidden in examples, tables or code count as much as headlined principles.

**3. Compare with what the book already says.** Each idea gets one label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New** | not yet in the book |
| **Repeat** | already explained; I point to where |
| **Refinement** | sharpens or narrows something already there |
| **Contradiction** | conflicts with the book; the earlier text gets revised or the conflict is kept visible |
| **Evidence or example** | supports or challenges an existing idea |
| **Open** | not yet understood well enough to place |

**4. Check that it is true, not just that it is written.**
- **Logic and mathematics:** I re-derive the argument and look for counterexamples.
- **Claims about software:** I look at the actual code, configuration or records. Examples:
  - I recounted all 144 authority records to test a claim based on 20;
  - I checked whether a check that claims to block actually blocks.
- **Decisions:** I separate ideas someone proposed, ideas decided only inside an exploratory track, and decisions formally adopted.
- **Unverifiable claims** keep a visible "not verified" mark. They are never quietly accepted or dropped.

**5. Write it into the book immediately.**
- I explain the idea in my own words: what it means, why it matters, how it connects, and what is still uncertain.
- If an existing section covers it, I improve that section instead of adding a new one.
- If the idea proves an earlier conclusion wrong, I revise the earlier text and note why.

**6. Keep a trace for every idea.**
- A register records, for each idea: where it came from, its label, how it was checked, and where it sits in the book.
- Rejected or out-of-scope ideas stay in the register with the reason.
- Nothing disappears silently.

**6a. Prove completeness against the source, not against the register** *(added 2026-10-18, Principal correction 3)*.
- After each file, I compare **three things directly**: the original source, the idea register and the relevant book sections.
- The register alone cannot prove completeness: it may lack an idea that was never noticed.
- Every meaningful idea must end with an explicit disposition:
  - integrated into the theory;
  - already explained elsewhere (with a pointer);
  - recorded as evidence;
  - kept as unresolved;
  - rejected with reasons;
  - excluded from the theory's scope, with a justification.
- **A file is not complete merely because every registered idea has a status.**
- Not every source statement becomes a principle. Repeats point to the existing explanation, corrections change the earlier text, and wrong ideas stay in the record with the reason they were rejected.

**7. Look back twice after every file.**
- **"Did I miss anything?"** I re-read the source against the book and register.
- **"Does the book still make sense?"** I check definitions, logic and labels across chapters.
- I fix what I find, and keep what I can't fix visible.

## Quality checks above the file level

- **After every five files:** a review of the chapters as one connected work, and a decision: **continue, correct, or pause**.
- **Independent audit by a fresh reviewer**, who checks:
  - that corrections were really made;
  - that references support the claims;
  - that the mathematics holds.

  The first such audit, of Chapters 0–2, is running now.
- **Every 100 corpus files, once access is open:** a fresh reviewer re-reads a random sample of the source files *before* seeing my notes, and a separate adjudicator settles disagreements.
  - The V-2 test demonstrated that independent re-extraction and adjudication **can** reveal omissions missed by the primary reader. That is evidence for using independent review at defined checkpoints. **It does not show that this is the only effective review method** *(Principal correction 2)*.
- **Reviewer access is separately authorized** *(Principal correction 1)*.
  - Opening corpus access for me does **not** automatically allow an independent reviewer to receive corpus material.
  - Before any reviewer receives restricted content, I verify that the Principal's separate written approval is recorded, together with the applicable filter and gate approvals.
  - **If it is missing, the restricted review does not take place.** Reviews of permitted background material are not affected.

## Four rules I hold myself to

1. **Source, check and opinion are always labelled separately.** "The document says", "I verified", "I deduced" and "this is a hypothesis" never blur.
2. **Repetition is not proof.** Ten documents saying the same thing may share one origin.
3. **"Unknown" is an answer.** Missing information is reported as missing, never filled in or treated as "no".
4. **The book matters more than the paperwork.** Registers and reviews exist only to keep the book honest.
