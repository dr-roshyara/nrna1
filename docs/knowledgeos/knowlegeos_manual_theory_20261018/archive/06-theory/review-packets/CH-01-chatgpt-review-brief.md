# Brief for the ChatGPT chapter review — Chapter 1 (with the related parts of Chapters 0 and 2)

**What to review:** `06-theory/manuscript/chapter_01/CH-01-knowledge-authority-execution.md`, with `chapter_00/CH-00-foundations.md`, `chapter_02/CH-02-rules-and-conflict.md` and `APPENDIX-glossary.md` for context.

**What the chapter is about, in one paragraph.** Chapter 1 develops how knowledge in an organisation gains, keeps and loses **authority**. The central rule: only a recorded human decision can make something count. Being executable, popular, old or stored by a machine never does. On that basis the chapter explains:
- when a decision counts **today**, not just when it was made (§1.14.1);
- when an automatic check may **stop** someone's work (§1.14.5);
- what it means for a decision to be **valid** versus **well-founded** (§1.17.2).

**Sources.** An AI-generated, exploratory set of architecture documents (`docs/knowledge_tranfer/`, August 2026). Its rulings are exploratory, not adopted organisational decisions. The chapter marks what the sources say (Src), what was verified (Ind), and what is the author's interpretation (Int), deduction (Ded) or hypothesis (Hyp).

**Please assess:**
1. **Conceptual clarity.** Can a business reader follow §1.14.1, §1.14.5 and §1.17.2? Each now starts with a plain-language summary. Do the summaries match the precise text?
2. **Logic.** Do the conclusions follow from the stated premises? In particular:
   - the formula for current authority (§1.14.1);
   - the conditions for an admissible block, which hold only if premise P-E is accepted (§1.14.5).
3. **Completeness.** Is anything important about authority, its endings, enforcement or reasons missing?
4. **Traceability.** Are claims tied to their sources, and are inferences marked as such?
5. **Coherence** with Chapters 0 and 2 and with the glossary (one meaning per term).

**Deliberately left open (please do not treat as defects, but say if they are framed badly):**
- **R1, ending policy:** does the end of an authority (revocation, expiry, suspension …) also end the decisions made under it? Per kind of decision.
- **R2, premise P-E:** should a machine never produce the effect of a decision unless a person with authority decided it?
- **R3, I-2 "reasoned":** does a decision recorded without any reason still count?
- **R4:** does making a rule mandatory already authorize blocking on it?
- **L-RT, record time:** if an authority is later found to have been issued in error, are decisions made under it void from the start, or only from then on?

**Known limits:**
