# EKS-32 — A ruling that fixes the size of the persistence kernel exists only in the working notes of the lane that consumes it

**Status:** **BACKLOG · GOVERNANCE TRACEABILITY EXPOSURE** — registered from `P-82` (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/100-P82-…`). ⛔ **Not commissioned; activation requires a Human/PO-ARB authorization act.**
**Class:** operating-model problem — where governance decisions are recorded.
**Registered by:** Lane T (theory extraction), 2026-09-09.

> ### ⛔ **This item records a problem and a candidate requirement. It commissions nothing, questions no decision, and proposes no register format.**

---

## 1 · The problem in plain terms

A research programme has spent several days establishing **how many things a knowledge system must not
lose**. Its headline result is a number: **eleven**.

⭐⭐⭐ **That number is eleven and not ten because of a single decision, taken once, by a person.**

The decision itself is sound, it was properly taken, and the lane that received it recorded it
carefully — it wrote down what was decided, who decided it, what the decision does and does not cover,
that it is **a decision rather than a measurement**, and under what condition it would have to be
re-confirmed. ⭐ **As an act of intellectual honesty the record is exemplary.**

$$\boxed{\textbf{But it was written into the } \mathbf{research\ lane's\ own\ working\ notes} \textbf{, and nowhere else.}}$$

⚠️ **The estate keeps a place for exactly this kind of act.** There is a governance folder; it holds
nine records; several of them are explicit *authorization* and *adoption* decisions, filed in pairs, in
the estate's normal way. ⛔ **This decision is not among them.** The most recent dated entry there
predates it by two weeks.

## 2 · What actually happened

| | |
|---|---|
| **the question** | a research derivation reached a fork: one reading of an existing decision record gives **eleven** obligations, the other gives **ten** |
| ⭐ **the lane could not settle it** | the document at issue belongs to a **different work stream**, and the lane said so plainly: it *"cannot resolve it"* |
| **the ruling** | the human principal decided, as the programme's governance authority |
| ⭐⭐ **how it was recorded** | as the **first formal admission record** the lane ever wrote — a good procedure, invented for the purpose and used correctly |
| ⛔ **where it was recorded** | ⭐⭐⭐ **only there.** The governance folder has no entry |
| **the consequence carried forward** | every downstream result is stamped as *"derived from a stipulated premise"*, correctly — **but the premise itself is unfindable from outside the lane** |

## 3 · Why this is a business problem

**a. The programme cannot be handed over.** The whole point of this work is that it eventually goes to
a governance body to be adjudicated and frozen. ⭐⭐ **Whoever runs that act will look in the governance
folder for the decisions the theory rests on. The one that fixes its headline number is not there.**

**b. A reader outside the lane reaches the wrong conclusion — and one already did.** Several documents
were written **before** the ruling and correctly state the question as open. ⭐ **A later review read
one of those, did not know the ruling existed, and reported a governance conflict that does not
exist.** ⚠️ **That wrong finding was published and stood until the next review withdrew it.** The
documents were not at fault; the ruling was simply not where anyone would look.

**c. The decision cannot be maintained.** The lane's own record notes that if the other work stream
changes the document the ruling is about, **nothing will tell this lane.** ⭐ Combined with
non-registration, the decision is **invisible from both directions** — absent from the place that
records decisions, and disconnected from the thing it decides. *(The change-notification half is
already tracked as `EKS-13`; this item is about the registration half.)*

**d. Nobody can audit what the theory assumes.** The programme is scrupulous about marking which of its
results rest on decisions rather than evidence. ⭐⭐ **But that marking is only useful if the decisions
can be enumerated**, and today enumerating them means reading a hundred research documents.

**e. It is not an isolated case.** The programme's own forward plan already records that **four
ratification stamps and two decision records sit in research lanes rather than in the register**, and
proposes an act to move them. ⭐⭐⭐ **This ruling is a seventh, taken after that list was drawn up — so
the leak is ongoing, not historical.**

## 4 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **The decision is not questioned.** Its content, its author's standing, and its correctness are
outside this record entirely.

⛔ **The lane did not act improperly.** It recorded more than most acts of this kind ever get: the
authority, the class of source, the explicit marking as a stipulation, the scope limits, and the
revocation condition. ⭐ **The gap is that a well-made record was filed in the wrong drawer** — and
arguably in the **only** drawer the lane had, since no procedure told it otherwise.

⛔ **This is not about the governance folder's format.** Whether the entry should be a decision record,
a pointer, or an index row is a design question and is not proposed here.

⛔ **This is not a duplicate of the closed scope question.** That item asked *whether any authority has
reach over this material* and was closed as working-as-designed. ⭐ **This item takes the opposite
starting point: an authority DID act, properly, and the act was not registered.**

## 5 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> ⭐ **When a governance ruling is admitted as a premise of research work, the fact of the ruling — what
> was decided, by whom, its scope, and that it is a decision rather than a finding — should be
> recorded in the estate's governance register, not only in the record of the lane that consumes it.**

⭐⭐ Two properties matter more than the format:
1. **Registration and consumption are different acts.** A lane's admission record says *"we now reason
   from this"*; a register entry says *"this was decided."* ⭐ **The lane can only write the first.**
2. ⭐ **The register entry must carry the decision's own qualification.** This ruling was admitted
   *explicitly as a stipulation, not as evidence.* **A register that recorded it as a finding would be
   worse than no entry**, because it would silently upgrade the whole theory's status.
