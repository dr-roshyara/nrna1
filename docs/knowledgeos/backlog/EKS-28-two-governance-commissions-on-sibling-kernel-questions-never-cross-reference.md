## 1 · The problem in plain terms

The programme has, at different times, authorized **two separate governance commissions** to answer
what is, at heart, the same question asked about two different halves of one system:

- **Which object is the canonical minimal kernel?** — adjudicated through ruling `D-FA-4` (part of
  `GN-31`), which classifies the 8-primitive candidate ("M₄₉") as an "L2 candidate" and explicitly
  leaves **"membership at the object level... open (OQ-2)."** This finding is stated in the corpus's
  own ratified book (*"Three Kernels,"* Part III.9) as a deliberate, permanent layering — the
  architecture chooses not to crown one kernel, and names OQ-2 as the specific thing left open.

- **Which operations are the canonical minimal registry?** — adjudicated through a later, separate
  commission (`HPA ruling GN-79`, `commission GN-80`, independent falsification `GN-83/86`, decision
  procedure `GN-85`, dated 2026-08-31). This commission runs a real, executed minimality test over the
  system's *operations* (as opposed to its *state object*), reaches a first verdict, has that verdict
  independently falsified and corrected, and formally hard-stops with the same shape of finding: real
  derivation work exists, but the registry is **"NOT ESTABLISHED,"** blocked on named prior decisions.

**Both commissions operate over the same ratified primitives, the same twelve invariants (I-1…I-12),
and the same decision-contract 6-tuple. Neither commission's own deliverables cite, reference, or
appear aware of the other's existence or findings**, despite being separated by only a few days and
sitting in adjacent parts of the same governed directory tree.

## 2 · Why this is a business problem, not a technical curiosity

**a. The two open questions are not independent, and treating them as independent risks incompatible
answers.** The operation-registry commission's own work depends on typing several operations (e.g.
`Reject`, `Commit`, `ComputeZero`) whose *existence and shape* is exactly what the state-kernel
question (OQ-2) would also need to settle if it were ever closed. A future act that resolves OQ-2 by
selecting a specific object shape could silently invalidate assumptions the operation-registry
commission's own candidate universe already depends on, and vice versa — because nothing connects the
two decision records to each other.

**b. Two named prior-decision registers now exist in parallel, addressing overlapping ground, with no
shared index.** The state-kernel side names its own open question as OQ-2, tracked in `FA-6`'s open-
questions register. The operation-registry side names **ten** (later revised to **eleven**, with
`P-11` found to be prior to all the others) separate named prior decisions (`P-1`…`P-11`), each with
its own consequence table. A reader — or a future commission — trying to understand "what must be
decided before KnowledgeOS has a canonical kernel" has to independently discover and reconcile *two*
separate registers, in two separate directories, authorized by two separate rulings, to get the full
picture.

**c. This is the same failure pattern the estate has already caught once, now recurring at the
governance level rather than the research level.** The operation-registry commission's own
independent falsification pass found that an earlier, separate research thread's "14-forced/18-upper"
result had been *widely cited as derived* when it was in fact a hand-authored table — a citation
failure inside one research lane. This ticket is the same failure mode one level up: two *governed,
HPA-authorized* commissions, working on sibling questions, in the same period, never citing each
other.

**d. The two commissions independently reached a strikingly similar terminal shape** — "substantial
real work exists; the registry is not established; here is a precise, named, ordered list of prior
decisions blocking it" — **without either one knowing the other had reached the same shape of
conclusion.** That convergence is scientifically interesting (it suggests the underlying obstruction
is structural, not accidental to either commission's own method) but it is currently invisible,
because nothing links the two records together for a reader to notice it.

## 3 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **Not a claim that either commission did anything wrong.** Both are unusually rigorous, self-
critical, and explicit about their own limits — exactly the discipline this estate asks for.

⛔ **Not a call to merge the two tracks, or to have one commission re-adjudicate the other's ground.**
The state-object question and the operation question are genuinely different questions, and nothing
here proposes collapsing them.

⛔ **Not a claim about which ruling should win if a conflict is later found.** No conflict has been
found; the point is that nothing currently exists to *check* for one.

## 4 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> **When a new governance commission is authorized to adjudicate any question touching the system's
> kernel, primitives, invariants, or canonical object shape, the authorizing act should require a
> check against any other commission (open or closed) touching the same ratified surface, and the
> resulting decision record should cross-reference it — even if only to state "no conflict found."**

The cheapest version costs one lookup per commission: does an open-questions or prior-decision
register already exist for adjacent ground, and if so, does this commission's own work depend on
anything it leaves open?

## 6 · Urgency

**Moderate, and growing with each new commission.** Neither current commission is blocked by the
other's absence from its own record — both reached usable, honest verdicts on their own. The risk is
prospective: the next commission authorized to touch kernel-adjacent ground has no mechanism today
that would surface either of these two records to it automatically.
