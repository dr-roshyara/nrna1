# EKS-26 — A completed survey's coverage claim keeps its confident wording while the thing it surveyed keeps growing

**Status:** **BACKLOG · EVIDENCE-DECAY EXPOSURE** — registered from `P-73` (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/91-P73-…`). ⛔ **Not commissioned; activation requires a Human/PO-ARB authorization act.**
**Class:** operating-model problem — durability of coverage statements.
**Registered by:** Lane T (theory extraction), 2026-09-09.

> ### ⛔ **This item records a problem and a candidate requirement. It commissions nothing, proposes no re-survey, and criticises no survey.**

---

## 1 · The problem in plain terms

When a review surveys a body of material, it records how much it covered. ⭐ **Good surveys in this
estate do this unusually well** — one states its file counts per area and even how deeply each part was
actually read, rather than implying uniform thoroughness.

⭐⭐⭐ **But a coverage statement is a measurement of a moving thing, and it is written as if it were
permanent.** The survey says *"this area contains 413 documents and we inventoried all of them."* Twelve
days later the area contains **564**. The sentence has not changed; what it is about has.

$$\boxed{\textbf{The claim stays confident. Its truth quietly drops from } \mathbf{100\%\ to\ 73\%.}}$$

⚠️ **And nothing marks the change.** A reader finding the survey today reads a completed inventory and
has no reason to suspect it now describes fewer than three-quarters of the material.

## 2 · It has happened three times, in three different forms

| | |
|---|---|
| **a** | A **governance-recorded** census inventoried an area at **413** documents and declared coverage complete. That area now holds **564** at the same level — **151 added after the survey closed** — and the closing report is unchanged. |
| **b** | A review published per-area file counts as its coverage statement. ⭐ **They were correct when taken.** Within days three of the areas had grown, so a later review citing those counts was citing stale figures. |
| **c** | A review's coverage statement was **narrower than it read**, because a second folder of the same name existed. ⭐ That instance is recorded separately; ⚠️ **the decay here is a different cause with the same effect.** |

⭐ **All three involve careful work whose statements were true when written.**

## 3 · Why this is a business problem

**a. It converts an honest measurement into a misleading one, with no one at fault.** ⭐⭐ Nobody wrote
anything false. **The claim ages into inaccuracy**, which is harder to catch than an error, because there
is no mistake to find.

**b. Completed surveys are exactly what later decisions lean on.** ⚠️ A survey is cited *because* it is
finished. **The more authoritative it looks, the less likely anyone re-measures it** — so the decay is
worst where the reliance is heaviest.

**c. It undermines negative findings specifically.** ⭐⭐⭐ *"We inventoried the area and found no
treatment of X"* is a valuable statement. **If a quarter of the area arrived after the inventory, the
finding no longer supports the conclusion drawn from it** — and this estate's method leans heavily on
negative findings.

**d. The gate may be closed.** ⚠️ In case (a) the survey belonged to a phase that formally **completed
and stopped**. So the material added afterwards sits behind a closed gate: **nothing is scheduled to look
at it, and the closing report says the area was covered.**

**e. The remedy costs one line.** ⭐⭐ A coverage statement that records **what it counted and when**
lets any later reader re-run the count in seconds and see the drift. **A statement without a date cannot
be checked at all.**

## 4 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **No survey is criticised** — the ones involved are the most careful artifacts in the estate, and the
best of them already record read-depth honestly. ⛔ **No re-survey is requested** — that is work, and a
backlog item may not commission it. ⛔ **Growth is not a fault**; an active research corpus is supposed
to grow. ⛔ **And this is not a claim that any specific past conclusion is wrong** — only that the
coverage supporting it is no longer the coverage stated.

## 5 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> ⭐ **A coverage statement should record the count and the date it was taken, and a claim of absence
> should say which population and which moment it is an absence in.**

⭐⭐ **The second half is what protects the negative findings.** *"Not present"* is a statement about a
population at a time; without both, it cannot be re-checked, and it silently becomes *"not present,
ever."*

## Fourth instance, 2026-09-09 — and this one is inside the estate's most disciplined artifact

⭐⭐ **The instances above concerned document counts. This one concerns a findings register, and the gap
is larger.**

The independent verification programme keeps an artifact index describing what it produced. That index
records its findings as running **from the first to the nineteenth**. ⭐⭐⭐ **The findings themselves run
to the eighty-eighth — eighty-eight distinct identifiers.**

$$\boxed{\textbf{The index accounts for } \mathbf{19}\textbf{. The programme produced } \mathbf{88}\textbf{. } \mathbf{Sixty\text{-}nine\ findings\ are\ not\ mentioned\ where\ a\ reader\ would\ look.}}$$

### Why this instance is the most consequential of the four

**a. It is not a count of documents but a count of results.** ⭐⭐ A stale file count costs a reader some
coverage confidence. **A stale findings register hides conclusions** — and among the unlisted ones are a
genuine mathematical error, a genuine falsification of five of eight stated laws, six internal
contradictions inside a single document, and an identifier-range collision between two parts of the work.

**b. The index is the only map.** ⚠️ That programme's material is not timestamp-ordered, so **the index
is how anyone navigates it.** When the index under-reports by three-quarters, the navigation
under-reports by three-quarters.

**c. It compounds with the programme being stopped.** ⭐⭐⭐ The same programme is recorded elsewhere in
this backlog as **halted pending a supervisory review that has not happened**. So the situation is: a
completed body of work, waiting for a reader, **whose own index tells that reader it is a quarter of its
actual size.**

**d. And it was found only by counting.** ⭐ Nothing declared the gap. It surfaced because a review
extracted every finding identifier and compared the range against the index — **a check that takes
seconds and that nothing asks for.**

### What this does *not* change

⛔ **The programme is not criticised** — it produced far more than it advertised, which is the opposite
of overclaiming. ⛔ **The index is not accused of error**; it was almost certainly accurate when written,
which is precisely this item's subject. ⛔ **No re-index is requested.**

**Evidence:** `docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/93-P75-…` §3, §8 — the eighty-eight identifiers, the
index's nineteen, and the four named findings among the unlisted range.
