# EKS-30 — A research programme has no register of which of its own questions are settled, so the same question is commissioned and paid for twice

**Status:** **BACKLOG · OPERATIONAL EXPOSURE (DUPLICATE SPEND & CONTRADICTORY RECORD)** — registered from `P-81` (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/99-P81-…`). ⛔ **Not commissioned; activation requires a Human/PO-ARB authorization act.**
**Class:** operating-model problem — research **output** management.
**Registered by:** Lane T (theory extraction), 2026-09-09.

> ### ⛔ **This item records a problem and a candidate requirement. It commissions nothing, proposes no tooling, and criticises no document.**

---

## 1 · The problem in plain terms

A research programme has produced **ninety-nine consecutive numbered reviews** over several days. Each
one answers a narrow question, reaches a verdict, and ends by naming the next question. The record is
careful: every verdict is stated, every open item is carried forward, nothing is deleted.

⭐⭐⭐ **But there is no list of which questions have been ANSWERED.**

The verdicts exist — one per document, ninety-nine documents. To know whether a question has already
been settled, somebody must remember that it was, or open the right file. ⭐ **Nobody maintains a page
that says *"this question is closed, here is the verdict, here is where."***

$$\boxed{\textbf{The programme tracks what it still has to do. ⛔ It does not track what it has already finished.}}$$

## 2 · What actually happened

| | |
|---|---|
| **on day one** | review number 33 was commissioned to settle a specific structural question. It settled it, with a full argument, and recorded the exact condition under which the answer would have to be revisited |
| **the next day** | review number 98 reached the same question from a different direction and ended by proposing it as *"the load-bearing question the theory leaves open"* |
| **review 99** | was commissioned to answer it — and discovered, in the first minute, that **the file name of review 33 is the question** |
| ⭐⭐ **worse** | review 98 also **endorsed a structural claim that review 34 had already dismantled**, and that endorsement stood in the published record until review 99 withdrew it |

⭐ **All of this happened inside one directory, between documents written by the same lane, one day
apart.**

## 3 · Why this is a business problem and not a filing quibble

**a. The work is bought twice.** Two full reviews were spent on a closed question. ⭐ **In a programme
whose entire output is reviews, re-answering is the most expensive possible failure** — it consumes the
same scarce effort as new work and adds nothing.

**b. The record briefly contradicted itself, in public.** Between review 98 and review 99 the
programme's own output asserted two incompatible things about the same structure. ⚠️ **Anyone reading
only the newer document would have taken the wrong one** — and newer documents are exactly what readers
reach for.

**c. Later work inherits the confusion.** A question re-opened without citing its earlier closure looks
like a *genuinely* open question to everyone downstream. Plans, priorities and estimates get built on
it. ⭐ **This programme's forward plan lists "test the third individuation axis" as remaining work; it
was finished on the previous day.**

**d. The programme cannot report its own progress.** Asked *"what is settled?"*, the only honest answer
today is *"read all ninety-nine."* ⭐⭐ **A body of work that cannot state its own conclusions cannot be
handed to anyone** — not to a reviewer, not to a successor, not to the governance act that must
eventually adjudicate it.

**e. It gets monotonically worse.** ⭐ Ninety-nine documents today. Every new one adds a verdict to
remember and lengthens the list nobody is keeping. ⚠️ **The failure rate rises with the programme's own
productivity.**

## 4 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **The verdicts are not missing.** Every one is stated plainly, in a consistent vocabulary, with its
own conditions and its own falsification criteria. ⭐ **The quality of the closures is not in question —
review 33's closure was correct, and review 99 confirmed it independently.**

⛔ **The file names are not bad.** The file name *was* the question. ⭐ **A better naming convention
would not have helped, because nobody read the directory listing.**

⛔ **This is not a search problem.** ⚠️ **This is the distinguishing point.** The material was not
merely findable — it was in the same folder, five files from a document that had just been read
end to end. **Better searching does not fix "nobody thought to look at what we ourselves produced."**

⛔ **No tool, index format, database or automation is proposed here.** Designing the remedy is outside
this record.

## 5 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> ⭐ **A research programme should maintain a single, current register of its own settled questions —
> the question, the verdict, the conditions the verdict depends on, and where it was decided — and
> consulting that register should be an owed step before a new question is commissioned.**

⭐⭐ Two properties matter more than the format:
1. **It must be one place, not ninety-nine.** A verdict recoverable only by reading the document that
   produced it is not registered.
2. ⭐ **A closure must carry its conditions.** Most of this programme's verdicts are explicitly
   *boundary-relative* and record what would reopen them. **A register that drops those conditions
   would be worse than none**, because it would present conditional closures as final ones.

## 7 · Urgency

⚠️ **Rising, and mechanically so.** The programme is at ninety-nine artifacts and adding roughly one an
hour during active sessions. ⭐⭐ **The first duplicate cost two reviews; the register that would have
prevented it would have taken a fraction of one.**

⭐ **And there is a near-term trigger:** the programme's own forward plan reaches a governance
adjudication stage at which the accumulated results must be handed over as a body. ⛔ **That handover
cannot happen from ninety-nine files.**
