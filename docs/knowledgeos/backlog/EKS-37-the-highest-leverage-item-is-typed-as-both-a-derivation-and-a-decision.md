# EKS-37 — The programme's highest-leverage open item is described as a *derivation* by one authority and a *decision* by another, so nobody can be asked to do it

**Status:** **BACKLOG · WORK-ROUTING EXPOSURE** — registered from `P-86` (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/107-P86-…`). ⛔ **Not commissioned; activation requires a Human/PO-ARB authorization act.**

**Registered by:** Lane T (theory extraction), 2026-09-09.

> ### ⛔ **This item records a problem and a candidate requirement. It commissions nothing, settles nothing, and takes no position on which characterisation is right.**

## 1 · The problem in plain terms

One item sits at the root of the programme's remaining work. Everything downstream — how many
evaluation outcomes the theory needs, whether contradiction can be represented, whether the
satisfaction test can ever be computed — depends on it first being settled.

⭐ **It is the list of distinctions the system is *required* to be able to make.**

$$\boxed{\textbf{Two authoritative documents describe it in ways that send it to } \mathbf{different\ owners.}}$$

| document | what it says | who that means |
|---|---|---|
| the programme's forward plan | enumerating the required-distinction set is ⭐ **"a derivation, not a decision"**, and **"the most evidenced blocker in the estate"** — blocked in **66 of 66** tested worlds | ⭐ **research** |
| the experiment report the plan itself commissioned | required distinctions are ⭐ **"the DECISION that sets everything"**, placed at the **root** of the dependency chain | ⭐ **governance** |

⛔ **Both are current. Neither cites the other. Nobody has reconciled them.**

## 2 · Why the difference is not academic

**A derivation and a decision are done by different people, with different authority, and produce
different kinds of artifact.**

| | a derivation | a decision |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | a researcher | an authorised body or person |
| **what evidence settles it** | the corpus | ⭐ **nothing — it is chosen** |
| **what it produces** | a result that can be checked | ⭐ **a ruling that must be recorded** |
| **what happens if the corpus is silent** | ⭐ **the work is blocked** | ⭐ **the work proceeds; silence is the reason a decision is needed** |

⭐⭐⭐ **So the same silence in the corpus means "we are stuck" under one reading and "this is exactly
when someone must choose" under the other.** ⛔ **The two readings give opposite instructions.**

## 3 · Why this is a business problem

**a. The next piece of work cannot be commissioned.** ⭐ Asking a researcher to *derive* something that
is in fact a choice produces an unresolvable search. ⛔ Asking a governance body to *rule* on something
that is in fact derivable produces an arbitrary ruling where an answer existed. **Both waste the
effort, and neither failure is obvious while it is happening.**

**b. Everything downstream inherits the stall.** ⭐⭐ The measured result is that **the minimum number of
evaluation outcomes the theory needs is a computation over this very set**, and it ranges from **three
to twenty-one** depending on which distinctions are required. ⛔ **So a large block of work sits behind
an item whose owner is undetermined.**

**c. It has already caused visible motion in the wrong place.** ⭐ Several recent efforts debated
whether a particular evaluation should have two, three or four outcomes. ⭐⭐⭐ **That question has no
fixed answer — the number falls out of the required-distinction set — so the debate could not have
been settled by the people having it.**

**d. Nobody currently owns the reconciliation itself.** Each document is correct within its own lane.
⭐ **The conflict exists only between them, and no one is responsible for the space between two
lanes' vocabularies.**

**e. The evidence to settle it is already written.** ⭐⭐ Four documents in a readable folder analyse the
required-distinction set directly. **This is not a research gap; it is an unasked question.**

## 4 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **Neither characterisation is wrong here.** It is entirely possible that part of the set is derivable
and part must be chosen — ⭐ **which would itself be a useful answer, and is not what either document
says.**

⛔ **This is not a request to settle the item.** Whether the set is derived or decided, and what it
contains, are outside this record.

⛔ **This is not about a missing register entry.** Both statements are recorded, clearly, in their own
places. ⭐ **The defect is that they disagree and nothing brings them together.**

⛔ **No process, arbitration procedure or vocabulary is proposed here.**

## 5 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> ⭐ **Before an open item is queued, it should carry an agreed type — derivation, decision, experiment
> or definition — and where two sources type it differently, the disagreement should be visible on the
> item rather than discoverable only by reading both sources.**

⭐⭐ Two properties matter more than the format:
1. **The type determines the owner.** ⛔ An item without an agreed type **cannot be assigned**, however
   well it is described.
2. ⭐ **"Partly derivable, partly chosen" must be expressible.** A vocabulary that forces a single label
   will produce exactly this conflict again on the next item that genuinely has both parts.

## Appended 2026-09-09 — the conflict is now **diagnosed**, and the item stands

**From `P-87`** (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/108-P87-…`), filed hours after this item.

⭐⭐⭐ **The two descriptions refer to one object at two layers, and the choice sits one level higher
than either states.**

The programme's own completeness theorem reads:

> *"Let `𝒯` be the set of minimal operational tasks required of KnowledgeOS. `ℛ_req` is structurally
> complete **iff** every minimal information requirement for all `τ ∈ 𝒯` is mapped to a non-collapsing
> partition in `ℛ_req`."*

$$\boxed{\begin{array}{c}\textbf{So } \mathcal R_{req} \textbf{ } \mathbf{IS} \textbf{ derivable — } \mathbf{given}\ \mathcal T. \textbf{ And } \mathcal T \textbf{ is the decision.}\\[4pt] \textbf{⭐⭐ The forward plan is right that the } \mathbf{enumeration} \textbf{ is a derivation.}\\ \textbf{⭐⭐ The experiment report is right that a } \mathbf{decision} \textbf{ sets everything.}\\ \textbf{⛔ Neither names } \mathcal T\textbf{, so both appear to be talking about the same layer.}\end{array}}$$

⭐ **And `𝒯` is *exemplified, not enumerated*** — the corpus gives *"**including** contradiction
isolation, Zero-state invariant checking, epistemic query resolution, and temporal garbage
collection"*: **four exemplars behind an open list.**

### Why the item is not withdrawn

⛔ **The diagnosis does not remove the exposure.** ⭐ **Nothing in the estate records that these two
statements are about different layers** — a reader still meets one description or the other and still
routes the work to a different owner. ⚠️ **And the diagnosis moves the question rather than closing it:
the same *derivation-or-decision* problem now applies to `𝒯`, which has no owner either.**

⭐⭐ **What changes is the remedy's target.** §5's candidate requirement asked for an agreed type on the
item. ⭐ **It should be read as applying to `𝒯` first** — because typing `ℛ_req` while `𝒯` is untyped
merely relocates the same ambiguity.

## Appended 2026-09-09 — the question is now **answerable**, and the item is *closer to actionable*

**From `P-88`** (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/109-P88-…`).

⭐⭐⭐ **At the layer where the choice actually sits, the corpus has already answered this item's
question.**

A dedicated audit of the operation family states, in its own boxed conclusion:

> ⭐ **"The SCHEMA is derivable (and largely derived). MANDATORY MEMBERSHIP is not. No route derives
> membership from already-ratified material."**

and records that **eight derivation routes were tried**, that the only route with no unmet precondition
is *"declare mandatory membership"*, classified **NORMATIVE**, and that the resulting act has a named
owner: **ARB**.

$$\boxed{\begin{array}{c}\textbf{So the answer for this layer is } \mathbf{DECISION}\textbf{, the owner is } \mathbf{named}\textbf{,}\\ \textbf{and } \mathbf{eight\ attempts\ to\ avoid\ needing\ it\ are\ already\ on\ the\ record.}\end{array}}$$

⭐ **A further correction it supplies:** *"not enumerated" ≠ "not closed"*. **The classification is
closed; the membership is not.** ⚠️ **So the work owed is not more enumeration — it is a rule saying
which members are mandatory.** ⛔ **Enumerating harder cannot close it, and no research act can.**

### Why the item still stands

⛔ **Nothing connects that answer to the statements that raised this conflict.** The documents that
type the item as *"a derivation"* and as *"the DECISION that sets everything"* are in a different lane
from the audit that settled the layer beneath them, and **none of the three cites another**. ⭐ **A
reader arriving at any one of them still routes the work to a different owner.**

⭐⭐ **What changes:** this item is no longer an open research question. **It is a reconciliation that
someone can perform today**, from three documents that already exist — ⚠️ **and one of them lives
outside the folders the reading lane is routinely pointed at**, which is part of why the three have
never met.

⛔ **No position is taken on the ARB routing, and no act is proposed.**
