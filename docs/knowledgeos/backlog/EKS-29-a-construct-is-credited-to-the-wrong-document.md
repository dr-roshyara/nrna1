# EKS-29 — An eleven-part construct is credited throughout to a document that contains five parts and none of the other eight

**Status:** **BACKLOG · ATTRIBUTION EXPOSURE** — registered from `P-79` (`docs/knowledgeos/theory-extraction/97-P79-…`). ⛔ **Not commissioned; activation requires a Human/PO-ARB authorization act.**
**Class:** operating-model problem — provenance of a research construct within one work stream.
**Registered by:** Lane T (theory extraction), 2026-09-09.

> ### ⛔ **This item records a problem and a candidate requirement. It commissions nothing, proposes no renaming, and questions nothing about the construct's content.**

---

## 1 · The problem in plain terms

A work stream produced a set of **eleven obligations** and has referred to it, consistently, as coming
from **one particular document**. That document is where the work began, and the attribution has never
been questioned.

⭐⭐⭐ **The document contains five of the eleven.** The other six were created three reviews later, in a
different document, and the originating document **does not contain their names at all** — not once.

$$\boxed{\textbf{Eleven parts are credited to a document holding } \mathbf{five}\textbf{, which never mentions the other } \mathbf{six}.}$$

⭐ **Two intermediate documents were checked as well**, in case the split happened gradually. It did not:
they mention the six no more than the original does — **zero times each.** The whole split occurred in a
single later document.

## 2 · Why this is a business problem, not a citation nicety

**a. It attributes work to the wrong stage, and stages carry different weight.** ⭐⭐ Work done at the
outset of an investigation and work done after three rounds of internal challenge are **not equally
settled**. Crediting the later work to the earlier document **makes six of the eleven look older, and
therefore steadier, than they are.**

**b. It hides which parts have been through challenge.** ⭐⭐⭐ The five original parts were argued,
attacked and defended over dozens of reviews. **The six later ones inherited that reputation without
having earned it separately** — and a reader tracing the construct to the original document finds no
argument for them there, because there is none.

**c. It makes the record unauditable in the one way that matters.** ⚠️ Anyone asking *"where was this
obligation established, and what challenged it?"* is sent to a document that does not contain it.
**The trail ends at the wrong place**, and the reader must either guess or give up.

**d. It has already propagated.** ⭐ The misattribution appears in roughly **forty** documents produced
by that work stream, and in **one item on this backlog**. ⚠️ **Every additional citation makes the
correction more expensive**, and none of them is wrong on purpose — they are all copying the same
inherited phrase.

**e. And the correct source is known and unambiguous.** ⭐⭐ This is not a case of lost provenance. **The
document where the six were created is identified, dated, and holds them by name.** The remedy is
naming it.

## 3 · What is *not* the problem

⛔ **Nothing about the eleven obligations themselves is questioned here** — not their content, their
evidence, or their standing. ⛔ **No renaming is proposed**; the construct's *name* is a separate matter
already recorded elsewhere on this list. ⛔ **Nobody misled anyone**: the attribution is a natural
shorthand that hardened into a fact. ⛔ **And no re-citation of forty documents is requested** — that
would be work, which a backlog item may not commission.

## 4 · Candidate requirement (a direction, not a design)

> ⭐ **A construct assembled across several documents should be attributed to where each part was
> actually established — and where a shorthand is used for the whole, it should say which document holds
> which parts.**

⭐⭐ **The cheapest sufficient version is one line wherever the construct is introduced**, naming both
documents and which parts came from each. **It costs nothing and restores the trail.**

## 6 · Urgency

⭐ **Low cost, low urgency, and it only grows.** Nothing breaks today. ⚠️ **But the fix is one line and
the cost of the situation is that six of eleven obligations cannot be traced to where they were
made** — and that matters most at exactly the moment someone tries to rely on them.
