# 2. Why this text, for this audit

The audit's subject is **what may be discarded while preserving what the inquiry requires.** The
Gītā's chapter 2 contains a sustained treatment of exactly that distinction — between what is
**discarded** and what is **preserved through** the discarding.

# 3. The loci

## 3.1 `vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni` — the reduction that preserves the inquiry · 2.22

> *as a person casts off worn-out garments and takes on new ones …*

**The structure of the verse is a preservation contract.** The garments are the representation; what
persists across the change is what the inquiry is about. **Change of representation, invariance of the
thing inquired after.**

> **Corroboration C-1** — for the audit's central distinction: `Π(T(D)) = Π(T(D∖S))` asks **not**
> whether `T` preserves `D`, but whether it preserves **what the contract requires**. The verse makes
> the same separation and makes nothing else.
>
> ⚠️ **What it does NOT supply:** any way to *measure* preservation. `N_viol`, `Ĥ(Q|R)`, `A_n`, `F_n` —
> the entire metric vector — have **no counterpart here.** The lens frames; it does not quantify.

## 3.2 `nāsato vidyate bhāvo` — the sufficiency question · 2.16

> *of the unreal there is no being; of the real there is no non-being*

**The verse asserts a sharp boundary between what has standing and what does not.** The audit's
question is where that boundary *falls* under a given contract — the **last adequate stage** before
preservation fails (§21D).

> **Lens L-1 — "there is a boundary, and it is not where convenience puts it."** `[EXT]`, and **weak**:
> the verse asserts that a boundary exists. **The audit's whole difficulty is that the boundary is
> contract-relative and possibly non-monotone** (§21D warns adequacy may not be monotone). **The text
> offers no help with that, and a reading that claimed otherwise would be reading in.**

## 3.3 `guṇa`-typed `tyāga` — reduction has a ground · 18.7–9

Already recorded in the previous study, and it earns its place again for a **new** reason: the audit
separates **adequacy** (`H(Q|T)=0`, an optimal decoder exists) from **realization** (`F(T)=1`, the
*specified* decoder achieves it).

> **Corroboration C-2** — the Gītā's `tyāga` taxonomy classifies a renunciation **by its ground, not
> its outcome**. **Adequacy vs realization is the same shape of distinction:** *the information is
> present* is a different claim from *this operator recovers it*. **Two eliminations with identical
> outcomes can differ in whether they were warranted.**

## 3.4 `yoga: karmasu kauśalam` — and the trap `[NEG]` for this study

> *yoga is skill in action* (2.50)

**A tempting reading:** reduction is *skill* — the art of doing more with less.

> ## ⚠️ REFUSED.
> **This is the "5 digits → 2 digits" trap in Sanskrit dress.** The handoff §25 is explicit: *"digit
> count is only one representation-capacity measure … a two-digit representation may have a larger
> information capacity than a five-digit one depending on the encoding and alphabet."*
>
> **A reading that treats compactness as a virtue would import exactly the error the mandate
> forbids.** The audit's own §19 refuses to assume `H(R5) > H(R4) > …`. **Recorded as a refusal,
> because it was the most attractive reading available and it is wrong.**
