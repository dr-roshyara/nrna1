## One correction: the empirical classification

This statement is not quite right:

> \(0<M_{ij}<1\) → correlated / conditionally dependent

A conditional probability being between 0 and 1 **does not establish correlation or dependence**.

For example, if:

$$
P(Z_i=1)=0.3
$$

and:

$$
P(Z_i=1\mid Z_j=1)=0.3,
$$

then the two variables can be independent despite:

$$
0<M_{ij}<1.
$$

So we need to compare the conditional probability with the marginal probability.

### Better metrics

For each pair:

$$
P_i=P(Z_i=1)
$$

$$
P_{ij}=P(Z_i=1,Z_j=1)
$$

$$
M_{ij}=P(Z_i=1\mid Z_j=1)
$$

and compare:

$$
\boxed{
M_{ij}\stackrel{?}{=}P_i
}
$$

Equivalently:

$$
\boxed{
P_{ij}\stackrel{?}{=}P_iP_j
}
$$

for empirical independence.

I would additionally calculate an association measure such as:

$$
RD_{ij}
=
P(Z_i=1\mid Z_j=1)
-
P(Z_i=1\mid Z_j=0)
$$

which is especially compatible with the way we already analyzed KR-BRIDGE.

---

# More importantly: separate logical entailment from statistical dependence

This is crucial for Knowledge Algebra.

There are actually **three different relationships** we should test.

### A. Logical implication

$$
Z_j\Rightarrow Z_i
$$

Empirically:

$$
P(Z_i=0,Z_j=1)=0
$$

within the tested corpus.

This is evidence of **empirical implication**, not universal logical proof.

### B. Statistical association

$$
P(Z_i,Z_j)\neq P(Z_i)P(Z_j)
$$

This tells us the Zero phenomena are statistically associated.

### C. Independence

$$
P(Z_i,Z_j)=P(Z_i)P(Z_j)
$$

This tells us that, in the tested distribution, one gives no information about the other.

These must not be conflated.

---

# I would therefore modify your matrix

Instead of only:

$$
M_{ij}=P(Z_i=1\mid Z_j=1),
$$

make the pairwise output:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal M_{ij}
=
\left(
P_i,
P_j,
P_{ij},
M_{ij},
RD_{ij},
I_{ij}
\right)
}
$$

where \(I_{ij}\) is an association/dependence statistic if needed.

Then classify:

| Relationship                  | Empirical criterion                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Empirically entailed**      | \(P(Z_i=0,Z_j=1)=0\)                  |
| **Empirically incompatible**  | \(P(Z_i=1,Z_j=1)=0\)                  |
| **Associated**                | \(P_{ij}\neq P_iP_j\)                 |
| **Approximately independent** | \(P_{ij}\approx P_iP_j\)              |
| **Non-entailing**             | both directions have counterexamples  |
| **Undetermined**              | insufficient informative observations |

This is much safer.

# One more methodological issue: conditioning

Your experiments should not pool everything immediately.

We already learned this lesson from `KR-BRIDGE-01/02`.

If:

$$
Z_{\text{elim}}
$$

is generated primarily by a particular transformation \(T\), while:

$$
Z_{\text{cont}}
$$

is generated by a different mechanism, pooled association can be completely misleading.

Therefore calculate:

$$
P(Z_i\mid Z_j,T,Q,\mathfrak C)
$$

and potentially stratify by:

$$
T,\quad Q,\quad \mathfrak C,\quad \text{knowledge-state class}.
$$

Otherwise a common transformation can become a hidden confounder.

So the experiment should have:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Pooled analysis}
+
\text{stratified analysis}
}
$$

rather than pooled analysis alone.
