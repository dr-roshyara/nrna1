## Part 1: Core Bayesian Rules — Formal Definitions

### 1.1 The Probability Axioms (Kolmogorov)

From Chapter 2, Section 2.2:

**Non-Negativity:** For any proposition \( P \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) \geq 0}
\]

**Normality:** For any tautology \( T \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(T) = 1}
\]

**Finite Additivity:** For any mutually exclusive propositions \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \vee Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P) + \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

### 1.2 Consequences of the Probability Axioms

From Chapter 2, Section 2.2.1:

**Negation:** For any proposition \( P \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(\sim P) = 1 - \operatorname{cr}(P)}
\]

**Maximality:** For any proposition \( P \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) \leq 1}
\]

**Contradiction:** For any contradiction \( F \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(F) = 0}
\]

**Entailment:** For any propositions \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \), if \( P \models Q \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) \leq \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

**Equivalence:** For any propositions \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \), if \( P \equiv Q \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) = \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

**General Additivity:** For any propositions \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \vee Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P) + \operatorname{cr}(Q) - \operatorname{cr}(P \wedge Q)}
\]

**Decomposition:** For any propositions \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) = \operatorname{cr}(P \wedge Q) + \operatorname{cr}(P \wedge \sim Q)}
\]

**Partition:** For any finite partition of propositions in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\sum_i \operatorname{cr}(P_i) = 1}
\]

---

### 1.3 The Ratio Formula

From Chapter 3, Section 3.1.1:

For any \( P \) and \( Q \) in \( \mathcal{L} \), if \( \operatorname{cr}(Q) > 0 \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(P \wedge Q)}{\operatorname{cr}(Q)}}
\]

**Key Property:** Conditional credences supervene on unconditional credences.

---

### 1.4 Bayes's Theorem

From Chapter 3, Section 3.1.3:

For any \( H \) and \( E \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H)}{\operatorname{cr}(E)}}
\]

**Expanded form (with catchall):**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H)}{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H) + \operatorname{cr}(E \mid \sim H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(\sim H)}}
\]

**Multiple hypotheses form:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(H_i \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H_i) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H_i)}{\sum_j \operatorname{cr}(E \mid H_j) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H_j)}}
\]

---

### 1.5 Conditionalization

From Chapter 4, Section 4.1:

For any time \( t_i \) and later time \( t_j \), if \( E \) in \( \mathcal{L} \) represents everything the agent learns between \( t_i \) and \( t_j \), and \( \operatorname{cr}_i(E) > 0 \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_j(H) = \operatorname{cr}_i(H \mid E)}
\]

**Equivalently:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_j(H) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}_i(H \wedge E)}{\operatorname{cr}_i(E)}}
\]

## Part 2: Advanced Bayesian Rules

### 2.1 Law of Total Probability

From Chapter 3, Section 3.1.2:

For any proposition \( P \) and finite partition \( \{Q_1, Q_2, \ldots, Q_n\} \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) = \sum_i \operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q_i) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(Q_i)}
\]

**Conditional form:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid E) = \sum_i \operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q_i \wedge E) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(Q_i \mid E)}
\]

---

### 2.2 Probabilistic Independence

From Chapter 3, Section 3.2:

\( P \) is **probabilistically independent** of \( Q \) relative to \( \operatorname{cr} \) just in case:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P)}
\]

**Equivalent forms:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P \mid \sim Q)}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(Q \mid P) = \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

**Multiplication Rule:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \wedge Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

**General conjunction rule:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \wedge Q) = \operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(Q)}
\]

---

### 2.3 Conditional Independence

From Chapter 3, Section 3.2.1:

\( P \) is **probabilistically independent** of \( Q \) **conditional on** \( R \) just in case:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q \wedge R) = \operatorname{cr}(P \mid R)}
\]

---

### 2.4 Screening Off

From Chapter 3, Section 3.2.1:

\( R \) **screens off** \( P \) from \( Q \) when:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q) \neq \operatorname{cr}(P)}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q \wedge R) = \operatorname{cr}(P \mid R)}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q \wedge \sim R) = \operatorname{cr}(P \mid \sim R)}
\]

---

### 2.5 Regularity

From Chapter 4, Section 4.2:

**Regularity Principle:** In a rational credence distribution, no logically contingent proposition receives unconditional credence 0.

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(P) > 0 \text{ for all contingent } P}
\]

---

### 2.6 Principal Principle

From Chapter 5, Section 5.2.1:

Let \( \mathrm{Ch}_i(A) = x \) be the proposition that the chance of \( A \) at time \( t_i \) equals \( x \). Let \( E \) be admissible information.

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(A \mid \mathrm{Ch}_i(A) = x \wedge E) = x}
\]

**Weighted average form:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(A \mid E) = \sum_i x_i \cdot \operatorname{cr}(\mathrm{Ch}(A) = x_i \mid E)}
\]

---

### 2.7 Reflection Principle

From Chapter 5, Section 5.2.2:

For any proposition \( A \), real number \( x \), and times \( t_i < t_j \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_i(A \mid \operatorname{cr}_j(A) = x) = x}
\]

---

### 2.8 Jeffrey Conditionalization

From Chapter 5, Section 5.5:

For any \( t_i < t_j \), any \( A \) in \( \mathcal{L} \), and a finite partition \( \{B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\} \) in \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_j(A) = \sum_i \operatorname{cr}_i(A \mid B_i) \cdot \operatorname{cr}_j(B_i)}
\]

**Rigidity Condition:**

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_j(A \mid B_i) = \operatorname{cr}_i(A \mid B_i)}
\]

---

### 2.9 Countable Additivity

From Chapter 5, Section 5.4:

For any countable set \( \{Q_1, Q_2, Q_3, \ldots\} \) of mutually exclusive propositions:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(Q_1 \vee Q_2 \vee Q_3 \vee \ldots) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \operatorname{cr}(Q_i)}
\]

---

### 2.10 Conglomerability

From Chapter 5, Section 5.4:

For each proposition \( P \) and partition \( \{Q_1, Q_2, Q_3, \ldots\} \):

\[
\boxed{\inf_i \operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q_i) \leq \operatorname{cr}(P) \leq \sup_i \operatorname{cr}(P \mid Q_i)}
\]

**Finite Conglomerability** follows from the probability axioms via the Law of Total Probability.

## Part 3: Derivation of Bayes's Theorem

### 3.1 Proof

From Chapter 3, Section 3.1.3:

**Step 1:** By the Ratio Formula:

\[
\operatorname{cr}(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(H \wedge E)}{\operatorname{cr}(E)}
\]

**Step 2:** By the Ratio Formula (symmetric):

\[
\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \wedge H)}{\operatorname{cr}(H)}
\]

\[
\operatorname{cr}(E \wedge H) = \operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H)
\]

**Step 3:** Since \( H \wedge E \equiv E \wedge H \):

\[
\operatorname{cr}(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H)}{\operatorname{cr}(E)}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}(E \mid H) \cdot \operatorname{cr}(H)}{\operatorname{cr}(E)}}
\]

## Part 4: Derivation of Conditionalization's Key Properties

### 4.1 Certainties are Created

If an agent learns \( E \) between \( t_i \) and \( t_j \), then:

\[
\operatorname{cr}_j(E) = \operatorname{cr}_i(E \mid E) = 1
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_j(E) = 1}
\]

### 4.2 Certainties are Retained

If \( \operatorname{cr}_i(H) = 1 \), then:

\[
\operatorname{cr}_j(H) = \operatorname{cr}_i(H \mid E) = \frac{\operatorname{cr}_i(H \wedge E)}{\operatorname{cr}_i(E)} = \frac{\operatorname{cr}_i(E)}{\operatorname{cr}_i(E)} = 1
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cr}_i(H) = 1 \Rightarrow \operatorname{cr}_j(H) = 1}
\]

### 4.3 Conditionalization is Cumulative

If \( \operatorname{cr}_j(X) = \operatorname{cr}_i(X \mid E) \) and \( \operatorname{cr}_k(Y) = \operatorname{cr}_j(Y \mid E') \), then:

\[
\operatorname{cr}_k(Z) = \operatorname{cr}_i(Z \mid E \wedge E')
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{Conditionalization on } E \text{ then } E' = \text{Conditionalization on } E \wedge E'}
\]

### 4.4 Conditionalization is Commutative

Order of update does not matter:

\[
\boxed{\text{Update on } E \text{ then } E' = \text{Update on } E' \text{ then } E}
\]

## Part 5: Odds Formulation

### 5.1 Definition

From Chapter 2, Section 2.3.4:

Odds for \( P \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Odds}(P) = \operatorname{cr}(P) : \operatorname{cr}(\sim P)}
\]

Odds against \( P \):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Odds}_{\text{against}}(P) = \operatorname{cr}(\sim P) : \operatorname{cr}(P)}
\]

### 5.2 Bayes Factor

From Chapter 4, Section 4.1.2:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Odds}_j(H) = \operatorname{Odds}_i(H) \cdot \frac{\operatorname{cr}_i(E \mid H)}{\operatorname{cr}_i(E \mid \sim H)}}
\]

Where:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bayes Factor} = \frac{\operatorname{cr}_i(E \mid H)}{\operatorname{cr}_i(E \mid \sim H)}}
\]
