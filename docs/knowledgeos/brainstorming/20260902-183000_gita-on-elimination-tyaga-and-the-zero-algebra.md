# 3. The loci

## 3.1 `sannyāsa` vs `tyāga` — **what is eliminated: the thing, or its result?** · 18.2

> `sannyāsaṃ karmaṇāṃ ... tyāgaṃ phala-tyāgam`
> *renouncing actions* — versus — *renouncing the fruits of actions*

**The Gītā's opening move in chapter 18 is to refuse to let these collapse.** Two distinct
eliminations: remove the **act**, or remove the **result**.

> **Corroboration C-1** — for `KR-ZERO-ALGEBRA` §4, the Remainder result. The experiment found three
> candidate Remainder constructions and measured that **two coincide exactly (A ≡ C, 1 182/1 182)
> while the third does not** — `B`, the *transformation residual*, is a different object from what the
> contract leaves unresolved.
>
> **The distinction the experiment was ordered to preserve is precisely the distinction 18.2 opens
> with: the residue of the operation is not the residue of the concern.**

## 3.2 Complete elimination is unavailable · 18.11, 3.5

> `na hi dehabhṛtā śakyaṃ tyaktuṃ karmāṇy aśeṣataḥ` (18.11)
> *no embodied being can relinquish actions **without remainder***

`aśeṣataḥ` — *without remainder* — is the operative word, and 3.5 sharpens it: *no one remains even a
moment without action.*

> **Lens L-1 — "there is always a remainder."** A total-elimination operator is not merely
> hard to build; **the target state may not exist.**
>
> **Note what the experiment did NOT find.** It found termination (`H3`, ≤ 2 iterations) and
> monotonicity (`H4`) — the process *does* halt with something left. **The lens is consistent with
> that and does not establish it.** Consistency is not evidence.

## 3.3 Do not eliminate what is flawed but load-bearing · 18.48

> `sahajaṃ karma kaunteya sa-doṣam api na tyajet`
> *one should not abandon the work born of one's own nature, though it be flawed*

**The instruction is precisely counter-intuitive in the way the experiment's `RQ8` is:** the thing
*looks* removable — it is flawed, it is a defect — **and removing it is still wrong.**

> **Corroboration C-2** — for the boundary-preservation result. **196 of 1 223 otherwise-tempting
> eliminations (16.03 %) were blocked once provenance, uncertainty, scope and contradiction state
> were in the contract.** *Same visible result, changed boundary ⟹ not Zero.*
>
> **One elimination in six is prevented by something other than the visible answer** — which is what
> 18.48 asserts normatively and the experiment measured empirically. **Independent agreement; not
> shared evidence.**

## 3.4 Elimination has a **ground**, and grounds differ in kind · 18.7–9

The Gītā classifies `tyāga` **three ways by its ground, not by its outcome**:

| | ground | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **tāmasa** (18.7) | from delusion — abandoning what ought not be abandoned | rejected |
| **rājasa** (18.8) | from fear of difficulty | rejected |
| **sāttvika** (18.9) | done because it ought to be, attachment to fruit relinquished | accepted |

**The same physical removal is classified differently according to why it was done.**

> **Corroboration C-3 — the sharpest in this study.** `KR-ZERO-ALGEBRA`'s central methodological
> requirement was **two paths kept apart by construction**: the formal counterfactual (`PATH A`) and
> the declared heuristic (`PATH B`). Result: **55.75 % agreement — and the disagreement is
> asymmetric. 1 559 elements the heuristic would remove are not Zero; only 301 that are Zero it
> keeps.**
>
> **The heuristic removes the right-looking things for the wrong reason, and over-removes.** That is
> the `tāmasa`/`rājasa` distinction stated in measurements: **an elimination is not adequately
> characterised by what it removes; it is characterised by the ground on which it removes it.**

## 3.5 `mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr` — the contract is not the act · 2.47

> *let not the fruit of action be your motive*

**Lens L-2 — the criterion of a good elimination is not located in the elimination.** In the
experiment's vocabulary: **eliminability is `Π`-indexed, not intrinsic to `x`.** `H8` was **REFUTED**
— 495 differences — establishing that Zero is genuinely **contract-relative**.

> **Lens only.** The experiment established contract-relativity by measurement; the verse makes the
> question askable and settles nothing.
