### Gap 2: Revisability as a Constitutional Right (Fallibilism)
Truth-seeking requires that **all knowledge is revisable**. Your TMS/AGM research touched on revision, but it was never turned into a constitutional law for the kernel.

- **The Gap:** Most systems treat "committed" knowledge as permanent truth. KnowledgeOS must enforce fallibilism—meaning no knowledge state is ever permanently locked, and revision is always a first-class operation, not a bug.

- **Proposed Invariant:**
> **H-KOS-Revisability-001:** *No knowledge state SHALL be irrevocably finalized. The kernel MUST provide a governed path for revision (retraction, replacement, or refinement) for every knowledge object, preserving the revision history as part of its identity.*

---

### Gap 3: The "Inference vs. Observation" Boundary
You have added "Reasoning and Logic" to the truth-seeking process. However, logic produces **derived (inferred) knowledge**, while sensors/observation produce **base (observed) knowledge**. 

- **The Gap:** These two types have entirely different authority models. Base knowledge's evidence is *external* (the real world). Derived knowledge's evidence is *internal* (the reasoning chain). The kernel currently treats all "evidence" uniformly.
- **Without this:** The system will conflate "I read this in a document" (base) with "I logically deduced this from a theorem" (derived), leading to catastrophic epistemic category errors.
- **Proposed Invariant:**
> **H-KOS-Inference-001:** *KnowledgeOS SHALL distinguish between observational knowledge (evidenced externally) and inferential knowledge (evidenced internally via logic). The provenance of inferential knowledge MUST include its entire reasoning chain, and its authority is dependent on the validity of that chain.*

---

### Gap 4: Justification Strength / Type (Monotonic vs. Non-monotonic)
Since you are adding logic, you need a way to classify *how* a conclusion follows from evidence.

- In **monotonic** logic (deductive), if premises are true, the conclusion is absolutely true. Justification is binary (valid/invalid).
- In **non-monotonic** (defeasible) logic (inductive/abductive), new evidence can overturn previous conclusions. Justification is probabilistic or provisional.

- **The Gap:** The kernel currently has no constitutional vocabulary to express the *logical force* of the justification.
- **Without this:** You cannot tell the difference between a mathematical theorem (certain, deductive) and a medical diagnosis (probabilistic, abductive). 
- **Proposed Invariant:**
> **H-KOS-Justification-001:** *KnowledgeOS SHALL preserve the logical type (monotonic/non-monotonic) and justification strength (deductive, inductive, abductive, probabilistic) of the reasoning applied. This logical metadata SHALL govern how contradictions are resolved and how evidence is weighted.*
