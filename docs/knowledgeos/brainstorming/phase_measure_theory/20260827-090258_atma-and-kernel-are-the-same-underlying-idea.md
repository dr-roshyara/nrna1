They are **the same underlying idea**, but we shifted the terminology slightly. I would now tighten the definition so we don't accidentally create two different concepts.

Previously, I defined Ātma roughly as:

> **the invariant identity underlying changing states.**

Then, in your latest formulation, we said:

> **Ātma = the Knower.**

These can be reconciled:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Ātma} = \text{the persistent/invariant Knower}
}
$$

So **"Knower" describes its role**, while **"invariant/persistent" describes its mathematical property**.

### The complete definition

I recommend we use:

> **Ātma is the persistent Knower whose identity remains invariant while its knowledge, understanding, observations, experiences, and represented states change over time.**

Formally, let:

$$
\boxed{A=\text{Ātma}}
$$

and:

$$
K_t=\text{Knowledge State of }A\text{ at time }t
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{A_{t_1}=A_{t_2}}
$$

while:

$$
\boxed{K_{t_1}\neq K_{t_2}}
$$

Thus:

```text
             ĀTMA
        Persistent Knower
               │
       ┌───────┼───────┐
       ▼       ▼       ▼
      K₀      K₁      K₂
   state 0  state 1  state 2
       │       │       │
       └───────┼───────┘
               │
          same Knower
```

### What changed?

Only this:

**Earlier:**

$$
\text{Ātma}=\text{Invariant Identity}
$$

**Now:**

$$
\text{Ātma}=\text{Knower}
$$

**Refined:**

$$
\boxed{
\text{Ātma}=
\text{Invariant Identity of the Knower}
}
$$

or, even more precisely:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Ātma}=
\text{Persistent Knower}
}
$$

That is the definition I would carry forward in the KnowledgeOS theory.

And this gives us a very important separation:

$$
\boxed{
\underbrace{\text{Ātma}}_{\text{Who knows}}
\quad
\underbrace{K_t}_{\text{What is known}}
\quad
\underbrace{I_t}_{\text{What should be known}}
}
$$

This is, in my view, **much cleaner than identifying Ātma with the Knowledge State itself**.

One caution remains: this is our **KnowledgeOS mathematical/architectural abstraction inspired by Ātman**, not a claim that the software has mathematically captured the metaphysical Ātman of the Gītā.
