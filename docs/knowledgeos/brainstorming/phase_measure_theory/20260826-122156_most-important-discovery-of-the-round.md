Previously: "A dimension is a statement about an observation."

**Revised**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Dimension} = \text{a distinguishable axis, component, property, state, relationship, or mode through which an observation can be characterized.}
}
\]

**Insight**: A statement **expresses** a dimension; it is not the dimension itself.

---

### 1.4 Fact

**Newly defined**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Fact} = \text{Proposition} + \text{Observation} + \text{Context} + \text{Time} + \text{Provenance}
}
\]

**Insight**: A fact is not just a proposition — it has **epistemic embedding**.

---

### 1.5 Knowledge

Previously: Knowledge ≈ correct determination.

**Revised**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Knowledge} = \text{Representation} + \text{Provenance} + \text{Observation Context} + \text{Determination Process}
}
\]

**Insight**: Knowledge includes understanding of both the **field** and its **Knower**, and their relationship.

---

### 1.6 Reason

**Newly defined**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Reason} = \text{a justified transformation from premises/evidence to a conclusion using an explicit or declared logical/theoretical rule.}
}
\]

**Insight**: Reason is inspectable, traceable, and separable from wisdom.

---

### 1.7 Wisdom

**Newly distinguished**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Wisdom} = \text{understanding the broader structure, consequences, and principles governing determinations.}
}
\]

**Insight**: Reason and wisdom should not be collapsed.

## 2. What the Gita Helps Us Distinguish

### 2.1 Completeness vs. Confidence vs. Sufficiency vs. Priority

\[
\boxed{
\text{Completeness} \neq \text{Confidence} \neq \text{Sufficiency} \neq \text{Priority}
}
\]

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Completeness** | How much of the knowledge space is represented? |
| **Confidence** | How likely is a determination to be correct? |
| **Sufficiency** | Is current knowledge enough to support a decision? |
| **Priority** | Which dimensions matter most for a given decision? |

**Insight**: A system can have high confidence in a limited set of dimensions, yet still have low completeness; it can have limited completeness yet be sufficient for a decision.

---

### 2.2 Trust vs. Truth vs. Evidence

\[
\boxed{
\text{Trust} \neq \text{Truth} \neq \text{Evidence}
}
\]

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Trust** | Epistemic weight assigned to a source |
| **Truth** | Correspondence with reality |
| **Evidence** | Supporting data/observations |

**Insight**: Trust influences evidence evaluation but does not determine truth.

---

### 2.3 Knowledge vs. Action

\[
\boxed{
\text{Knowledge} \neq \text{Action}
}
\]

**Insight**: Knowledge is not sufficient for action. Action requires **decision** and **will**.

---

## 3. What the Gita Challenges

### 3.1 Knowledge = Difference Between States

Our earlier model:
\[
\text{Knowledge} \approx \text{Observed State} - \text{Ideal State}
\]

**Challenge**: Knowledge is not merely a comparison. It includes the **Knower**, the **process**, and the **understanding** of both field and observer.

**Revised**:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Knowledge} = \text{justified representation of the observed state, including understanding of the field, the knower, and their relationship}
}
\]

---

### 3.2 State as a Flat List of Values

**Challenge**: The field is structured, with components, relationships, and qualities.

**Revised**: State is a structured field.

---

### 3.3 Knowledge = Complete Representation

**Challenge**: Knowledge can be sufficient for action even when incomplete.

**Revised**:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Sufficiency}(K_t, D, C) \neq \text{Completeness}(K_t)
}
\]

---

## 4. What Remains Genuinely Undefined

After applying the Gita through the Lord and Zero lenses, these are the **five core questions** that still require our own theory:

### Q1: What is the formal ontology?

We have candidate definitions for observation, state, dimension, value, and relationship. But they are **candidate definitions**, not final formalizations.

**Status**: Partially defined; needs formalization.

---

### Q2: What is the formal epistemology?

We have candidate definitions for evidence, trust, reason, determination, confidence, and uncertainty. But we need:
- Formal relationships between them
- Formal inference rules
- Formal validity conditions

**Status**: Partially defined; needs formalization.

---

### Q3: What is the formal knowledge state?

We have candidate structure:
\[
K_t = \langle D_t, V_t, R_t, E_t, Q_t, C_t, P_t, H_t \rangle
\]

But we need:
- Invariants
- Transitions
- Validity conditions

**Status**: Candidate model; needs formalization.

---

### Q4: What is the formal relationship to the infinite space?

We have conceptual mappings:
- Lord Lens → Ω
- Field → K_t
- Knower → Observer

But we need:
- Formal definition of Ω
- Formal relationship between K_t and Ω
- Formal relationship between D_t and D^*

**Status**: Conceptual; needs mathematical formalization.

---

### Q5: What is decision sufficiency?

We need a formal definition:
\[
\text{Sufficiency}(K_t, D, C) = ?
\]

**Status**: Identified as a necessary concept; needs formalization.

From the Gita through Zero Lens:

\]

### Zero-2: Dimension Boundary
\[
d \notin D_t
\]

### Zero-3: Relationship Boundary
\[
R(d_1, d_2) = ?
\]

### Zero-4: Representation Boundary
\[
R \not\rightarrow K_t
\]

**Insight**: Zero operates at multiple levels, not just "unknown value."

## 6. The Knower/Observer Lens

The Gita strongly suggests a third lens:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Knower Lens: Who knows? From what position? Through what means? With what limitations?}
}
\]

This is different from:
- **Lord Lens**: What is the whole space?
- **Zero Lens**: What is missing?

**Status**: Identified; not yet formalized as a lens.

## 9. The Strongest Conclusion

After applying the Bhagavad Gita through the Lord and Zero lenses, the **single most important formulation** is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Lord Lens: There is always a larger space against which the current model must be understood.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{Zero Lens: Never confuse the boundary of our representation with the boundary of reality.}
}
\]

**The Gita does not prove our Infinite Knowledge Space theory. But it provides a coherent set of design hypotheses that align with our research direction and expose the gaps that remain.**
