This question is actually **central to the KnowledgeOS architecture**. The answer exposes a very important distinction between an LLM and KnowledgeOS.

## 1. Where does a ChatGPT-like LLM get its information?

There are two fundamentally different mechanisms.

### A. Information learned during training

Foundation models are trained on large datasets. OpenAI describes three broad sources for its models:

1. publicly available information,
2. information obtained through third-party partnerships,
3. information provided or generated by users, human trainers, and researchers. ([OpenAI][1])

During training, the model adjusts its parameters to learn patterns. The model is therefore **not functioning like a document database** from which it retrieves the original documents when answering a question. OpenAI explicitly describes its models as learning machines rather than databases. ([OpenAI][2])

Conceptually:

```text
Millions/billions of documents
          │
          ▼
       Training
          │
          ▼
   Model parameters
          │
          ▼
        LLM
          │
          ▼
      Generated answer
```

So the model has **learned representations**, not a KnowledgeOS-style explicit Knowledge State.

---

### B. Information retrieved at inference time

Modern ChatGPT can also retrieve external information.

For example, ChatGPT Search can search the web, obtain current information, and provide citations to the underlying sources. OpenAI describes this as going beyond the model's built-in training knowledge. ([OpenAI][3])

Conceptually:

```text
User question
     │
     ▼
   LLM
     │
     ▼
Search / Retrieval
     │
     ▼
External sources
     │
     ▼
Retrieved context
     │
     ▼
   LLM
     │
     ▼
Answer + citations
```

This second mechanism is much closer to what **KnowledgeOS needs**.

## What This Means for Implementation

1. **Source Connectors** are first-class citizens. They are not just "data loaders"; they are epistemic components that understand the provenance, reliability, and context of their sources.

2. **Semantic Reconstruction** is a core capability. Parsers must extract not just text but structured observations, entities, dimensions, and values.

3. **Candidate Assertions** are held in a pending state until evaluated by the epistemic engine. They are not automatically promoted to knowledge.

4. **Evidence Objects** link observations to assertions and are stored with full provenance.

5. **Zero drives Retrieval**: Gaps identified by Zero become the basis for investigation plans generated by Sārathi.

6. **LLMs are Tools**, not the Knowledge State. They interpret, generate, and reason, but their output is processed through the epistemic pipeline.
