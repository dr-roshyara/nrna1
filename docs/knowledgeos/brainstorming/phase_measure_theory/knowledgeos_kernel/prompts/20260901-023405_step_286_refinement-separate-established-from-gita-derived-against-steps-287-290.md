## The most important refinement

I would summarize the entire correction in one sentence:

> **The Gītā does not define the KnowledgeOS Kernel; it gives us a remarkably rich vocabulary for discovering what a knowledge-transforming system may need to discriminate, transform, preserve, reject, qualify, and ultimately transcend—but every such correspondence must still survive mathematical, empirical, DDD, and governance tests.**

That is consistent with the strongest lesson from Steps 285–290: **research has advanced substantially, but equality, transformation semantics, operation space, and canonical kernel selection must not be silently promoted into architecture.** 

### Therefore I would make the next formal research target:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{STEP 291 — CANDIDATE KERNEL OPERATION AND DISCRIMINATION DISCOVERY}
}
$$

with **two separate registries**:

1. **Discrimination Registry \(B\)** — what the Kernel must be able to distinguish.
2. **Transformation Registry \(\mathcal O/\mathcal T\)** — what the Kernel may actually do to \(K_t\).

That separation is, in my judgment, the key refinement that makes the Gītā-inspired KnowledgeOS model mathematically and architecturally tractable.
