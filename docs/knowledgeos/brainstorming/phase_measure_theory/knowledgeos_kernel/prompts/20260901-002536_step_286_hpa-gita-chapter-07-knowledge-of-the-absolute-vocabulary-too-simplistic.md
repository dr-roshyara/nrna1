Exactly. **Buddhi should be understood primarily as the power/faculty of discrimination** — not simply "intelligence" and not simply "decision-making."

For our KnowledgeOS philosophical lens, I would sharpen it to:

$$
\boxed{\text{Buddhi} = \text{discriminative power}}
$$

Its essential function is **to distinguish one thing from another**.

### In KnowledgeOS terms

We can therefore investigate Buddhi as the philosophical analogue of a capability such as:

```text
                 BUDDHI
          discriminative power
                    │
        ┌───────────┼───────────┐
        ↓           ↓           ↓
    distinguish   compare    discriminate
        │           │           │
        └───────────┼───────────┘
                    ↓
              qualification
                    ↓
              epistemic state
```

This is more precise than my previous formulation of "Buddhi → qualification/decision."

### The important distinction

**Buddhi is not the knowledge itself.**

Rather:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Buddhi}
\quad\text{operates on}\quad
\text{what is presented to it}
}
$$

So philosophically:

```text
Manas
  ↓
receives / coordinates
  ↓
Buddhi
  ↓
discriminates
  ↓
Jñāna
  ↓
knowing
```

And potentially:

```text
Observation
      ↓
  processing
      ↓
 discrimination
      ↓
 qualification
      ↓
  knowledge
```

That makes **Buddhi particularly relevant to `Qualify`** in KnowledgeOS.

But we should be careful: **we cannot yet claim `Buddhi = Qualify`**. The legitimate research statement is:

> **Buddhi provides a philosophical lens for investigating whether KnowledgeOS requires an explicit discriminative capability, and whether `Qualify` is the technical manifestation of such a capability.**

That distinction is exactly consistent with our Step 286 discipline:

$$
\text{correspondence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{identity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{primitive}
$$

### An even deeper consequence

If Buddhi is **discrimination**, then its opposite failure is not merely "lack of knowledge."

It could be:

$$
\boxed{
\text{failure to distinguish}
}
$$

For example:

```text
true / false
known / unknown
relevant / irrelevant
same / different
consistent / contradictory
evidence / assertion
observation / interpretation
authority / guidance
knowledge / belief
```

And this is extremely interesting for KnowledgeOS because **many of our existing theoretical problems are precisely discrimination problems**.

For example, Step 287 had to distinguish:

$$
= \neq \equiv \neq \approx \neq \cong_\lambda
$$

The research itself had to **discriminate between four relations that were previously at risk of being conflated**.

So there is a potentially very strong independent correspondence:

> **KnowledgeOS needs discrimination because epistemic correctness frequently depends not merely on possessing information, but on correctly distinguishing categories, states, relations and meanings.**

That is a much stronger hypothesis than simply saying "Buddhi means intelligence."

I would therefore record the philosophical terminology as:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Buddhi — discriminative faculty/power}
}
$$

and investigate **`Discrimination` as a possible cross-cutting Kernel capability**, rather than prematurely making Buddhi or Discrimination a primitive.
