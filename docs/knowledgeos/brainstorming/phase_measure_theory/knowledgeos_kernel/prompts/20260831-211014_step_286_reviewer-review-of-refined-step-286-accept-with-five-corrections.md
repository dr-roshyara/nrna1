## Overall verdict

**Step 286 is substantially improved and, structurally, I would accept it as a research-result artifact.** It correctly prevents the philosophical material from becoming an architectural authority and keeps the promotion chain disciplined.

However, I would make **5 corrections before freezing it**. Two are particularly important:

1. **Cavell's three items must not yet be called "candidate primitives" without qualification.**
2. **The reclassification of `Qualify` is insightful, but the conclusion "a total `Qualify` cannot exist" is stronger than the evidence establishes.**

There is also one important governance/registry issue inherited from the current state: **Step 286 should not itself imply that the GK register has been corrected.** Your §3 mostly handles this correctly, but the distinction should be made even sharper.

# 5. ⚠️ Second main issue: the `Qualify` conclusion is too strong

This paragraph is intellectually interesting:

> "If justification terminates by construction, a total `Qualify` cannot exist..."

I would **not freeze that sentence in that form**.

Cavell's statement that reasons come to an end does support a potentially important architectural question:

> **Where does justification legitimately terminate, and what records that termination?**

That is a very good reframing.

But it does **not mathematically prove**:

> a total `Qualify` cannot exist.

There are several possibilities still open:

* `Qualify` may be partial.
* `Qualify` may terminate at an explicit authority boundary.
* `Qualify` may return a terminal-but-not-affirmative state.
* `Qualify` may require human/governance input.
* `Qualify` may be intentionally non-total.
* `Qualify` may have a total contract whose result includes "cannot qualify".

So I would change:

> **"a total `Qualify` cannot exist"**

to something like:

> **"This raises a strong hypothesis that `Qualify` need not be total over all cases; justification may legitimately terminate at an explicit boundary."**

Then:

> **"The research question therefore shifts from 'what are necessary-and-sufficient criteria for all cases?' to 'where may qualification legitimately terminate, and what records that termination?'"**

That is much stronger academically because it preserves the discovery without prematurely resolving it.
