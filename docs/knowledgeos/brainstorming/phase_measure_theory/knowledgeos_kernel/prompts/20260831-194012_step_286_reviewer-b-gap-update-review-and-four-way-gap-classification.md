I’ve read the three gap-update artifacts you provided. The combined picture is quite clear:

### Current research position

**The investigation has converged on two genuinely irreducible formal gaps:**

1. **`Qualify` has no executable/formal body — `G1`**

   * This remains the blocker for making `π_K` computable.
   * Sañjaya recovers the missing **referent/observation layer** (`W`, `Ω`, `O`) but does not solve `Qualify`.

2. **Three corpus equality relations lack decision procedures — also `G1`**

   * `≡` semantic equality: named, but no procedure.
   * `≈` observational equality: requires a declared permitted-observation set.
   * `≅_λ` provenance-sensitive equality: requires a definition of “relevant provenance.”
   * Therefore equality is **not missing as a concept**, but is **under-specified as an executable formalism**.

That is an important distinction: the research should **not invent another equality relation** to make D285-5 work.

### The strongest methodological result

The most significant development is actually methodological rather than Sanskrit-related:

> **A hypothesis cannot be tested until its types and equality relation are explicit.**

The seven-stage protocol plus the seven-section D285 artifact template now give you a much stronger research discipline:

`Source proposition → KOS translation → formal hypothesis → type/equality audit → independent KOS derivation → falsifiable test → classification`

And the four equality relations must be treated as **non-interchangeable**.

### D285-5 is now substantially narrower

The original:

`δ is non-injective`

is **too strong**.

The defensible result is:

> There exist distinct operations whose resulting states are semantically equivalent **under a particular projection/quotient that discards Π**.

But under provenance-sensitive equality `≅_λ`, the same witness is injective.

So D285-5 demonstrates something about **a quotient of the result space**, not about `δ` itself.

That correction is important enough that I would treat the old stronger wording as **superseded**, not merely annotated.

### Sañjaya changes the πK diagnosis

This is also a meaningful recovery:

`Domain Reality (W) → Sañjaya (Ω) → State Knowledge (O) → KnowledgeOS/Sārathi`

So the earlier claim that the verification lane lacked an entire observation/referent layer is no longer valid.

The correct diagnosis is now:

> **The structural ingredients for observation are present in the corpus; the missing piece is the executable qualification function.**

In other words:

**Before:**
`π_K` blocked by missing `W + Ω + Qualify`

**Now:**
`π_K` blocked by **`Qualify` alone**

That is a much sharper research target.

### The Sanskrit work has also become more disciplined

`Jñāna → Knowledge` was rejected.

The stronger interpretation is:

`Jñātā` → Knower
`Jñāna` → Knowing / transformation
`Jñeya` → Known

And importantly:

`Jñāna ∼ δ`

rather than:

`Jñāna = δ`

That follows the new promotion rule:

> **Correspondence ⇏ Type ⇏ Primitive ⇏ Canonical Architecture**

This should prevent the project from gradually converting philosophical analogies into architectural claims.

### One thing I would change in the status language

I would be careful with statements such as:

> “three gaps closed”

because some of these are really **discovery gaps closed by corpus recovery**, while the underlying formal problem remains.

For example, `Observation` being recovered closes the **discovery gap**, but the executable semantics of `Ω` and `Qualify` are not thereby solved.

I would therefore distinguish explicitly:

* **Discovery gap:** concept was missing from our model but exists in corpus.
* **Formal gap:** concept exists but lacks sufficient definition/procedure.
* **Architectural gap:** consequence for KnowledgeOS architecture remains unresolved.
* **Research blocker:** prevents the next formal claim from being established.

That classification would make the next rounds much cleaner.
