Yes. I have now read **Chapter 5 itself in the supplied *Bhagavad Gita As It Is***, rather than reasoning only from the earlier discussion.

And I think there is a **very important result for KnowledgeOS**, but I would correct one part of the proposed formulation:

> **Ātma is not best modelled as “knowledge.”**
>
> **Ātma is better modelled as a bounded knower / knowledge-bearing locus.**
>
> **Paramātmā is not simply “the whole Knowledge Space.”**
>
> It is much closer to a **universal knower**, whose scope of awareness is not bounded to one body.

That distinction is actually much stronger for our theory.

# 1. The strongest passage for KnowledgeOS: 5.15–5.18

Chapter 5 gives us an unusually clean epistemic structure.

The text says that the living entity is, by its superior nature, **full of knowledge**, but is limited; the Supreme is described as **vibhu**, possessing unlimited knowledge, while the living entity is **aṇu**, limited. 

Then 5.16 says that when ignorance is destroyed, **knowledge reveals**—using the sun as the metaphor: knowledge makes what was obscured visible. 

And the commentary makes the distinction explicit:

* individual living entity → limited
* Supreme → unlimited
* both conscious
* but not equal in scope

This is extremely interesting for our theory.

This may be the most valuable technical abstraction.

The commentary says:

> the individual soul is conscious only within the limited jurisdiction of the body, whereas the Supersoul is conscious of all bodies. 

This suggests that **knowledge should not be modelled merely as a set of propositions**.

We potentially need:

$$
\operatorname{Scope}(N)
$$

where \(N\) is a Knower.

For example:

$$
\operatorname{Scope}(\mathcal A_K)
=
\text{bounded domain/context}
$$

while hypothetically:

$$
\operatorname{Scope}(\mathcal P)
=
\text{universal domain}
$$

This is potentially much more important than the simple:

$$
K_t \subseteq \mathcal K
$$

because it introduces the distinction:

$$
\boxed{
\text{knowledge} \neq \text{knowing scope}
}
$$

## Proposed Step 290 research conclusion

I would therefore formulate the result as:

> **Chapter 5 does not establish that Ātma is knowledge or that Paramātmā is the Knowledge Space. It establishes, within the supplied interpretation, a distinction between a limited conscious knower and an unlimited Supreme Knower. For KnowledgeOS this provides strong philosophical corroboration for separating Knower, Knowledge State and Knowledge Space, and it raises two research hypotheses not yet represented in the corpus: epistemic scope/boundedness and the distinction between absence of knowledge and obscuration of knowledge. No new primitive is promoted.**

And there is one particularly elegant equation I think we should keep for further research:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Knower}
\;\xrightarrow[\text{bounded scope}]{\text{knowing}}\;
K_t
\subseteq
\mathcal K
}
$$

while the philosophical hypothesis corresponding to Paramātmā would be:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Universal Knower}
\;\xrightarrow[\text{unbounded scope}]{\text{knowing}}\;
\mathcal K
}
$$

**The second equation is a KnowledgeOS abstraction, not a claim that the Gītā literally defines \(\mathcal K\).**

That distinction should be preserved exactly under the Step 286 methodology.
