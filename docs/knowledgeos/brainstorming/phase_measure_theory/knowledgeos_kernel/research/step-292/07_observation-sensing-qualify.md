# 07 — `Observation`, sensing, and `Qualify` — **boundary preserved**

> **The mandate's own caution: the correct outcome may be that Reiter gives a rigorous framework
> AROUND these boundaries rather than solving them. That is the outcome.**

## The five questions the mandate poses

| question | answer | status |
|---|---|---|
| Is `Observation` an **action**? | Reiter's sensing actions *are* actions whose effect is on knowledge — so the shape exists. **But the corpus reopened `Observation` as `OPEN` in v1.2 and has not closed it.** | **OPEN** |
| Is `Observation` a **fluent**? | not decidable from either source | **OPEN** |
| Is sensing an action whose effect is on knowledge? | **in Reiter, yes** — `[EXT]`. Whether KnowledgeOS's `Observation` is that kind of thing is **not** settled by Reiter's having a construct of that shape | **OPEN** |
| Is `Qualify` a **precondition**? | **REFUTED** — `P4`. `Poss(a,s)` gates *whether an action may occur*; `Qualify` concerns *whether a qualification holds of a knowledge item*. Different type, different pipeline position | **REFUTED** |
| Is `Qualify` an **operation**, or an **epistemic boundary rather than a computable function**? | **not decided.** And `KR-CONTR-EVAL` makes the third option live: the programme has already found one case where an apparent function was really a **boundary** (`reason`) | **OPEN** |

## Why sensing does not transfer cleanly

Reiter's sensing works because knowledge is modelled by an **accessibility relation** and is
**factive**. **`R1` decided that KnowledgeOS's object is `A_t`, an attribution, and that no component
may assert `Knows`.**

> `[NEG]` **Reiter's knowledge operator and KnowledgeOS's object are different predicates by
> governance decision, not by oversight.** Sensing's formal guarantees are guarantees about a factive
> operator and **do not carry over to attribution.**
