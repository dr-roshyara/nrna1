## 4. `GK-13 Jñāna` — Priority 1, and it produces an immediate negative result

`[S]` `Jñāna` — knowledge/knowing (Ch. 4, 7, 13). The prompt maps it to **`Knowledge`**.

`CORPUS` — two measurements:
1. **`Knowledge` is absent from the 8 ratified primitives** while `Observation` and `Proposition` are
   present (`concern-map.md`).
2. `semantic-lineage-map.md`: `Knowledge` is *"absent from the 8-primitive kernel **and** from Steps
   100–158 vocabulary."*

`CORPUS` — and the corpus's own Sanskrit reading is **not** "Knowledge":
> `ज्ञान (Jñāna) — Knowing | Reasoning / transformation process`
> and it sits in the **Tripuṭī**: `Jñātā · Jñāna · Jñeya` = **knower · knowing · known**.

> ### **`Jñāna → Knowledge` is the wrong mapping. `Jñāna → Knowing`, i.e. the transformation, not the state.**
>
> `Jñātā` → the **Knower** (already ratified as `Kṣetrajña`).
> `Jñeya` → the **known**, i.e. `Proposition`/`Assertion` content.
> **`Jñāna` → the transformation `δ` / the act of knowing — not `K`.**
>
> **This is a genuine correction and it is well-evidenced:** the architecture deliberately does not
> have `Knowledge` as a primitive, and mapping a Priority-1 Gītā term onto a deliberately-absent
> primitive would have reintroduced it by the back door. **Verdict `R4`** — falsifiable, useful, and
> it **reinforces** the ratified decision rather than challenging it.

**Gap movement:** `GK-13` does **not** open a `Knowledge`-primitive gap. It **corroborates**
`Knowledge`'s deliberate absence and relocates the term to the transformation layer.
