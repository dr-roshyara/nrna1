Yes. I think you are pointing to a **more fundamental definition of “fact”** than simply “something observed.”

In the KnowledgeOS context, I would distinguish **fact determination** from **fact extraction**.

### 1. A fact is not merely extracted

If we say:

> “A fact is extracted from previous actions, doings, principles and methods”

we risk treating everything that happened in the past as a fact.

Instead:

$$
\text{Past record}
\rightarrow
\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\text{interpretation}
\rightarrow
\text{determination}
\rightarrow
\text{fact}
$$

The crucial point is that **determination is an epistemic act**.

A historical action can be observed:

> “Person X approved Y on date Z.”

But a higher-level fact might be:

> “Approval of Y requires authority X.”

That second statement is not simply extracted from one event. It is **determined from a body of previously determined actions, principles, decisions and methods**.

---

## 2. I would model the source of a fact as a lineage

Something like:

$$
F
=
D(O,P,M,A,H)
$$

where:

* \(F\) = determined fact
* \(O\) = observations
* \(A\) = previous actions/doings
* \(P\) = previously established principles
* \(M\) = established methods
* \(H\) = historical determinations
* \(D\) = determination process

So the important distinction is:

> **Extraction retrieves material. Determination establishes what that material warrants asserting.**

This fits very closely with the distinction already discovered in the research between expression, interpretation, epistemic assessment and admission. 

---

# 3. There are actually several kinds of "facts"

I would introduce this distinction before designing anything.

### A. Observed fact

Something directly recorded:

> `Action X occurred at time T.`

Structure:

$$
Observation \rightarrow Fact
$$

provided the observation itself is trustworthy.

---

### B. Historical fact

Repeated or corroborated observations establish:

> `Action X has repeatedly been performed in this way.`

$$
O_1,O_2,\ldots,O_n
\rightarrow
H
$$

This is already an inference.

---

### C. Procedural fact

Previous actions reveal an established method:

> `When situation S occurs, organization performs action X.`

That is extracted from **doing**, not merely from documentation.

---

### D. Normative fact

Previous decisions/principles establish:

> `Action X is required/permitted/prohibited under condition S.`

This is fundamentally different from:

> `Action X happened.`

The first is **normative**; the second is **descriptive**.

---

### E. Derived fact

Multiple established facts produce a new determination:

$$
F_1 + F_2 + F_3
\rightarrow
F_4
$$

This is where reasoning becomes important.

---

# 4. Your phrase "previously determined actions and doings and principles and methods" is therefore important

Because you are describing a **recursive epistemic system**.

Something like:

```text
                    OBSERVATIONS
                         │
                         ▼
                  previous doings
                         │
                         ▼
                 determinations
                         │
             ┌───────────┼───────────┐
             ▼           ▼           ▼
          ACTIONS     PRINCIPLES   METHODS
             │           │           │
             └───────────┼───────────┘
                         ▼
                   NEW EVIDENCE
                         │
                         ▼
                   NEW CLAIM
                         │
                         ▼
                    DETERMINATION
                         │
                         ▼
                       FACT
```

So KnowledgeOS isn't merely accumulating facts.

It is potentially accumulating **previous determinations that become evidence for later determinations**.

That is a much deeper model.

---

# 5. But we must avoid circularity

This immediately introduces a very important invariant.

Suppose:

$$
F_1
$$

was determined using:

$$
F_2
$$

and later we use \(F_1\) to justify \(F_2\).

We have circular justification:

$$
F_1\rightarrow F_2\rightarrow F_1.
$$

Therefore every determined fact needs something like a **justification ancestry**.

Not necessarily a tree—it may be a directed graph:

$$
F_1 \rightarrow F_3
$$

$$
F_2 \rightarrow F_3
$$

$$
F_3 \rightarrow F_4.
$$

That connects directly to the Navya-Nyāya relational lens and to the existing finding that claims, evidence and justification should not simply be collapsed into one object. 

---

# 6. And this gives us a better definition of determination

I would currently formulate it as a **research hypothesis**:

> **Fact determination is the disciplined establishment of a claim as warranted, based on observations and/or previously determined knowledge, under applicable principles, methods, and contextual constraints.**

Then:

$$
\boxed{
Determination \neq Extraction
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Extraction\ supplies\ material
}
$$

while:

$$
\boxed{
Determination\ establishes\ warrant
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Admission\ records\ the\ resulting\ epistemic\ status
}
$$

This is consistent with the existing research's distinction between **evidence, justification, epistemic determination and knowledge**. 

---

## 7. The next question is even more interesting

If facts are determined from previous actions, principles and methods, then we need to ask:

> **Where did the first determinations come from?**

Eventually the chain reaches:

$$
Observation
$$

or some external source of evidence.

So we get:

$$
\boxed{
Observation
\rightarrow
Evidence
\rightarrow
Determination
\rightarrow
Fact
\rightarrow
Future\ Evidence
\rightarrow
New\ Determination
}
$$

That means **Knowledge has memory**, but memory itself is not knowledge.

And this is where your earlier **Gaṇeśa lens — observation → memory → discrimination → revision → integration** becomes particularly powerful. 

I think this may be the more fundamental domain model we should investigate next:

> **How does a system move from what happened, to what was determined about what happened, to what can subsequently be used as a basis for determining something else?**

That is potentially more fundamental than the current **Candidate → Knowledge** model.
