### 1.2 The First Teaching: The Eternal Self (Verses 2.11-30)

**Text:**
```
"The wise never lament for the living and the dead."

"From the beginning of time, there was never a time when I did not exist,
nor you, nor all these warriors and kings,
nor shall we ever cease to live in the future."

"Just as the soul passes through the transformation of bodies
from childhood, youth, and then old age,
similarly, the soul passes into another body.
The wise never gets deluded by this transformation."

"The contact between the senses and the sense objects
creates sensations of cold, heat, pleasure, and distress.
These are not permanent, and come and go like seasons."

"Of that which is temporary, there is no eternal existence;
of that which is eternal, there is no destruction."

"You must know, the individual consciousness that pervades the entire body
is indestructible, and nobody can destroy this imperishable consciousness."

"Only the material body is perishable,
but the embodied consciousness within is indestructible, eternal, and infinite."

"The soul can never take birth, nor does it die.
It was never created. It's unborn, eternal, indestructible, and ageless."

"Just as a person removes old clothes and wears new ones,
similarly soul gives up the old body and accept the new bodies."

"Weapons can't cut the soul; fire can't burn it;
water can't wet it, and air can't dry it."

"The soul is unbreakable and incombustible.
It is everlasting and is present, it can't be changed,
and it existed from the beginning of time."

| **All-pervading** | Present everywhere | Knowledge Ātma underlies all knowledge |

**KnowledgeOS Application:**

| **Body** | Assertion \( A \) |
| **Reincarnation** | Reinstantiation |
| **Clothes** | Different representations |
| **Weapons, fire, water, air** | Operations that cannot destroy identity |

### 1.4 The Third Teaching: The Yoga of Action (Verses 2.39-53)

**Text:**
```
"O Arjuna, I have revealed to you the knowledge of soul and consciousness
called the Sankhya yoga or the analytical knowledge.
Now I will reveal buddhi yoga to you, which is also known as the yoga of intellect.
When you act upon this knowledge, you'll release yourself from the bondage of karma."

"Acting with this state, one will have no loss,
and even the slightest of effort can save one from the greatest fears."

"The intellect of those who are on this spiritual path has a one-pointed focus.
While the intellect of those who desire the mundane enjoyment is many-branched."

"Those who have limited intelligence misinterpret the holy Vedas
and only get attracted to those words of Vedas that are pleasing to their senses."

"Your right is to perform your duty and your work,
you are not the cause of the results of your actions,
nor should you get attached to inaction."

"Perform your duties and abandon your attachments from the results,
i.e., Success and failure; such a balance is known as yoga."

"Seek shelter in divine knowledge and discard behaviors that are reward-seeking."

"A wise man will always practice the work without getting attached to the outcomes;
Whether it's good or bad. That's Yoga."

"Once your intellect surpasses this dense jungle of illusion,
you will be indifferent to whatever has been heard
and whatever is yet to be heard."

"When your mind and consciousness are no longer affected by
the fruitful and alluring sections of Vedas,
then you will attain the perfect state of Yoga."

| **Balance** | Equanimity | Non-attachment |
| **Abandon attachments** | Detachment | EC = TRUE not required |

### 1.5 The Fourth Teaching: The State of Steady Wisdom (Verses 2.54-72)

**Text:**
```
Arjuna said:
"O Keshav, what are the traits and characteristics of a person
situated in divine consciousness? How does that person sit?
How does he speak, and How does he walk?"

Lord Shri Krishna said:
"O Partha, when one discards all selfish, materialistic desires
and becomes satisfied within,
then that person can be considered as situated in divine knowledge."

"The one whose mind remains undisturbed from pain,
who doesn't crave for pleasure,
Who is free from all the attachments, fear, and anger
is called a sage of steady wisdom."

"One who is not attached to any condition in this world,
the one who not happy and joyful when fortune favors him
and not get resentful when the fortune is against him,
he is called a sage with perfect knowledge."

"The one who withdraws senses and stops pleasing his senses,
the way a tortoise withdraws its limbs into the shell.
Then he has firmly established himself in divine wisdom."

"These senses are so strong, restless, and unsettled,
O son of Kunti, that they can steal the mind of
even a wise person who has been practicing self-control."

"By constantly meditating on these objects of senses that give temporary pleasure,
one becomes attached to it, and attachment flourishes desire.
Eventually, desire manifests anger."

"Anger clouds the judgment, resulting in confusion;
confused memory destroys the intellect,
and when the intellect is destroyed, one is destroyed."

"However, the one who can control his mind and senses
and can free himself/herself from the attachments,
even while using those sense objects, attains the grace of the divine."

"The one who gives up all the desires lives free from the sense of greed,
possessiveness, and ego attains peace."

"O, Partha! After attaining the realization of the absolute truth,
one is never deluded. If any person is in this state of mind
at even the last hour of death,
one can be released from this vicious cycle of birth and death
and reach the supreme abode of pure consciousness."
```

**Philosophical Contribution:**

The **state of steady wisdom** (Sthita-Prajna) is the **ideal epistemic state**:

| **Free from attachment** | Detachment | Non-attachment |
| **Free from fear and anger** | Impartiality | No bias |
| **Tortoise withdrawing limbs** | Controlled senses | Governance over epistemic processes |
| **Desire → Attachment → Anger → Delusion** | The cycle of epistemic failure | The path to Asuri state |
| **Control → Peace → Liberation** | The path to epistemic success | The path to Daivi state |

**The Principle:**

$$

\text{SteadyWisdom}(K_t) = \text{NoAttachment}(K_t) \land \text{NoDesire}(K_t) \land \text{NoFear}(K_t) \land \text{NoAnger}(K_t)
}
$$
