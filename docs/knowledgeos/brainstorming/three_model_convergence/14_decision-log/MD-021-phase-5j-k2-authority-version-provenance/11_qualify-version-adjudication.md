# Phase 5J — `Qualify` Version Adjudication (now 4 variants, up from 3 in Phase 5H/5I)

## The four variants

1. `Qualify: Observation → Evidence` (seq 0630 §49.76, 1 conceptual argument, as an equation).
2. `Qualify: Observation × Policy → Evidence` (D285-6, 2 arguments).
3. `CaptureAndQualify(O)` (seq 0795 §170.4, 1 argument, differently named).
4. **`Qualify(o,c,π) → e`** (Step 272A §272A.6, **new this phase** — 3 arguments: observation, context,
   policy).

## Testing the required classification

- **Version progression**: **Weakly plausible by dating** — variant 4 (2026-08-30) sits chronologically
  between variant 3 (2026-08-29) and variant 2 (2026-08-31), which *could* suggest a progressive
  addition of arguments (1→3→2, roughly) — but per `04`'s own mandatory discipline, dating alone does
  not establish this, and no document states it.
- **Specialization**: **The most defensible reading, extended from Phase 5I** — variant 1 (1-arg) could
  be a simplification of variant 4 (3-arg, dropping context/policy as implicit); variant 4 could itself
  be a specialization of variant 2 (both include Policy; variant 4 adds Context, variant 2 does not
  name it) — **but variant 2 and variant 4 do not agree on arity either** (2 vs. 3 arguments), so even
  this reading does not cleanly chain all four together.
- **Parameterization**: Plausible for variants 2 and 4 specifically (both make Policy an explicit
  argument) — but they still disagree on whether Context is also explicit (variant 4) or implicit
  (variant 2).
- **Implementation refinement**: **Not supported** — none of the three executable scripts implements
  any of the four variants (Phase 5I `07`, reconfirmed this phase: no script calls a `Qualify`-shaped
  function).
- **Incompatible definitions**: **Also applies, descriptively** — the four variants do not share one
  consistent arity or argument list.
- **Unresolved competition**: **The governing verdict** — no document reconciles all four, and no
  explicit statement anywhere names one as authoritative over the others.

## New finding: `Qualify`'s own status classification (Step 272A §272A.24)

`Qualify` is the **only** one of the four variants to carry an explicit corpus-native status label:
**"REQUIRED / POLICY-DEPENDENT."** This is a genuine, if partial, authority signal — Step 272A treats
`Qualify` as a load-bearing, required member of `𝒪_sem`, explicitly conditioned on policy. **This
status label does not by itself pick among the four arity variants** — it describes `Qualify`'s
*importance*, not its *signature*.

## Verdict

**E — UNRESOLVED**, now with 4 (not 3) documented, mutually-inconsistent arity variants, one
(Step 272A's own) carrying the corpus's own clearest importance/status marker but not resolving the
arity question. No governance act, revision statement, or migration mapping unifies them.
