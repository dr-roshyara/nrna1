# Final Verdict

## Primary verdict

**B — FRAMEWORK IS A TEST APPARATUS BUT REQUIRES AN EXTERNAL SEMANTIC DEFINITION.**

## Why B, and not the other four

- **Not A** — ruled out directly in `03`: nothing in the framework generates or derives content;
  every named component is a bare slot.
- **Not C (framework itself insufficiently specified)** — the framework's own *comparison logic*
  (the equivalence condition on `B`, the preservation condition on `P`, the capability-closure gate,
  the invariant-custody cost function) is well-formed and, in two cases, fully formal and reused from
  already-admitted machinery (`02`). What is missing is not the shape of the apparatus but the
  content of its inputs — a meaningfully different, narrower gap than "the apparatus itself is
  incoherent."
- **Not D (framework cannot address the question at all)** — too final. The framework is not
  structurally incapable of ever bearing on the missing predicate; `Warrant` is explicitly named as
  one of its own ten dimensions, giving it a designated place to receive a definition, should one
  ever be supplied. "Cannot address" would misstate a "not yet operable without an input" finding as
  a permanent incapacity.
- **Not E (evidence insufficient)** — the fresh cold read of `19` §7, the component-by-component
  census (`02`), and the outcome-classification (`03`) together give a definitive, well-evidenced
  answer; nothing about this study's own evidence base was found lacking for this specific question.

## Exact source facts determining this verdict

`19` §7's own text names `B`'s six and `P`'s ten components without defining any of them (`02`); the
same document states the framework should not be run before Levels 2/3 are answered, and its own
restructure table marks both `OPEN` (`02`); `Warrant` is the one `P`-dimension most directly adjacent
to "surviving a defeater," and it receives no more definition than any other component (`03`/`04`).
