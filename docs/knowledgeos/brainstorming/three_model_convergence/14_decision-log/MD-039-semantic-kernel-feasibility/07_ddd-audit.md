# DDD Audit — Does the Framework Itself Introduce Domain Semantics?

## Does the Semantic Kernel Equivalence framework introduce actual domain semantics for `Defeater`,
`Challenge`, `Verdict`, or "survival"?

**No.** The framework's own vocabulary (`B`'s six components, `P`'s ten components) is stated at a
higher level of abstraction than the carrier/atom/capability taxonomy this whole sequence has
consistently found governs the series (MD-033 `07`, MD-034 `07`, MD-036 `08`, MD-037 `07`) — it
proposes comparing *tuples of named, undefined quantities* across two kernel configurations, without
attaching identity, lifecycle, or aggregate structure to any of `Defeater`/`Challenge`/`Verdict`/
"survival" specifically. `Warrant` (the `P`-dimension closest to the missing predicate) is named,
not defined, and is not given DDD structure by anything in `19`.

## Does the framework change an aggregate invariant, command semantics, lifecycle, domain
responsibility, domain-service behaviour, or bounded-context semantics?

**No, on every count.** The framework is itself a proposed *comparison methodology* (compare two
kernel configurations' external behaviour and epistemic-preservation vectors), not a domain model —
it operates one level of abstraction above the carrier/operator layer this reconstruction has
consistently found to be where all prior findings live.

## Preserved conclusion

Consistent with every prior audit in this sequence: **the issue remains primarily at the formal/
type-system layer.** The framework's own proposal does not move it into DDD territory, and this
study does not promote any of its named variables into an entity, value object, domain event, policy,
domain service, or invariant without source support — none is found.
