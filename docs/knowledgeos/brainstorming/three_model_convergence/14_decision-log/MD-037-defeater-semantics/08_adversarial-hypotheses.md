# Adversarial Hypotheses

## H1 — A formal definition of "surviving a defeater" exists somewhere in the searched material
but was overlooked

**Fails.** All 17 files were read in full (`02`), not sampled or grep-skimmed for the zero-hit
files; every `defeater`-family occurrence across the entire population was quoted and classified
(`03`). No definition was missed.

## H2 — The corpus's own repeated, consistent use of the term across multiple documents constitutes
a de facto operational definition through convention

**Partially succeeds, precisely qualified.** The *label* is used consistently — every occurrence
(`12`, `15`, `17`, `18`) denotes the same thing (V6's own added `Defeater`-input requirement), and no
document contradicts another's usage. But consistent *labeling* is not the same as a *checkable
definition* — no document states what distinguishes "surviving" from "not surviving" in operational
terms (`04`). **Succeeds as evidence the concept is coherent across the series; fails as evidence a
formal definition exists.**

## H3 — The `I9` invariant ("`Verdict ⇒ Defeater` consideration") is equivalent to "surviving a
defeater," and this study wrongly treated them as different

**Fails.** `I9` (`18` §5.3) states only that a `Defeater` was *considered* — a weaker condition than
*surviving* one. The source itself never equates the two; a defeater could be considered and then
itself be defeated (i.e., not survive) without that violating `I9` as literally stated. Treating
them as distinct is the source-faithful reading, not an artifact of this study's own analysis.

## H4 — The absence of a formal definition means the concept itself is not well-formed

**Fails, as an overclaim.** The series' own treatment of the question (labeled "real open
falsifier," "substantive but non-blocking") treats the concept as a legitimate, worthwhile research
target that has simply not yet been formalized — not as an ill-formed or incoherent notion. Absence
of a definition and absence of well-formedness are different claims; only the former is supported.

## H5 — The corpus's own "open question" framing settles the epistemic-status question this study
was asked to investigate

**Succeeds, narrowly.** It settles *this study's own central question* (does a formal definition
exist — no, confirmed independently by the corpus's own self-audit) but does **not** settle the
*deeper* question of whether V6 is semantically superior to V0 — that remains, and the corpus's own
open-questions register agrees it remains, unresolved (`17` Q-6/Q-7 are explicitly non-blocking, not
answered).

## Summary

| Hypothesis | Result |
|---|---|
| H1 (definition overlooked) | Fails |
| H2 (consistent use = de facto definition) | Partially succeeds — coherent usage, not a checkable definition |
| H3 (I9 = surviving a defeater) | Fails |
| H4 (absence of definition = ill-formed concept) | Fails |
| H5 (corpus's own open-question framing settles this study's question) | Succeeds, narrowly — confirms the negative finding, does not resolve the deeper V0/V6 question |
