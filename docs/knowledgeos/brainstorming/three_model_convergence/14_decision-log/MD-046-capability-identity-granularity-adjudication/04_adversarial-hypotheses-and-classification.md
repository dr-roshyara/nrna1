# MD-046 §4 — Adversarial Hypotheses (Phase G) and Final Classification

## Ten hypotheses

**H1 (capability identity is being defined by its name).** **SUPPORTED as the corpus's own de facto
practice** — the MinKer chain's own 13 names and `reviews/kernel/`'s own 9-item map are each treated,
in their own respective contexts, as if the name individuates the capability; the chain's own later
self-critique (`021025`) explicitly identifies this as the flaw, but no alternative was ever executed.

**H2 (capability identity is being defined by its current implementation).** **NOT SUPPORTED** — the
MinKer chain explicitly and repeatedly rejects this (`≡_sem` is designed precisely to allow different
implementations of the same capability); no evidence found of implementation-based identity being
used anywhere.

**H3 (capability identity is being defined by Kernel membership).** **NOT SUPPORTED** — no place
found circularly defines a capability by whether it belongs to a candidate Kernel; the chain's own
`ρ` mapping is explicitly designed to keep these separate (a capability can belong to the system
without belonging to the Kernel).

**H4 (capability identity is being defined by responsibility alone).** **NOT SUPPORTED** as a
completed practice — a responsibility mapping (`ρ`) is proposed but never instantiated, so nothing is
currently "being defined" by it either way.

**H5 (capability identity is being defined by input/output alone).** **NOT SUPPORTED** — the
`(I_c,O_c,Γ_c,ℐ_c)` shape is explicitly held back from adoption (file 9), precisely to avoid this.

**H6 (capability identity is being defined by observed behaviour alone).** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED as
the chain's own intended future direction** — `Beh_𝔠(K)`-based identity is the direction the chain is
heading, but it is not yet instantiated for any real capability, so this remains a design intention,
not a completed practice.

**H7 (the 13-capability decomposition is assumed rather than evidenced).** **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** —
directly, by the census's own central finding (`01_...md`): zero corpus evidence outside the chain's
own self-contained discussion supports any of the 13 names' boundaries, and the chain's own two most-
discussed names (`Interpret`, `Determine`) are discussed only as illustrative risk examples, never as
evidenced boundaries.

**H8 (`DetectGap`/`Qualify` conclusions are decomposition-dependent).** **SUPPORTED**, unchanged from
MD-045's own finding, now with the added, harder fact that no independent corpus vocabulary exists to
cross-check either finding against.

**H9 (semantic equivalence can be established without choosing a canonical decomposition).**
**NOT SUPPORTED BY ANY EVIDENCE FOUND** — no example of this being achieved exists anywhere in the
searched corpus.

**H10 (the corpus already contains enough material to derive a non-arbitrary criterion).**
**NOT SUPPORTED** — the census (`01_...md`) and the adequacy test (`03_...md`) both conclude no
sufficient criterion, or even a strong candidate for one, exists in the material searched.

## Explicit answer: can MinKer safely proceed beyond the MD-045 hard stop?

**No.** Not only does no capability-identity criterion exist for the MinKer chain's own 13-name
universe — this reconstruction's own separately-developed kernel/capability research track
(`reviews/kernel/`, `brainstorming/kernel/`) has produced a **second, non-overlapping vocabulary**
with its own internal, unresolved tensions (a documented vocabulary-collision registry; an
aggregate-hypothesis falsification for one vocabulary that has never been attempted for the other;
a direct, recorded contradiction between a "Kernel as god object, too large" finding and a "minimal
9-item map, possibly too small" finding). **Proceeding with MinKer's own 13-capability universe now
would not merely risk one arbitrary decomposition choice — it would additionally ignore a second,
equally-unresolved candidate vocabulary already on record in this same corpus, with no evidence
connecting the two.**

## Final classification

**C — NO CORPUS-GROUNDED CRITERION FOUND.**

The smallest possible new research question this finding names, precisely, not answered here: **does
any evidence exist — anywhere in this corpus, including material not yet searched under this
reconstruction's own firewall discipline — establishing a decomposition-independent criterion for
when two capability/concept descriptions denote the same semantic object, given that this corpus's
own two separately-developed capability vocabularies (MinKer's 13 names, and `reviews/kernel/`'s own
9-item "existing law" map and its distinct aggregate-member list) share no names, and have never been
cross-checked against each other anywhere this reconstruction has read?** This is named as a future
research/design question, not answered, and not invented an answer for.
