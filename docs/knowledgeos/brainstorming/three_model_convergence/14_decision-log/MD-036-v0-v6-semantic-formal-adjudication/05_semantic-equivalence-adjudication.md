## Ruling out each level in turn, from the top

**Not 1 (identity)**: the two rules have different domains (`06` line 41: two-element input sets for
`V0`; `12`'s own definition: an additional `Defeater` element for `V6`) and a demonstrated difference
in reachability consequence (`04`). Not identical.

**Not 2 (formal equivalence)**: formal equivalence would require the two rules to be provably
interchangeable (e.g., every world reachable under one is reachable under the other via some
translation). The reachability table (`12` lines 196–199) directly demonstrates a world-class where
they diverge (no `Challenge` present) — not interchangeable.

**3 — STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE: the classification this study reaches.** A demonstrable,
source-grounded structural relationship exists: `V6`'s own rule is `V0`'s own rule with exactly one
additional required carrier (`Defeater`) added to the input set, everything else (atom label,
output carrier, the `{Claim,Evidence}`-vs-`{Hypothesis,Evidence}` disjunction) held fixed. This is a
precise, checkable transformation from one rule to the other — not a resemblance in name or
vocabulary alone (which would be level 1–2 in the cross-model ladder's own lower tiers, not
applicable here since both rules already share exact vocabulary by construction), and not a mere
functional analogy (level 4, which would apply if the relationship were looser — e.g., "both rules
serve a similar purpose" without a precise, statable transformation). The transformation itself
(add one required carrier to the domain) is explicit and exact.

**Why not 4 (functional analogy) instead**: functional analogy is the right classification when a
precise structural transformation cannot be stated — e.g., MD-029's own B `Validate` ↔ C1 P-3
comparison, where the mapping was a "disclosed, constructed" inference across two independently-
developed models with no shared vocabulary. Here, by contrast, both rules are stated in the *same*
document series, using *identical* vocabulary, and the transformation between them is a single,
precisely nameable operation (domain-extension by one carrier) — a stronger, more exact relationship
than "analogy."

**Why not 5 (partial correspondence)**: partial correspondence would apply if only *some* fields
matched and others were simply unaddressed or unrelated. Here, every field that both objects share
(atom-adjacent framing, output carrier, general shape of the rule) matches exactly except the one,
precisely-identified domain difference — this is not a partial, patchy correspondence but a complete
one modulo one exact, nameable change.

**Why not 6 (incompatible)**: incompatibility would require the two rules to assert mutually
exclusive claims about the same fixed question. They do not — `V6` does not contradict `V0`'s own
stated rule; it is explicitly framed as an *alternative design choice* for the same derivation step
(`12` line 132, "seven alternative reasonable models"), not a competing claim about what is
"actually" true.

**Why not 7 (unresolved)**: the relationship is not unresolved — it is precisely characterized by
`04` and `06_mathematical-comparison.md` (domain difference, output identity, exact transformation).
Reserving "unresolved" for cases where the evidence genuinely does not support a classification would
misrepresent how much *is* established here.

## Verdict for this section

**STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE.** The strongest justified relationship between `V0` and `V6` is a
precise, source-grounded structural transformation (domain-extension by the `Defeater` carrier),
not identity, not formal equivalence, and not a looser functional analogy, partial correspondence,
incompatibility, or unresolved state.
