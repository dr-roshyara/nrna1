# Governance Authority

## Applying the established discipline

Reusing the same `LegitimateAuthority(a, scope, source, validity)` predicate this reconstruction has
applied consistently to the K-1/K-2 governance track (MD-022) — not inventing a new one.

- `Authority(a, scope)`: theoretical — this reconstruction can *represent* the idea that some party has
  authority over corpus-boundary decisions.
- `LegitimateAuthority(a, scope, source, validity)`: an **organizational fact requiring independent
  establishment.** MD-010 and MD-011 are themselves recorded as *"ADOPTED (human instruction)"* — they
  identify that a human instruction produced them, but neither document names a standing role, body, or
  charter authorized to make *future* corpus-boundary decisions of this kind.

## Search performed

Checked `00_control/protocol.md`, MD-010, MD-011, and `14_decision-log/model-boundary-decisions.md` in
full for any statement of who holds ongoing authority over the corpus boundary. **None found.**

## Verdict

**`LEGITIMATE AUTHORITY NOT ESTABLISHED IN CORPUS.`** The organizational question this package hands
forward, alongside DQ-1 itself: **which role, body, or delegated authority in the real organization is
empowered to make this specific class of decision (corpus-boundary admission)?** This mirrors exactly
the same open question the K-1/K-2 track's own MD-022 already poses for a different decision class
(GK-5K-1 through GK-5K-5) — not because the two are the same decision, but because this reconstruction
has now found the identical authority gap twice, independently, for two unrelated governance
questions. Worth naming as a pattern, not merely a coincidence.
