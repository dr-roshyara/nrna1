# Adjudications and Contradictions

Three parts, per the authorization: (1) the strict kernel adjudication required by §7; (2) the
representation-dependence hard-constraint check required by §8; (3) the dedicated cross-model

## 1. Strict kernel adjudication (authorization §7)

### 1a. What "kernel" means inside Model A, reconstructed on its own terms first

Model A's evidence base does **not** converge on a single kernel concept. It contains, preserved
exactly as Phase 1 left them, three genuinely distinct sub-threads that must not be collapsed:

- **A four-way `unresolved_equivalence` family of typed-schema candidates** (§F of Model A's concept
  register): 0219's Proposition/Position/Claim, 0216's KnowledgeClaim/Evidence/Determination/
  Authority, "K-1"'s KnowledgeAggregate+ConflictRecord+VerificationPort, and Kernel Candidate
  v0.2/v0.3's eight-field structure. None is shown structure-preserving-equivalent to another; none
  is canonical.
- **Ten self-flagged `alternative_minimal_kernel: true` proposals** across the Sañjaya/Arjuna
  architecture cluster (0219, 0430, 0435, 0436, 0441, 0443, 0451, 0800, 0801, 0808) — an
  architecturally rich two-layer system (Sañjaya/Arjuna), a DDD Entity/Role distinction
  (Krishna≠Sārathi), Decision Readiness, and Second-Order Observation, none of which were ever
  cross-examined against the four-way family above, or against each other, for structural
  equivalence.
- **The KR-SIM companion series' six-cycle "zero kernel candidate" finding** (§N, scope-corrected at
  the Phase-1 audit) — a genuinely well-supported *negative* result, but narrow: it establishes only
  that one specific mathematical test apparatus finds no new formal primitive when driven by Gītā
  chapter content, not that Model A's evidence as a whole proposes nothing kernel-relevant.

**No single "Model A's kernel" exists to compare against Model B's.** Any cross-model kernel claim
must specify which of these three sub-threads it means, and none of the three has itself been
resolved even within Model A's own evidence.

### 1b. What "kernel" means inside Model B, reconstructed on its own terms first

Model B's evidence base likewise does not converge on one kernel object, but its unresolvedness has a
different *character* — differentiated by an explicit four-state discipline rather than left as an
undifferentiated family:

- **An executed, falsified, and re-resolved minimality result** (§B/§P-1/§P-2): the original
  13-operator candidate C0 is proven not uniquely minimal; four distinct cardinality-8 minimal
  kernels are found instead, under different admissible semantic algebras — a **TESTED** and
  **ESTABLISHED**-quality finding (representation-dependence itself is established, even though no
  single kernel is).
- **A dedicated four-state kernel-candidate table** (§P, 15 rows) distinguishing ESTABLISHED (2
  rows) / TESTED→REJECTED (6 rows, plus one cardinality-level rejection paired with a replacement
  established finding) / PROPOSED→UNTESTED (2 rows) / UNRESOLVED (1 row) / a named fifth
  "tested-survives-not-established" state (2 rows) — none conflated with another.
- **A four-way conflation diagnosis** (§L): Model B's own corpus independently caught itself
  repeatedly collapsing Knowledge Space, Epistemic State, Kernel, and History under one "Kernel"
  label, and disciplined itself against it (M0085) — a *methodological* parallel to Model A's own
  need to keep its three kernel sub-threads separate (§1a above), arrived at completely
  independently.

### 1c. The cross-model question, answered as rigorously as the evidence permits

**Is there evidence that Model A's kernel concept and Model B's kernel structures are the same kind
of object, different representations of the same object, structurally corresponding objects, or
merely analogous concepts?**

No corpus-internal act in either model references, compares, or tests against the other. Beyond
that absence of any direct evidence, the two are built from **different formal categories**: Model
A's candidates are typed schemas (catalogs of *kinds of thing* — claims, evidence, propositions);
Model B's candidates are operator sets (catalogs of *actions* — Observe, Interpret, Relate). A schema
and an operator set are not directly comparable without a stated translation (e.g., "operator X
produces/consumes instances of schema-type Y"), which neither corpus provides.

**Recorded per the authorization's own explicit instruction for exactly this situation: UNRESOLVED.**
This is not a weak or incomplete adjudication — it is the correct outcome given that neither corpus
was constructed with the other's kernel material in view, and given that even *within* each corpus
the kernel question is not settled.

## 2. Representation-dependence hard constraint (authorization §8)

Model B establishes, as a tested result, that kernel minimality is representation-dependent (§P-2):
operator count, apparent simplicity, or diagrammatic similarity are never sufficient evidence for
cross-model identity, because Model B's own evidence already contains multiple non-equivalent minimal
kernels under different representations.

**Application to `02_correspondence-matrix.md`**: this constraint binds against any row reaching
**STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE** or above, requiring an explicit check of whether the claimed
correspondence depends on a specific choice of representation/operators/equivalence relation/
invariants/reduction procedure/composition rule.

**No row in the matrix reached STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE or above** (`02_correspondence-matrix.md`'s
summary tally: 0 rows at STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE, FORMAL EQUIVALENCE ESTABLISHED, or IDENTITY
ESTABLISHED). The representation-dependence hard constraint therefore has no candidate to bind
against in this adjudication pass. **Recorded as inapplicable-by-vacuity, not silently skipped** —
the constraint remains fully in force for any future Phase-3-adjacent work that might later propose a
stronger correspondence; it simply had nothing to test this pass.

## 3. Cross-model contradiction/disagreement register (authorization §9)

**Finding, stated first because it governs how to read the rest of this section**: this adjudication
pass found **no case where Model A asserts X and Model B asserts not-X about the same specifically
identified object.** Genuine contradiction requires a shared claim space precise enough for two
models to actually conflict; given `02_correspondence-matrix.md`'s result (no row above PARTIAL
CORRESPONDENCE), the two models are not, in the main, making claims specific enough to collide — they
are largely talking about different objects that happen to share vocabulary. This absence of
contradiction is reported honestly, not manufactured into false tension, per the authorization's own
instruction not to resolve genuine disagreement by convenient reformulation — the inverse failure
(inventing disagreement that is not there) is equally a violation of evidentiary discipline.

Two **apparent** contradictions were examined and found, on inspection, to be term-collisions rather
than substantive disagreements — recorded here so a future reader does not have to re-discover that
they are not genuine conflicts:

### CMC-1 — Apparent conflict over decision/determination authority (resolved: not a genuine contradiction)

Model A asserts a governance invariant that the human Knower, never the system, holds final decision
authority (`KnowledgeOS =/=> Decision`, §J; Sārathi Function 9, "Preserve human agency," Row 5 of the
matrix). Model B's kernel `Determine` operator carries **no** stated authority-boundary constraint —
it is defined and ablation-tested as an isolated formal primitive.

**Classification: conditionally reconcilable, not irreconcilable.** The absence of an authority
constraint in Model B's operator definition is not itself an assertion that the system (rather than a
human) should hold final authority — Model B's kernel is a description of an isolated formal
capability, not a deployed system, and nothing in Model B's evidence rules out wrapping a
`Determine`-containing kernel in a Model-A-style governance layer that reserves final authority to a
human. The two claims occupy different layers (formal-primitive-definition vs. deployment-governance-
rule) and do not collide. **This is recorded as an absence of a shared constraint in Model B, not as
a contradiction of Model A's constraint.**

### CMC-2 — Apparent conflict over what "invariant" governs (resolved: term-collision, not a contradiction)

Model A's invariants (EKI catalog, §H; the governance invariant, §J) are deontic — statements of what
must never happen. Model B's invariant custody (`Custody(I)`, §F) is mathematical — a property
preserved under a projection. Both use the word "invariant."

**Classification: term-collision, not a contradiction.** The two senses do not make competing claims
about a shared object; they are different technical uses of the same English word, exactly as
`02_correspondence-matrix.md` Row 7 records (INCOMPATIBLE, high confidence — the correct
classification for a non-correspondence, not for a disagreement). Recorded here explicitly so this
term-collision is not later mistaken for a substantive cross-model tension.

**No third case was found.** This section's contradiction register is therefore short by design, not
by omission — it reflects the matrix's own finding that the two models rarely make specific enough
claims about shared objects to either agree or disagree.
