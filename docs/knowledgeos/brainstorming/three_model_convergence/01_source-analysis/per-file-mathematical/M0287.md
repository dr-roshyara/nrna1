## Summary

Reformulates the knowledge-state model from K=(d_1,...,d_n) to K=(D,R), where D is the set of dimensions/factors and R is the set of relations among them, on the grounds that a factor alone (e.g. d_1=evidence) is not yet knowledge until related to something else (e.g. a claim, via R(d_1,d_2)). Reinterprets d_3=0 as 'R(d_3,d_j)~0 for the relevant relationships' rather than an intrinsic property, and gives a worked example showing that zeroing then reactivating dimensions can expose interaction effects among the remaining ones. Softens the stronger claim 'without relationship you have no knowledge' to 'a distinguishable factor becomes epistemically informative only through a relation to something else under a question or purpose,' noting a single factor can itself carry internal relational structure (e.g. a temporal sequence).
