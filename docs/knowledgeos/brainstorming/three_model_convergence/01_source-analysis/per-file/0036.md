## What Is This Document About?
Twelve concepts and eleven invariants are projected unchanged into three domains. **Ten of eleven
invariants survive**, including — notably — the corrected I-11 (0027's restatement), explicitly
validated *because* the original stronger wording would have failed against a clinical protocol that
plainly informs order generation without executing. **One invariant fails outright: I-4** (a
projection is derived and cited by nothing) — a discharge summary and a signed financial statement
are both **generated and legally authoritative**, proving derivation and authority are independent
dimensions, not correlated ones. This falsification is then **withdrawn and re-diagnosed** in the same
document (matching the mechanical explanation already established at 0014/0018/0027): the field
conflates provenance with standing, and the real defect is that `authorities.yaml` has no governed
transition structure, so "attested" cannot be expressed.

Two further results are genuinely new relative to everything read so far. **F-2**: "one PKS per
product" fails for configurable products — a single ERP deployed to fifty customers with different
charts of accounts raises an unanswered question the ontology cannot even pose (is this one PKS with
fifty bindings, or fifty PKSs?), exposing that `PRODUCT` is treated as atomic when it is not. **F-3**:
the authority chain has no place for **external, imposed-but-not-owned** authority (a regulator, an
accounting standard) — "the ontology can express *who decides*; it cannot express *who must be
obeyed*."

§6 delivers the document's structural insight: four missing relationships (`specializes`,
`constrains`, `instantiates`, `attests`) are shown to share **one shape**, not four unrelated gaps —
existing relationships (`contains`/`references`/`generates`/`depends on`) are all **structural** (what
a thing is made of); the missing ones are all **transformational** (what happens to a thing). The
document states this as the strongest possible form of a negative result: *"that is a single coherent
gap, not four unrelated ones — which makes it far more likely to be a real omission than an artifact
of my three examples."*

The closing methodological point is the document's real payload: **PublicDigit masked two of the
three failures.** Its own multi-tenancy (`organisation_id` in code) and its own election-law-as-
product-domain-knowledge both instantiate F-2 and F-3 respectively, invisibly, because the single
product under prior validation never exposed either gap. *"A single-product validation could never
have found them — which is the argument for cross-product projection, now made by evidence rather
than by principle."*

## Main Argument / Purpose
**Argument:** the ontology describes composition, not transformation — and this is not visible from
one product, because that product's own instances of the gap are silently absorbed into its
implementation rather than surfacing as a modelling failure. **Purpose:** to demonstrate, by evidence
rather than argument, why cross-product validation is methodologically necessary and cannot be
substituted by deeper single-product analysis.
