## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK: purpose-relative sufficiency and decision readiness fully formalized.** Executes
Step 13, opening with the practical question: "When can KnowledgeOS legitimately say: 'We know enough to
proceed'?" — distinct from "we know everything," since complete knowledge is impossible in an open world.
Delivers **`Truth≠Knowledge≠Completeness≠Sufficiency`** and **`Sufficiency≠DecisionReadiness`**. Formalizes
purpose-relative completeness via a requirement set `𝒬(P_r)`.

**Delivers the file's central cautionary finding**: **`Coverage≠Sufficiency`** — a worked example shows
all six migration requirements answered while `BackupStatus=Contested` leaves the state unsafe. Delivers
three-valued requirement satisfaction (`Satisfied/Unsatisfied/Unknown`, `Unknown≠Unsatisfied`). Delivers a
second cautionary finding: a single unresolved CRITICAL requirement can block readiness even at 95%
coverage — "percentage completeness cannot replace requirement-level evaluation."

**Delivers the file's central architectural result**: **`Sufficient≠Authorized≠Ready`** — three distinct
decision layers (epistemic/governance/operational), "an extremely important DDD boundary." **Delivers the
file's most consequential epistemic-integrity safeguard**: **`RiskAcceptance does not eliminate epistemic
uncertainty`** — an accepted risk over an unresolved requirement must never be silently promoted to
"Known"; `KnowledgeGap=True` and `RiskAccepted=True` must coexist explicitly — "exactly the kind of
distinction an enterprise knowledge system needs." Delivers a crucial counter-case: `Sufficient=False` can
coexist with `Ready=True` when criticality is only Medium and the business explicitly accepts risk —
"epistemic insufficiency does not always imply operational prohibition."

**Connects Step 13 to the Ideal-State framework**: introduces a purpose-specific discrepancy `Δ_t^{P_r}`
and a **purpose-specific Ideal State `I_t(P_r)`** replacing one universal ideal — "there is no universal
'complete knowledge.'" **Delivers the file's clearest normative statement**: readiness must NEVER be a
scalar formula (`Ready=0.95×KnowledgeScore` is "a serious theoretical mistake") — readiness is a **decision
predicate, not a continuous metric**. Delivers purpose-dependent risk thresholds (exploratory/architecture/
production) and three never-to-be-collapsed policy layers (Epistemic/Acceptance/Decision Policy).

**Delivers a six-concept DDD model** and reconnects Zero/Lord/Sārathi: Zero's `Δ_t^P` gets a five-value
classification (Blocking/Non-blocking/Informational/Unknown/Risk-accepted); Lord's objective is redefined
from "reduce all discrepancy" to "reduce the discrepancy relevant to the purpose" — "a major improvement";
Sārathi is "DecisionOrchestration under epistemic and governance constraints." Delivers a crucial temporal
caution: readiness is dynamic — a decision justified yesterday may not be justified today.

**Delivers a genuinely valuable new artifact — the `ReadinessCertificate`**:
`RC=(Purpose,KnowledgeStateVersion,Requirements,SatisfiedRequirements,OutstandingGaps,RiskAcceptances,
Authority,PolicyVersion,Timestamp)`, giving an auditable answer to "why was this decision considered
ready?" — worked via a concrete 18-requirement migration-approval audit reconstruction, "much stronger
than 'the AI said it was okay.'"

**Delivers the file's central formal deliverable — ten canonical invariants (S1-S10)**: Truth≠Sufficiency;
Completeness is purpose-relative; Coverage≠Sufficiency; Unknown≠Unsatisfied; Sufficient≠Authorized;
Authorized≠Ready; RiskAcceptance does not remove KnowledgeGap; Readiness is policy-governed; Readiness is
temporally valid; Scalar completeness/readiness scores are derived, not fundamental.

**Step 13 verdict**: STRUCTURALLY RESOLVED — "KnowledgeOS does not attempt to know everything. It
determines whether the available knowledge is sufficient for a defined purpose under a defined policy."
Consolidates two parallel loops: the main control loop and a readiness loop
(`K_t→Sufficiency→Readiness→Decision`).

**Closes opening "Step 14 — Causality, Dependency, and Counterfactual Reasoning"**:
`Correlation≠Dependency≠Causation≠LogicalImplication` — moving KnowledgeOS toward "why did this happen?",
"what caused this?", "what will happen if we take this action?" — "the point where KnowledgeOS starts
moving from a knowledge repository into a genuine reasoning and decision system."
