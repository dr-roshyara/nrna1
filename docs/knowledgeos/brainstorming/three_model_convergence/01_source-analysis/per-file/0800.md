## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — "we now test one of the deepest architectural observations from Bhagavad Gita Chapter 4
against our KnowledgeOS model... can the past be reconstructed from the current state alone? The answer is
generally no."** Delivers a **worked numeric counterexample** (`S_0=100`; History A `100→80` vs. History B
`120→80`, both yielding identical `S_1=80`), formalizing `History→CurrentState` as **many-to-one**, boxed:
**"historical state is not identifiable from current state alone,"** importing the statistical
**identifiability** lens directly.

**§175.7-175.13 delivers the four-model test** promised at 0799's close: **Model A (state only)**: boxed
**`FAIL`** — overwriting `Knowledge.status` loses the original basis. **Model B (versioned)**: delivers
**`Versioning≠Provenance`** — a version tells us there was a newer version, not why. **Model C (event
history)**: explicitly resists "therefore everything must be event-sourced" — named one implementation
strategy among several. **Model D (snapshot+lineage)**: "particularly suitable for KnowledgeOS."

**§175.14-175.19 delivers the key requirement**, boxed: **"a consequential artifact must remain anchored
to the epistemic state against which it was created"** — stronger than "keep audit logs" ("audit logs are
technical. Anchoring is semantic"). Delivers three distinct required query types: `WhatIsTrueNow(x)`,
`WhyWasDecisionMade(d,t)=Explain(D,t)`, `WhatChanged(x,t_1,t_2)`.

**§175.20-175.22 delivers the direct translation into a formal rule**, boxed: **"CurrentState does not
imply HistoricalKnowledge"** — immediately bounded: not `StoreEverythingForever`, but policy-driven
`RetentionRequirement=f(Risk,Law,Governance,Traceability)`.

**§175.23-175.24 delivers the file's most quotable capstone**: **"this is where our Gita lens and
KnowledgeOS converge"** — boxed **`Memory≠Knowledge`**: a bare event record is memory; "the architecture
was approved because evidence X supported determination Y under rule Z" is structured knowledge/
provenance. "Memory can support knowledge reconstruction. It does not automatically constitute it."

**§175.25-175.26 delivers a directly self-referential AI-context-window discussion**: "an AI agent may
operate today with `Context_t`. Tomorrow it receives `Context_{t+1}`. The new context may not contain
everything that mattered yesterday" — boxed **`AIContext_{t+1}≠History`**, "the agent should not be
assumed to 'remember' merely because the platform has current knowledge." Distinguishes `AgentMemory` from
`OrganizationalKnowledge`, boxed: **"the agent consumes organizational memory; it does not become its
authoritative owner"** — a principle unusually precisely applicable to this very reconstruction's own
cross-compaction operating structure.

**§175.27-175.29 delivers a Bayesian-posterior analogy** and **the file's strongest formalization**,
boxed: **`Decision_t=f(K_t,Policy_t,Authority_t)`** — explicitly "one of the strongest formalizations we
have produced," unifying the arc's five prior separate Chapter-4-derived distinctions (0794-0797, 0799)
under one precise mathematical fact.

**§175.30-175.34 delivers `NewKnowledge≠RewrittenHistory`** (policy changes trigger explicit
`Reassessment`, never automatic historical invalidation), boxed **"history is a sequence of epistemic and
governance states, not merely a sequence of database updates,"** and the refinement that "the old state is
not one thing" — domain-relevant temporal anchors preventing overengineering, boxed: **"preserve the
information necessary to reconstruct the meaning of consequential transitions"** — not every byte.

**§175.35-175.37 delivers the experiment result**: a full four-model comparison table, explicitly
rejecting "event sourcing wins" in favor of **"semantic temporal provenance is required where historical
reconstruction matters."** **Delivers the central meta-statement**: "the Chapter 4 lens has generated an
architectural testable proposition... this is now more than a metaphor." Delivers the Step 175 verdict:
the revised temporally-anchored core model and the fundamental rule that `K_{t+1}` may supersede `K_t` but
must not silently rewrite `Decision_t`'s historical meaning.
