## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK: Step 5 resolved — "what is the mathematical object being aggregated?"** Opens with
the foundational separation: `Evidence≠Support≠Belief≠Probability≠Truth`. Establishes that evidence
objects (API responses, PDFs, database rows, screenshots, human observations, LLM outputs, ADRs) are
artifacts FROM WHICH propositions are extracted, not propositions themselves.

**Delivers the file's central cautionary finding**: a bare `c(e,P)=0.8` is dangerous — it could mean at
least eight different things (probability, support, reliability, LLM confidence, likelihood ratio, degree
of belief, plausibility, source quality) — "0.8 has no epistemic meaning until its semantics are
specified." **Distinguishes seven concrete semantic domains** previously blurred together: Reliability,
Relevance, Evidential support, Probability, Belief (Dempster-Shafer), Plausibility, Information/
information-gain. Formalizes an Evidence Contribution Domain `𝒬` (NOT necessarily `[0,1]` — could be an
ordered scale, vector, belief function, likelihood ratio, argument structure).

**Delivers the file's central architectural recommendation**: the kernel should NOT itself choose Bayesian
or Dempster-Shafer — `KnowledgeOS Kernel = Evidence Semantics+Provenance+Relations`; `Assessment Calculus =
Pluggable Policy`. **Delivers the file's second major finding**: the most fundamental representation is
RELATIONAL — `Rel(E,P)` (e.g. supports/derived-from/contradicts/stale-supports), not `Score(E,P)` — "this
relational structure is the epistemic foundation." Given `Rel(E,P)`, a policy computes
`Assessment_ρ(P|E)` — Bayesian `Pr(P|E)`, Dempster-Shafer `(Bel(P),Pl(P))`, or Argumentation
`(Arguments^+,Arguments^-)` — "not necessarily convertible into one universal number."

**Explicitly and definitively resolves** the corpus's recurring "should KnowledgeOS use one evidence
algebra?" question: **"No"** — the kernel defines what evidence means and how it relates; the reasoning
policy determines the mathematics. Delivers a common `AssessmentResult=(P,Support,Opposition,Uncertainty,
Basis,Context,Policy)` interface whose `Support` field's meaning differs by chosen calculus. Delivers
`EvidentialSupport≠Acceptance` (a policy decides the acceptance threshold over a computed support value)
and reconfirms `Acceptance≠Truth`. Reinforces "Probability requires a model" (real priors/likelihoods/
dependency assumptions KnowledgeOS cannot simply infer from a document's existence).

**Delivers a factored contribution function** `c_ρ(e,P,C)=g_ρ(Rel,Qual,Relia,Valid,Context,Dependency,
Polarity)` and a clean six-stage decomposition (Evidence semantics→Evidence relation→Proposition
relation→Assessment→Conclusion→Knowledge State) with six matching DDD concepts explicitly not to be merged.

**Delivers the file's most significant new insight**: KnowledgeOS likely needs **two complementary
mathematical families** — a Quantitative calculus (Bayesian/Statistical/Possibility, for genuine numerical
uncertainty) and a Qualitative/argumentative calculus (for conflicting interpretations, rules,
architectural principles, normative claims), worked through a concrete ADR-conflict example — crystallized
as a new principle: **"Not every epistemic problem should become a probability problem."**

**Delivers a refined Kernel/Policy split** paralleling 0545's. **Delivers the file's final and arguably
most consequential distinction**: **`InformationGain≠EvidenceStrength`** and
**`EvidenceValue≠EvidenceStrength`** — "a weak piece of evidence can sometimes have enormous information
value if it resolves a previously critical uncertainty" — reframing Lord's objective from raw evidence
strength to expected discrepancy reduction.

**Delivers the closing verdict**: "Step 5... Theoretical status: substantially resolved" — the fundamental
object is not a scalar; Evidence Contribution is a policy-interpreted semantic relation; probability,
belief, likelihood, information gain, and argument strength are genuinely different constructs. **Names
"Step 6 — Information Gain and Value of Evidence"**: formalizes `Value(e|K,I,P)` and
`e*=argmax_e ExpectedDiscrepancyReduction(e)`, giving Lord/Sārathi "a mathematically precise capability:
not merely 'find more evidence,' but identify the next evidence acquisition that provides the greatest
expected reduction in uncertainty or discrepancy under the current purpose and policy."

**Bridge candidate**: trace whether Step 6 is executed as its own dedicated file.
