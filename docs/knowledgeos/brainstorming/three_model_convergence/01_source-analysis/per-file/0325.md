## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — the corpus's most direct textual confirmation yet of its founding intuition.** Engages
Brachman & Levesque's *Knowledge Representation and Reasoning*. Central supporting quotation: "there may
be an infinite number of propositions believed, while only a finite number are ever represented; reasoning
bridges the gap" — matched directly to the corpus's founding infinite-Knowledge-Space intuition and
formalized as `R_t^A ⊂ K_t^A ⊂ Ω` (represented ⊂ believed ⊂ unbounded space). Reconfirms representation ≠
knowledge via the source's own "John loves Mary" symbol/proposition example. Introduces the **Knowledge-
level vs. Symbol-level** distinction, mapped directly onto the Kernel-contract/implementation boundary; the
**Knowledge Representation Hypothesis** (representations both describe epistemic content and causally
participate in system behaviour, illustrated by two near-identical Prolog programs); a second, differently
formalized **Explicitness Principle** (`E_t ⊆ I_t`, explicit vs. implicit belief within a KB/entailment
framework, distinct from 0321's Brandom-derived version); and the foundational **Expressiveness ↔
Tractability** tradeoff (FOL entailment is undecidable in general). Produces three new named Kernel
principles — **Representation-Reasoning Separation**, **Finite Representation/Unbounded Knowledge
Principle** (explicitly called the strongest direct support found anywhere for the corpus's original
infinite-space intuition), and **Expressiveness-Tractability Principle** — plus a five-part
`Capacity_A = (Representation, Reasoning, Observation, Memory, Computation)` tuple and an explicit
three-way separation of Representation/Reasoning/Knowledge-projection capacity as a promising research
direction. Reconfirms (independently of Gärdenfors and Gelfond & Kahl) that knowledge cannot always be
monotonic, and (independently of Gelfond & Kahl) the multidimensional-measurement-vector approach over a
single score, via the source's uncertainty-theory survey. Closes with an unusually disciplined **explicit
self-audit**: names exactly what the book does and does *not* establish (literal infinity, universal
representation, a single Kernel logic remain the corpus's own unproven architectural hypotheses, not
textually supported claims).
