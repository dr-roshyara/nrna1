## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK EXTERNAL-LENS ENGAGEMENT (Stanley Cavell, ordinary-language philosophy). First file
processed from the corpus's separate `kernel/` directory.** Applies Cavell's philosophy of acknowledgment
(Austin/Wittgenstein-influenced) against the Kernel's assumptions, extending the corpus's external-
adversarial-test tradition (Pritchard/0397, Berger & Luckmann/0398, Quine/0408, Beyond Measure/0510) to a
new domain.

**Central finding**: Knowledge≠Acknowledgment — acknowledgment (response to a claim, given or withheld) is
not reducible to knowledge (true/false relation to propositions); "the failure is not one of truth, but of
relationship." Other findings: meaning depends on position in the language game, not intentions/definitions
("storing signs without storing their sense"); fraudulence (genuineness uncertainty) is a feature of the
human condition, not a bug; the "problem of other minds" is managed via acknowledgment, not solved by proof;
saying≠meaning, so propositions cannot be self-contained meaning units.

**Delivers ten observation/architectural-question pairs** (Part 1): knowledge-vs-acknowledgment; the
categorial "must" of an utterance (e.g. "I know" necessarily implies confidence/standing/responsibility —
not optional pragmatics); the ordinary as plain-but-unseen (failure of acknowledgment, not information);
fraudulence as endemic; the self concealed in assertion; the end of justification (a necessary stopping
point, not a gap); taste as partiality (judgment is always personal); the first-person-plural "we" as an
invitation to self-test; tragedy as failure of acknowledgment (Lear fails from inability to bear being seen,
not lack of knowledge); audience implication (misperceiving = refusing to see).

**Delivers five surprising observations with architectural implications** (Part 3): Knowing≠Acknowledging
suggests a separate **Claims** category — "the standing of one person upon another... not reducible to
propositions"; the categorial "must" suggests assertions have a performative dimension beyond propositional
attitude; the ordinary-not-obvious point means more information won't fix avoidance-based failures;
fraudulence-as-feature suggests modeling **Trust and Betrayal** as fundamental categories, not verification
failures; audience-implication suggests modeling the knower's own implication in what they know.

**Delivers Part 5's central disciplinary safeguard**: an explicit **five-item "should NOT influence
architecture without further evidence" restraint list** (Shakespeare readings; fraudulence-as-essential;
self-concealed-in-assertion needing psychological commitments; taste-as-partiality; audience-implicated-in-
tragedy) — a genuine, proactive safeguard functioning identically in spirit to the corpus's Gita-engagement
discipline (0494, 0500-0505) but newly applied to a Western philosophical source. Frames Cavell as an
"anti-reasoner" (Part 6): protecting against conflating knowing/acknowledging, stripping words of their
"must," demanding infinite justification, refusing to see the ordinary, and assuming more information is
always the solution — "Cavell is not a theorist of knowledge — he is a diagnostician of the refusal to
acknowledge." Delivers ten DDD-blind-spot questions (Part 7) contrasting standard DDD assumptions against
Cavell-derived challenges. Closes with a consolidated ten-question "Final Statement" (Part 8), explicitly
framed as "not questions about missing capabilities" but "questions about the assumptions the Kernel makes
about the nature of knowledge, meaning, and human relationship."

**Bridge candidate**: the proposed Claims/Positions/ultimate-grounds candidate Kernel extensions should be
compared against 0377/0380/0504/0510's four prior independently-derived Kernel-content hypotheses.
