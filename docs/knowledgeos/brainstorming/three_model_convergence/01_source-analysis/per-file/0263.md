toward architectural formalization. Separates logical state `L(P)∈{T,F,B,N}` from confidence `C(P)`,
evidence quantity `E(P)`, and evidence quality `Q(P)` as four independent, non-collapsible dimensions —
worked example shows "strong conflicting evidence" and "insufficient evidence" both yielding 50%
confidence while representing radically different epistemic situations. Formalizes Zero as
`Z=Invariant(O1,...,On)` ("Zero is not nothing; it is the minimum invariant structure that survives changes
in representation"), and distinguishes mathematical/semantic/constitutional invariants — explicitly warning
against conflating them. Explicitly declines to adopt the source's satya/asatya/mithyā-as-three-valued-logic
and Schrödinger's-cat analogies as facts. Distinguishes multi-valued logic from fuzzy logic as combinable,
independent dimensions (`P=(L,μ)`). Proposes a `KnowledgeClaim` structure (adding `transformations[]` and
`invariants[]` to the prior object), six ML-operational roles (evidence weighting, contradiction detection,
latent-state estimation, model comparison, change-point detection, knowledge decay), and formalizes the
inverse-statistical idea as Forward `E→K` vs. Inverse `K→E*` (minimum evidence to justify a target state),
plus an epistemic distance `D(K_current,K_required)` giving an actionable evidence-gap list rather than a
bare "not enough knowledge." Refines the corpus's founding intuition through three explicit versions,
landing on "knowledge is a dynamically evolving network... with an explicit epistemic state," and states
Knowledge Quality ≠ Truth Probability — knowledge quality is multidimensional. Closes with the sub-arc's
first explicit "freeze" of a 13-lens research stack (still not reconciled with the differently-composed
ten-lens lists at 0259/0260/0262) and reframes the central research direction toward constructing a
measurable, 8-axis Knowledge State Space rather than reading further philosophical sources — adding a
tenth and eleventh variant to the open Knowledge-vector reconciliation problem.
