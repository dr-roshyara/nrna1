## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK: "The Elements of Statistical Learning" fused with Zero and Leonardo into a
six-level assurance hierarchy.** Central discovery: "Zero tells us when learning should not start.
Leonardo tells us whether the learning problem has been sufficiently contextualized. Statistical Learning
tells us how to learn once those conditions are satisfied" — Zero→Leonardo→Statistical Learning→
Assessment→Knowledge.

The book's X→Y/f̂(X)≈f(X) formulation, with real systems containing unmeasured variables and measurement
error, supports Reality→Observations→Knowledge state→Model→Prediction — "the model is not the knowledge
identity." **Sharpens Zero beyond basis-checking**: statistical machinery can still produce a result on
insufficient data, but "Zero says that producing a result is not necessarily legitimate" — a layer ABOVE
the statistical learner. **Gives Leonardo a precise statistical interpretation**: "have we identified the
relevant data-generating conditions?" **Reframes the curse of dimensionality as a Leonardo failure**:
Context completeness≠information volume — "an important correction to naïve RAG architectures."

**Introduces Loss**: AI architecture should ask "which action minimizes expected loss?" not "which answer
is most probable?" — crystallizing **Probability≠Decision**, `Decision=argmin_a E[L(a,Y)|X]`. Chains
`ZERO→LEONARDO→LOSS→LEARNING`. Reframes bias-variance as an architectural principle governing model
routing: `ModelSelection=f(Bias,Variance,Loss,Context,Cost)`. Delivers **`ModelError≠DecisionError`** (from
the book's own demonstration that classification error can stay zero despite substantial squared bias),
motivating **Prediction Quality≠Decision Quality≠Knowledge Quality**.

**Delivers the file's most consequential lesson via cross-validation leakage**: the book's own
demonstration of incorrect CV (feature selection on the full dataset before validation: CV error≈3% vs true
test error=50%) is "essentially knowledge contamination," directly analogous to an AI agent that
hypothesis-confirms via targeted search — crystallized as **Verification evidence ⊄ Uncontrolled inference
evidence** when independence is required. **Delivers the corpus's first six-level formal assurance
hierarchy**: Basis (Zero) / Context (Leonardo) / Model (Statistical learning) / Generalization
(Cross-validation) / Stability (Bootstrap/ensembles) / Governance. **Introduces a formal Stability Test**:
`Stability(Q,E)=P(Decision(Q,E')=Decision(Q,E))` over admissible evidence perturbations. Reconfirms
**Agreement≠Truth** via the Gödel lens applied to ensembles.

**Delivers a genuinely important disambiguation**: "Zero" has **two completely unrelated meanings** —
Zero-prerequisite (NO_BASIS, "don't infer") and Zero-observed-error (ERROR=0, a model that happened to
perform perfectly on a sample) — grounded in the book's own high-dimensional zero-validation-error warning
(`ObservedError=0` may coexist with `TrueError≫0`) — "an excellent reason to keep epistemic states separate
from statistical metrics." Extends Leonardo to feature selection itself (never an invisible preprocessing
step).

**Culminates in a full adaptive-inference optimization equation**: `M*=argmin_M[K(M)+V(M)+E[L(M)]]` subject
to `B=1`, `C≥C_min`, `Assurance(M)≥A_min` — "a serious architecture specification rather than merely an
intuition." Formally names two new principles: the **Decision-Loss Adequacy Principle** (inference method
selection must be governed by decision loss, validated context, evidence, and required assurance — never
confidence/capability/cost alone) and the **Independent Validation Principle** (evidence used to construct
an inference shall not be silently reused as independent validation for it). Closes ranking the book "very
high" — not for its ML techniques, but for "the discipline underneath the techniques," concluding its real
contribution is a candidate **Inference Theory layer**.

**Bridge candidate**: the "Leonardo" lens is treated here as settled prior corpus vocabulary — its
originating file should be located and cross-referenced.
