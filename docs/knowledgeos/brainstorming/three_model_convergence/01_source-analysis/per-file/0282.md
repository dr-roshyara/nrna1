## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — landmark file.** Fagin/Halpern/Moses/Vardi's *Reasoning About Knowledge* gives the corpus its
**first formal, mathematically-defined Knowledge Space model** (a Kripke structure), directly unifying
McGinn/Floridi/Dretske into a genuinely formalizable core. Reframes knowledge state as "the set of states
not yet ruled out," gives the first concrete quantification of epistemic uncertainty
(`|Ω_i(s)|`, entropy over possible worlds), formalizes the common/distributed-knowledge hierarchy (directly
relevant to governance — "everyone knows the rule" is strictly weaker than "the rule is common knowledge,"
illustrated via the muddy-children example: a public announcement changes knowledge without changing the
world). Radically broadens "agent" beyond persons (defined by epistemic participation), and distinguishes
semantic/implicit knowledge from operational/computable/algorithmic knowledge — the corpus's most direct
connection yet to how AI agents actually behave, enumerating eight distinct meanings of "I don't know."
**Most architecturally significant: proposes Pramāṇa as one of several pluggable "Knowledge Regimes"** the
Kernel could support alongside possible-world semantics, evidence-based epistemology, statistical
inference, and constitutional/engineering knowledge — a genuinely novel, non-merging reconciliation
architecture for the corpus's long-tracked Evidence-Integrity↔Pramāṇa correspondence. Produces the corpus's
first formal Knowledge Space tuple `K=(S,P,π,A,~,T,E,C)` (an eighth, explicitly provisional Kernel-structure
candidate), then **explicitly pauses** the philosophical reading program to attempt the first real
four-book synthesis (McGinn × Floridi × Dretske × Fagin) next — directly enacting 0279's
cross-book-intersection discipline.
