## Tier 2
**NOT TRIGGERED.** A concrete methodological experiment directly addressing 0392's Q2 ("is every fact a
dimension?") via a worked six-statement Nexus document. Treating every sentence as a dimension reveals they
are **not independent** — they form a dependency/relationship structure (two dimensions jointly implying a
third "compliance concern" dimension), extending 0396's relational-state principle to document statements.
Identifies the "Nexus is secure" problem: one statement can implicitly aggregate dozens of sub-dimensions,
proposing a statement may be a **claim about** dimensions rather than an atomic dimension itself — an
explicitly unresolved directionality question. Resolves a deeper two-level distinction (dimension=Version
vs. the whole sentence) in favor of `Claim=(Subject,Dimension,Value,Context,Time)`. Introduces an
**eight-role statement taxonomy** (Observation/Fact-claim/Interpretation/Relationship/Determination/Rule/
Decision/Reference-assumption), warning that conflating genuinely different statement roles under
"dimension" loses essential distinctions.

Refines the dimension/statement definition precisely: a dimension is a stable aspect, a statement is a
time-indexed assertion about it — `Dimension=Version` (constant), `Statement_t=(Version,2.69,t)` (time-
varying) — "cleaner than making each sentence a permanent dimension." Sharpens the infinite-dimension
hypothesis: the unbounded quantity is possible **aspects** of characterization, not the number of sentences
— "the document is evidence about the state, not the state itself." Closes proposing a concrete next
experiment: build a three-layer Sentence→Claim→Dimension/Value analysis of one real document, empirically
discovering each sentence's actual role — "may tell us more about what a 'smallest unit of knowledge'
actually is than another abstract definition."
