## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK: prioritization retargeted from deficiencies to Resolution Opportunities/Actions;
weighted sum demoted to one policy implementation.** Part A proposes prioritizing "deficiencies" via a
scalar π(d,S_t,P_t,C_t)∈[0,1], seven factors combined by weighted sum, five named strategies, a simple
Sort(Δ_t,π,descending), and an Arjuna example with asserted rankings.

Part B (review), explicitly building on 0488's Discrepancy≠Deficiency: **rejects "deficiencies" as the
priority target**, generalizing to {Gap,Conflict,Finding,Question,Investigation,Action,...}. **Corrects
priority from an intrinsic property to a context-relative relation** Priority(d|S_t,P_t,C_t,R_t). **Fully
separates Severity≠Urgency≠Priority** (a critical/low-urgency gap → medium priority; a minor/high-urgency
issue → high priority) — priority is "a decision relation, not another intrinsic property." **Demotes the
weighted-sum formula from THE definition of priority to one policy implementation**: π=F_policy(factors) is
fundamental, since weighted addition assumes numeric representability, commensurable scales, and acceptable
compensability that generally don't hold (a critical governance violation may have to dominate regardless of
feasibility). **Corrects Cost's sign** (high cost should reduce, not increase, priority) and **splits
Dependencies into DependencyBlock vs. DependencyLeverage** as distinct concepts. **Splits Impact into
Consequence/Benefit/Leverage**. **Demotes Feasibility-First** from a general principle to one situational
policy strategy. **Models dependencies as a graph** G_D=(V,E), converting prioritization into a
**constrained ordering problem** Order(Δ,G_D,π,Γ) — a hard prerequisite overrides raw priority score even
when π(d_1)<π(d_2).

**The file's largest DDD correction**: prioritization must produce an **action plan**, not an ordered list
of findings — "a finding is not necessarily actionable" (evidence-insufficiency admits acquire-evidence,
ask-clarification, observe, consult, defer, or accept-uncertainty as distinct candidate actions) — introduces
**Finding≠Action** and the **Resolution Opportunity** object O=(Target,Action,ExpectedEffect,Cost,
Prerequisites,Context) as the proper prioritization target. Corrects Sārathi from "select the highest-priority
deficiency" to "selects or recommends the next action based on the prioritized resolution opportunities and
the current purpose." **Removes priority's assumed [0,1] range** — may be an ordered set under a
policy-defined ordering ≻, numeric scoring merely one optional implementation. Reworks the Arjuna example:
original rankings reframed as "illustrative policy outcome, not a logical consequence of the model," with a
table of candidate actions Sārathi must actually evaluate.

Closes with a revised mathematical model (Findings F_t=Diagnose(Δ_t,...); Resolution opportunities
O_t=GenerateActions(F_t,...); π:O_t×S_t×P_t×C_t→𝒫; Dependency graph G_t=(O_t,E_t); Constrained ordering
Plan_t=Order(...); Sārathi a_t=Sārathi(S_t,Plan_t,P_t,C_t)), ten invariants (notably "Sārathi chooses the
next action, not merely the highest-scoring deficiency"), a numeric verdict (Math 6/10→~9/10 post-correction),
and states State→Discrepancy→Finding→Resolution Opportunity→Priority→Action→New State as the foundation for
**Question 23: how does KnowledgeOS know when to stop investigating and present a decision-ready state?**
