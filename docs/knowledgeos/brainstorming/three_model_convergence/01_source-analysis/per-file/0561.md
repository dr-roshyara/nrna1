## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED — LANDMARK: nine typed rule kinds; explicit paraconsistency and non-monotonicity adopted.**
Executes Step 18, opening: "when KnowledgeOS derives something, what exactly gives it the right to derive
it?" Delivers the governing principle **`Rule≠Inference≠Truth≠Policy`**: a single arrow `A⇒B` could mean
logical implication, mathematical theorem, business rule, architectural constraint, causal claim,
statistical relationship, heuristic, or LLM hypothesis — "fundamentally different." Formalizes
`R=(RuleID,Kind,Premises,Conclusion,Semantics,Context,Authority,Validity,Version,Provenance)`, with `Kind`
the most important field.

**Delivers the file's central formal deliverable — nine named rule kinds** `{Logical,Mathematical,
Semantic,Normative,Causal,Statistical,Constraint,Heuristic,Procedural}`, each separately worked: Normative
(`NormativeRule≠DescriptiveRule`); Causal (`CausalRule≠LogicalRule`, reusing 0556); Statistical
(`P(Failure|A)=0.8` does NOT yield `A⇒Failure` — **`Probability≠Logical Implication`**, "one of the most
important safeguards against bad AI reasoning"); Heuristic (produces candidates, not guaranteed
conclusions); Procedural (`Procedure≠Inference`).

Formalizes inference as distinct from rule (`I:(K,R)→C`, "Rule is the specification; Inference is the
application"), an auditable inference record `J=(InferenceID,Premises,Rule,Conclusion,Context,Model,
Timestamp,Provenance)`, and `DerivedKnowledge≠DirectObservation` with derived support depending on
`Support(Premises)+Trustworthiness(ApplicableRule)`. Delivers Rule applicability
(`Applicable(R,K,Ctx,t)`), generalized context-and-time-dependent inference
(`Inference=f(K,R,Context,Time,Policy)`, connecting to 0560's semantic model), Rule precedence/conflict
(`RuleConflict(R_1,R_2,Ctx,t)`, same lifecycle as the established conflict model), and **`Priority≠Truth`**
— higher rule priority under governance is not "more true." Normative conflict reframed as policy conflict
requiring contextual resolution, not logical contradiction.

Formalizes three-valued rule evaluation `Eval(P,K)∈{True,False,Unknown}` with explicit truth-table
semantics (`T∧U=U`, `F∧U=F`, etc.) and **`Unknown premise does not become True`** — essential for
open-world reasoning.

**Delivers the file's second major architectural principle — `KnowledgeOS should not mandate one universal
logic`**: three-valued logic is insufficient everywhere; some domains need four-valued, paraconsistent,
probabilistic, temporal, modal, fuzzy, or default logics — a reasoning framework hosting multiple inference
semantics is needed. **This is a direct, explicit statement of the corpus's recurring "no universal
formula" pattern** (previously confirmed at 0545/0546, 0554, 0556; arguably 0560), this time applied
without qualification to logic/reasoning itself.

**Delivers the file's central theoretical commitment — explicit adoption of paraconsistent reasoning**:
worked via the recurring `Nexus=3.69`/`3.72` example, formally rejecting the classical principle of
explosion (`A,¬A⊢B`, "which would be disastrous in an enterprise knowledge system") — **`Contradiction is a
state to manage, not necessarily a system failure`**; contradictory evidence is itself knowledge. Delivers
`Unknown≠Conflict`.

Formalizes Inference closure `K*=Cl_R(K)` for finite monotonic rule sets, then **delivers the file's third
major theoretical commitment — KnowledgeOS is explicitly declared fundamentally non-monotonic**: defeasible
conclusions `K⊢_def P` that later knowledge can invalidate (`K'⊬_def P`), worked via a HealthyHost/
MaintenanceWindow example, connected to the corpus's temporal/revision machinery — "a major theoretical
property."

Delivers Rule provenance (a worked ProductionChangeRequiresApproval rule with source/authority/effective
date/version) and **`LLMGeneratedRule≠AuthorizedRule`** — an LLM-proposed rule is only a `CandidateRule`.
Delivers a full Rule lifecycle (Candidate→Reviewed→Validated→Approved→Active→Superseded→Retired, paralleling
established assertion/decision lifecycles), Rule versioning, and Reproducible inference
(`Premises+RuleVersion+SemanticModelVersion+PolicyVersion+Context+TemporalScope`), extending 0558's/0560's
reproducibility formalizations.

**Delivers the file's central architectural deliverable — the Reasoning Engine architecture**: NOT one
giant "AI Reasoner," but `ReasoningEngine=InferenceOrchestrator+SpecializedReasoners` (Logical/Semantic/
Temporal/Constraint/Causal/Statistical/Decision reasoners), with an explicit compiler analogy
(`NaturalLanguage→SemanticRepresentation→FormalRepresentation→Reasoner→DerivedKnowledge`) — "much safer
than asking an LLM to perform the entire reasoning chain in one opaque step."

Formalizes Proof objects `π=(P_1,...,P_n,R,C)` with `Verify(π)=True/False`, and delivers **`Proof≠
Explanation`** — the LLM generates a human-readable explanation FROM the formal proof object, "an excellent
architectural boundary" (worked ProductionChange(Nexus)⇒RequiresApproval(Nexus) example). Delivers a
consolidated reasoning pipeline (Input→Extraction→...→Knowledge State) and a precise **Zero/Lord/Sārathi
reasoning-regime differentiation**: Zero uses semantic/constraint/temporal/epistemic rules; Lord uses
causal models, heuristics, statistical models, domain rules; Sārathi uses decision rules, policies, utility
models, constraints, authority — "the three lenses are not three identical AI agents."

Delivers a computability reassessment (finite rule systems are computable; some reasoning is
computationally expensive, undecidable, probabilistic, or incomplete) — `KnowledgeOS must not promise
Universal reasoning`. **Delivers the file's most important single property**: the kernel must know when it
cannot conclude — `NotDerivable≠False`, `Derivable≠TrueInReality`. Formalizes **four reasoning result
states** `{Derived,Refuted,Undetermined,Inapplicable}` — "substantially better than true/false for an
open-world enterprise system" — and a formal reasoning result object `J=(Conclusion,Status,Rule,Premises,
Proof,Context,TemporalScope,ModelVersion,Confidence/Support,Provenance)`.

**Delivers the file's central formal deliverable — fourteen canonical invariants (R1-R14)**: Rule≠
Inference; Inference≠Truth; different rule kinds require different semantics; LLMGeneratedRule≠
GovernedRule; DerivedKnowledge≠DirectObservation; every consequential inference is traceable; Unknown≠
False; Undetermined≠Refuted; SemanticInference≠CausalInference; StatisticalAssociation≠
LogicalImplication; contradiction does not imply arbitrary conclusions; KnowledgeOS supports non-monotonic
revision; RuleVersion is part of derivation provenance; a conclusion is valid only relative to its declared
reasoning semantics.

**Step 18 verdict**: STRUCTURALLY RESOLVED, with the explicit architectural decision **"KnowledgeOS should
not be one universal logic engine"** — a reasoning orchestration architecture invoking different formal
systems by question type, "mathematically safer and architecturally cleaner." Delivers a consolidated
fifteen-stage pipeline cross-cut by `Time+Identity+Provenance+Context+Governance`.

Closes by identifying that the corpus has treated `K_t` as one coherent knowledge state, but real
KnowledgeOS ingests contradictory heterogeneous sources of differing authority/reliability/scope, posing
how one governed Knowledge State is built "without simply voting, averaging, or blindly trusting a
source." **Opens "Step 19 — Authority, Trust, Evidence Weighting, Source Reliability, and Knowledge
Commitment,"** stated to be "the next major piece needed before we can claim that the KnowledgeOS
mathematical model is truly closed."

**Bridge candidates**: trace whether Step 19 is executed as its own file and whether it does close the
mathematical model as predicted; whether the explicit "no universal logic" statement here counts as a
clean additional confirmed instance of the recurring "no universal formula" pattern in the eventual
canonical-theory reconciliation pass.
