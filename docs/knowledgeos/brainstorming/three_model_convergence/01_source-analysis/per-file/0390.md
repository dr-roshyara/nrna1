**TRIGGERED — direct formalization of 0389's landmark discovery**, crystallizing "that is the key
distinction": the uncertainty is not primarily "what is the value of this known dimension?" but "what
dimensions are we **missing**?" Works a concrete Nexus example (known: version/CPU/memory/repositories/
network; candidate undiscovered: identity integration/backup dependency/certificate dependency/licensing/
external services/disaster recovery/operational dependency). Formalizes the **two extraction problems**
precisely for the first time: the **dimension-discovery problem** (`D_t→D̂_t`, which dimensions
exist/are relevant) and the **state-extraction problem** (`I_t(D̂_t)→K_t`, what do we know about discovered
dimensions).

accuracy 99%, Reasoning confidence 90% — three distinct, non-collapsible properties). Proposes
`P(D_t,I_t|O_≤t)` as closer to the phenomenon than `P(F|O)`, explicitly flagged as an untested research
hypothesis. Draws the central KnowledgeOS consequence: the system must preserve not only accumulated facts
but its own believed-relevant-dimension-set and confidence in that set's completeness — cautionary contrast
between an AI falsely claiming "I know everything about Nexus" (having modeled only five dimensions) and an
honest "strong evidence on these dimensions, unresolved uncertainty about others." Crystallizes: "Uncertainty
begins not only when we don't know the value of something. It begins earlier — when we don't know what
things or dimensions are relevant to know about," yielding Knowledge-completeness = dimension-coverage +
information-completeness-within-those-dimensions.

Closes with a genuinely new, unresolved methodological challenge: is "**dimension**" the right primitive at
all, or is the underlying phenomenon more fundamentally a **space of possible questions/propositions** about
the ideal state? — explicitly flagged as "decisive for the eventual mathematical model," reconnecting this
research thread back to 0366's earlier `Q_t^D` relevant-question-space formulation, without resolving which
framing is more fundamental.
