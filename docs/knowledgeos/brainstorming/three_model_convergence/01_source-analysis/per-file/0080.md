## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED.**

**Nine convergent dimensions.** Ranked by evidence strength: strong — Identity, Provenance,
Temporality, Authority, Decision-and-Rationale, Lineage, Evidence, Governed-Lifecycle,
Integrity-Assurance; weak — knowledge graphs, AI/RAG, ontologies, workflow engines, analytics,
visualization, event sourcing. Central claim: trustworthy organizational knowledge is fundamentally
about "preserving epistemic accountability over time, not retrieval performance."

**Authority ≠ confidence.** Externally sourced: authority is organizational (who may treat a
proposition as settled), confidence is epistemic (how well-supported it is) — "a proposition may be
well-supported but unauthorized, or authorized but uncertain." A candidate pipeline is offered:
`Observation → Evidence → Claim → Inference → Belief → {confidence, authority} → Decision`, explicitly
marked "still a hypothesis, not our kernel design." This sharpens, with named external grounding,
the authority-vs-provenance distinction this corpus has tracked internally since 0014/0018.

**Provenance is not merely metadata.** Provenance functions simultaneously as governance, trust, and
domain information — flagged as a strong candidate for "the kernel-boundary investigation," connecting
directly to the EKS durability work already read at 0061/0066.

**Temporal knowledge.** Distinguishes valid time / transaction time / observation time / decision time
/ effective time; the central organizing question is "what was considered authoritative at time T?"
A future kernel state may need `knowledge_state + validity + authority + lineage`, not merely a
current-value snapshot — explicitly deferred: "we should not design that yet."

**Two negative results.** (1) Knowledge System ≠ Knowledge Graph: weak evidence that graphs are the
kernel itself, strong evidence they function as an integration/query/semantic layer — a direct
corrective to the 0057-family Knowledge-Graph-Architecture proposals. (2) Event history is valuable;
event sourcing as an implementation strategy is not universally necessary.

**The OS/Linux analogy, correctly contained.** Validated as inspiration (stable primitives, minimal
trusted core, mechanism-vs-policy separation, composability) but explicitly *not* a literal
specification — knowledge systems lack OS-level deterministic execution and universal semantics. This
sharpens 0071's Linux-kernel analogy from illustrative comparison to an explicit, reasoned boundary.

**Direct answer to 0079's complex-number question.** "Possibly as a formal modeling device, but not as
the kernel primitive itself unless research and the EKS/PKS/AIP comparison demonstrate an actual
need." The external evidence points toward a *multi-dimensional epistemic state* (identity, provenance,
validity, authority, evidence, confidence, lineage, decision) rather than any particular mathematical
representation — complex numbers are explicitly kept as a research hypothesis, not adopted into
architecture. This is the corpus's first direct cross-reference resolving a prior file's open question
about mathematical representation.

**Closing proposal.** Recommends stopping broad "what is KnowledgeOS?" commissioning and instead
building a 10-row comparison matrix (Identity, Provenance, Temporal validity, Authority, Evidence,
Decision, Lineage, Lifecycle, Conflict, Assurance) × {External evidence, EKS, PKS, AIP, Cross-cutting?,
Kernel candidate?} — cells left unfilled by this document, framed explicitly as future work whose
purpose is to let EKS/PKS/AIP evidence *eliminate* kernel candidates, not merely confirm the literature.

**Bridge candidates recorded (not confirmed):** the authority/confidence pipeline as a possible future
bridge to C2's Buddhi/purification language (no supporting C2 evidence yet); the
`knowledge_state+validity+authority+lineage` shape as a possible M-model bridge target (no
mathematical formalization exists yet to confirm it beyond a naming proposal).
