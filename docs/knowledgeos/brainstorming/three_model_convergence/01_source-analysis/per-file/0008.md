## What Is This Document About?
The experiment tests one claim: *can the Minimum Viable Kernel bootstrap a real second product?*
The verdict is **FAIL**, and the report's central achievement is being precise about *what* failed.
A scope correction inserted before the verdict states it plainly: the FAIL invalidated the
**extraction boundary** (a named 15-file set), **not the platform** — the engineering execution
protocol transferred with zero translation, and the tactical DDD principles rejected five candidates
in a foreign domain.

The method is unusually careful about **contamination**, and this is the report's methodological
core. The author states they *could not be the fresh session* — having read the corpus, any
bootstrap they attempted would be contaminated by knowledge they *"cannot unsee."* An isolated
session with no prior context was given read access to exactly 15 paths (244 KB), with the
instruction to *record rather than follow* any need for material outside that boundary. Handing over
**paths rather than a bundle** was chosen for two reasons: a standing rule forbids materialization,
and reference-passing makes *reachability itself measurable*. Residual contamination is disclosed
rather than denied: the isolated session still inherited a project-level instruction file, was told
to treat it as out-of-boundary, and **caught and refused two pulls from it while disclosing a third**.

What the kit *did* produce is recorded generously: a capability with a protected business rule, one
bounded context with three plausible alternatives **deliberately rejected as role separations rather
than boundaries**, seven ubiquitous-language terms with two homonyms guarded *before* the language
was written, three invariants, a decision record satisfying eight integrity properties with no
template available, a RED test boundary, and five rejected modelling candidates. The most telling
single result: the tactical principles **demoted "two staff may adjust the same item at once" from
invariant to mechanism** — a governance kit doing discriminating work in a domain it was never
written for.

The out-of-boundary register records **14 reaches, 3 of them contamination**. Three are structural:
no strategic-DDD method was reachable; no execution gate permitted a solo bootstrap to *lawfully
begin* (the protocol forbids one party holding both engineer and reviewer roles, forbids skipping
stages, and forbids implementing before approval, while the light gate is registered as the source
project's property); and no genesis path existed, because the only sanctioned route to an
architecture requires operational evidence that does not exist on day one and explicitly forbids
`idea → ADR → implementation`. A fourth is a **collision between two closed verdict vocabularies**
that differ in membership with nothing scoping them.

The single most important line in the report is **reach #13**: the bootstrap *sequence itself* came
from the experiment brief, not from the kit. The author discloses this on the explicit ground that
it *"materially flatters the result"* — **the true state is worse than FAIL suggests, not better.**

Three findings are then restated with honest bounds. F-1 (the protocol cannot be satisfied at
bootstrap) is **team-size dependent** and would be WARN, not FAIL, for an organisation with a named
architect. F-2 (no genesis path) and F-3 hold at any team size. F-3 is then **refuted in place on
the same day**: a strategic-DDD method *does* exist — the blocker is not absence but product
coupling. And a second refutation lands against the report's own prior reasoning: three methodology
documents comprising **73% of the kit by volume** are mostly non-portable case law, which refutes
part of an earlier boundary claim and yields the sharp lesson **domain-free ≠ portable** — a
document can contain no domain vocabulary and still be almost entirely one project's case history.
