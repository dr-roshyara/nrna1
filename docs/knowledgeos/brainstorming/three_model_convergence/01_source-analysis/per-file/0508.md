Resolves a prior objection about Knowledge elements being "intrinsically overlapping" with a sharper
reframing: "the underlying phenomenon is complex, and our observations do not provide a clean separation of
the elements that constitute it." Formalizes: 𝒦 contains distinguishable elements k_1,k_2,...; a complex
phenomenon O_t is generated by multiple elements and their relationships; the inverse O_≤t→{k_1,...,k_n} is
difficult since the map may not be one-to-one. **Frames this as a genuine inverse problem**: X--F-->Y, with
F(X_1)=F(X_2) possible for distinct X_1,X_2 — "that is exactly where uncertainty enters."

**Delivers the central rigor upgrade**: **`K≠P(K|O)`** — Knowledge itself is formally distinct from the
probabilistic inference about which elements constitute an observed phenomenon; "probability describes our
epistemic uncertainty about the extraction," "much more rigorous than saying 'knowledge is probabilistic.'"
Grounds complexity as the actual source of difficulty via a worked interacting-elements example (A↔B, B→C,
A,C→D combining into one observed phenomenon whose decomposition isn't directly observable) — crystallizing
Observation≠underlying structure and Extraction=inference in the general case. **Introduces a clean
three-way split**: Knowledge element (k_i), Phenomenon (Φ, complex configuration of elements+relations),
Observation (O, what can actually be recorded) — `{k_i,R_ij}→Φ→O`, extraction attempting the reverse.

**Reformulates "we try our best but never reach the best" with precision**: `K̂_t`=best available
reconstruction from O_≤t, while `K̂_t≠K_t` may persist even as evidence accumulates; "better" requires an
explicitly defined optimization criterion (itself an open research problem). **Applies the Gaussian/
least-squares analogy carefully**: the real principle is "given incomplete/noisy observations and a model,
we construct an approximation according to an optimization criterion" (worked `x̂=argmin‖Ax-b‖²`), never
guaranteed `x̂=x_true` — "Best reconstruction under regime R ≠ complete underlying Knowledge." **Precisely
scopes measure/probability theory**: Knowledge Space→Complex Phenomenon→Observations→Uncertainty→
Extraction, with probability belonging specifically to the Observations→probabilistic-reconstruction step,
while K_t itself "remains conceptually prior to the probabilistic extraction."

**Poses a new, explicitly unresolved research question**: is the underlying Knowledge Space observable in
principle, or fundamentally latent? If observable in principle, O→K might converge with enough evidence; if
fundamentally latent, O→P(K|O) may always remain inference — "we should not assume the answer." Closes
consolidating everything into an explicit **seven-part research hypothesis** — "one of the strongest
formulations we have developed" — naming the needed combined programme: **Inverse problems + latent-state
estimation + epistemology + Knowledge Space Theory**, aimed at answering "what does it mathematically mean
to extract a Knowledge State from a complex observed phenomenon?"

**Bridge candidate**: this file's Knowledge-element/Phenomenon/Observation split should be reconciled with
0507's independently-developed, computability-focused Observation definition.
