## Tier 2
**TRIGGERED.** Opens Step 25L directly executing 0589's closing commitment: what happens when knowledge is
produced independently in multiple places and later brought together — affecting evidence independence,
provenance, conflict, merge, and Knowledge Atma.

Delivers `Merge(K_A,K_B,K_C)` as fundamental, rejecting a naive "A+B=combined" model. Delivers mandatory
merge-provenance preservation and **reconfirms the KnowledgeMeaningIdentity/RecordIdentity split from
0587 in a distributed setting**: independent systems can produce `KAID_A=KAID_B` while
`RecordID_A≠RecordID_B`. Delivers the deduplication danger: discarding provenance loses independent
corroboration — **`Semantic deduplication must not mean provenance deduplication`**.

Delivers a distributed event model as the foundational choice: `H_{merge}=H_A∪H_B`,
`K_{merge}=Derive(H_{merge},Ω,EC,M)` — cleaner than merging opaque states. Delivers the crucial distinction
that **identical events are NOT necessarily independent** (replication≠new evidence,
`Independent(E_1,E_2)=False`, requiring `OriginEventID` preservation), a worked causality example, and the
causal/temporal boundary `E_1≺E_2` (causal precedence) as stronger than timestamp ordering. Delivers the
explicit restraint that KnowledgeOS reuses established distributed-systems mechanisms rather than
reinventing them.

**Delivers the file's central formal deliverable — a convergence theorem candidate**: matching
`H,Ω,EC,M` across two instances implies semantically equivalent derived KnowledgeStates, worked through a
Node A/Node B disjoint-event-set example — no "one magical central brain" needed.

**Delivers the file's most conceptually important single result**: **`Convergence≠Consensus`** — nodes
that both receive contradictory evidence should converge to `Conflict(A,¬A)`, not agreement on truth —
**`AgreementOnEpistemicState`**, not `AgreementOnTruth` — "exactly appropriate for epistemic systems," a
genuinely novel principle distinguishing KnowledgeOS from classical distributed-consensus theory.

**Delivers the CRDT connection made formal**: event-history union `H_1⊔H_2=H_1∪H_2` proven trivially
commutative, associative, and idempotent (grow-only structure). **Delivers the key qualifying correction,
directly echoing 0589's monotonicity finding at the distributed layer**: the DERIVED KnowledgeState does
NOT automatically inherit these properties (temporal state, retraction, conflict resolution, authority,
policy) — **`EventHistory can be monotonic while DerivedKnowledge can be non-monotonic`**, "actually
ideal" — cleanly separating distributed convergence from epistemic semantics.

Delivers a worked distributed-retraction scenario showing differing node states are not necessarily
inconsistency, and a precise new distinction: **`KnowledgeState` vs. `KnowledgeCompleteness`** —
`Consistent(K)` does not imply `Complete(K)` — **`LocalConsistency≠GlobalCompleteness`**. Delivers
`Frontier(K)` as the set of knowledge/events currently known to a node.

**Delivers a substantial DDD-bounded-context reframing**: recommends AGAINST a single universal ontology
in favor of context-specific ontologies with explicit cross-context mappings — "textbook DDD thinking
applied to epistemic architecture" — reconfirmed via a worked "Approved" example. **Delivers a genuinely
new consequence — cross-context mappings are themselves epistemic objects requiring their own provenance**,
yielding a recursively self-describing architecture: KnowledgeOS can hold **`Knowledge About
KnowledgeMappings`** — explicitly named **metaknowledge**, the corpus's first explicit metaknowledge
concept.

**Delivers a sharpened statistical-independence warning for the multi-context setting**: evidence
consumed by two bounded contexts from one shared source is correlated, not independent, regardless of
organizational separation — **`Independence must be derived from provenance, not organizational
ownership`**, extending 0562's/0578's/0580's/0588's evidence-independence discipline.

Delivers a ten-step conceptual distributed Merge algorithm, `O(n)` union/deduplication complexity, and the
reconfirmed conclusion "the expensive problem is semantic interpretation, not distributed merging itself."
**Delivers seven labeled falsification tests (1-7), ALL PASS**: duplicate replication; independent
corroboration; conflicting evidence; eventual synchronization; different ontology versions (legitimately
differing states are `ModelVersionDifference`, not inconsistency); missing events; same conclusion via
different provenance.

**Delivers Step 25L's verdict**: **"25L — PASS,"** preferring `Distributed Event History→Deterministic
Knowledge Derivation` over `Distributed Mutable Knowledge Objects`, with an explicit, carefully-scoped
limitation: **`Same inputs+same semantics⇒same derived state`** is the correct claim — NOT that all
KnowledgeOS nodes converge to identical knowledge regardless of differing observations/permissions/
contexts. Reconfirms normal-PC feasibility via conventional distributed technologies.

**Delivers the file's central architectural crystallization — a formal three-layer decomposition**:
**Layer 1 — Historical** (`H`, immutable events); **Layer 2 — Knowledge derivation**
(`K=Derive(H,Ω,EC,M)`); **Layer 3 — Decision/control** (`Zero→Lord→Sārathi→Decision→Action`) — the
corpus's cleanest single top-level structural summary of the entire mathematical program to date.

**Closes by distinguishing "contradictory" from genuinely "wrong" knowledge**, worked through a
misattributed-server observation example, separating Correction, Retraction, Refutation,
InvalidObservation, and ModelError as NOT the same thing. **Opens "Step 25M — Error, Refutation,
