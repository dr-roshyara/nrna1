## What Is This Document About?
The commission asks *"what are we actually building?"* The document answers by identifying five
strategic products and testing each against the reuse litmus, then refusing to do the part of the
commission it judges unauthorised.

That refusal is stated **before** the analysis rather than inside it, which is the file's clearest
methodological move. The commission asked for Evans/Vernon relationship patterns; a standing ruling
says *"the strategic relationship is the conclusion, not the starting point."* The document resolves
the conflict using the commission's own rule (*"only identify relationships that are evidenced"*)
and delivers **one evidenced relationship, five named candidates, and zero assigned patterns** —
recording that *"a context map with one edge is not a failure of this discovery; it is the
measurement."*

Two of the five products are **reclassified against the commission's own framing**: the AI Runtime
is demoted from *product* to **adapter boundary** (*replaceable ≠ reusable*), and Operational
Learning is refined from *"missing"* to **"dispersed, governed in six places, and never once
traversed"** — with the sharp corollary that *"naming SP-5 as a new strategic domain would not make
evidence flow; it would give a non-functioning mechanism a better title."*

The document's most structurally interesting observation is a **dependency inversion**: the platform
supplies rules *downstream* to the product, while the product holds authority *upstream* over the
platform's right to evolve. **Knowledge flow and authority flow run in opposite directions.**

§6 defines the **Minimum Viable KnowledgeOS** by asking a genuine minimality question — *if the
product disappeared tomorrow, what is the smallest coherent KnowledgeOS that could still bootstrap a
completely different product?* — and lists ten layers of content. It then states, unprompted, the
limitation that would later destroy it: **the litmus tests each *element* for portability; it does
not test the *set* for completeness.** *"A set can be entirely portable and still fail to bootstrap
anything, because portability is a property of parts and sufficiency is a property of the whole."*
The MVK is therefore declared a **hypothesis with per-element evidence and no whole-set evidence,
n = 0 bootstraps.**

§10 recommends testing that hypothesis rather than acting on it — *"TEST THE MVK. Do not extract
it."* — with a falsifiable design and a definition of done. Annotations then record that the test was
run, returned `FAIL`, and that the author's own inclusion of three methodology documents rested on a
mistaken inference (**domain-free ≠ portable**). AR-1, the risk the document itself rated HIGH, is
marked **REALIZED — and caught before extraction.**

## TIER 2 — DEEP ANALYSIS
**Trigger status: TRIGGERED** — poses the corpus's **first kernel minimality question**, states a
**compositional (parts↛whole) result**, and exposes a **third variant of a supposedly closed
vocabulary**. Three independent triggers.

### Mathematical derivation
None. But §6 contains the corpus's first genuinely **structural** argument, stated informally:

```
∀ x ∈ MVK : portable(x)          — established element-by-element by the litmus
⊬  sufficient(MVK)               — NOT entailed
```

*"Portability is a property of parts and sufficiency is a property of the whole."* This is a correct
and non-trivial observation about the failure of a universally-quantified member property to lift to
a set property. It is reasoning **about** structure without any formalism, and it is the closest the
C1 lineage comes to a mathematical argument in the first eleven files.

### Formal definitions
**MVK is defined extensionally** — ten layers of named artifacts — and the minimality question is
posed **intensionally**: *"the smallest **coherent** KnowledgeOS that could still bootstrap a
completely different product."* **The two do not meet.** No coherence criterion is given, no
bootstrap-success criterion is given, and no removal test is defined. So the question is well-posed
in words and unanswerable as stated — which is precisely why the answer offered is a list and why the
list was refutable but not minimisable.

### Contradictions
- `[CT]` **A THIRD closed verdict vocabulary.** §6 lists seven members — *PASS · PASS AFTER
  CORRECTION · WARN · FAIL · INCONCLUSIVE · EMERGENT · CERTIFIED*. File 0008 recorded two closed sets
  in collision: `{PASS, PASS AFTER CORRECTION, WARN, FAIL}` and `{PASS, FAIL, WARN, INCONCLUSIVE}`,
  the latter *forbidding* emission of a member of the former. **The set here is a superset of both
  and adds two further members.** So the corpus contains **at least three mutually incompatible
  "closed" verdict vocabularies**, and one of them silently reconciles the collision by union — which
  is exactly what a closure claim forbids. **This materially strengthens ledger entry 8.**
- `[CT]` **The strongest relationship candidate is defeated by location, not by content** (AR-4).
  The methodology is genuinely shared, so *Shared Kernel* fits; but the material sits in a product
  path, so the kernel is **unbounded**. *"One rule → one home" is satisfied by accident of location,
  not by boundary* — a structural property being met coincidentally rather than by construction.
- `[CT]` **The knowledge/authority flow inversion.** Rules flow platform → product; authority over
  the platform's evolution flows product → platform. Not an error — the document names it as
  counter-intuitive and canon-stated — but it means the C1 lineage's dependency graph is **not
  acyclic** when knowledge and authority edges are taken together.
