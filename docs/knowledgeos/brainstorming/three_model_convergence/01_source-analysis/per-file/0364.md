**Layer 2** (lines 523-1001, "Analysis: Knowledge Space as Overlapping Field") is a rigorous, book-grounded

Establishes KST's overlap is **structural/combinatorial** (shared skills, "notions" — equally informative

assessment"), flagging the regime-specific-decomposition hypothesis as plausible but KST-unestablished.
Closes with a fourteen-item what-KST-strengthens/what-KST-challenges register and eight new research
questions.

**Layer 3** (lines 1003-1457) reframes the whole discussion as a genuine **inverse problem**: distinguishable
elements `{k_1,...,k_n}` combine via relations into a complex phenomenon `Φ`, generating observations `O_t`
— extraction `O_≤t → {k_1,...,k_n}` is ill-posed because the forward map `F(X_1)=F(X_2)` may be
non-injective. Formalizes `K≠P(K|O)` as "much more rigorous than saying knowledge is probabilistic" —
probability captures uncertainty about **extraction** specifically because the forward map is many-to-one.
Introduces the chain `{k_i,R_ij}→Φ→O` with reverse extraction `O→P({k_i,R_ij}|O)`. Poses a new open
question: is the Knowledge Space observable in principle, or fundamentally latent? — explicitly refusing to
assume the answer. Closes with a boxed six-clause inverse-problem research hypothesis, called "one of the
strongest formulations we have developed," naming the next research direction as inverse problems +
latent-state estimation + epistemology + Knowledge Space Theory.
