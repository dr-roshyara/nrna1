# 1. Why this text, for this audit

The audit's decisive finding was not a computational error. **It was an assumption two parties held
and neither tested** — that `desc = (n+1) − asc`. I asserted it; review restated it in better
vocabulary; **the tie-break falsified it, 21 369 times.**

**The Gītā's opening situation is precisely an untested assumption held with conviction** — and its
first instruction is not an answer but a re-examination.

# 2. The loci

## 2.1 `aśocyān anvaśocas tvaṁ prajñā-vādāṁś ca bhāṣase` · 2.11

> *you grieve for those who should not be grieved for — and you speak words of wisdom*

**Kṛṣṇa's first substantive line is not a correction of Arjuna's conclusion. It is the observation
that his reasoning is well-formed and his premise is not examined.** `prajñā-vādān` — *words of
wisdom* — is not sarcasm; the argument is competent. **The defect is upstream of the argument.**

> **Corroboration C-1** — for what the audit gate exists to catch. `results.md` §5 was well-formed,
> reviewed, and improved in wording by a second party. **Neither pass touched the premise.** The
> audit did, and only because its protocol said *"formally prove"* rather than *"state"*.
>
> ⚠️ **This corroborates the VALUE OF AN AUDIT STEP. It supplies no method** — no way to know which
> premise to examine. The protocol's checklist did that, not the verse.

## 2.2 `saṁśayātmā vinaśyati` and its opposite · 4.40, 4.42

> *cut the doubt … with the sword of knowledge*  (`jñāna-asinā`)

**The Gītā does not counsel living with doubt. It counsels CUTTING it — by knowledge, decisively.**

> **Lens L-1 — an unexamined assumption and a live doubt are different failure modes.**
> `saṁśaya` is doubt one *has*; the §5 error was an assumption one *does not know one has*. **The text
> addresses the first and is silent on the second.** Recorded because the distinction is the whole
> difference between review and audit: **review interrogates what is stated; audit interrogates what
> is assumed.**

## 2.3 `karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana` · 2.47

> *your right is to the work, never to its fruits*

**The audit was run knowing it might destroy the experiment's headline result. It did.**

> **Corroboration C-2** — and the most apt of the study. The verse's structure is exactly the
> methodological commitment: **execute the check; do not select it by its expected outcome.** A
> checklist run only where one expects to pass is not a checklist.
>
> **`[EXT]` only.** The programme reached this position from its own failures — the vacuity guard, the
> paired-design correction, the positive control. **The verse agrees; it did not teach.**

## 2.4 The refusal — `sarva-dharmān parityajya` · 18.66

> *abandoning all duties, take refuge in Me alone*

**A tempting reading:** at the end, set the framework aside and trust the synthesis.

> ## ⚠️ REFUSED.
> **This is the most dangerous verse in the text for a research programme**, because it licenses
> exactly what the governance freeze forbids: **a shortcut from evidence to conviction.**
>
> The current frozen transition is `EXPERIMENT → AUDIT → ADJUDICATION → THEORY v1.3`, with **no
> shortcut into theory or kernel.** A reading of 18.66 as *"trust the synthesis"* would dissolve that
> chain. **Recorded as a refusal, and it is the second time this strand has had to refuse its most
> quotable verse** — 2.50 (`skill in action`) was refused in the previous study for the same class of
> reason.
