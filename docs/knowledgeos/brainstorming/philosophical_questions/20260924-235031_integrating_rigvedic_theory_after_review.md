## Part II: What the Review Gets Wrong or Overstates

### II.1 The Review Conflates Philosophy with Formal Theory

The review treats the Rigvedic theory as if it were a **formal theory**. It is not. It is a **philosophy** — a worldview, a tradition, a set of concepts.

**The precise distinction:**

- **Philosophy:** Concepts, arguments, intuitions.
- **Formal theory:** Language, axioms, inference rules, proofs.

The Rigvedic theory provides **concepts**. It does not provide a **formal theory**.

The review's formalizations are **candidate formalizations**, not established theory.

### II.2 The Review Adds Axioms Without Proof

The review proposes:

$$
\text{R1} : \Pi : \mathcal{F} \times \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{S}

Similarly, the review proposes:

$$

This is a **conjecture**, not a theorem. It has not been proven.
