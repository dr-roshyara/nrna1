# Step 382 — Boundary Finding Irreducibility Attack

We continue from Step 381.

The question is now precise:

$$
\boxed{
BoundaryFinding
\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{ordinary identity-bearing semantic relation instance}
}
$$

If this reduction succeeds, we obtain an important architectural result:

$$
\boxed{
Zero\text{ is a lens/process, while its findings are ordinary semantic structures.}
}
$$

We must test this rather than assume it.

---

## 382.1 Competing hypotheses

### \(H_0\)

A boundary finding requires an independent Kernel primitive:

$$
BoundaryFinding\in B_K.
$$

### \(H_1\)

A boundary finding is reducible to:

$$
(IID,\rho,args)
$$

with semantic contract:

$$
\Lambda_\rho=(C_\rho,T_\rho,M_\rho).
$$

### \(H_2\)

The basic relation instance is sufficient, but boundary findings additionally require some irreducible fourth semantic component.

The attack therefore focuses on \(H_1\) versus \(H_2\).

$$

$$

$$

No additional ontological structure is immediately necessary.

# 382.6 Could one boundary finding have multiple reasons?

Yes.

For example:

$$
b=(i_b,BoundaryFinding,x)
$$

with relations:

$$
HasReason(b,InsufficientEvidence)
$$

$$
HasReason(b,Conflict).
$$

But there is a subtle modeling issue.

Should `BoundaryFinding` be a universal relation type?

Not necessarily.

The semantic type may instead be:

$$
Boundary_\Gamma(b).
$$

The particular reasons are relations/types associated with \(b\).

We should therefore avoid prematurely freezing:

$$
BoundaryFinding
$$

as an ontological category.

# Step 383 — Next attack: Boundary Classification Completeness

We should now attack the remaining weak point.

We have shown that different unresolved conditions are semantically distinguishable. But we have **not** shown that our candidate taxonomy is complete.

The next question is:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{candidate}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\mathcal B_{relevant}
}
$$

where:

$$
\mathcal B_{candidate}
=
\{
Unobserved,
Unobservable,
Uninterpreted,
Ambiguous,
InsufficientEvidence,
Underdetermined,
Conflict,
OutOfScope,
\ldots
\}.
$$

We must test whether there are boundary classes that cannot be reduced to these—or whether all apparent new classes are merely combinations/refinements of existing dimensions.

The attack should use **orthogonal boundary axes**, rather than simply adding more labels:

$$
\boxed{
Observation\ Axis
\times
Interpretation\ Axis
\times
Evidence\ Axis
\times
Determination\ Axis
\times
Scope\ Axis
\times
Temporal\ Axis
\times
Model\ Axis
}
$$

and determine whether boundary state is better represented as a **product of typed dimensions** rather than a flat enumeration.

That may be the next major architectural simplification.
