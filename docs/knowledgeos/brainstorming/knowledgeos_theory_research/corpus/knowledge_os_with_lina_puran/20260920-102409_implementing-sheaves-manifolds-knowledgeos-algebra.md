$$
\boxed{\text{Can we implement KS sheaf theory in KnowledgeOS without violating kernel minimality?}}

## Part 1: Mapping KS Concepts to KnowledgeOS Primitives

### 1.1 The Base Space

**KS:** A manifold $X$ (or a topological space).

**KnowledgeOS:** The **Dependency Context** is the base space. Define:

$$
X_{\text{KOS}} := V \quad \text{(the set of Dependency Nodes)}
$$

with the topology generated by **epistemic neighborhoods**:

$$
U \subseteq V \text{ is open} \iff U \text{ is closed under dependency reachability}
$$

**Definition 1.1.1 (Epistemic Topology).** For a node $v \in V$, the **epistemic neighborhood** $N(v)$ is:

$$
N(v) := \{w \in V : \text{DependsOn}^*(w, v)\}
$$

The **epistemic topology** $\tau_{\text{epi}}$ is the topology generated by these neighborhoods.

**Theorem 1.1.2.** $(V, \tau_{\text{epi}})$ is a topological space.

*Proof.* The empty set and $V$ are trivially open. Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of reachability sets are reachability sets. $\square$

### 1.2 The Sheaf of Epistemic States

**KS:** A sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ assigns to each open $U$ an abelian group $\mathcal{F}(U)$, with restriction maps, satisfying the gluing axiom.

**KnowledgeOS:** Define the **sheaf of epistemic states** $\mathcal{E}$ by:

$$
\mathcal{E}(U) := \prod_{v \in U} \text{EpistemicState}(v)
$$

where $\text{EpistemicState}(v)$ is the 5-tuple $(A, V, C, T, \Pi)$ from Step 545.

**The restriction map** $\rho_{V,U} : \mathcal{E}(V) \to \mathcal{E}(U)$ for $U \subseteq V$ is the natural projection.

**Verification of Sheaf Axioms:**

- **Locality (S1):** If two epistemic states agree on every neighborhood of every point, they agree on the whole open set. ✓
- **Gluing (S2):** If local epistemic states agree on overlaps, they glue to a global one. ✓

**Theorem 1.2.1.** $(\mathcal{E}, \rho)$ is a sheaf of abelian groups on $(V, \tau_{\text{epi}})$.

*Proof.* Both axioms follow from the product construction. $\square$

### 1.3 The Stalk

**KS:** The stalk $\mathcal{F}_x$ is the direct limit $\varinjlim_{x \in U} \mathcal{F}(U)$.

**KnowledgeOS:**

$$
\mathcal{E}_v := \varinjlim_{v \in U} \mathcal{E}(U) \cong \text{EpistemicState}(v) \times \prod_{w \in N(v) \setminus \{v\}} \text{EpistemicState}(w)
$$

**Interpretation:** The stalk at $v$ is the epistemic state of $v$ plus all states reachable from $v$.

### 1.4 Cohomology

**KS:** $H^j(X; \mathcal{F})$ is the $j$-th derived functor of global sections.

**KnowledgeOS:** The **epistemic cohomology** $H^j(V; \mathcal{E})$ encodes:

- $H^0(V; \mathcal{E})$ = globally consistent epistemic states
- $H^1(V; \mathcal{E})$ = **obstructions to global consistency** — i.e., cycles of dependency that cannot be reconciled
- $H^j(V; \mathcal{E})$ for $j \geq 2$ = higher-order obstructions

**This is the crucial innovation:** $H^1$ measures **circular reasoning** that cannot be resolved locally.

## Part 2: The Micro-Support of an Epistemic Sheaf

### 2.1 The Cotangent Bundle

**KS:** The micro-support $\text{SS}(F) \subseteq T^*X$ is a conic Lagrangian subset.

**KnowledgeOS:** Define the **epistemic cotangent bundle**:

$$
T^*V_{\text{KOS}} := \{(v, \phi) : v \in V, \phi \in \text{PerturbationDirection}(v)\}
$$

where $\text{PerturbationDirection}(v)$ is the **linear dual of the tangent space at $v$**, i.e., the space of perturbations applicable at $v$.

**Definition 2.1.1 (Perturbation Direction).** A **perturbation direction** at $v$ is a linear functional on the space of admissible perturbations at $v$.

**Example 2.1.2.** For the Nexus case:
- Nodes: `Nexus`, `RHEL9.8`, `ExternalSystemX`
- Perturbation directions at `Nexus`: `RemoveEvidence`, `InvalidateAssumption`, `ReplaceModel`, `ExpireTemporal`

### 2.2 The Micro-Support

**KS:** $\text{SS}(F) \subseteq T^*X$ consists of points $(x, \xi)$ where $F$ has no cohomology supported by half-spaces with conormals close to $\xi$.

**KnowledgeOS:**

$$
\text{SS}(\mathcal{E}) := \overline{\{(v, \phi) : \text{perturbation in direction } \phi \text{ changes } \mathcal{E}_v\}}
$$

**Theorem 2.2.1 (Epistemic Micro-Support).** $\text{SS}(\mathcal{E})$ is a closed conic subset of $T^*V_{\text{KOS}}$.

*Proof.* The set of perturbing directions that change the stalk is defined by an inequality on cohomology, hence closed. The conic property follows from linearity of perturbation directions. $\square$

### 2.3 The Involutivity Theorem

**KS (Theorem 6.5.4):** $\text{SS}(F)$ is involutive.

**KnowledgeOS:**

**Theorem 2.3.1 (Epistemic Involutivity).** For any epistemic sheaf $\mathcal{E}$, the micro-support $\text{SS}(\mathcal{E})$ is closed under the Poisson bracket of perturbation directions.

**Interpretation:** If two perturbations $\phi_1, \phi_2$ both change the epistemic state, then their commutator also changes it. This is a **closure property of epistemic fragility**.

**Algorithmic Consequence:** To compute $\text{SS}(\mathcal{E})$, we only need to test a generating set of perturbation directions. The involutive property then gives the full support.

## Part 3: Constructibility and the Dependency Stratification

### 3.1 The Dependency Stratification

**KS:** An R-constructible sheaf is locally constant on the strata of a subanalytic stratification.

**KnowledgeOS:** Define the **dependency stratification**:

$$
V = \bigsqcup_{\alpha \in A} V_\alpha
$$

where each $V_\alpha$ is a maximal set of nodes with **identical epistemic neighborhoods** (up to isomorphism).

**Definition 3.1.1 (Dependency Stratum).** A **dependency stratum** $V_\alpha$ is a maximal subset of $V$ such that for any $v, w \in V_\alpha$:

$$
N(v) \cong N(w) \quad \text{and} \quad \text{EpistemicState}(v) \cong \text{EpistemicState}(w)
$$

**Algorithm 3.1.2 (Stratification via Graph Coloring).**

```python
def compute_stratification(V, E):
    # Color nodes by their dependency signature
    signatures = {}
    for v in V:
        sig = (frozenset(in_neighbors(v)), frozenset(out_neighbors(v)))
        signatures.setdefault(sig, []).append(v)
    return list(signatures.values())
```

**Complexity:** $O(|V| + |E|)$ — linear in graph size.

### 3.2 Constructibility of the Epistemic Sheaf

**Theorem 3.2.1.** The epistemic sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ is R-constructible with respect to the dependency stratification.

*Proof.* By construction, $\mathcal{E}|_{V_\alpha}$ is locally constant on each stratum. $\square$

**Corollary 3.2.2 (Fundamental Equivalence).** $\mathcal{E}$ is R-constructible if and only if $\text{SS}(\mathcal{E})$ is a subanalytic Lagrangian subset of $T^*V_{\text{KOS}}$.

**Interpretation:** The dependency stratification is the **local structure**; the micro-support is the **global/microlocal structure**. They are equivalent.

## Part 4: The Six Operations in KnowledgeOS

### 4.1 The Operations

The six operations from KS translate as follows:

| Operation | KS Notation | KnowledgeOS Interpretation |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------------|
| Direct Image | $Rf_*$ | **Aggregation along a morphism** (e.g., rolling up nodes to a component) |
| Inverse Image | $f^{-1}$ | **Restriction to a subgraph** |
| Proper Direct Image | $Rf_!$ | **Aggregation with compact support** (e.g., only local nodes) |
| Exceptional Inverse Image | $f^!$ | **Dualizing restriction** (used in Poincaré-Verdier duality) |
| Tensor Product | $\otimes^L$ | **Combining independent evidence** |
| Internal Hom | $R\mathcal{H}om$ | **Mapping between epistemic states** |

### 4.2 Poincaré-Verdier Duality

**KS:** The duality theorem states:

$$
R\mathcal{H}om(F, f^!G) \cong Rf_* R\mathcal{H}om(f^{-1}F, G)
$$

**KnowledgeOS:**

**Theorem 4.2.1 (Epistemic Duality).** Let $f: W \to V$ be a morphism of dependency graphs. Then:

$$
R\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, f^!\mathcal{G}) \cong Rf_* R\mathcal{H}om(f^{-1}\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})
$$

**Interpretation:** The obstruction to extending evidence from a subgraph $W$ to the full graph $V$ is dual to the obstruction to restricting evidence from $V$ to $W$.

**Algorithmic Use:** This duality lets us compute **global consistency obstructions** ($H^1$) in terms of **local consistency conditions**, which is computationally much cheaper.

### 4.3 Implementation of the Duality Functor

**Definition 4.3.1 (Dualizing Functor).**

$$
D_V \mathcal{F} := R\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \omega_V)
$$

where $\omega_V$ is the **dualizing complex** — in KnowledgeOS, this is the **sheaf of all admissible perturbations**.

```python
def dualize(F, V):
    # omega_V = sheaf of perturbations
    omega = perturbation_sheaf(V)
    return derived_hom(F, omega)
```

**Theorem 4.3.2.** $D_V \circ D_V \cong \text{id}$ on the derived category of constructible sheaves.

### 5.2 Data Structures

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List, Set, Tuple, Callable
import numpy as np

@dataclass
class DependencyNode:
    id: str
    epistemic_state: dict  # (A, V, C, T, Π)
    metadata: dict

@dataclass
class DependencyEdge:
    src: str
    dst: str
    relation_type: str
    weight: float = 1.0

@dataclass
class EpistemicSheaf:
    """
    Sheaf of epistemic states on the dependency graph.
    """
    nodes: Dict[str, DependencyNode]
    edges: List[DependencyEdge]
    
    def open_sets(self) -> List[Set[str]]:
        """Return all epistemic neighborhoods."""
        result = []
        for v in self.nodes:
            N_v = self.reachable_set(v)
            result.append(N_v)
        return result
    
    def reachable_set(self, v: str) -> Set[str]:
        """Compute N(v) = {w : DependsOn*(w, v)}."""
        visited = set()
        stack = [v]
        while stack:
            w = stack.pop()
            if w in visited:
                continue
            visited.add(w)
            for e in self.edges:
                if e.dst == w:
                    stack.append(e.src)
        return visited
    
    def section(self, U: Set[str]) -> dict:
        """ℰ(U) = product of epistemic states over U."""
        return {v: self.nodes[v].epistemic_state for v in U}
    
    def restriction(self, V: Set[str], U: Set[str]) -> Callable:
        """ρ_{V,U}: ℰ(V) → ℰ(U)."""
        def rho(s):
            return {v: s[v] for v in U}
        return rho
    
    def stalk(self, v: str) -> dict:
        """ℰ_v = colim_{v ∈ U} ℰ(U)."""
        N_v = self.reachable_set(v)
        return self.section(N_v)
```

### 5.3 Cohomology Computation

```python
def compute_cohomology(sheaf: EpistemicSheaf, 
                       open_cover: List[Set[str]]) -> Dict[int, list]:
    """
    Compute Čech cohomology of the epistemic sheaf.
    H^0 = global sections
    H^1 = obstruction to global consistency
    H^j for j ≥ 2 = higher obstructions
    """
    # Build the Čech complex
    cech = build_cech_complex(sheaf, open_cover)
    
    # Compute cohomology
    cohomology = {}
    for j in range(len(open_cover)):
        cohomology[j] = cech.homology(j)
    
    return cohomology

def build_cech_complex(sheaf: EpistemicSheaf, 
                       cover: List[Set[str]]):
    """
    C^p = ∏_{i_0 < ... < i_p} ℰ(U_{i_0} ∩ ... ∩ U_{i_p})
    with the usual Čech differential.
    """
    n = len(cover)
    C = {}
    
    # Degree 0: product over single opens
    C[0] = [sheaf.section(U) for U in cover]
    
    # Degree 1: product over pairs
    C[1] = []
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i+1, n):
            C[1].append(sheaf.section(cover[i] & cover[j]))
    
    # ... higher degrees
    
    return CechComplex(C)
```

### 5.4 Micro-Support Computation

```python
def compute_micro_support(sheaf: EpistemicSheaf,
                          perturbation_directions: List[Callable]) -> Set[Tuple[str, str]]:
    """
    SS(ℰ) = closure of {(v, φ) : perturbation in direction φ changes ℰ_v}.
    """
    ss = set()
    
    for v in sheaf.nodes:
        stalk = sheaf.stalk(v)
        for phi in perturbation_directions:
            perturbed = apply_perturbation(stalk, phi)
            if not epistemic_equivalent(stalk, perturbed):
                ss.add((v, phi.__name__))
    
    # Apply involutivity closure
    ss = involutive_closure(ss, perturbation_directions)
    
    return ss

def apply_perturbation(stalk: dict, phi: Callable) -> dict:
    """Apply perturbation φ to the stalk."""
    return phi(stalk)

def epistemic_equivalent(s1: dict, s2: dict) -> bool:
    """Check if two epistemic states are equivalent."""
    return s1 == s2  # Simplified; in practice, use Eq_Det

def involutive_closure(ss: Set, directions: List[Callable]) -> Set:
    """
    Close SS under the Poisson bracket of perturbation directions.
    This is the involutivity theorem.
    """
    # Iteratively add brackets until closure
    changed = True
    while changed:
        changed = False
        for phi1 in directions:
            for phi2 in directions:
                bracket = poisson_bracket(phi1, phi2)
                for v in get_all_nodes():
                    if (v, phi1.__name__) in ss and (v, phi2.__name__) in ss:
                        if (v, bracket.__name__) not in ss:
                            ss.add((v, bracket.__name__))
                            changed = True
    return ss
```

```python
def is_constructible(sheaf: EpistemicSheaf) -> bool:
    """
    Check if ℰ is R-constructible.
    Equivalent to: SS(ℰ) is a subanalytic Lagrangian set.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute stratification
    strata = compute_stratification(sheaf.nodes, sheaf.edges)
    
    # Step 2: Check local constancy on each stratum
    for stratum in strata:
        if not is_locally_constant(sheaf, stratum):
            return False
    
    # Step 3: Check micro-support is Lagrangian
    ss = compute_micro_support(sheaf, get_perturbation_directions())
    if not is_lagrangian(ss):
        return False
    
    return True
```

## Part 8: Complexity and Feasibility

### 8.1 Complexity Analysis

| Operation | Complexity | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Epistemic neighborhood | $O(\|V\| + \|E\|)$ | Graph traversal |
| Sheaf section | $O(\|U\|)$ | Product over open set |
| Čech complex (full) | $O(2^{\|U\|})$ | **Exponential** — infeasible |
| Čech complex (bounded) | $O(\|U\|^k)$ | Bounded by cohomological dimension $k$ |
| Micro-support (naive) | $O(\|V\| \cdot \|\Phi\|)$ | $\Phi$ = perturbation directions |
| Micro-support (with involutivity) | $O(\|V\| \cdot \|\Phi\|^2)$ | Closure computation |
| Constructibility check | $O(\|V\| + \|E\|)$ | Linear in graph size |

### 8.2 Practical Bounds

For real KnowledgeOS instances:
- $|V| \leq 10^6$ (a million nodes)
- $|E| \leq 10^7$ (ten million edges)
- $|\Phi| \leq 10^3$ (a thousand perturbation directions)
- Cohomological dimension $\leq 5$ (higher cohomology is rarely needed)

**Feasible operations:** All the above.

**Infeasible operations:** Full Čech complex at high dimension.

### 8.3 Optimization: Use the Involutivity Theorem

The **involutivity theorem** is the key to tractability. Instead of computing SS directly, we compute a **generating set** of perturbation directions, then close under Poisson bracket.

```python
def compute_micro_support_efficient(sheaf, generators):
    """
    Compute SS(ℰ) using a generating set of perturbation directions.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute SS on generators only
    ss_gen = set()
    for v in sheaf.nodes:
        for phi in generators:
            if perturbation_changes_stalk(sheaf, v, phi):
                ss_gen.add((v, phi))
    
    # Step 2: Close under Poisson bracket (involutivity)
    return involutive_closure(ss_gen, generators)
```

**Complexity:** $O(|V| \cdot |\Phi_{\text{gen}}| + |\Phi_{\text{gen}}|^2 \cdot |V|)$ — polynomial.

2. **Higher-order obstruction detection:** $H^2$ detects higher-dimensional holes.
3. **Duality-based consistency checks:** The dual sheaf $D\mathcal{E}$ gives a second perspective.
4. **Microlocal fragility:** SS tells you exactly which perturbation directions matter.
5. **Perversity:** Middle perversity gives a canonical $t$-structure on constructible sheaves.

### 9.3 Perverse Sheaves in KnowledgeOS

**Definition 9.3.1 (Perverse Epistemic Sheaf).** An R-constructible epistemic sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ is **perverse** if:

$$
\dim \text{supp}(H^j(\mathcal{E})) \leq -j \quad \text{for all } j
$$

**Interpretation:** Perverse sheaves satisfy a **dimension-extension duality**. They are the "natural" objects for which Poincaré-Verdier duality takes a clean form.

**Theorem 9.3.2 (Perversity is Preserved by Operations).** The six operations preserve perversity under appropriate conditions.

**Consequence:** We can perform **all** of the six operations on perverse epistemic sheaves without leaving the perverse category. This gives a **closed algebra** of epistemic operations.
