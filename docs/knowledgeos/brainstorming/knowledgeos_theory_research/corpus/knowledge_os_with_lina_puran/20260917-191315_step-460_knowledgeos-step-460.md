# KnowledgeOS — Step 460

## Change, Evolution, Structural Drift, Semantic Drift, Schema Evolution, Ontology Evolution, Concept Drift, Identity-Preserving Change, Contract Evolution and the Mathematics of Change

We continue directly from Step 459.

The previous step established:

$$
\boxed{
Equivalence,\ Compatibility,\ Refinement,\ Simulation,\ Bisimulation,\ Transformation
}
$$

can be represented and evaluated without adding new Kernel primitives.

But that creates a deeper problem.

A system may change while still being "the same system."

A representation may change while its meaning remains unchanged.

A schema may change while domain identity remains stable.

A domain concept may change while its representation remains unchanged.

A machine-learning model may drift while its schema remains identical.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Change\ itself\ cannot\ be\ treated\ as\ one\ undifferentiated\ concept.
}
$$

The central research question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can all relevant forms of change be reconstructed from identity, relations,}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{history and semantic transition laws, without introducing a universal Change primitive?}
}
$$

The current candidate answer is **yes**, but we need to attack it rigorously.

---

# 460.1 Change

### Definition

**Change** means a difference between two states, representations, relations, behaviors, meanings or configurations under a specified comparison contract.

Let:

$$
S_t
$$

be a state at time \(t\), and:

$$
S_{t+1}
$$

the later state.

Then:

$$
Change(S_t,S_{t+1},\Gamma)
$$

means that the selected contract \(\Gamma\) distinguishes them.

Crucially:

$$
S_t\neq S_{t+1}
$$

does not imply:

$$
Identity_t\neq Identity_{t+1}.
$$

This confirms Step 456.

---

# 460.2 Difference

### Definition

A **difference** is a distinction detected by a comparison relation.

$$
Diff_\Gamma(A,B).
$$

A difference may concern:

* value;
* relation;
* structure;
* behavior;
* identity;
* meaning;
* time;
* provenance;
* policy.

Therefore:

$$
Difference\neq Change
$$

in every context.

Two stored representations can differ because they encode the same thing differently.

---

# 460.3 State transition

### Definition

A **state transition** is a change from one state to another under specified transition semantics.

$$
T_\Gamma(s,x,s').
$$

This is already part of the Kernel semantic-law model from Step 362.

We therefore do **not** need:

```text
ChangePrimitive
```

or:

```text
StateTransitionPrimitive.
```

The transition is represented by relation instances and transition semantics.

---

# 460.4 Event versus change

A critical distinction:

$$
Event\neq Change.
$$

An event is an occurrence.

Change is a semantic difference between states or structures.

Example:

```text
09:00  Employee promoted
09:01  Employee has new role
```

The promotion event is:

$$
Promotion(A,Manager).
$$

The resulting state change is:

$$
Role_t(A)=Employee
$$

to:

$$
Role_{t+1}(A)=Manager.
$$

Therefore:

$$
Event\rightarrow Change
$$

may hold under a domain contract, but:

$$
Event=Change
$$

is incorrect.

---

# 460.5 Evolution

### Definition

**Evolution** means a sequence or process of changes through time.

$$
S_0\rightarrow S_1\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow S_n.
$$

Evolution therefore requires:

* temporal ordering;
* state/structure comparison;
* transition semantics.

All already exist in KnowledgeOS.

Thus:

$$
Evolution
=
Projection(H,\Gamma).
$$

No primitive is required.

---

# 460.6 Evolution versus change

A single change:

$$
S_0\rightarrow S_1
$$

is not necessarily an evolution.

Evolution normally refers to a sequence/process:

$$
S_0\rightarrow S_1\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow S_n.
$$

Therefore:

$$
Change\neq Evolution.
$$

This is useful when analyzing:

* software versions;
* organizations;
* ML models;
* policies;
* knowledge;
* biological systems.

---

# 460.7 Structural change

### Definition

**Structural change** occurs when the structure or relations of a represented domain change under a structural contract.

Example:

Before:

```text id="a3b5nk"
Company
 ├── IT
 └── Finance
```

After:

```text id="y1d8f2"
Company
 ├── Technology
 │    └── IT
 └── Finance
```

The structural relation graph changed.

Formally:

$$
G_{t}\not\equiv_{\Gamma}^{struct}G_{t+1}.
$$

---

# 460.8 Attribute change

### Definition

An **attribute change** changes the value associated with an entity while potentially preserving identity and structure.

Example:

$$
Employee(A).salary=70000
$$

becomes:

$$
Employee(A).salary=75000.
$$

This is a change.

But:

$$
Identity(A,t)=Identity(A,t+1).
$$

Therefore:

$$
AttributeChange\neq IdentityChange.
$$

---

# 460.9 Behavioral change

### Definition

**Behavioral change** occurs when an entity/system responds differently under the same relevant input or environment.

Formally:

$$
Behavior_t(x,input)\neq Behavior_{t+1}(x,input).
$$

Example:

A software service previously returned:

$$
HTTP\ 200
$$

and now returns:

$$
HTTP\ 403.
$$

Its internal structure may remain identical.

Therefore:

$$
BehaviorChange\neq StructuralChange.
$$

---

# 460.10 Semantic change

### Definition

**Semantic change** occurs when the meaning assigned to a representation, relation, term or structure changes under the relevant interpretation contract.

Example:

A field called:

```text
status
```

originally means:

```text
currently active
```

but later the organization defines it as:

```text
approved for processing
```

The database schema may remain identical.

Yet:

$$
Meaning_t\neq Meaning_{t+1}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
SchemaEquality\not\Rightarrow SemanticStability.
}
$$

---

# 460.11 Representation change

### Definition

A **representation change** changes how information is encoded without necessarily changing its meaning.

Example:

```text id="0aj72h"
"Germany"
```

becomes:

```text id="1v1d5g"
"DE"
```

if the mapping contract establishes equivalence.

Then:

$$
Rep_t\neq Rep_{t+1}
$$

but:

$$
Sem_t\equiv_\Gamma Sem_{t+1}.
$$

This distinction is essential for migrations.

---

# 460.12 Representation change versus semantic change

We now have four possibilities:

| Representation | Meaning   | Interpretation           |
| -------------- | --------- | ------------------------ |
| same           | same      | stable                   |
| different      | same      | representation evolution |
| same           | different | semantic drift           |
| different      | different | both changed             |

This simple matrix is extremely useful.

---

# 460.13 Schema

### Definition

A **schema** is a formal specification of permitted structural forms of data.

It may specify:

* fields;
* types;
* cardinality;
* relations;
* constraints.

Example:

```text id="w1o4ep"
Customer
  id: UUID
  name: String
  email: String
```

A schema is not the data itself.

Therefore:

$$
Schema\neq Data.
$$

And:

$$
Schema\neq DomainMeaning.
$$

---

# 460.14 Schema evolution

### Definition

**Schema evolution** is change to a schema over time.

$$
Schema_t\rightarrow Schema_{t+1}.
$$

Example:

```text id="t5xgj9"
v1:
Customer(name)

v2:
Customer(name,email)
```

The addition of `email` is structural schema change.

But whether it changes domain semantics depends on the contract.

---

# 460.15 Backward-compatible schema evolution

Suppose old readers can consume new records.

Then:

$$
Compatible_\Gamma(Schema_t,Schema_{t+1})
$$

may hold.

But compatibility does not imply equivalence.

A new field can introduce additional semantics.

Therefore:

$$
BackwardCompatible\neq SemanticEquivalent.
$$

---

# 460.16 Breaking schema evolution

A **breaking schema change** is one that violates a declared compatibility contract.

Example:

```text id="g0k7eq"
email: String
```

becomes:

```text id="3n52e4"
email: Integer
```

under a contract requiring textual email values.

Then:

$$
\neg Compatible_\Gamma.
$$

But another system might interpret the new field differently.

Again:

$$
Breakingness\ is\ contract\ relative.
$$

---

# 460.17 Ontology evolution

### Definition

**Ontology evolution** is change in the concepts, relations or semantic constraints used to describe a domain.

Example:

Version 1:

$$
Customer\rightarrow Account.
$$

Version 2 introduces:

$$
Customer\rightarrow Subscription.
$$

or changes the meaning of `Customer`.

Ontology evolution is therefore more profound than schema evolution.

$$
SchemaChange\not\Rightarrow OntologyChange.
$$

And:

$$
OntologyChange\not\Rightarrow SchemaChange.
$$

---

# 460.18 Example: ontology change without schema change

Database:

```text id="3o6l8b"
person
role
status
```

remains unchanged.

But the organization changes the meaning of:

```text
role = "Architect"
```

from:

> technical design authority

to:

> advisory technical role without approval authority.

Same schema.

Different semantics.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SemanticEvolution\ can\ occur\ without\ SchemaEvolution.
}
$$

---

# 460.19 Contract evolution

### Definition

A **contract** is a declared set of semantic expectations, constraints, interpretations or transition rules.

**Contract evolution** means the contract itself changes:

$$
\Gamma_t\rightarrow\Gamma_{t+1}.
$$

Example:

Before:

$$
ReportsTo(x,y)
$$

is allowed to have one direct manager.

Later:

$$
ReportsTo(x,y_1)
\land
ReportsTo(x,y_2)
$$

is allowed for matrix organizations.

The data may not change.

The semantics of valid structure changed.

This is extremely important.

---

# 460.20 Contract change versus data change

We can have:

$$
Data_t=Data_{t+1}
$$

while:

$$
\Gamma_t\neq\Gamma_{t+1}.
$$

Then:

$$
Eval_{\Gamma_t}(K,r)
\neq
Eval_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(K,r).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Same\ data\ +\ different\ contract
\Rightarrow
different\ semantic\ evaluation.
}
$$

This explains why historical replay must preserve policy/contract versions.

---

# 460.21 Model evolution

### Definition

**Model evolution** means a change to a computational/statistical/ML model.

$$
M_t\rightarrow M_{t+1}.
$$

Examples:

* new parameters;
* new training data;
* new architecture;
* new feature set;
* new calibration;
* new algorithm.

Model evolution is distinct from knowledge evolution.

$$
ModelChange\neq KnowledgeChange.
$$

---

# 460.22 Model drift

### Definition

**Model drift** can refer to degradation or change in model behavior over time, often due to changing data or environment.

We should distinguish it from:

* data drift;
* concept drift;
* model version change.

A model can change deliberately without drifting.

Therefore:

$$
ModelEvolution\neq ModelDrift.
$$

---

# 460.23 Data drift

### Definition

**Data drift** means the statistical distribution of observed input data changes.

For example:

$$
P_t(X)\neq P_{t+1}(X).
$$

A model can remain unchanged while data drift occurs.

Therefore:

$$
DataDrift\neq ModelChange.
$$

---

# 460.24 Concept drift

### Definition

**Concept drift** means the relationship between inputs and target changes:

$$
P_t(Y|X)\neq P_{t+1}(Y|X).
$$

Example:

A fraud-detection model learned:

$$
P(Fraud|Pattern)
$$

under old attacker behavior.

Attackers change behavior.

The relationship changes.

This is concept drift.

---

# 460.25 Data drift versus concept drift

A useful distinction:

$$
P(X)\text{ changes}
$$

may occur without:

$$
P(Y|X)\text{ changing}.
$$

Conversely:

$$
P(Y|X)
$$

may change even when:

$$
P(X)
$$

looks stable.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
DataDrift\neq ConceptDrift.
}
$$

This is a direct extension of Step 405 and Step 438.

---

# 460.26 Structural drift

### Definition

**Structural drift** means that the structure or relation pattern of a domain changes over time.

For example:

$$
ReportsTo_t
\neq
ReportsTo_{t+1}.
$$

Or:

$$
DependencyGraph_t
\not\equiv
DependencyGraph_{t+1}.
$$

This can be detected through graph comparison.

---

# 460.27 Semantic drift

### Definition

**Semantic drift** occurs when the meaning associated with concepts or relations changes over time.

For a relation \(R\):

$$
\mathsf{Sem}_t(R)\neq\mathsf{Sem}_{t+1}(R).
$$

This is more dangerous than structural drift because the graph may look unchanged.

Example:

```text
GovernedBy(Nexus,CloudFirst)
```

remains exactly the same relation.

But the organization's interpretation of `CloudFirst` changes.

Then:

$$
Structure_t=Structure_{t+1}
$$

while:

$$
Meaning_t\neq Meaning_{t+1}.
$$

---

# 460.28 Semantic drift detection

KnowledgeOS should therefore monitor:

$$
\Delta_{sem}
=
Diff(\Gamma_t,\Gamma_{t+1}).
$$

Possible signals:

* changed definitions;
* changed policy wording;
* changed relation constraints;
* changed authority;
* changed applicability;
* changed interpretation contract.

ML can help identify candidate semantic changes through:

* embedding comparison;
* terminology change detection;
* semantic similarity;
* clause comparison;
* topic modeling;
* NLI.

But ML must not decide authoritative semantic change automatically.

---

# 460.29 Identity-preserving change

### Definition

A change is **identity-preserving** when the identity criterion continues to identify the same entity across the change.

$$
IC_\Gamma(x_t,x_{t+1})=True.
$$

Example:

A server receives:

* new RAM;
* new disk;
* new operating system.

Its service identity may remain unchanged.

Thus:

$$
StateChange\land IdentityPreserved.
$$

---

# 460.30 Identity-breaking change

### Definition

A change is **identity-breaking** when the declared identity criterion no longer considers the later entity the same entity.

$$
IC_\Gamma(x_t,x_{t+1})=False.
$$

Example:

A legal company is dissolved and a completely new legal entity is created.

The technical database may reuse a similar identifier.

But:

$$
IdentifierReuse\neq IdentityContinuity.
$$

---

# 460.31 Identity ambiguity

Sometimes:

$$
IC_\Gamma
$$

cannot determine whether identity persists.

Then:

$$
IC_\Gamma(x_t,x_{t+1})=Unknown.
$$

This should not be forced into:

$$
True
$$

or:

$$
False.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
IdentityAssessment
\in\{Same,Different,Unknown\}
}
$$

under an appropriate regime.

This connects correspondence uncertainty to identity continuity.

---

# 460.32 Version

### Definition

A **version** is a distinguishable state/representation/model/contract instance associated with an ordered or otherwise related evolution sequence.

$$
Version(M_t)=v_t.
$$

Version identity is not necessarily entity identity.

For example:

$$
Software_v1
$$

and:

$$
Software_v2
$$

may be versions of the same software product.

Therefore:

$$
Version\neq Identity.
$$

---

# 460.33 Version lineage

### Definition

**Version lineage** records how one version derives from another.

$$
DerivedFrom(v_2,v_1).
$$

It may include:

* predecessor;
* successor;
* branch;
* merge;
* reason;
* author;
* model;
* contract.

This is already naturally representable using relation instances.

---

# 460.34 Branch

### Definition

A **branch** is an alternative evolution path from a common predecessor.

$$
v_0\rightarrow v_1
$$

and:

$$
v_0\rightarrow v_2.
$$

Then:

$$
v_1\neq v_2.
$$

Branching is not necessarily conflict.

For example:

```text
Product v1
 ├── Cloud edition
 └── On-prem edition
```

Both can be valid.

---

# 460.35 Merge

A **merge** combines branches or structures under a transformation/merge contract.

$$
Merge(A,B)\rightarrow C.
$$

But merge may:

* preserve both;
* resolve conflict;
* discard information;
* create a third representation.

Therefore:

$$
Merge\neq Union
$$

universally.

This reinforces Step 384.

---

# 460.36 Change-point

### Definition

A **change-point** is a time at which the statistical or structural generating regime changes.

For a time series:

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_t
$$

we may detect:

$$
P(X_{1:t_0})\neq P(X_{t_0+1:n}).
$$

In ML/statistics this is useful for:

* drift monitoring;
* fraud detection;
* organizational changes;
* policy changes.

But statistical change-point detection does not itself identify the semantic cause.

Thus:

$$
StatisticalChangePoint\neq SemanticChangeCause.
$$

---

# 460.37 Change detection versus change explanation

This distinction is extremely important.

### Change detection

> Something changed.

### Change explanation

> Why did it change?

### Change attribution

> Which actor/process/event caused it?

These are different tasks.

$$
Detection\neq Explanation\neq CausalAttribution.
$$

KnowledgeOS must preserve the distinction.

---

# 460.38 Example: Nexus

Suppose KnowledgeOS detects:

$$
CloudMaturity_t
\neq
CloudMaturity_{t+1}.
$$

This establishes change.

It does not establish:

> "The Enterprise Architect caused the change."

That requires causal/governance analysis.

Therefore:

$$
ChangeDetection\not\Rightarrow Responsibility.
$$

This directly connects to Step 433.

---

# 460.39 Change impact

### Definition

**Change impact** means the set of structures, behaviors, decisions or requirements potentially affected by a change.

If:

$$
A\rightarrow B
$$

and:

$$
B\rightarrow C,
$$

a change to \(A\) may affect \(C\).

Impact analysis can use dependency closure:

$$
Impact(A)=Reachability_{DependsOn}(A).
$$

But:

$$
Dependency\neq Causality.
$$

So this is impact under dependency semantics, not proof of causal effect.

---

# 460.40 Change propagation

### Definition

**Change propagation** means that a change in one structure causes or requires corresponding changes elsewhere under an explicit dependency/contract.

Example:

```text id="v7y5nq"
Schema change
      ↓
API change
      ↓
Client change
      ↓
Test change
```

A dependency contract may state:

$$
SchemaChange\Rightarrow APIReview.
$$

Again, this is a semantic rule.

---

# 460.41 Change propagation versus causal propagation

A required engineering response:

$$
SchemaChange\rightarrow ReviewRequired
$$

is a governance/constraint relation.

It does not mean the schema change physically causes the review in a causal-science sense.

Therefore:

$$
ChangePropagation\neq CausalPropagation.
$$

---

# 460.42 Regression

### Definition

A **regression** occurs when a change causes a previously satisfied property to fail under the relevant test/evaluation contract.

$$
Sat_{\Gamma_t}(K_t,r)
$$

but after change:

$$
\neg Sat_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(K_{t+1},r).
$$

However, because universal \(Sat\) remains unresolved, regression is always relative to a concrete evaluation regime.

This is an important practical use of the Gate B boundary.

---

# 460.43 Semantic regression

### Definition

A **semantic regression** occurs when a change violates previously preserved meaning or required semantic behavior.

Example:

A migration changes:

```text
Customer
```

from:

> contractual customer

to:

> any registered user.

The schema may still pass all database tests.

Yet semantic behavior regressed.

This is why:

$$
Testing\neq SemanticAssurance.
$$

---

# 460.44 Compatibility across versions

For versions:

$$
V_1,V_2
$$

we can define:

$$
Compat_\Gamma(V_1,V_2).
$$

But compatibility can be directional.

For example:

$$
Compat_{read}(V_1,V_2)
$$

may hold while:

$$
Compat_{write}(V_1,V_2)
$$

does not.

Therefore compatibility is not one scalar.

---

# 460.45 Version compatibility matrix

A practical KnowledgeOS artifact can be:

| Dimension  | v1 → v2 | v2 → v1 |
| ---------- | ------: | ------: |
| Read       |     Yes |     Yes |
| Write      |     Yes |      No |
| Semantic   |     Yes | Partial |
| Temporal   |     Yes |     Yes |
| Governance | Unknown |      No |

This is much more informative than:

```text
Compatible = true
```

G_E=(V,R_E).

where:

* \(V\) = versions/states;
* \(R_E\) = evolution relations such as:

  * predecessor;

Again:

$$
EvolutionGraph
$$

is a projection.

No new Kernel primitive.

# 460.48 Can "Change" be reduced?

Yes.

Suppose:

$$
K_t
$$

and:

$$
K_{t+1}.
$$

A change detector computes:

$$
Diff_\Gamma(K_t,K_{t+1}).
$$

The result itself can be represented:

$$
d=(IID,\rho_{Change},K_t,K_{t+1},\Gamma).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Change\subseteq Inst(\mathcal R^\star).
}
$$

The semantic meaning of change is supplied by \(\Gamma\).

### Verdict

**PASS — Change Reduction.**

# 460.49 The difficult question: what causes identity to persist?

Step 456 established:

$$
IdentityCriterion\ determines\ Persistence.
$$

Step 460 strengthens this.

For an entity \(x\):

$$
Persist_\Gamma(x,t_1,t_2)
$$

is evaluated by:

$$
IC_\Gamma(x_{t_1},x_{t_2}).
$$

Different domains may use:

* material continuity;
* legal continuity;
* functional continuity;
* organizational continuity;
* identifier continuity;
* lineage continuity.

None is universal.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Persistence\ is\ contract-relative.
}
$$

# 460.50 The four-dimensional change matrix

We can now classify changes across four independent dimensions:

$$
\boxed{
C=(Representation,Structure,Semantics,Identity)
}
$$

For a transition \(t\rightarrow t+1\):

| Representation | Structure | Semantics | Identity  | Interpretation        |
| -------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------------------- |
| same           | same      | same      | same      | stable                |
| different      | same      | same      | same      | representation change |
| same           | different | same      | same      | structural evolution  |
| same           | same      | different | same      | semantic drift        |
| different      | different | same      | same      | structural migration  |
| different      | same      | different | same      | semantic re-encoding  |
| different      | different | different | same      | broad evolution       |
| different      | different | different | different | identity transition   |

This is far superior to a single `changed=true` field.

---

# 460.51 A fifth dimension: behavior

For executable systems we should add:

$$
Behavior.
$$

Then:

$$
C=(R,S,M,I,B)
$$

where:

* \(R\) = representation;
* \(S\) = structure;
* \(M\) = meaning;
* \(I\) = identity;
* \(B\) = behavior.

This explains why two software versions can be:

$$
RepresentationDifferent
$$

but:

$$
BehaviorEquivalent.
$$

Or:

$$
RepresentationSame
$$

but:

$$
BehaviorDifferent.
$$

# 460.52 A sixth dimension: contract

A still deeper dimension is:

$$
\Gamma.
$$

Because the meaning of the other dimensions depends on the contract.

Thus the full change analysis is:

$$
\boxed{
ChangeAssessment
=
F(
ID,
Structure,
Representation,
Behavior,
Semantics,

}
$$

This is a derived analytical function.

A normal PC can implement multiple change detectors.

### Statistical

* CUSUM;
* Page-Hinkley;
* Bayesian change-point methods;
* distribution-distance tests.

### Structural

* graph edit distance;
* graph matching;
* relation-set diff;
* degree/distribution comparison.

### Semantic

* embedding drift;
* terminology drift;
* NLI;
* semantic diff;
* document version comparison.

### ML

* autoencoders;
* anomaly detection;
* concept-drift detectors;
* sequence models.

But every detector produces:

$$
CandidateChange.
$$

It does not automatically establish:

$$
SemanticChange.
$$

# 460.54 Example of ML false alarm

Suppose policy text changes:

> "Cloud-first strategy"

to:

> "cloud first strategy."

Embedding or textual comparison detects a difference.

But semantically:

$$
Meaning_t\equiv Meaning_{t+1}.
$$

This is a representation change, not semantic drift.

Conversely, a tiny textual modification such as:

> "must use cloud"

to:

> "should evaluate cloud"

may produce a small textual difference but a **major normative semantic change**.

Therefore:

$$
TextualDistance\not\propto SemanticImpact.
$$

This is an important ML principle.

P(SemanticImpact>0\mid\Delta Text).

But the result is probabilistic.

For governance:

$$
NormativeImpact
$$

may require authoritative interpretation.

Therefore:

$$
MLScore\rightarrow CandidateImpact
$$

not:

$$
MLScore\rightarrow BindingPolicyChange.
$$

# 460.56 Change impact graph

KnowledgeOS can create:

```text id="5by7si"
Changed Relation
       │
       ├── affects → Requirement
       ├── affects → Model
       ├── affects → Decision
       ├── affects → Authorization
       └── affects → Test
```

# 460.62 Temporal semantic leakage

### Definition

**Temporal semantic leakage** occurs when later semantic knowledge, policies, models or definitions are incorrectly used to interpret an earlier state as though they were already available.

Example:

A decision in 2025 was made under:

$$
Policy_{2025}.
$$

In 2026 the policy changes.

Replaying the 2025 decision using:

$$
Policy_{2026}
$$

is not historical replay.

It is retrospective reassessment.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
HistoricalReplay\neq RetrospectiveReassessment.
}
$$
