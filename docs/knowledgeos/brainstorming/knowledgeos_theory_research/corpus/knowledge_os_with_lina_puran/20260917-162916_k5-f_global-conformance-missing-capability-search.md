# K5-F — Global Conformance and Missing-Capability Search

## F.0 Research question

Our current candidate semantic generator family is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G=\{ER,H,U,D\}
}
$$

with

$$
ER=(I,C,X,V,\rho)
$$

and

$$
H=(SH,EH,P).
$$

The question is:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G\stackrel{?}{\models}\mathcal I
}
$$

where \(\mathcal I\) is the currently validated KnowledgeOS invariant set.

But "models" must be made precise.

For every invariant \(i\), we ask:

$$
\exists R_i,\ RC_i:
R_i(\mathcal G,Q,EC)\models i
$$

where \(RC_i\) is an explicit reconstruction contract.

We classify the result:

1. **D — Directly represented**
2. **R — Reconstructible under explicit contract**
3. **E — Externalizable but semantically anchored**
4. **M — Missing capability**
5. **B — Blocked by unresolved formalism**
6. **N — Not a generator-level invariant**

This avoids falsely treating every concept as a Kernel component.

# F.1 The validated invariant families

We should use the strongest existing invariant set rather than inventing new invariants.

### K1 — Typed values / explicit unresolved state

$$
Value\neq Unknown
$$

and an unresolved condition must not be silently represented as an ordinary value.

### K2 — Stable dimension identity

A dimension must retain its semantic identity independently of its current value.

### K3 — Explicit inquiry/context

Adequacy is relative to:

$$
Q=(Target,Purpose,Context,Requirements,Constraints).
$$

### K4 — Requirement-relative faithfulness

A representation may compress information provided that the distinctions required by the inquiry are preserved.

Informally:

$$
\ker(\rho)\subseteq \sim_{req}.
$$

### K5 — Transformation safety

After transformation \(T\):

$$
\ker(T\circ\rho)\subseteq\sim_{req}.
$$

### K6 — Epistemic separation

At minimum:

$$
Value\neq Uncertainty\neq Unknown
$$

and:

$$
Unknown\neq False.
$$

# F.3 Test the major separation invariants

Now we use the stronger set of distinctions accumulated during the earlier experiments.

## Reality ≠ Observation

This is not generated by probability or epistemic state alone.

KnowledgeOS must preserve the distinction between:

$$
Reality
$$

and:

$$
Observation.
$$

Our candidate can preserve this because \(ER\) distinguishes epistemic relations, e.g.

$$
Observed(a,p,x,V)
$$

from

$$
Knows(a,p,x,V).
$$

But this does **not** mean ER represents Reality itself.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Representation\neq Reality}
$$

is preserved.

**Verdict: R.**

---

# F.4 Observation ≠ Evidence

Consider:

$$
Observed(a,o)
$$

versus:

$$
Evidence(e,h).
$$

An observation may become evidence under an evidential regime, but the concepts are not identical.

Thus the relation type \(\rho\) can preserve:

$$
Observed\neq Evidence.
$$

However, the actual evidential semantics belong to an external assessment regime:

$$
EA(e,h,H_Q,M,S,C).
$$

Therefore:

$$
ER+\text{external evidence regime}
$$

can preserve the distinction.

**Verdict: E/R.**

This is an important pattern:

> A semantic distinction can be preserved without the Kernel owning the mathematical regime that evaluates it.

---

# F.5 Evidence ≠ Interpretation

Similarly:

$$
Evidence\neq Interpretation.
$$

Evidence concerns evidential status; interpretation assigns semantic meaning.

A typed \(\rho\) can distinguish:

$$
Evidence
$$

from:

$$
Interpreted.
$$

But the interpretation mechanism itself may belong to a domain-specific context.

**Verdict: R/E.**

---

# F.6 Interpretation ≠ Hypothesis

Again:

$$
Interpretation\neq Hypothesis.
$$

A hypothesis is a candidate proposition/model under consideration.

The relation type can preserve this distinction.

But hypothesis-space semantics:

$$
H_Q
$$

are inquiry/regime dependent.

**Verdict: R/E.**

---

# F.7 Hypothesis ≠ Determination

This is where our previous work becomes decisive.

We have:

$$
Det(E_t,Q_t,C_t,S_t)=A_t\subseteq H_Q.
$$

Therefore:

$$
Hypothesis\neq Determination.
$$

More strongly:

$$
|A_t|=0,1,>1
$$

has meaning that cannot be captured merely by changing the epistemic relation type.

Therefore we must classify Determination separately.

$$
\boxed{
Determination\notin ER
}
$$

But can it be generated by an operator?

Potentially:

$$
Det:
(E_t,Q_t,C_t,S_t)\rightarrow A_t.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Determination=\text{semantic operation/result, not generator}
}
$$

for the current model.

**Verdict: N — not a generator-level primitive.**

This is not a failure.

It is an architectural separation.

---

# F.8 Determination ≠ Knowledge

This distinction is even more important.

Suppose:

$$
Det(E,Q,C,S)=\{H_1\}.
$$

We have unique determination.

But:

$$
UniqueDetermination
\not\Rightarrow
Knowledge.
$$

Why?

Because determination can be conditional upon:

* an incomplete evidence base,
* a model,
* institutional rules,
* standards,
* assumptions.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Determination\neq Knowledge.
}
$$

Knowledge requires its own factive epistemic semantics.

The factivity constraint remains:

$$
Knows(a,p,c,t)\Rightarrow True(p,c,t).
$$

So the Knowledge relation can live in \(ER\), while determination remains a separate operation/result.

---

# F.9 Belief ≠ Knowledge

This is cleanly preserved:

$$
Believes(a,p)
\not\Rightarrow
True(p)
$$

while:

$$
Knows(a,p,c,t)
\Rightarrow
True(p,c,t).
$$

Therefore typed \(\rho\) is doing real semantic work.

This strengthens the previous K5-D′ result:

$$
\boxed{
ER\text{ must be typed and law-bearing.}
}
$$

An untyped generic relation is insufficient.

# F.10 Rejection ≠ False

Likewise:

$$
Rejects(a,p)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg True(p).
$$

So:

$$
Rejects
$$

is an epistemic relation, not a truth predicate.

Again:

$$
ER
$$

can preserve this distinction.

**Verdict: D.**

# F.11 Probability ≠ Truth

Our earlier probability experiments already establish:

$$
P(H)=0.5
$$

does not determine whether:

$$
True(H)
$$

holds.

Thus:

$$
U
$$

cannot replace truth semantics.

This gives:

$$
\boxed{
U\nRightarrow Truth.
}
$$

And conversely:

$$
Truth\nRightarrow U.
$$

A proposition can be true without a probability model being attached to it.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Truth\notin\mathcal G.
}
$$

It remains an external semantic constraint/regime.

# F.12 Probability ≠ Evidence Weight

Likewise:

$$
P(H)
$$

is not automatically evidence weight.

For example, Bayesian updating can relate evidence and probability, but that is one mathematical regime:

$$
P(H|e).
$$

Another regime could use:

$$
LR(e;H_1,H_2)

or qualitative evidential assessment.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Probability\text{ is an implementation of }U,
\text{ not }U\text{ itself.}
}
$$

# F.13 Information Quantity ≠ Evidence Weight

An information-theoretic quantity such as:

$$
I(X;Y)
$$

does not automatically tell us whether \(Y\) is evidence for a hypothesis.

Likewise:

$$
H(X)
$$

does not equal evidential strength.

Therefore:

$$
InformationQuantity
\neq
EvidenceWeight.
$$

This is preserved because the mathematical regime is external to the semantic generators.

# F.16 Transition ≠ State History

The states can be identical while transition semantics differ.

\boxed{

Conversely, events may fail to encode every state detail:

$$

Thus H cannot safely collapse into either snapshots or events.

# F.17 Temporal validity ≠ occurrence time

# F.24 The missing-capability test

Suppose an invariant \(i\) cannot be reconstructed.

### Case A — Missing generator

$$
\mathcal G\nrightarrow i

because semantic information is absent.

### Case B — Missing operator

### Case C — Missing contract

The representation is sufficient, but no explicit:

RC

### Case D — External regime

The capability is intentionally delegated to a domain-specific mathematical or institutional regime.

### Case E — Invalid invariant

The supposed invariant itself has not been sufficiently validated.

This five-way classification prevents uncontrolled ontology growth.
