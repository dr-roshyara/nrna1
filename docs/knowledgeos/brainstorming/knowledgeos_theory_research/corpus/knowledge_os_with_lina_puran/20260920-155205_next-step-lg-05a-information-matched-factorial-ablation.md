# 1. The precise research question

> Does cohomology help KnowledgeOS?

We now replace it with four separate questions:

### Q1 — Decision capability

Can B3 determine whether a configuration is globally realizable?

### Q2 — Diagnostic capability

### Q3 — Representation capability

### Q4 — Computational capability

Can B3 perform some task more efficiently or scalably than an information-matched non-cohomological solver?

These are four different hypotheses.

# 2. Definitions

## Constraint

A **constraint** is a condition that a valid configuration must satisfy.

Example:

$$
A\oplus B=1.
$$

It says that \(A\) and \(B\) must have different binary states.

---

## Configuration

A **configuration** is an assignment of values to the relevant KnowledgeOS variables.

For example:

$$
x=(A,B,C)=(0,1,0).
$$

---

## Realizability

A configuration of constraints is **globally realizable** if there exists an assignment satisfying all constraints simultaneously.

Formally:

$$
Realizable(C)
\iff
\exists x:\;x\models C.
$$

---

## Local compatibility

A constraint assignment is locally compatible when it satisfies the compatibility conditions imposed by the local/higher-order structure.

In our cellular model:

$$
d^1b=0.
$$

---

## Global obstruction

A **global obstruction** occurs when all specified local compatibility conditions are satisfied, but no global realization exists.

For our model:

$$
b\in Z^1
$$

but:

$$
b\notin B^1.
$$

Therefore:

$$
[b]\neq0\in H^1.
$$

---

## Cohomology

For the current finite linear model:

$$
H^1=Z^1/B^1.
$$

where:

* \(Z^1\) = cocycles, locally compatible edge assignments;
* \(B^1\) = coboundaries, assignments generated by a global vertex state;
* \(H^1\) = equivalence classes of locally compatible assignments that cannot be generated globally.

The experiment demonstrates:

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Local\ incompatibility\neq Global\ obstruction.
}
$$

For example, consider:

$$
b=(1,1,1).
$$

### Filled triangle

$$
d^1b=1+1+1=1\pmod2.
$$

Therefore:

$$
b\notin Z^1.
$$

Diagnosis:

$$
LOCAL\_INCOMPATIBILITY.
$$

---

### Triangle with hole

There is no 2-cell, so:

$$
d^1=0.
$$

Thus:

$$
b\in Z^1.
$$

But:

$$
b\notin B^1.
$$

Therefore:

$$
[b]\neq0.
$$

Diagnosis:

$$
GLOBAL\_OBSTRUCTION.
$$

Yet both systems are globally UNSAT.

This gives us the important distinction:

$$
\boxed{
UNSAT\neq SemanticDiagnosis.
}
$$

Instead of storing a large conflict explanation, we might store:

### Compositionality

### Generalization

The same mathematical machinery may work across different complexes without designing a new diagnostic rule for each topology.

### Complexity

There may be cases where computing a cohomological invariant is cheaper than solving the corresponding global problem.

These are now the **real hypotheses** we should test.

# 12. New metric: Witness Size

A **witness** is a concrete object demonstrating a computational result.

For SAT:

$$

$$

For UNSAT:

$$

$$

can serve as a certificate/witness.

For cohomology:

$$

$$

can represent the obstruction.

Define:

$$
WS=
\text{size of the validated explanation/certificate}.
$$

Then ask:

$$
WS_{B3}<WS_{B2'''}
?
$$

If yes, cohomology may have a **representation advantage** even when it has no decision advantage.

# 13. New metric: Structural Sensitivity

This is particularly important for ML.

Suppose two instances have almost identical text:

$$
SemanticSimilarity(X_1,X_2)\approx1
$$

but different topology:

$$
Topology(X_1)\neq Topology(X_2).
$$

A structurally sensitive system must detect that difference.

Define:

$$
StructuralSensitivity
=
P(
\hat Y(X_1)\neq\hat Y(X_2)
\mid
SemanticSimilarity(X_1,X_2)\approx1
).
$$

This is a good adversarial ML benchmark.

# 17. A deeper discovery

There is a subtle mathematical point here.

For finite systems, almost anything computable by cohomology can in principle often be computed by another algorithm.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Mathematical\ expressiveness
\neq
Computational\ necessity.
}
$$

This is extremely important for KnowledgeOS.

We should not say:

> “Cohomology expresses the problem, therefore KnowledgeOS needs cohomology.”

Instead:

$$
\text{Does cohomology provide a capability that matters operationally?}
$$

That is the correct engineering question.
