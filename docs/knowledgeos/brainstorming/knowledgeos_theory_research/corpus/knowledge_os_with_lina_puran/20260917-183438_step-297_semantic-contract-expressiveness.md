# Step 297 — Semantic Contract Expressiveness

## 297.1 The problem

We currently have:

$$
r=(iid,\rho,args)
$$

with:

$$
\rho=(\Sigma_\rho,\Lambda_\rho).
$$

If we permit:

$$
\Lambda_\rho=\text{arbitrary program},
$$

then every behavior can be encoded.

That would make the claim

$$
(ID,\mathcal R^\star)
$$

trivially sufficient.

But it would no longer tell us anything mathematically meaningful.

So we impose a **non-oracle requirement**:

> A relation contract must have a defined syntax and semantics whose behavior can be inspected independently of the particular implementation.

Formally, introduce a candidate contract language:

$$
\mathcal L_\Lambda.
$$

Then:

$$
\Lambda_\rho\in\mathcal L_\Lambda.
$$

The interpreter is:

$$
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket:
\mathcal L_\Lambda\rightarrow SemanticBehavior.
$$

The crucial constraint is:

$$
\boxed{
\llbracket\Lambda_\rho\rrbracket
\text{ must be defined independently of the implementation of }\rho.
}
$$

---

# 297.2 First candidate: precondition, postcondition, invariant

The smallest obvious language is:

$$
\mathcal L_0=
\{Pre,Post,Invariant\}.
$$

Can this express all required semantics?

Let's test it rather than assume it.

---

# 297.3 `Knows`

Suppose:

$$
Knows(a,p,c,v).
$$

The factivity requirement is:

$$
Knows(a,p,c,v)\Rightarrow True(p,c,v).
$$

Can:

$$
Pre/Post/Invariant
$$

express this?

Potentially:

$$
Invariant:
Knows(a,p,c,v)\Rightarrow True(p,c,v).
$$

So factivity can be represented as a semantic invariant **if truth is available in the relevant regime**.

But this reveals an important boundary:

$$
True(p,c,v)
$$

is not generated by the contract itself.

The contract asserts a requirement concerning truth; it does not provide a truth oracle.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Factivity\ is\ expressible\ as\ a\ contract\ constraint,
but\ Truth\ remains\ external.
}
$$

This is exactly consistent with the KnowledgeOS factivity invariant.

---

# 297.4 `Believes`

For:

$$
Believes(a,p,c,v)
$$

we explicitly do **not** impose:

$$
Believes(a,p,c,v)\Rightarrow True(p,c,v).
$$

Thus the relation can have a different contract.

This gives us a simple but important test:

$$
\Lambda_{Knows}\neq\Lambda_{Believes}.
$$

Yet both can belong to the same contract language:

$$
\Lambda_{Knows},\Lambda_{Believes}\in\mathcal L_\Lambda.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Different\ epistemic\ semantics
do\ not\ require\ different\ kernel\ mechanisms.
}
$$

---

# 297.5 `Supports`

Suppose:

$$
Supports(e,p).
$$

A basic contract might say:

$$
Pre:
e\text{ is admissible evidence}.
$$

and:

$$
Post:
Supports(e,p)\text{ is recorded}.
$$

But does support imply:

$$
True(p)?
$$

No.

Nor:

$$
Knows(a,p)?
$$

No.

Therefore the contract can preserve the distinction:

$$
EvidenceWeight\neq Truth
$$

and:

$$
Evidence\neq Knowledge.
$$

### Result

`Supports` is expressible without introducing a new primitive.

**PASS.**

---

# 297.6 `Contradicts`

Suppose:

$$
Contradicts(p,q).
$$

The contract can specify:

$$
Pre:
p,q\text{ are comparable under the relevant semantic regime}.
$$

and:

$$
Post:
Conflict(p,q).
$$

But what happens after contradiction?

Possibilities include:

$$
Keep(p,q)
$$

$$
Reject(p)
$$

$$
Reject(q)
$$

$$
MarkConflict(p,q)
$$

$$
RequireAdjudication(p,q).
$$

These are **not universally determined by the relation itself**.

Therefore:

$$
Contradicts
$$

can be represented by the kernel, while the consequences of contradiction may belong to a logical/governance regime.

This confirms:

$$
\boxed{
ConflictRepresentation\neq ConflictResolution.
}
$$

**PASS.**

---

# 297.7 `Retracts`

Now:

$$
Retracts(r_1,r_2).
$$

A contract can express:

$$
Pre:
Exists(r_2)
$$

and:

$$
Post:
Retracted(r_2).
$$

But it must also preserve:

$$
HistoricalExistence(r_2).
$$

Therefore:

$$
Retract(r_2)
\not\Rightarrow
Delete(r_2).
$$

This can be expressed using state/history invariants.

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
Retraction\ semantics\ fit\ the\ contract\ language.
}
$$

**PASS.**

---

# 297.8 `Supersedes`

Consider:

$$
Supersedes(r_2,r_1).
$$

The contract can specify:

$$
Pre:
r_1\text{ is supersedable}
$$

and:

$$
Post:
r_2\text{ supersedes }r_1.
$$

But:

$$
Supersedes(r_2,r_1)
\not\Rightarrow
False(r_1).
$$

Nor necessarily:

$$
Delete(r_1).
$$

Again, this is representable through relational semantics and lifecycle constraints.

**PASS.**

---

# 297.9 `Before`

Consider:

$$
Before(r_1,r_2).
$$

A relation contract can contain structural laws:

$$
\neg Before(r,r)
$$

and, where appropriate:

$$
Before(r_1,r_2)\land Before(r_2,r_3)
\Rightarrow
Before(r_1,r_3).
$$

But we must **not** impose transitivity universally.

For example, some temporal/causal relations may not satisfy exactly the same algebra.

Therefore:

$$
\Lambda_{Before}
$$

defines the appropriate order structure.

This is an important methodological result:

$$
\boxed{
Algebraic\ properties\ belong\ to\ relation\ contracts,
not\ automatically\ to\ the\ whole\ kernel.
}
$$

**PASS.**

---

# 297.10 `SourceOf`

Consider:

$$
SourceOf(S,r).
$$

The contract can express:

$$
SourceOf(S,r)\Rightarrow
S\text{ is a provenance source of }r.
$$

But provenance can become more complex:

$$
S
\rightarrow
e_1
\rightarrow
e_2
\rightarrow
r.
$$

If transitive lineage is required, that can be defined by a provenance regime.

Therefore the kernel need only preserve the relations and their contracts.

**PASS — representation.**

---

# 297.11 `Authorizes`

Consider:

$$
Authorizes(A,d).
$$

The relation can be represented.

But whether:

$$
Authorizes(A,d)
$$

is valid depends on a governance policy.

For example:

$$
Role(A)=BoardMember
$$

might be sufficient in one organization but not another.

Therefore the relation contract can contain policy references/constraints, but the full authorization regime should remain external.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
AuthorizationRepresentation\neq AuthorizationPolicy.
}
$$

**PASS — kernel representation; governance semantics external.**

We do not need separate primitive categories such as:

$$
RetractionLaw

\boxed{

is strongly supported.

# 297.13 But is Pre/Post/Invariant enough?

Here the first serious limitation appears.

Suppose we have:

$$
Supports(e,p)
$$

with an evidential weight:

$$
w(e,p).
$$

The weight might be computed using:

$$
w=
\log\frac{P(e|H_1)}{P(e|H_2)}.
$$

Can `Pre/Post/Invariant` itself compute this?

No.

The contract can say:

$$
Post:
Weight=EvidenceModel(e,p).
$$

But then:

$$
EvidenceModel
$$

is an external mathematical function.

That is acceptable.

Similarly:

$$
EU(d|K)
$$

can remain an external decision-theoretic operation.

Therefore we obtain:

$$
\boxed{
Contract\ can\ constrain\ and\ reference\ computation
without\ being\ the\ computation.
}
$$

This is exactly the boundary we need.

# 297.14 Three classes of semantic expression

The experiment therefore suggests three levels.

### Level 1 — Structural predicates

$$
Type,\ Identity,\ Exists,\ RefersTo,\ldots
$$

### Level 2 — Contract constraints

$$
Pre,\ Post,\ Invariant,\ RelationLaw.
$$

### Level 3 — External semantic functions

$$
Probability,
EvidenceAssessment,
Inference,
Optimization,
CausalModel,
DecisionTheory,\ldots
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\Lambda
\neq
\text{complete mathematics}.
}
$$

It is a **semantic contract language**, not a universal mathematical language.

# 297.15 This solves an important circularity

Without this separation, one might define:

$$
\Lambda_{Knowledge}
=
\text{“whatever makes the proposition knowledge.”}
$$

That is circular.

Instead, a contract must be constructed from explicit primitives and externally defined functions:

$$
\Lambda_\rho=
(
Signature,
Pre,
Post,
Invariant,
References
).
$$

The interpretation must be independently evaluable.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
PrimitiveTypes
\rightarrow
ContractSyntax
\rightarrow
ContractSemantics
\rightarrow
Transition/Projection
}
$$

rather than:

$$
Knowledge
\rightarrow
KnowledgeLaw
\rightarrow
Knowledge.
$$

# 297.16 Non-oracle criterion

We can now define a useful formal criterion.

A contract language \(\mathcal L_\Lambda\) is **non-oracular relative to \(\mathcal Q\)** if:

$$
\forall\Lambda\in\mathcal L_\Lambda:
$$

1. its syntax is finitely describable,
2. its semantic interpretation is defined independently,
3. its referenced external functions are explicitly typed,
4. its outputs are observable/reproducible under fixed versions,
5. no semantic predicate is defined merely by asserting the desired conclusion.

The fifth condition is particularly important.

For example:

### Invalid

$$
IsKnowledge(x)\iff x\text{ satisfies Knowledge}.
$$

This is circular.

### Valid candidate

$$
Knows(a,p,c,v)
\Rightarrow
Factivity(p,c,v)
$$

where `Factivity` has independently defined semantics.

# 297.17 Relation contract as a typed specification

We can therefore propose:

$$
\boxed{
\Lambda_\rho=
(\Sigma_\rho,
Pre_\rho,
Post_\rho,
Inv_\rho,
Ref_\rho)
}
$$

where:

* \(\Sigma_\rho\) — argument signature,
* \(Pre_\rho\) — admissibility,
* \(Post_\rho\) — transition/result requirements,
* \(Inv_\rho\) — persistent constraints,
* \(Ref_\rho\) — explicitly typed external semantic functions.

This is a **candidate normal form**, not yet frozen.

# 297.18 Why `Ref` matters

Consider:

$$
Assess(e,h).
$$

The contract may reference:

$$
EA(e,h,H,M,S,C).
$$

The kernel does not need to implement Bayesian statistics, likelihood ratios, TOPSIS, PROMETHEE, causal inference, etc.

Instead:

$$
Ref_\rho
$$

can identify the applicable external regime/model.

For example:

$$
EA_{Bayesian}
$$

or:

$$
EA_{Qualitative}.
$$

This preserves:

$$
\boxed{
MathematicalRegime\neq KnowledgeOntology.
}
$$

# 297.19 Determinism question

For reproducibility, we require that a fixed contract plus fixed external model produces deterministic interpretation where determinism is required.

For example:

$$
\llbracket\Lambda_\rho\rrbracket
(r,\Omega_v,EC_v,M_v)
\rightarrow
Result.
$$

Then:

$$
SameInputs+SameVersions
\Rightarrow
SameResult.
$$

This connects directly to Step 25K:

$$
K_t=
Derive(H_{\le t},\Omega_v,EC_v,M_v).
$$

Therefore contract semantics become part of the reproducibility chain.

# 297.20 But do all contracts need to be deterministic?

No.

This is another important statistical distinction.

A relation contract may reference:

$$
P(X)
$$

and produce a probabilistic result.

The underlying representation can still be reproducible while the regime's result is probabilistic.

Thus:

$$
DeterministicRepresentation
\neq
DeterministicWorld
\neq
DeterministicInference.
$$

The kernel should not force:

$$
Probability\rightarrow DeterministicValue.
$$

# 297.25 Expressiveness vs minimality

This distinction is now essential:

### Expressiveness test

$$
D\subseteq Expressible(\mathcal L_\Lambda)
$$

### Minimality test

$$
\forall c\in\mathcal L_\Lambda:
Expressible(\mathcal L_\Lambda-\{c\})
\neq D.
$$

We have largely established the first.

We have **not** established the second.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\Lambda
\text{ is currently an expressive candidate, not a proven minimal law algebra.}
}
$$

# Step 298 — Law Algebra Irreducibility Test

The next step should now be more rigorous than merely designing more syntax.

We should perform **controlled ablation of the contract language itself**:

$$
\Lambda_0=
\{
Signature,Pre,Post,Invariant,Reference
\}
$$

and test:

$$
-\Sigma
$$

$$
-Pre
$$

$$
-Post
$$

$$
-Invariant
$$

$$
-Reference
$$

followed by composite ablations:

$$
-(Pre+Post)
$$

$$
-(Post+Invariant)
$$

$$
-(Pre+Invariant).
$$

The key question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Which contract capabilities are semantically irreducible, and which are merely convenient syntax?}
}
$$

This is potentially the point where we discover that even

$$
Pre,\ Post,\ Invariant
$$

are not fundamental—but merely different projections of an even smaller **constraint/transition semantics**.

We should therefore resist freezing them.

The current research frontier is:

$$
\boxed{
(ID,\mathcal R^\star)
\rightarrow
\Lambda_\rho
\rightarrow
\text{minimal law algebra}
}
$$

and only after that should we return to the full closure question:

$$
\mathcal K\times Operation
\rightarrow
\mathcal K.
$$

This keeps the reduction mathematically honest and prevents the DDD model from hardening before the semantic basis is actually established.
