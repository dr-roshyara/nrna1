# Step 546 — Hidden, Noisy and Adversarial Dependency Discovery

I have read the attached material again before proceeding. The document is useful as a **candidate architecture-improvement specification**, but its strongest mathematical claims must remain hypotheses until they survive controlled counterexamples. In particular, the proposed dependency lattice, matroid, common-mode operator and regime-adapter formalism should not be promoted merely because they are elegant. 

Step 545 gave us an important baseline:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Typed Dependency Graph + Perturbation + Replay}
}
$$

already detects basic common dependencies.

Step 546 now attacks the much harder problem:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can KnowledgeOS discover dependencies when provenance is incomplete, noisy, contradictory, semantically disguised, or generated by ML?}
}
$$

This is the right point to test whether the proposed ML and advanced mathematical machinery actually earns its place.

---

# 546.1 The experimental hierarchy

We construct progressively more difficult information conditions:

$$
D_0=\text{Complete Provenance}
$$

$$
D_1=\text{Partial Provenance}
$$

$$
D_2=\text{Missing Provenance}
$$

$$
D_3=\text{Contradictory Provenance}
$$

$$
D_4=\text{Semantic Paraphrase}
$$

$$
D_5=\text{Temporal Drift}
$$

$$
D_6=\text{Hidden Transformation}
$$

$$
D_7=\text{Hidden ML/Data Dependency}.
$$

This gives us a controlled degradation ladder.

# 546.2 New terms

## 1. Provenance

**Provenance** is information describing the origin, derivation, transformation, custody, or history of an artifact, assertion, evidence item, or result.

Example:

$$
E_1
\xleftarrow{DerivedFrom}
Dataset_1.
$$

Provenance is not merely metadata. It can be epistemically relevant because it determines dependency structure.

---

## 2. Complete Provenance

**Complete Provenance** means that all dependency relationships relevant to the benchmark's declared dependency universe are explicitly represented.

$$
G_D=G_D^*.
$$

This is benchmark-relative.

---

## 3. Partial Provenance

Some true dependencies are represented and others are missing:

$$
E_D^{known}\subset E_D^*.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Known dependency graph}
\neq
\text{complete dependency graph}.
$$

---

## 4. Hidden Dependency

A **Hidden Dependency** exists in the reference world but is not explicitly exposed to the system.

$$
(x,y)\in E_D^*
$$

but:

$$
(x,y)\notin E_D^{observed}.
$$

---

## 5. Noisy Provenance

**Noisy Provenance** contains erroneous, ambiguous, incomplete, or conflicting provenance information.

Example:

```text
Report A → Dataset X
Report A → Dataset Y
```

while only X is actually correct.

---

## 6. Adversarial Case

An **Adversarial Case** is deliberately constructed to expose a predictable weakness in a system.

It does not necessarily mean malicious input.

Example:

> five documents use different wording but all derive from one hidden source.

---

## 7. Dependency Discovery

**Dependency Discovery** is the process of generating candidate dependency relationships not explicitly available in the observed provenance.

$$
Discovery:
X\rightarrow\widehat E_D.
$$

---

## 8. Dependency Validation

**Dependency Validation** determines whether a candidate dependency is sufficiently supported under a declared contract.

$$
Validate_\Gamma(\widehat e)
\rightarrow
\{Established,Refuted,Unresolved,Conflicted\}.
$$

We now have:

$$

$$

$$

ValidatedDependency

$$

These must not collapse.

The pipeline is:

$$
\boxed{

\rightarrow

$$

# 546.4 Why this matters for ML

Suppose an LLM says:

> Reports A, B and C appear to originate from the same source.

This is:

$$
CandidateDependency.
$$

It is **not**:

$$
Evidence.
$$

It is certainly not:

$$
Knowledge.
$$

The LLM has proposed a hypothesis.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
LLMOutput\rightarrow Candidate
}
$$

and not:

$$
LLMOutput\rightarrow Knowledge.
$$

This directly preserves our Epistemic Firewall.

# 546.6 Benchmark World D1 — Partial provenance

True:

```text id="g6xqjm"
S1 → E1
S2 → E2
S3 → E3
S4 → E4
S5 → E5
```

Observed:

```text id="v6s5ab"
S1 → E1
S2 → E2
S4 → E4
```

The system does not observe:

$$
S3\rightarrow E3
$$

or:

$$
S5\rightarrow E5.
$$

A correct system must not conclude:

$$
Independent(E3,E5).
$$

It can only conclude:

$$
DependencyStatus(E3,E5)=Unknown
$$

if independence has not separately been established.

# 546.7 Zero enters dependency discovery

This is an excellent application of the Zero Lens.

$$
\boxed{
MissingDependencyEvidence
\neq
DependencyAbsence.
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
NotObserved(S\rightarrow E)
$$

must not become:

$$
NoDependency(S,E).
$$

This is exactly analogous to:

$$
NoEvidence(A)\neq Evidence(\neg A).
$$

# 546.8 Benchmark World D2 — Completely missing provenance

Suppose:

```text id="pr8v1w"
Report A
Report B
Report C
Report D
Report E
```

No source information is available.

A naïve system might infer independence:

$$
Indep(A,B,C,D,E).
$$

That would be a serious logical error.

The correct result is:

$$
\boxed{
DependencyStatus=Unknown.
}
$$

unless other evidence establishes independence.

---

# 546.9 Important logical result

From:

$$
\neg DependsOn(x,y)
$$

we cannot infer:

$$
Independent(x,y)
$$

unless the dependency representation is complete under a closed-world contract.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
OpenWorldDependency:
NotKnownDependent\neq Independent.
}
$$

If an organization explicitly declares:

> all dependencies are recorded,

then we may use a closed-world interpretation.

That is a **contract**, not a universal truth.

# 546.10 New term: Dependency Completeness Contract

A **Dependency Completeness Contract** specifies whether the dependency representation is assumed complete for a defined scope.

$$
DCC=
(Scope,DependencyTypes,Sources,Time,CompletenessClaim,Validation).
$$

Example:

> All software build dependencies are recorded in the SBOM.

Then absence of a dependency can have a different meaning than when no such completeness claim exists.

This is a powerful connection to our earlier Requirement Completeness work.

# 546.11 Benchmark World D3 — Contradictory provenance

True:

$$
S\rightarrow E.
$$

Observed:

```text
Source A: E derived from S
Source B: E independently produced
```

We must preserve:

$$
Conflict(DependencyEvidence).
$$

Not:

$$
True
$$

and not:

$$
False.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
DependencyStatus=Conflicted.
}
$$

This is an important extension of our conflict-preservation principle.

# 546.12 Benchmark World D4 — Semantic paraphrase

Now the dependency is hidden linguistically.

Source:

> "Nexus repository currently has 512 GB available."

Report A:

> "The repository platform's available storage is approximately 512 gigabytes."

Report B:

> "Current free capacity of the artifact repository is 512 GB."

Lexically these differ.

Semantically they may represent the same observation.

The dependency is:

$$
Source\rightarrow
Observation\rightarrow
A,B.
$$

A lexical system may miss it.

An embedding system may detect similarity.

An LLM may infer the semantic correspondence.

But none of those alone establishes provenance.

---

# 546.13 ML architecture

We therefore use:

$$
Lexical
\rightarrow
Embedding
\rightarrow
LLM
$$

as progressively richer **candidate generators**.

For example:

$$
\hat P(SharedSource|A,B)
$$

may be estimated.

But:

$$
\hat P=0.94
$$

does not mean:

$$
SharedSource=True.
$$

It means:

$$
CandidateConfidence=0.94.
$$

---

# 546.14 New term: Candidate Confidence

**Candidate Confidence** is a model-dependent numerical assessment of how strongly a candidate generator predicts a relation.

$$
CC_\theta(x,y)
=
P_\theta(DependsOn(x,y)).
$$

It is not:

$$
TruthProbability
$$

unless a probabilistic interpretation and calibration contract explicitly establish that meaning.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CandidateConfidence\neq EpistemicCertainty.
}
$$

---

# 546.15 Calibration

If the ML system outputs:

$$
0.8
$$

then among candidates with approximately 0.8 confidence, roughly 80% should be correct under the calibration population and definition.

That is:

$$
P(Y=1\mid \hat P\approx0.8)\approx0.8.
$$

This is **calibration**, not correctness of any individual prediction.

Therefore we need:

$$
CalibrationCurve
$$

and:

$$
BrierScore
$$

or similar measures.

But these are evaluation tools, not KnowledgeOS semantics.

---

# 546.16 False dependency is asymmetric in consequence

Suppose the model falsely says:

$$
E_1\rightarrow E_2.
$$

KnowledgeOS may unnecessarily discount evidence.

But if the model falsely says:

$$
Independent(E_1,E_2)
$$

when they are actually dependent, it can produce:

$$
DoubleCounting
\rightarrow
Overconfidence
\rightarrow
WrongDetermination.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
FalseIndependence
}
$$

must receive explicit assurance treatment.

---

# 546.17 New term: Epistemic Cost of Error

The **Epistemic Cost of Error** measures the downstream consequence of a dependency-discovery error.

$$
ECE(e)=
Impact_\Gamma(e)
$$

where impact can include:

* evidence-weight distortion,
* determination change,
* decision change,
* governance consequence.

Thus:

$$
FP_D
$$

and:

$$
FN_D
$$

should not necessarily have equal costs.

---

# 546.18 This changes our ML objective

A conventional classifier optimizes:

$$
Loss_{classification}.
$$

KnowledgeOS may instead need:

$$
Loss_{epistemic}
=
\lambda_{FP}FP
+
\lambda_{FI}FI
+
\lambda_{FN}FN
+\cdots
$$

where the coefficients are contract-specific.

But these weights must **not** be universal.

This follows our earlier:

$$
NoUniversalUtilityFunction
$$

principle.

# 546.19 Benchmark World D5 — Temporal drift

Suppose:

```text
2026-09-15:
Dataset D1 → E1,E2,E3

2026-09-20:
Dataset D2 → E4,E5
```

A similarity-based model might group all five.

But temporal provenance shows:

$$
D1\neq D2.
$$

Therefore:

$$
TemporalSimilarity\neq SharedDependency.
$$

Conversely, two reports published months apart may both depend on the same frozen historical snapshot.

So:

$$
TimestampDifference
\not\Rightarrow
DependencyDifference.
$$

---

# 546.20 New term: Temporal Dependency

A **Temporal Dependency** is a dependency whose validity or effect depends on a temporal relation.

Example:

$$
E_t
\xrightarrow{BasedOn}
Snapshot_{t-1}.
$$

We must preserve:

$$
ValidTime
$$

and:

$$
ObservationTime.
$$

This connects dependency analysis directly to bitemporal semantics.

# 546.22 Benchmark World D7 — Hidden ML dependency

Now:

```text id="z6f9ks"
Dataset D
 ├── Model M1 → E1
 ├── Model M2 → E2
 ├── Model M3 → E3
 └── Model M4 → E4
```

The models are different.

Their predictions may even disagree.

Yet:

$$

Thus:

$$
PredictionDisagreement
\not\Rightarrow

This is extremely important for a future KnowledgeOS that uses multiple AI models.

# 546.23 A deeper ML issue

Suppose models are trained on overlapping datasets:

$$
D_1\cap D_2\neq\varnothing.
$$

Then:

$$
M_1\neq M_2
$$

but:

$$
DataDependency(M_1,M_2)
$$

may still exist.

Therefore dependency discovery must sometimes operate at:

$$
Model
\rightarrow Dataset
\rightarrow Source
$$

rather than only:

$$
Model\leftrightarrow Model.
$$

This is why a graph representation remains very useful.

# 546.24 Dependency hyperstructure

We encounter a potential problem.

Suppose:

$$
D
$$

is shared by:

$$
E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4.
$$

The dependency is conceptually one-to-many.

A binary graph represents this using multiple edges:

$$
D\rightarrow E_i.
$$

No problem.

But some dependency claims concern the **set as a whole**:

$$
Dependency(D,\{E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4\}).
$$

This could motivate a hypergraph.

---

# 546.25 Does this justify a hypergraph?

Not yet.

A hypergraph can itself be represented by:

$$
HyperedgeID
$$

plus typed relations:

$$
MemberOf(E_i,HyperedgeID).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Hypergraph\ capability
\subseteq
ReifiedTypedRelation\ capability.
}
$$

Again the Kernel does not expand.

---

# 546.26 New term: Dependency Cluster

A **Dependency Cluster** is a set of objects grouped because they share a specified dependency pattern.

$$
Cluster_\Gamma(E)
$$

Example:

$$
C_1=\{E_1,E_2,E_3\}
$$

because all depend on:

$$
DatasetD.
$$

A cluster is a derived object.

It is not necessarily an equivalence class.

---

# 546.27 Why cluster ≠ equivalence

Suppose:

$$
E_1,E_2
$$

share source S, while:

$$
E_2,E_3
$$

share model M.

Then:

$$
E_1\sim E_2
$$

and:

$$
E_2\sim E_3
$$

under different relations.

But:

$$
E_1\sim E_3
$$

does not necessarily hold.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
DependencySimilarity
\neq
UniversalEquivalence.
}
$$

# 546.29 Evaluation matrix

We should record:

| Metric                     | Meaning                                                             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dependency Precision       | correctness of discovered dependencies                              |
| Dependency Recall          | coverage of true dependencies                                       |
| False Dependency Rate      | invented dependencies                                               |
| False Independence Rate    | missed dependencies treated as independent                          |
| Abstention Rate            | unresolved cases                                                    |

# 546.32 Counterexample-driven evaluation

The most important test is not:

> How often is the model correct?

It is:

> Can we deliberately construct cases in which the model is wrong, and does the architecture contain the error?

This gives:

$$
Candidate
\rightarrow
Validation
\rightarrow
Abstention

$$

# 546.33 New term: Error Containment

**Error Containment** means that an incorrect candidate does not silently propagate into downstream epistemic state.

Formally:

$$
CandidateError
\nrightarrow
Knowledge
$$

unless an independent validation path accepts it.

This is one of the most important architectural properties of KnowledgeOS.

# 546.34 Error containment test

Inject a false dependency:

$$
\widehat{DependsOn}(E_1,S)
$$

where:

$$
DependsOn^*(E_1,S)=False.
$$

The system should produce:

$$
FalseDependency
$$

and prevent it from changing the authoritative dependency graph.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
ML\ error\ must\ be\ quarantinable.
}

# 546.35 New term: Candidate Quarantine

A **Candidate Quarantine** is a storage/state boundary in which unvalidated ML or heuristic outputs are retained without entering authoritative epistemic state.

Example:

```text id="0js9w0"
ML Candidate Store
       ↓
Validation
   ┌───┴────┐
   ↓        ↓
Accepted  Rejected
   ↓
Authoritative Graph
```

This is a strong DDD boundary.

# 546.36 DDD ownership

The **Candidate Discovery Context** owns:

* candidate generation,
* candidate confidence,
* candidate ranking,
* candidate provenance.

The **Dependency Context** owns:

* validated dependencies,
* dependency contracts,
* authoritative dependency graph,
* dependency status.

This is cleaner than allowing the ML context to write directly into the dependency graph.

# 546.37 Anti-Corruption Layer

An **Anti-Corruption Layer (ACL)** is a DDD translation boundary preventing an external model's vocabulary or semantics from contaminating the receiving bounded context.

Here:

$$
LLM/Embedding
\rightarrow
ACL
\rightarrow
DependencyCandidate.
$$

The ML model cannot directly mutate:

$$
DependencyGraph.
$$

This is an excellent concrete application of DDD.

# 546.42 Does the dependency lattice survive?

At this stage:

$$
Graph
$$

has successfully handled:

* hidden dependencies,
* common dependencies,
* transitive dependencies,
* dependency clusters,
* temporal relationships,
* transformations,
* model lineage.

A lattice has not yet demonstrated a unique capability.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
DependencyLattice=[PROP]
}
$$

and remains outside the minimum implementation.

---

# 546.43 Does the matroid survive?

The matroid is more interesting because higher-order independence can genuinely differ from pairwise relations.

But we have not yet demonstrated that the actual independence relation satisfies:

$$
Hereditary
$$

and:

$$
Exchange.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
IndependenceMatroid=[PROP]
}
$$

not a KnowledgeOS primitive.

---

# 546.44 Does category theory survive?

The regime-adapter abstraction is useful conceptually:

$$
T_{AB}.
$$

But we still have not demonstrated the categorical conditions required for:

$$
NaturalTransformation.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CategoryTheory=[RESEARCH\ REGIME].
}
$$

It may eventually provide an elegant formalization of interoperability, but it has not earned architectural necessity.

# 546.46 Dependency hypothesis

$$

$$

$$

$$

$$

may eventually establish:

$$

$$

This unifies dependency discovery with the general KnowledgeOS epistemic lifecycle.
