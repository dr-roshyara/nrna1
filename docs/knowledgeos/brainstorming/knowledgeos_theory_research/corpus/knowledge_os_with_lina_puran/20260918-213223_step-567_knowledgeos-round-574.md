## Mathematical Foundation Closure and the Mathematical Admission System

I have continued from Round 573 and re-read the attached roadmap section. The roadmap makes a very deliberate change at this point:

The proposed admission rule is:

$$
\boxed{

It also says that the next logical work is explicit logical-regime contracts, inference rules, proof/derivation objects, logical dependencies, harmony/stability, conservative extension, cross-regime translation, and proof-theoretic versus semantic validity.

We have accumulated many mathematical theories:

# 2. First define “mathematical theory”

A **Mathematical Theory** is a formally specified mathematical framework containing objects, definitions, axioms/assumptions, operations and valid results.

Examples:

$$
(\mathbb R,+,\cdot)
$$

for real arithmetic, or:

$$
(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)
$$

for a probability space.

KnowledgeOS does not automatically adopt all of the theory.

---

# 3. Define mathematical regime

A **Mathematical Regime** is a declared mathematical framework under which particular KnowledgeOS computations or assessments are valid.

For example:

$$
\Gamma_{prob}
=
(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)
$$

may be a probability regime.

A metric regime might contain:

$$
(X,\delta)
$$

with a declared distance structure.

A constructive regime may require:

$$
Witness+\Procedure+\ConstructiveRules.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MathematicalTheory\neq MathematicalRegime.
}
$$

The theory is the mathematical source.

The regime is its **explicit, bounded application inside KnowledgeOS**.

---

# 4. Define property

A **Property** is a mathematical characteristic supplied by a theory that can be used by KnowledgeOS.

Examples:

### Probability

$$
P(A)\in[0,1].
$$

### Metric

$$
\delta(x,y)\geq0.
$$

### Triangle inequality

$$
\delta(x,z)\leq\delta(x,y)+\delta(y,z).
$$

### Compactness

A property of a topological space under a specified definition.

### Constructive witness

A computable object demonstrating existence under a constructive regime.

The critical point:

$$
\boxed{
KnowledgeOS\ imports\ properties,\ not\ mathematics\ indiscriminately.
}
$$

---

# 5. Define applicability condition

An **Applicability Condition** specifies when a mathematical property may legitimately be used.

For example, statistical estimation may require assumptions about:

* sampling;
* estimator;
* population;
* independence;
* distribution;
* model;
* calibration.

Therefore:

$$
Theory\rightarrow Property
$$

is not enough.

We require:

$$
Theory
\rightarrow
Property
\rightarrow
ApplicabilityConditions.
$$

---

# 6. Define capability

A **KnowledgeOS Capability** is an operation or assessment that the system can perform because a mathematical property has been validly admitted.

For example:

$$

\rightarrow

\rightarrow
ValueOfInformation.
$$

Or:

$$

\rightarrow

\rightarrow

$$

Or:

$$

# 7. Define assurance

**Assurance** is evidence that the mathematical regime and its applicability conditions were satisfied sufficiently for the resulting capability.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Assurance\neq MathematicalResult.
}
$$

Example:

$$
\hat\theta=0.72
$$

is a result.

A certificate stating:

> estimator assumptions, scope, data conditions and model version were checked

is assurance.

# 25. Mathematical validity versus epistemic validity

This is a crucial distinction.

Suppose:

$$
x+y=y+x

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MathematicalValidity\neq EpistemicValidity.
}
$$

Likewise:

$$
StatisticalValidity\neq Truth.
$$

# 28. Logical regime

A **Logical Regime** specifies the logical language, inference rules, assumptions and semantics under which derivations are considered valid.

Symbolically:

\Gamma_L.
$$

Examples may include:

$$
\Gamma_{Classical}
$$

$$
\Gamma_{Constructive}.
$$

KnowledgeOS should not silently assume that every logical principle is universally available.

# 30. Define inference rule

An **Inference Rule** specifies how one or more premises can produce a conclusion under a logical regime.

Generic form:

$$
\frac{
\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n
}{
\psi
}
$$

under:

$$
\Gamma_L.
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{P\qquad P\rightarrow Q}{Q}.
$$

This is a derivation step.

---

# 31. Define derivation

A **Derivation** is a structured sequence/tree/DAG of inference steps leading from premises to a conclusion.

$$
D:
\{\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\}
\vdash_{\Gamma}
\psi.
$$

The derivation must preserve:

* premises;
* rules;
* order/dependencies;
* regime;
* assumptions;
* provenance.

---

# 32. Proof versus derivation

We should not immediately equate them.

A **Derivation** is a syntactic construction showing how a conclusion was generated.

A **Proof** is a derivation satisfying the proof conditions of the applicable logical system.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Derivation\neq Proof.
}
$$

A derivation may be malformed or use an invalid rule.

---

# 33. Proof-theoretic validity

A derivation is proof-theoretically valid if it conforms to the inference rules of the regime.

$$
Valid_{PT,\Gamma}(D).
$$

This answers:

> Was the conclusion derived according to the formal rules?

---

# 34. Semantic validity

Semantic validity asks whether the conclusion holds under the intended semantic interpretation/models.

$$
\models_\Gamma\varphi.
$$

This answers a different question.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
ProofTheoreticValidity\neq SemanticValidity.
}
$$

Under a sound logical system they may be related by a soundness theorem.

But KnowledgeOS should record which relation has actually been established.

---

# 35. Soundness

A logical system is **sound** for a semantics when:

$$
\vdash_\Gamma\varphi
\Rightarrow
\models_\Gamma\varphi.
$$

In words:

> everything formally provable is semantically valid.

This is a property of the logical regime.

KnowledgeOS must not assume it universally.

---

# 36. Completeness

A system is **complete** for a semantics when:

$$
\models_\Gamma\varphi
\Rightarrow
\vdash_\Gamma\varphi.
$$

Thus:

$$
Soundness\neq Completeness.
$$

And importantly:

$$
\boxed{
KnowledgeOS\ should\ not\ assume\ completeness\ merely\ because\ a\ logic\ is\ being\ used.
}
$$

---

# 37. This is directly relevant to KnowledgeOS

Suppose the system has:

$$
\models_\Gamma P
$$

but cannot construct:

$$
\vdash_\Gamma P.
$$

Then semantic truth may be available while formal derivability is not.

Conversely, in an unsound system, formal derivability would not establish semantic truth.

KnowledgeOS therefore needs to know **which kind of validity it possesses**.

# 42. Conservative extension

The roadmap explicitly identifies conservative extension as unresolved logical work.

A theory \(T_2\) is a **conservative extension** of \(T_1\) over language \(L_1\) when adding the new theory does not produce new consequences in \(L_1\) that were not already derivable in \(T_1\).

Informally:

$$
T_2\vdash\varphi
\Rightarrow
T_1\vdash\varphi
$$

for relevant:

$$
\varphi\in L_1.
$$

This is regime-specific.

---

# 43. Why KnowledgeOS cares

Suppose we extend a logical regime with a new inference mechanism.

We need to know whether old determinations change.

If:

$$
T_2
$$

is conservative over the relevant old language, then certain existing conclusions remain protected.

This could become a useful assurance property.

# 48. Regime coherence

We need to prevent impossible combinations.

For example, a mathematical capability may require:

$$

$$

$$

$$

$$
\boxed{
