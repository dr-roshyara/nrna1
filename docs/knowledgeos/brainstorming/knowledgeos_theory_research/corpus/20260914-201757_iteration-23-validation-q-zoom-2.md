## Part III — Q-L5-CATEGORY: Does L5 Extend to a Category?

### III.1 Precise statement

**L5 structures.**
- Three-sorted nodes: \(V = \mathcal{D} \sqcup \mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \sqcup \mathcal{H}\).
- Typed edges: \(E_{\text{rel}}, E_{\text{hyp}}, E_{\text{arg}}\).
- Evidential edges: \(E_{\mathrm{evid}} \subseteq E_{\text{hyp}}\), Heyting-valued at \(\tau\).

**Q-L5-CATEGORY.** Does the graph \(G = (V, E)\) (with edges as 1-cells and *paths* as composites) form a *category* \(\mathbf{G}\)? And how does \(\mathbf{G}\) relate to L4's \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)?

### III.2 Attempt 1 — \(G\) as a free category

**Candidate.** The *free category* on the graph \(G\): objects = nodes, morphisms = directed paths, composition = concatenation, identity = empty path.

**Analysis.** The free category \(\mathbf{Free}(G)\) is *always* well-defined for any directed graph. It is *canonical*.

**Falsification attempt.** Is \(\mathbf{Free}(G)\) *coherent* with the corpus?

- Nodes include *three sorts*: dimensions, inquiries, hypotheses.
- Edges are *typed*: \(E_{\text{rel}}\) connects dimensions to inquiries; \(E_{\text{hyp}}\) connects inquiries to hypotheses; \(E_{\text{arg}}\) connects hypotheses to dimension *pairs*.
- A *path* would be a sequence of typed edges. E.g., \(\mathcal{D} \to \mathbf{Inq} \to \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{D}^2\).

**Issue.** \(E_{\text{arg}}\) has *two* target dimensions (a *pair*), so \(G\) is not a *simple directed graph*; it is a *typed directed hypergraph*. The *free category* on a *hypergraph* is well-defined but *more complex* than on a simple graph.

**Verdict.** \(\mathbf{Free}(G)\) exists but requires *hypergraph* category theory.

### III.3 Attempt 2 — \(G\) as a *relational* structure, not a category

**Candidate.** Treat \(G\) as a *relational structure* \(\langle V; E_{\text{rel}}, E_{\text{hyp}}, E_{\text{arg}}\rangle\) with no *composite morphisms*.

**Analysis.** This is the *default* interpretation: a graph is a *set of nodes and edges*, not a category.

**Falsification attempt.** Does L5 require *composition*?

**Test via the Nexus example.** Consider the *path* \(Q \to h_{\text{CICD}} \to (\text{CICD}, \text{Egress})\). Does the corpus *derive* a composite morphism from \(Q\) to \((\text{CICD}, \text{Egress})\)?

**Analysis.** The composite would assert a *derived relationship* from the inquiry to the argument-pair. But the corpus does *not* derive such a relationship — the *evidential edge* \(Q \to h_{\text{CICD}}\) and the *argument edge* \(h_{\text{CICD}} \to (\text{CICD}, \text{Egress})\) are *distinct claims*, and their *composite* is not a *corpus-derived* claim.

**Verdict.** L5 is *not* a category in the corpus's derivation. It is a *relational structure*.

### III.4 Attempt 3 — \(G\) as a *category enriched in Heyting-valued relations*

**Candidate.** Elevate \(G\) to a *Heyting-enriched category* \(\mathbf{G}_\tau\) where:
- Objects are nodes.
- Hom-sets are *Heyting-valued relations* over \(E_{\mathrm{evid}}\) at threshold \(\tau\).

**Analysis.** This is *natural* given that evidential edges carry Heyting-valued strength (D57). But *other* edges (relational, argument) may *not* be Heyting-valued — they may be *binary*.

**Falsification.** Mixing Heyting-valued and binary edges in a *single* enriched category requires *canonical enrichment* of the binary edges to the Heyting level. This is *not forced* by the corpus.

**Verdict.** \(\mathbf{G}_\tau\) is *viable* but requires *additional* structure (enrichment of binary edges) not present in the corpus.

### III.5 Attempt 4 — \(G\) as a *2-sorted relational structure with a canonical Yoneda-style completion*

**Candidate.** Treat \(G\) as a relational structure; construct the *category of types* via Yoneda: each node \(v\) becomes the *presheaf* \(\text{Hom}(-, v)\).

**Analysis.** Yoneda *always* provides a canonical completion of a relational structure to a category. But the completion depends on the choice of *base category* — here, the *free category* \(\mathbf{Free}(G)\).

**Falsification.** If \(\mathbf{Free}(G)\) is the base, Yoneda gives \(\mathbf{Set}^{\mathbf{Free}(G)^{\mathrm{op}}}\). But the corpus does *not* require such completion. The *canonical* content of \(G\) is its *relational* structure.

**Verdict.** Yoneda completion is *canonical* but *over-constructs*. The corpus requires only the relational structure.

### III.6 The correct theorem

**Theorem (Q-L5-CATEGORY).** The Knowledge Graph \(G = (V, E)\) is canonically a **typed relational structure**, not a category. Specifically:

- **Nodes:** three-sorted \(V = \mathcal{D} \sqcup \mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \sqcup \mathcal{H}\).
- **Typed relations:** \(E_{\text{rel}}, E_{\text{hyp}}, E_{\text{arg}}\), plus the derived \(E_{\mathrm{evid}}\).
- **Evidential weight:** \(E_{\mathrm{evid}}\) edges carry Heyting-values at \(\tau\).
- **No canonical composition:** the corpus does not derive composite morphisms from \(G\)'s edges.

**Coherence with L4.** \(G\) is *not equivalent to* \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\). It is a *representation of the determined portion* of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\):
- \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects \((K_t, D_Q)\) project to \(G\) via \(D_Q \mapsto (Q, h, \text{evid-edge}(h, Q))\).
- \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s morphisms (determination-preserving pairs) do *not* correspond to paths in \(G\).

**Independence.** \(G\) and \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) are *coherent but independent*: \(G\) *represents* \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s determined content; \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) *carries* structure (morphism composition, adjunction) not present in \(G\).

**Proof.** Combine III.3 (G is relational, not categorical), III.6's projection. \(\square\)

### III.7 Consequences

**(C1) L5 is a *relational structure*, not a category.**

**(C2) L5 and L4 are *coherent but independent*.** L5 *represents* L4's determined content; L4 *extends* L5's relations to morphisms.

**(C3) L5's edges do *not* compose canonically.** Paths through the graph are *not* morphisms in a category derived from the corpus.

**(C4) Evidential weight is *local* to each edge.** The Heyting-value of an evidential edge \((h, Q)\) is *not* transported along paths; each edge has its own weight.

**(C5) L3.6's predicate typing applies *per edge*.** The Heyting-valued structure of L3.6 is *inherited* by L5's \(E_{\mathrm{evid}}\), but *only edge-wise*, not globally.

### III.8 Falsification attempts

**Attempt 1 — Does L4 *require* L5 to be categorical?**

L4's \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) operates on *determinations*, not on *graph edges*. It does *not* require L5's edges to compose. ✓

**Attempt 2 — Does L3.0's functorial structure require L5 to be categorical?**

L3.0's Zoom is a *functor* on \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\), not on \(G\). L3.0's Explore is *oplax monoidal* on \(\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid}\), not on \(G\). L3.0 does *not* require L5 to be categorical. ✓

**Attempt 3 — Could the corpus's discipline require L5 to be categorical for *coherence*?**

No. The corpus's discipline requires *coherence* between levels, not *categorical equivalence*. A relational structure *coherent with* a category is *sufficient*. ✓

**Attempt 4 — Does the corpus's use of \(\sqcup\) (colimit in \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\)) require L5 to be categorical?**

\(\sqcup\) operates on \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\), not on \(G\). L5 *represents* the result but does not *participate* in \(\sqcup\). ✓

**No falsification of the theorem.** The relational-not-categorical typing holds.

## Part V — Methodological Note (relational vs categorical)

Iteration 23's contribution is the *typing of L5 as relational, not categorical*. This is a *structural fact*: the corpus does *not* derive composite morphisms from graph edges, so L5 cannot be a category *without adding structure*.

**The relational-categorical distinction.** The corpus's earlier levels are *categorical* (L2.5, L2.75, L4). L5 is *relational*. The distinction matters: relational structures *represent*, categorical structures *compose*. The corpus has both, at *distinct levels*.

**The corpus's discipline.** New levels must be *typed* against the existing levels. L5 was *not typed categorically* by Iteration 22's derivation; this iteration supplies the typing.

**The kernel (U4) remains terminal.** Its home is now *five times typed*: \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) (L4), \(\mathbf{P}(\mu)\) (embedding), L5 relational (representational substrate), three-sorted nodes, edge-local Heyting weights.
