## Part III — The Revised Dimension Definition

### III.1 The revised definition

**Current (D11).** \(\mathcal{D}_t\) = recognized dimensions of \(K_t\).

**Revised (Lord Lens).** A dimension is *a distinguishable way in which an observation can be characterized*.

### III.2 Is the revision derivable?

**Test.**

- **Current** \(\mathcal{D}_t\) is *finite* at each \(t\).
- **Revised** allows *infinite* dimensions, *potential* dimensions, *undiscovered* dimensions.

**Derivation attempt.**

By D11, \(K_t\)'s dimensions are *recognized*. By Zero Lens §7 (derived), *unknown dimensions* are *distinct from unknown values*. Combining:

- *Recognized* dimensions: \(\mathcal{D}_t\).
- *Unrecognized* dimensions: outside \(\mathcal{D}_t\).

**Consequence.** The revised definition is the *union*: \(\mathcal{D}^* := \bigcup_t \mathcal{D}_t \cup \text{unrecognized}\). By D101 (ZI-10), the *ideal* space \(\mathcal{D}^*\) is *not derivable*; only \(\bigcup_t \mathcal{D}_t\) is derivable.

**Verdict.** The revised definition is *partially derivable*: it is derivable *if* we replace \(\mathcal{D}^*\) with \(\bigcup_t \mathcal{D}_t\); the *fully revised* form (with *unrecognized* dimensions) is *proposed*.

### III.3 The refined dimension theorem

**Theorem (Refined Dimension Definition).** The Lord Lens's revised dimension definition is *partially derivable*:

- **Derivable form:** a dimension is a *recognizable* way to characterize an observation; the *union over time* \(\bigcup_t \mathcal{D}_t\) is the *discovered* space.
- **Proposed form:** dimensions include *unrecognized* possibilities; \(\mathcal{D}^*\) is *ideal*.

**Consequence.** The revised definition *aligns* with the corpus in its *derivable form*, but *departs* from it in its *proposed form*.

### III.4 What this establishes

- **Revised dimension definition partially derivable.** (D145)
- **Proposed form requires \(\mathcal{D}^*\).** (D146)
