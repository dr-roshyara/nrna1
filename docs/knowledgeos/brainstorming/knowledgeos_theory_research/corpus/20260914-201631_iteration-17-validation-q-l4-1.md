### II.3 Test 3 — Is there a lower-level gap?

Reviewing Iteration 16's derivation of D44 (predicate lattice as Heyting-valued over \([0,1]\)):

**The derivation used:**
- D40 (Determine as conjunction).
- D41 (threshold \(\Delta \ge 0.10\)).
- D42 (dynamic competitors).

**But the derivation *assumed*:**
- That the conjunction was a *meet* in a *lattice*.
- That the lattice was *at least* Heyting.
- That the threshold was a *cutoff* on a continuous \([0,1]\)-valued predicate.

**Are these assumptions *forced*, or *chosen*?**

**Test A — Is the conjunction a meet?**
D40 says Determine = Supported ∧ CompetitorsExcluded. This is *syntactically* a conjunction. Whether it is a *meet* in a lattice depends on whether the predicates form a lattice. If they form only a *poset*, the meet may not exist. If they form a *semi-lattice*, only the meet may exist, not the join.

**Test B — Is the lattice at least Heyting?**
Heyting requires that for any two predicates \(P, Q\), there is an implication \(P \Rightarrow Q\) satisfying the adjunction \(R \wedge P \le Q \iff R \le P \Rightarrow Q\). Whether this holds depends on the *semantics* of predicates.

**Test C — Is the predicate value continuous?**
D41 defines a *threshold* \(\Delta \ge 0.10\), which is a *binary* cut on a *continuous* quantity. But the *continuity* of the predicate itself is not directly forced by D41; D41 forces a *numerical* threshold, which could be applied to a *discrete* predicate too.

**Gap identified.** D44's typing is *consistent* with the frozen contract but *not uniquely forced*. The corpus's contract is compatible with:
- **Bounded lattice** (finite lattice, not necessarily Heyting).
- **Heyting lattice over \([0,1]\)** (the typing from Iteration 16).
- **Complete Boolean algebra over \([0,1]\)** (a stronger typing).
- **Probabilistic Boolean algebra** (a weaker typing).

**This is a well-posed lower-level gap.**

## Part III — Q-ZOOM-DET-TYPE: Is the Predicate Lattice Uniquely Typed?

### III.1 Precise statement

**Q-ZOOM-DET-TYPE.** Does the frozen Determine contract D40 + the effect floor D41 + the decoy precondition D42 *uniquely determine* the type of the predicate lattice, or are there *distinct* predicate lattices, all consistent with the frozen contract, that give *different* Determine results in some cases?

If the typing is *not* unique, then Iteration 16's D44 is *a* typing, not *the* typing, and the corpus must specify which.

### III.2 The candidate lattices

**(L1) Bounded lattice (finite).** \(\text{Pred} = \{0, 1\}^n\) with bitwise min/max. Binary predicates. Determine is a *bitwise conjunction*.

**(L2) Heyting algebra over \(\{0,1\}\).** Predicates form a *Heyting algebra* (intuitionistic propositional logic). Meet = conjunction; there is a *weak* implication. Determine is *intuitionistic* AND.

**(L3) Heyting-valued over \([0,1]\).** Iteration 16's typing. Predicates take values in \([0,1]\); meet = min; threshold \(\tau\) cuts to binary.

**(L4) Boolean algebra over \([0,1]\) with negation.** Predicates take values in \([0,1]\); meet = min; join = max; negation is \(1 - p\). Determine is *classical* min-conjunction.

**(L5) Probabilistic (measure-theoretic).** Predicates are *probability measures* over \(\mathcal{H}_Q\). Conjunction is *conditional probability*. Determine is \(P(h \mid K, E) \ge \tau\).

**Each is *consistent* with the syntactic form of D40.**

### III.3 Testing each against the frozen conditions

**Test D42 (dynamic competitors).** D42 requires that competitors be *generated* and *excluded*. This implies:
- The predicate lattice must be *dynamic* — new competitors change the exclusion-value.
- **L1** can model this (add new bits).
- **L2** can model this (extend the Heyting algebra).
- **L3** can model this (new values).
- **L4** can model this (new bits over \([0,1]\)).
- **L5** can model this (new probability mass).

**All five are consistent with D42.**

**Test D41 (threshold \(\Delta \ge 0.10\)).** D41 defines a *magnitude threshold*, implying:
- Predicates must have *magnitudes*.
- **L1** fails: predicates are binary; no magnitudes to threshold.
- **L2** fails: predicates are intuitionistic values (no continuous magnitude).
- **L3** works: values in \([0,1]\).
- **L4** works: values in \([0,1]\).
- **L5** works: probabilities in \([0,1]\).

**L1 and L2 are eliminated by D41.**

**Test 5 — Do L3, L4, L5 give *different* Determine results?**

**Case A.** A single hypothesis \(h\) has \(\text{Supported}(h) = 0.7\), \(\text{CompetitorsExcluded}(h) = 0.15\), threshold \(\tau = 0.10\).

- **L3 (Heyting meet = min):** \(\min(0.7, 0.15) = 0.15 \ge 0.10\). **Determine ✓.**
- **L4 (Boolean min):** Same. **Determine ✓.**
- **L5 (probabilistic):** \(P(h \mid K, E) = 0.7\), need \(P(h \mid K, E) \ge 0.10\). **Determine ✓.**

**Convergence in this case.**

**Case B.** Two competitors \(h_1, h_2\) with \(\text{Supported}(h_1) = 0.6\), \(\text{Supported}(h_2) = 0.5\), \(\text{CompetitorsExcluded}(h_1) = 0.12\) (i.e., \(h_2\) not excluded), \(\text{CompetitorsExcluded}(h_2) = 0.11\). Threshold \(\tau = 0.10\).

- **L3 (Heyting meet):** \(\min(0.6, 0.12) = 0.12 \ge 0.10\) and \(\min(0.5, 0.11) = 0.11 \ge 0.10\). **Both determined.**
- **L4 (Boolean min):** Same. **Both determined.**
- **L5 (probabilistic):** Requiring \(P(h \mid K, E) \ge 0.10\) for *some* \(h\). Both satisfy. **Both determined.**

**Still converges.**

**Case C (adversarial).** Suppose \(\text{Supported}(h) = 0.15\), \(\text{CompetitorsExcluded}(h) = 0.15\) for a single hypothesis. But there are *three* competitors, each with \(\text{Supported} = 0.9\), and the exclusion rule is *mutual*: if any competitor is well-supported, all hypotheses fail exclusion.

- **L3 (Heyting meet with mutual exclusion):** \(\min(0.15, e_h)\) where \(e_h\) is *small* because competitors are strongly supported. **Determine ✗.**
- **L4 (Boolean min):** Same. **Determine ✗.**
- **L5 (probabilistic):** Suppose \(P(h \mid K, E) = 0.15 / (0.15 + 3 \cdot 0.9) = 0.053 < 0.10\). **Determine ✗.**

**Still converges.**

**Case D (adversarial).** Suppose the exclusion-value for \(h\) is *conditioned* on the hypothesis space size: \(e_h = 1 - \sum_{h' \ne h} P(h' \mid K, E)\).

- **L3 (Heyting meet):** Uses raw \(e_h\).
- **L4 (Boolean min):** Uses raw \(e_h\).
- **L5 (probabilistic):** \(e_h\) is *computed* as a probability, not read off as a value.

**Potential divergence:** If D42's rule for computing \(e_h\) differs between L3/L4 (read off) and L5 (compute), the results may differ.

**Consequence.** L3/L4 differ from L5 in *how* \(e_h\) is computed, not in the lattice structure per se.

**Verdict.** L3, L4, L5 all pass the frozen conditions, but they differ in *mechanism* (read vs compute). The *frozen* contract does *not* specify which is correct.

### III.4 The underdetermination

**Theorem (Q-ZOOM-DET-TYPE).** The frozen Determine contract D40 + D41 + D42 *does not uniquely* determine the predicate lattice type. At least three consistent typings exist: Heyting-valued over \([0,1]\) (L3), Boolean-valued over \([0,1]\) (L4), and probabilistic (L5). They agree on *most* cases but can differ in adversarial cases involving the *mechanism* for computing the exclusion-value.

**Proof.** Combine III.2 (candidate lattices) and III.3 (each is consistent with the frozen conditions; adversarial cases show potential divergence). \(\square\)

### III.5 Which typing is *correct* — derived from the corpus?

The corpus's *other* structures must select the typing.

**Argument for L3 (Heyting-valued).**
- D46 (Determine is natural in evidence) uses a *functor* structure. Functors preserve *internal logic*. Heyting logic is the *internal logic* of a topos. The corpus may be a topos.
- D42 (dynamic competitors) implies *defeasibility*, characteristic of Heyting logic.

**Argument for L4 (Boolean-valued).**
- D40 (conjunction) is classical AND.
- The corpus does not use intuitionistic logic elsewhere.

**Argument for L5 (probabilistic).**
- The corpus's *measure-theoretic layer* (L2) is probabilistic.
- Determining a hypothesis is closest to *Bayesian* determination.

**Neither the corpus nor the frozen contract uniquely forces one typing.**

### III.6 The correct resolution

**Theorem (Q-ZOOM-DET-TYPE, resolved).** The predicate lattice type is *not* uniquely forced. It is *chosen* by the corpus's *architectural commitment*. Three architecturally valid choices exist:

**(Type P-Heyting).** Heyting-valued over \([0,1]\). Aligns with the corpus's use of categorical logic (functorial structures at L2.5, L2.75). **Recommended if the corpus pursues categorical-logic coherence.**

**(Type P-Boolean).** Boolean-valued over \([0,1]\). Simpler; classical. Aligns with the corpus's use of standard analysis. **Recommended if simplicity is preferred.**

**(Type P-Prob).** Probabilistic. Aligns with the corpus's measure-theoretic layer. **Recommended if probabilistic interpretation is preferred.**

**The choice propagates to all downstream structures** — including Q-L4-1, Q-E4α-ii, and the eventual kernel.

**This is a *branch point*, not a *derivation*.** The corpus must *commit* to one typing before proceeding.

### III.7 Testing the three typings against Nexus

**Nexus 70 GB/day → GitLab Runner.**

- **Type P-Heyting.** The exclusion-value \(e_h\) is intuitionistic; the corpus does *not* assume that every competitor can be excluded *or* not excluded. This matches the *open-world* nature of infrastructure investigation. **Compatible with the Nexus model.**
- **Type P-Boolean.** The exclusion-value is classical; each competitor is either excluded or not. This is *over-confident* in the presence of unknown causes. **Less compatible with the open-world Nexus model.**
- **Type P-Prob.** The exclusion-value is a probability. Requires a *prior* distribution over hypotheses. The corpus does *not* provide priors. **Incompatible without additional structure.**

**Verdict.** Type P-Heyting is the *most compatible* with the Nexus model and the corpus's open-world commitments. Type P-Boolean is *simpler* but *over-confident*. Type P-Prob requires *unprovided priors*.

## Part V — Methodological Note (parsimony and the branch point)

The derivation of D49 (Type P-Heyting as default) is *not* a derivation of a *mathematical theorem*. It is a *parsimony argument*: Type P-Heyting is *minimally committed* among the three candidate typings consistent with the frozen contract.

**This is a *new type of derivation* for the corpus:** *selection under parsimony*, rather than *selection under forcing*. The corpus has previously derived *forced* results. Here, the corpus *selects* the most parsimonious typing, but *does not exclude* the alternatives.

**The exclusion criterion is *derivation*:** if downstream structures *require* classical or probabilistic structure, the current typing is *revised*, and the revision *must* be justified by a derivation, not by convention.

**This preserves the discipline** while allowing the corpus to *proceed* in the presence of *non-unique* derivations.

**The kernel (U4) remains terminal.** Its eventual home will be at L4 (integrated determinations) once Q-L4-1 is answered, at which point the kernel's *typing* will be *constrained* by the L4 category. If the L4 category is *only* definable with classical structure, the typing at L3.6 is *revised*; if it is definable with Heyting structure, the current typing *stands*.
