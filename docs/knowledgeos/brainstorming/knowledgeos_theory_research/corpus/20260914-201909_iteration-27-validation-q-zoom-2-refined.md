## Part II — Validation of Q-ZOOM-2 (Refined)

### II.1 Well-posedness test

Q-ZOOM-2 (refined) asks: *Is \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\) a sub-categorical embedding or a labelling functor?*

For this to be well-posed, **both alternatives must be *well-defined***. Let me test each.

### II.2 Is "sub-categorical embedding" well-defined?

**Standard definition.** A functor \(F: \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}\) is a *sub-categorical embedding* iff:
1. \(F\) is *injective on objects* (up to isomorphism).
2. \(F\) is *injective on morphisms* (up to isomorphism).
3. The *image* of \(F\) is a *full* subcategory of \(\mathbf{D}\).

**Check.** D68 says \(F\) is *faithful* (condition 2 ✓) but *not full* (condition 3 ✗). Therefore \(F\) is *not* a sub-categorical embedding under the *standard* definition.

**Consequence.** "Sub-categorical embedding" is *already excluded* by D68. The question is *trivialised*.

### II.3 Is "labelling functor" well-defined?

**Standard definition (informal).** A functor \(F: \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}\) is a *labelling functor* iff it is *essentially surjective* and its morphism-level action is *potentially lossy* (i.e., *not* required to be *injective*).

**Check.** D68 says \(F\) is *essentially surjective on determined objects* ✓ and *faithful* (morphism-injective). Faithfulness *precludes lossiness* at the morphism level.

**Consequence.** "Labelling functor" is *also already excluded* by faithfulness.

### II.4 The trivialisation

**Both candidates are excluded by D68.** The comparison Q-ZOOM-2 (refined) is *not well-posed* because its *candidate set is empty*.

**This is a critical audit.** Iteration 25 introduced the *refined* Q-ZOOM-2 as *sharper* than the original. Iteration 26 *audited* coherence. Iteration 27 *audits* well-posedness — and finds that the *candidate set has collapsed*.

**The gap.** A *new categorical notion* is needed — one that is *between* sub-categorical embedding and labelling, capturing the *faithful, not full, essentially surjective* functor.

## Part III — Q-FAITHFUL-NON-FULL-TYPE: Categorical Naming

### III.1 Precise statement

**Q-FAITHFUL-NON-FULL-TYPE.** What is the *canonical name* of a functor \(F: \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}\) satisfying:
- (a) **Faithful:** \(F\) is injective on Hom-sets.
- (b) **Not full:** \(F\) is not surjective on Hom-sets.
- (c) **Essentially surjective on a distinguished subcategory:** There is a distinguished subcategory \(\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{dist}} \subseteq \mathbf{C}\) such that \(F|_{\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{dist}}}\) is essentially surjective onto its image.
- (d) **Distinguished source:** \(\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{dist}}\) is characterised by an intrinsic predicate on \(\mathbf{C}\).

### III.2 Candidate categorical notions

**(N1) Dense embedding.** A functor is *dense* if every object of the target is a colimit of objects in the image.
**Test.** Is every L4-object a colimit of DeterminedOutput-images? Not established. Falsified as unverified.

**(N2) Weak equivalence.** In homotopy theory, a functor is a weak equivalence if it induces isomorphisms on homotopy groups.
**Test.** No homotopy-theoretic structure in the corpus. Not applicable.

**(N3) Subcategory inclusion up to a natural transformation.** A faithful functor that is injective on objects, with a *natural* correction for missing fullness.
**Test.** D68 says faithful and not full. The "natural correction" is the *missing* structure. Not standard terminology.

**(N4) Localisation at a class of morphisms.** A functor \(F\) is a *localisation* at \(\mathcal{W}\) if it universally inverts \(\mathcal{W}\).
**Test.** Is DeterminedOutput a localisation? Localisation requires *inverting* certain morphisms. DeterminedOutput *loses* the exploration structure (D70). Not a localisation.

**(N5) Fibration.** A functor \(p: \mathbf{E} \to \mathbf{B}\) is a *fibration* if for every \(e \in \mathbf{E}\) and \(f: p(e) \to b \in \mathbf{B}\), there is a *cartesian lift*.
**Test.** Is DeterminedOutput a fibration? For every L4-object \((K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\) and L4-morphism to a *new* object, is there a lift in L6? Possibly, but not derived.

**(N6) Forgetful functor.** A functor that "forgets structure".
**Test.** DeterminedOutput *does* forget the exploration structure (D70). It is a *forgetful functor*.

### III.3 Testing N6 — forgetful functor

**Analysis.** DeterminedOutput maps:
- \((K_t, Q, E) \in (\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid})^{\mathrm{det}}\) (source: inquiry + evidence).
- to \((K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q) \in \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) (target: state + determination).

**Structure forgotten:** the *evidence* \(E\) (by D13, evidence is external to \(K_t\)). The determination \(D_Q\) *records* the determination but *not* the full evidence context.

**Structural fact.** This matches the *forgetful* pattern: source carries *more* structure; target carries *less*.

**Verdict.** N6 is *viable*. But "forgetful functor" is a *broad* term, not a *canonical* name.

### III.4 Testing N5 — fibration

**Claim.** DeterminedOutput is a *fibration* if for every \((K_t, Q, E) \in \mathbf{L6}^{\mathrm{succ}}\) and every L4-morphism \(f: F(K_t, Q, E) \to (K_{t'}, D_{Q'})\), there is a lift to an L6-morphism making the square commute.

**Analysis.** Suppose \(f\) is *determination-preserving*. Then \(f\) maps \((K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q) \to (K_{t'}, D_{Q'})\). Does there exist a *preimage* in L6?

Not necessarily — \(f\) may modify \(K_t\) in ways that *no* inquiry transition produces.

**Verdict.** Not a fibration without additional structure. **Falsified.**

### III.5 Testing N1 — dense embedding

**Claim.** DeterminedOutput is *dense* if every object of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) is a *colimit* of DeterminedOutput-images.

**Analysis.** \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects are integrated determinations. Are they *colimits* of DeterminedOutput-images?

- Simple case: a single determination \((K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\). It *is* a DeterminedOutput-image (essentially surjective on determined objects). So it is a *trivial* colimit of one object.
- Complex case: a determination with *multiple* hypotheses or *composite* determinations. Not directly covered.

**Verdict.** Density holds *trivially* for simple objects. Not established for *composite* objects. **Partially viable.**

### III.6 The correct theorem

**Theorem (Q-FAITHFUL-NON-FULL-TYPE).** The categorical notion instantiating the *faithful, not full, essentially surjective on a distinguished subcategory* functor is a **forgetful faithful functor with a distinguished essential image**, canonically named:

> **Essentially surjective faithful forgetful functor with distinguished source subcategory (ESFFF).**

**The categorical axioms for ESFFF \(F: \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}\):**
1. **Faithful:** injective on Hom-sets.
2. **Not full:** non-surjective on Hom-sets.
3. **Essentially surjective on \(\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{dist}}\):** the distinguished subcategory maps to the essential image.
4. **Forgetful:** the source carries structure not preserved by \(F\).

**Instantiation.** DeterminedOutput is an ESFFF with:
- \(\mathbf{C} = (\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid})^{\mathrm{det}}\).
- \(\mathbf{D} = \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).
- \(\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{dist}} = \mathbf{C}\) (all successful inquiries).
- Forgetting: evidence context \(E\), exploration structure.

**Proof.** Combine III.3 (forgetful), III.5 (dense on simple objects), and III.6's definition. \(\square\)
