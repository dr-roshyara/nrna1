8. **Coherent with the ESFFF typing of DeterminedOutput.**

### III.2 Candidate types

**(T1) Object of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).** The kernel is an *object* of the DDD boundary algebra.
**Analysis:** The kernel would be an *integrated determination* \((K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\). But this is a *specific determination*, not a *general kernel*.
**Falsification:** A kernel should *generalize over* determinations, not *be* one.

**(T2) Morphism of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).** The kernel is a *morphism* in the DDD boundary algebra.
**Analysis:** A morphism \((f_K, f_E)\) is a *determination-preserving pair*. A kernel could be *the canonical such morphism* — but which one?
**Falsification:** No canonical candidate morphism is derivable.

**(T3) Functor \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu) \to \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).** The kernel is an *endofunctor*.
**Analysis:** An endofunctor would *transform* determinations. But what transformation? Not derived.
**Falsification:** No canonical endofunctor is derived.

**(T4) Natural transformation.** The kernel is a *natural transformation* between derived functors.
**Analysis:** Which functors? The *natural* candidates are \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\) and its composite with \(U\). But \(U\) is *relational, not functorial* (D73). So no natural transformation between them.

**(T5) 2-cell.** The kernel is a *2-cell* in a 2-categorical structure at L4.
**Analysis:** The corpus does *not* have a 2-categorical structure at L4. Falsified by absence.

**(T6) Limit / colimit.** The kernel is a *limit* (or *colimit*) of a canonical diagram in \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).
**Analysis:** Which diagram? The *canonical* candidate is the *diagram of determinations* at a fixed anchor. The *limit* would be the *most general* determination; the *colimit* would be the *most specific*.
**Test:** Is there a *canonical* diagram whose limit/colimit is the kernel?
**Falsification:** No canonical diagram is derived.

**(T7) Subobject classifier / internal logic object.** The kernel is the *subobject classifier* of the internal logic of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).
**Analysis:** By D44–D50, the internal logic is *Heyting-valued*. In a Heyting topos, the *subobject classifier* \(\Omega\) is a *canonical* object. If \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) is a topos (or a Heyting category), the *subobject classifier* would be *canonical*.
**Test:** Is \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) a topos? Not established.
**Partial verdict:** T7 is *viable* if L4 is a topos, but this is not derived.

**(T8) Section of the ESFFF.** The kernel is a *canonical section* of \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\).
**Analysis:** A section would be a functor \(s: \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu) \to (\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid})^{\mathrm{det}}\) with \(\text{DeterminedOutput} \circ s = \text{id}\). This would *recover* the inquiry structure from a determination.
**Falsification:** By D69, the *reverse* direction is *not canonical*. So no *canonical* section exists.

**(T9) Left adjoint of the ESFFF (if partial).** The kernel is the *partial left adjoint* of DeterminedOutput.
**Analysis:** By D54, the embedding \(F\) of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) into \(\mathbf{P}(\mu)\) has a *partial left adjoint* \(G_L\). This is *not* the same as an adjoint of DeterminedOutput. Falsified as a mismatch.

**(T10) Composite structure.** The kernel is a *structured object* combining multiple of the above.

### III.3 Testing the strongest candidates

**Test T7 — Subobject classifier.**

For T7 to hold, \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) must be a *Heyting category* (or topos). Is it?

**Sub-object classifier test.** A topos's sub-object classifier \(\Omega\) is an object such that \(\text{Hom}(X, \Omega)\) corresponds to subobjects of \(X\). Does \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) have such an object?

- Objects of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) are *integrated determinations*.
- Subobjects of an integrated determination would be *refinements* (e.g., sub-determinations with more exclusions).
- A *classifier* would be the *most general refinement pattern*.

**Falsification attempt.** Is there a *canonical* most-general refinement pattern? By D44, the internal logic is Heyting-valued. In Heyting categories, *subobject classifiers* exist *if and only if* the category is a topos. But \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) is a *subcategory of \(\mathbf{P}(\mu)\)*, not obviously a topos.

**Verdict.** T7 is *conditionally viable but not derived*. Falsified as *currently derivable*.

**Test T8 — Section of the ESFFF.**

Iteration 25 D69 says *no canonical reverse*. Therefore *no canonical section*.

**Verdict.** Falsified.

**Test T6 — Limit / colimit.**

Is there a *canonical diagram* in \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) whose limit is the kernel?

**Candidate diagram.** The *diagram of all determinations at a fixed anchor \(Q\)*. As exclusions are refined (adding new competitors), determinations *change*. The *limit* over refinement would be the *most-refined determination*; the *colimit* would be the *least-refined*.

### III.4 The structural constraint from ESFFF

**Key derivation.** By D75–D76, \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\) is an ESFFF: **Essentially Surjective Faithful Forgetful Functor with distinguished source subcategory**.

**Consequence.** The kernel must be *exactly* the structure that the ESFFF *forgets*. **The kernel is the "lost structure" of the ESFFF.**

**Reformulation.** The kernel is the **categorical invariant of the source subcategory** of the ESFFF, *up to* the information preserved by the target.

**Formal construction.** Define:

**Interpretation.** The *kernel* of a functor \(F: \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}\) is the *subcategory* of \(\mathbf{C}\) whose objects map to a fixed object of \(\mathbf{D}\). Here:
\[
