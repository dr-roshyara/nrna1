## Part 3: The Sperms as Prior Mass

### 3.1 The Prior Distribution

The millions of sperm represent the **prior distribution**:

$$
\boxed{
P(H) = \{\pi_1, \pi_2, \ldots, \pi_n\}
}
$$

Where:
- \(n\) = Number of hypotheses
- \(\pi_i = P(H_i)\) = Prior probability of hypothesis \(i\)
- \(\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i = 1\)

### 3.2 The Multiplicity Effect

The number of sperm \(n\) creates a **multiplicity penalty**:

$$
\boxed{
P(H_i \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H_i) \cdot \pi_i}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E \mid H_j) \cdot \pi_j}
}
$$

The evidence burden grows as:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Required Evidence} \propto 2\ln n
}
$$

### 3.3 The Diversity Effect

Not all sperm are identical:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Diversity}(P(H)) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^2
}
$$

The effective number of hypotheses:

$$
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^2}
}
$$

## Part 4: The Journey as Bayesian Updating

### 4.1 The Journey

The journey of the sperm through the reproductive tract is **Bayesian updating**:

$$
\boxed{
P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H)}{P(E)}
}
$$

| Stage | Biological | Probability | KnowledgeOS |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Release** | Sperms are released | Prior \(P(H)\) | Hypotheses generated |
| **Travel** | Sperms travel | Likelihood \(P(E \mid H)\) | Evidence evaluated |
| **Selection** | Sperms are filtered | Posterior \(P(H \mid E)\) | Bayesian update |
| **Fertilization** | One sperm enters | MAP \(\hat{H}\) | Knowledge selected |

### 4.2 The Likelihood Function

The likelihood function represents **evidence evaluation**:

$$
\boxed{
P(E \mid H_i) = \text{How well hypothesis } i \text{ explains the evidence}
}
$$

### 4.3 The Marginal Likelihood

The marginal likelihood normalizes:

$$
\boxed{
P(E) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(E \mid H_j) \cdot P(H_j)
}
$$

## Part 5: The Selection as Posterior Probability

### 5.1 The Posterior Distribution

The posterior distribution is:

$$
\boxed{
P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H)}{P(E)}
}
$$

### 5.2 The Selection Criterion

The ovum selects the sperm with the highest posterior:

$$
\boxed{
\hat{H} = \arg\max_i P(H_i \mid E)
}
$$

This is the **Maximum A Posteriori (MAP)** estimate.

### 5.3 The Set-Valued Selection

Selection can be set-valued:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Selection}(E) = \{H_i : P(H_i \mid E) > \tau\}
}
$$

Where \(\tau\) is an **admissibility threshold**.

## Part 6: The Offspring as MAP Estimate

### 6.1 The MAP Estimate

The offspring is the **MAP estimate**:

$$
\boxed{
\hat{H} = \arg\max_i \frac{P(E \mid H_i) \cdot P(H_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E \mid H_j) \cdot P(H_j)}
}
$$

### 6.2 The Information Lost

The MAP estimate discards:

| Lost Information | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Multiplicity** | Other hypotheses that were close |
| **Variance** | The uncertainty in the estimate |
| **Alternatives** | Other viable hypotheses |
| **Distribution Shape** | Bimodal, multimodal, etc. |

### 6.3 The Zero Lens

The Zero Lens examines what is lost:

$$
\boxed{
ZeroLens(K) = \text{Boundary}(P(H \mid E), \hat{H})
}
$$

It asks:
- Are there close alternatives?
- What is the uncertainty?
- Is the distribution multimodal?
- What evidence was discarded?
