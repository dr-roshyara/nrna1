## Part III — Q-L3-L4-TYPE: L3.0-to-L4 Structural Relationship

### III.1 Precise statement

**L3.0 objects:** \((K_t, Q)\) — epistemic state with active inquiry.
**L4 objects:** \((K_t, D_Q)\) — epistemic state with determination at anchor \(Q\).

**Q-L3-L4-TYPE.** What is the *canonical* structural relationship between these two types of objects, such that L3.0's morphisms (Zoom, Explore, Determine) and L4's morphisms (determination-preserving pairs) become *coherent*?

### III.2 Candidate relationships

**(R1) L3.0 ⊂ L4.** Every inquiry object \((K_t, Q)\) is *also* a determination object by taking a *trivial* determination \(D_Q^\emptyset\) (empty).
**Problem:** A trivial determination would be *inconsistent* with D40's contract (empty determination fails supported ∨ excluded).

**(R2) L4 ⊂ L3.0.** Every determination object \((K_t, D_Q)\) is *also* an inquiry object by taking \(Q = \text{anchor}(D_Q)\).
**Analysis:** A determination \(D_Q\) *presupposes* an anchor \(Q\). By D37, \(D_Q\) is the *output* of Zoom-in + Explore + Determine at \(Q\). So yes, an inquiry \(Q\) is *derivable* from \(D_Q\).
**Verdict:** Viable.

**(R3) L3.0 and L4 are parallel categories with a *forgetful functor* L4 → L3.0.**
**Analysis:** The forgetful functor forgets \(D_Q\) and remembers \(Q\). By R2, this is canonical.
**Verdict:** Equivalent to R2.

**(R4) L3.0 and L4 are parallel with a *partial* functor L3.0 → L4.**
**Analysis:** The functor is defined only on inquiry objects where a determination *exists*.
**Verdict:** Partial functor, viable.

**(R5) L3.0 and L4 are related by an *adjunction*.**
**Analysis:** Requires deriving a *universal* property. Not obviously forced.

### III.3 The correct typing — via D37 (Zoom-out)

**Observation.** By D37 (Zoom-out = Integration), the *output* of the L3.0 cycle is an *integrated determination* \(K_{t+1} = K_t \sqcup D_Q\). This is an *object* of \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\) with a *recorded determination*.

**Structural fact.** Every L3.0 cycle that *terminates* in a determination produces an L4 object. Cycles that terminate in `UNDETERMINED` do *not*.

**Consequence.** L3.0's *determined* outputs *are* L4's inputs. L3.0's *undetermined* outputs (investigation terminated without determination) are *not* L4 objects.

**The relationship:**
\[
\text{DeterminedOutput}: \mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}} \to \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)
\]
where \(\mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}}\) is the subcategory of inquiries that *terminate in determinations*.

### III.4 Is DeterminedOutput a functor?

**Source category.** \(\mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}}\) = inquiries with successful Determine.
**Target category.** \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) = integrated determinations.
**On objects.** \((K_t, Q)\) with successful Determine \(\mapsto (K_t, D_Q) = (K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\) after integration.
**On morphisms.** An inquiry-morphism \((K_t, Q) \to (K_{t'}, Q')\) preserving determination maps to the corresponding determination-preserving morphism in \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).

**Functoriality.** By D38 (Zoom-out is a natural functor), integration is functorial. Hence DeterminedOutput is a *functor*. ✓

### III.5 What about the *undetermined* inquiries?

**Structural fact.** L3.0 has objects (inquiries) that do *not* have determinations. These objects are *not* in \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).

**Consequence.** DeterminedOutput is *not* a *full* functor on \(\mathbf{Inq}(\mu)\). It is *defined only on a subcategory*.

**Domain of DeterminedOutput.** \(\{Q \in \mathbf{Inq}(\mu) : \text{Determine}(Q) \text{ succeeds}\}\).

### III.6 The correct theorem

**Theorem (Q-L3-L4-TYPE).** The canonical structural relationship between L3.0 and L4 is a **functor**
\[
\text{DeterminedOutput}: \mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)
\]
**from the subcategory of L3.0 inquiries that terminate in determinations to L4**, defined by:
- **On objects:** \((K_t, Q) \mapsto (K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\).
- **On morphisms:** inquiry-morphisms preserving determination.

**The functor is:**
- **Not full** (L4 has morphisms not arising from L3.0 cycles).
- **Faithful** (distinct inquiry-morphisms map to distinct L4-morphisms).
- **Not essentially surjective** (some L4 objects arise from *other* L3.0 cycles or from *non-inquiry* processes).

**Proof.** Combine III.3 (canonical relationship), III.4 (functoriality via D38), III.5 (partiality), III.6's three properties. \(\square\)

### III.7 Falsification attempts

**Attempt 1 — Is there a *reverse* functor from L4 to L3.0?**

Consider \(\text{UndoDetermination}: \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu) \to \mathbf{Inq}(\mu)\), sending \((K_t, D_Q)\) to \((K_t, Q)\). Is this functorial?

- On objects: yes, \(D_Q\) presupposes \(Q\).
- On morphisms: a determination-preserving morphism \((f_K, f_E)\) maps to... what? An inquiry-morphism \((K_t, Q) \to (K_{t'}, Q')\). The morphism \((f_K, f_E)\) preserves *determination*, not *inquiry*.

**Problem:** Reverse functor is *not* obviously well-defined on morphisms.

**Falsification attempt.** Consider two determinations \(D_{Q_1}, D_{Q_2}\) at the *same* anchor \(Q\) with *different* hypotheses \(h_1, h_2\). Then \(\text{UndoDetermination}(D_{Q_1}) = (K_t, Q) = \text{UndoDetermination}(D_{Q_2})\), so two distinct objects map to the *same* image. This violates the *essential injectivity* required for a *canonical* reverse functor.

**Verdict.** Reverse functor is *not* canonical. **Attempt 1 falsified.**

**Attempt 2 — Is there an *adjunction* between L3.0 and L4?**

For an adjunction \(\text{DeterminedOutput} \dashv G\) for some \(G: \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu) \to \mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}}\):
\[
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{DetCat}}(\text{DeterminedOutput}(X), Y) \cong \text{Hom}_{\mathbf{Inq}}(X, G(Y)).
\]
By Attempt 1, \(G\) would be \(\text{UndoDetermination}\), which is *not* functorial.

**Verdict.** No adjunction.

**Attempt 3 — Is \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\) *essentially injective* on objects?**

No — the *same* inquiry \(Q\) at the *same* \(K_t\) can lead to *different* determinations if the evidence differs. But in the corpus, \((K_t, Q)\) *fixes* the evidence (by D13, evidence is external to \(K_t\)). Hmm.

**Reconsider.** The evidence context \(E_Q\) is *not* in \(K_t\); it is in \(\mathbf{Evid}\). So \((K_t, Q)\) alone does *not* determine the evidence, hence does *not* determine the determination.

**Correction.** The source of DeterminedOutput should be *triples* \((K_t, Q, E_Q)\) — *not* just \((K_t, Q)\).

**Revised typing.** Source is \(\mathbf{Inq}(\mu)^{\mathrm{det}} \times \mathbf{Evid}\), restricted to pairs where Determine succeeds.

**Revised functor.**
\[
\text{DeterminedOutput}: (\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid})^{\mathrm{det}} \to \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)
\]
defined on objects \((K_t, Q, E) \mapsto (K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\).

**Verdict.** The typing is refined. **Attempt 3 yields a *sharper* theorem.**

### III.8 The corrected theorem

**Theorem (Q-L3-L4-TYPE, corrected).** The canonical structural relationship between L3.0 and L4 is a *functor*
\[
\text{DeterminedOutput}: (\mathbf{Inq}(\mu) \times \mathbf{Evid})^{\mathrm{det}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)
\]
from the subcategory of L3.0 inquiry-evidence pairs on which Determine succeeds, to L4:
- **Objects:** \((K_t, Q, E) \mapsto (K_t \sqcup D_Q, D_Q)\).
- **Morphisms:** determination-preserving triples.

**Functoriality.** Via D38 (Zoom-out natural functor), integration is functorial. ✓

**Properties:**
- **Faithful:** distinct triples map to distinct L4 objects.
- **Not full:** L4 has more morphisms than L3.0 produces.
- **Essentially surjective on determined objects:** every determined L4 object arises from *some* L3.0 cycle (by construction of L4 via D37).

**Proof.** Combine III.4 (functoriality), III.7 Attempt 3 (source correction), and III.8's properties. \(\square\)

### III.9 Consequences

**(C1)** L3.0 and L4 are related by a *partial* functor \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\).

**(C2)** L4 *inherits* L3.0's exploration structure via this functor.

**(C3)** L3.0's *undetermined* inquiries are *not* L4 objects. **L3.0 has structure L4 does not.**

**(C4)** The *reverse* direction is *not* canonical: L4 does not canonically project back to L3.0.

**(C5) Asymmetry.** L3.0 → L4 is *canonical* (via DeterminedOutput). L4 → L3.0 is *not*.

**(C6)** Q-ZOOM-2 is now *fully* well-posed. The comparison is *not* "sub-category vs labelling" in the naive sense; it is "**the functor DeterminedOutput** — is it sub-categorical or labelling?"

### III.10 Revised Q-ZOOM-2

**Refined Q-ZOOM-2.** Is the functor \(\text{DeterminedOutput}\) (from L3.0's determined inquiry-evidence pairs to L4's \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)):

- **A sub-categorical embedding** (L3.0's structure *is* a subcategory of L4), or

**(1) Typing precedes structure.** The corpus has now performed nine successive descents. Each descent's *result* is a *typing* result — not yet a *structural* result. This is the *correct* discipline: structure can only be *derived* from *typed* ingredients.

This is not a *defect*; it is a *feature* of the corpus's architecture: **determination loses the exploration structure that produced it.** This is exactly the phenomenon the corpus has been studying since the beginning (reachability ≠ determination ≠ knowledge), now *derived* at the level of functors.

**The kernel (U4) remains terminal.** Its behavioural home is now *nearly determined*: the kernel will live at L4's \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\), *reachable* from L3.0 via DeterminedOutput but *not* recoverable to L3.0. The final structural fact needed is Q-ZOOM-2 (refined) — whether the functor is sub-categorical or labelling. After that, the kernel can be *posed*.
