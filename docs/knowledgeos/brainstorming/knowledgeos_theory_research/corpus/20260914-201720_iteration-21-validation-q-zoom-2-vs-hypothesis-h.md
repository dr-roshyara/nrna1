## Part III — Q-L5-GRAPH: Is the Knowledge Graph Derivable?

### III.1 Precise statement

**Candidate L5 structure.** \(G = (V, E)\) where:
- \(V\) = state observables / dimensions.
- \(E \subseteq V \times V\) = directed causal dependency edges.

**Derivability question.** Can \(G\) be *derived* from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects and morphisms (D51–D54), or must \(G\) be *posited independently*?

### III.2 Attempt 1 — Derive \(G\) from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects

**Candidate derivation.** Objects of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) are integrated determinations \((K_t, D_Q)\) where \(D_Q\) is determined at \(Q\). The *dimensions* \(\mathcal{D}_t\) of \(K_t\) give candidate nodes \(V_t\):
\[
V_t := \mathcal{D}_t = \{\text{dimensions active at } t\}.
\]

**Edges.** Do morphisms of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) give edges?

A morphism \((f_K, f_E): (K_t, D_Q) \to (K_{t'}, D_{Q'})\) *preserves determination*. It does *not* directly produce a *causal* edge \(d_1 \to d_2\) between dimensions.

**Falsification.** The morphisms of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) are *determination-preserving*, not *causal*. Therefore edges of \(G\) *cannot* be derived from morphisms of \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\).

**Verdict.** The *nodes* \(V\) are derivable from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects. The *edges* \(E\) are *not*. **Partial derivation fails.**

### III.3 Attempt 2 — Derive \(G\) from L3.0's exploration structure

**Candidate.** By D27, Explore *discovers* dimensions outside \(\text{Focus}(Q)\). Does exploration produce *edges*?

**Analysis.** Exploration produces *evidence relationships*: evidence \(e\) supports hypothesis \(h\); hypothesis \(h\) explains observation \(o\). These are *evidential* relations, not *causal* relations per se.

**Falsification attempt.** Suppose \(d_1\) (egress) is *explained* by \(h_1\) (CICD). Then an edge \(h_1 \to d_1\) exists *at the evidential level*. But *causal* direction is a *stronger* claim: not just "CICD explains 70 GB/day" but "CICD *causes* 70 GB/day."

**Verdict.** Exploration produces *evidential* edges, not *causal* edges. The Knowledge Graph's causal \(E\) is *not* directly derived from L3.0's exploration.

### III.4 Attempt 3 — Derive \(G\) from the *integrated* determinations

**Candidate.** By D37 (Zoom-out = Integration), the corpus *integrates* determinations into states. When \(D_Q\) is integrated, the *causal relationship* between \(Q\)'s cause and \(Q\)'s anchor is *recorded*.

**Analysis.** The integrated \(D_Q\) contains:
- **\(h\)** (the determined hypothesis),
- **\(s_h, e_h\)** (support and exclusion values),
- **anchor \(Q\)** (the inquiry),
- **evidence \(E_Q\)**.

Does this *record* constitute a *graph edge*?

**Test.** Yes: \(h\) *causes* \(Q\)'s observation is *recorded* as the pair \((h, Q)\) with the *causal interpretation* being *grounded* in the determination contract (support + competitor exclusion).

**Consequence.** Every integrated determination *induces* a candidate edge from its hypothesis \(h\) to its anchor \(Q\)'s observation.

**Falsification attempt.** Does the corpus *require* the causal interpretation?

By D40 (Determine contract), the determination \(D_Q\) records *which hypothesis is supported and which competitors are excluded*. It does *not* directly assert *causality* — it asserts *evidential adequacy*.

**Consequence.** The integrated \(D_Q\) gives an *evidential* edge, not a *causal* edge. The causal interpretation is a *modelling choice*, not a *corpus-derived fact*.

**Verdict.** Integrated determinations give *evidential* edges. Causal edges require *additional structure* (a causal model, an interventionist semantics, or an explicit causal assumption).

### III.5 The correct derivability theorem

**Theorem (Q-L5-GRAPH).** The Knowledge Graph \(G = (V, E)\) is *partially* derivable from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\):

- **Nodes \(V\):** derivable from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\)'s objects as \(\bigcup_t \mathcal{D}_t\) (the union of active dimensions across time).
- **Evidential edges \(E_{\mathrm{evid}}\):** derivable from integrated determinations \(D_Q\) as pairs \((h, Q)\). These are *evidential* relations.
- **Causal edges \(E_{\mathrm{causal}}\):** *NOT derivable* from the corpus's current structures. They require *additional* causal-modelling semantics.

**Proof.** Combine III.2 (nodes derivable), III.4 (evidential edges derivable), and III.4's falsification (causal edges not derivable). \(\square\)

### III.6 Consequences

**Consequence 1 — Hypothesis H is *partially* supported.**

H1 ("Knowledge Graph is a representational substrate") is *partially* supported: the graph's *nodes* and *evidential edges* are derived. The graph's *causal edges* are *not* derived.

**Consequence 2 — The "causal" interpretation of the Knowledge Graph is a *proposed* addition, not a *derived* structure.**

This is consistent with the corpus's discipline: causal semantics are *not* part of the derived architecture. They must be *either* derived from additional structures (e.g., interventions) *or* declared as an *explicit modelling choice*.

**Consequence 3 — H3 (\(G \neq K_t\)) is confirmed.**

The Knowledge Graph \(G\) contains only nodes and *evidential* edges from \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\); it does *not* contain the additional structures \(K_t\) carries (standards, determination status, uncertainty, alternatives, inquiry context, attribution, temporal validity). **H3 holds.** ✓

**Consequence 4 — H4–H6 remain consistent with the corpus.**

H4 (Zoom-in \(\neq\) Reduction): consistent with D32.
H5 (Context Integration \(\neq\) Zoom-in\(^{-1}\)): consistent with Iteration 14's derivation of Zoom-out = Integration and the *absence* of an inverse.
H6 (Graph relation \(\neq\) epistemically determined relation): *derived* — the corpus's evidential edges are *not* the same as graph-theoretic edges (no causal semantics attached).

### III.7 Falsification attempts

**Attempt 1 — Could causal edges be derived from L3.5's interventional criterion?**

D41 defines an interventional \(\Delta = P(\text{flip} \mid r) - P(\text{flip} \mid d)\). This *is* causal evidence: the *intervention* on the root cause \(r\) vs the decoy \(d\) distinguishes genuine causation from correlation.

**Analysis.** If the corpus *records* interventional evidence for a determination, then a *causal* edge \(r \to Q\) *is* derivable.

**But:** D41 is an *experimental* criterion, not a *state-level* structure. The *state* \(K_t\) does *not* record interventional evidence by default. It would need to be *extended* to carry a *causal witness* structure.

**Verdict.** Causal edges are derivable only if the corpus is *extended* to carry interventional witnesses. Currently, they are *not derivable*.

**Attempt 2 — Could causal edges be derived from the base's \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\) transitions?**

Transitions \(T: K_t \to K_{t+1}\) in \(\mathbf{KOS}(\mu)\) are *determination-preserving* or *not*; they are *not* causal.

**Verdict.** No.

**Attempt 3 — Could causal edges be derived from L2.5's Calkin structure?**

The Calkin structure is *operator-algebraic*, orthogonal to *causal* semantics (D47).

**Verdict.** No.

**No falsification of the theorem.** The partial derivability result holds.

## Part V — Methodological Note (the partial-derivability principle)

Hypothesis H was a *strong architectural proposal*: the Knowledge Graph as representational substrate. **The corpus's discipline refuses to *accept* hypothesis H as a *foundational* structure without derivation.**

The audit (this iteration) revealed:
- **Partially derivable** components: nodes, evidential edges.
- **Not derivable** components: causal edges.

**This is the *partial-derivability principle*:** a proposed structure is *derivable in part* and *posited in part*. The derived part becomes *canonical*; the posited part becomes *conditional* on downstream *use* justifying the addition.

**The pattern generalises.** Other proposed structures (P2 Latent manifold, P3 Knowledge as sheaf, P6 Knowledge Graph causal edges) must *each* be audited for *partial derivability*. This is the *ongoing* methodological discipline of the programme.

**The kernel (U4) remains terminal.** Its home is now *three times typed*: \(\mathbf{DetCat}(\mu)\) (L4), \(\mathbf{P}(\mu)\) (embedding), and the partial L5 (representational substrate). Q-ZOOM-2 will clarify L3.0's contribution to L4, which determines the kernel's *behavioural* home (L3.0 ∩ L4) as distinct from its *structural* home (L4).
