computation — a projection can lose information in general while remaining sufficient for a
specific task. The rigorous four-part test, not yet applied here for any specific downstream use:

1. **Representation equivalence** — does a bijection `φ:𝒮₅↔𝒮₂` exist? **No** (immediate from

3. **Task sufficiency** — for a specific downstream function `f` (e.g. "count requirements known

4. **Semantic preservation** — does `π` preserve the distinctions KnowledgeOS's own downstream

## C. Canonical-construction experiment — full reconstruction (§5 of the commissioning)

- **Input**: a hand-curated table (`NEEDS`) mapping 18 named operations (14 "forced," 4 "band":
  `Transform, Merge, Split, Reintroduce`) to sets of component labels (`A,R,S,EL,Dt,PI,T,H`).
- **Perturbation**: "removal" = deleting one component label from the universe and checking which
  operations' `NEEDS` sets are no longer satisfiable.
- **Band**: the 4 operations whose *mandatory* status (per a separate, undetermined decision `D-1`)
  is not yet settled — the test asks whether *including or excluding* these 4 (in any of the 16
  possible subset combinations) changes the union of required components.
- **Invariance property tested**: exactly `components(LOWER+subset) == components(LOWER)` for all
  16 subsets — a **set-equality check**, not `f(K)=f(K\x)` in the commissioning's proposed form;
  the actual test is closer to "does the required-component set have a fixed point independent of
  the undetermined band," which is a narrower, correctly-scoped question than general invariance.
- **Result**: `True` for all 16 subsets (by construction, since every band operation's `NEEDS` set
  is already a subset of the 14 forced operations' combined `NEEDS`) — **and separately**, each of
  the 8 components breaks at least one forced operation if removed ("nothing removable").
- **What "canonical" means here**: **nothing** — the document explicitly declines the word for its
  own result ("NOT canonical"). The correct label for what was established is: **"every one of
  these 8 components is semantically necessary for at least one of the 14 operations already
  agreed to be forced, independent of how the 4 undecided band operations are resolved."** This is
  a narrower, well-supported claim — not a global minimality or canonicality theorem.

## D. Statistical experiment audit (§6)

- 14 forced + 4 band = 18 operations; 8 component labels; 16 band subsets tested (exhaustive, `2⁴`,
  not sampled); no control group in the conventional sense (the test is a closed combinatorial
  enumeration, not a sampled experiment); fully deterministic (no randomness); trivially
  reproducible (re-running the script yields identical output, confirmed by direct execution
