## 0. Correction of the S3 dry-run report

My S3 report said the 102 files "existed earlier in the repository's history, for example in `70fee73c8`, but not at
that exact commit", and that the 8 `.pyc` files "exist nowhere". Both statements were wrong, and I retract them:

- The "existed earlier" description came from tracing **S1513**, which turned out to be a different case (a corrupt
  commit string, §1). For the other 93 committed files the truth is the opposite: they were first committed
  **after** the recorded commit.
- The 8 `.pyc` files were never committed, but they **do exist as untracked working-tree files**.

## 1. Root cause: P1 recorded a commit, not the content it read

| Case | Files | What happened |
|---|--:|---|
| **Recorded commit string corrupt** | 1 (S1513, P1 batch B0037) | recorded `39fdef05dc…a26362a59191a591`, **not a git object**. The real commit `39fdef05dc…a26362a59191a35f` (same 36-char prefix, unique) **contains the file** |
| **Committed after the recorded commit** | 93 | recorded commit `39fdef05dc…a35f` (2026-09-11 02:01). The files were **uncommitted working-tree files when P1 read them** and were **first committed later** (92 on 2026-09-12, 1 on 2026-09-06 on another line of history). P1 recorded the then-current HEAD anyway |
| **Never committed** | 8 (all `.pyc`) | compiled Python bytecode caches, present only as untracked working-tree files |

The other **2,665 CONTENT files** are present at their recorded commit, so their census coverage is verifiable.

**Aggravating gap:** `02-FILES.jsonl` has **no content hash**, although v3.5 R11 defines identity as
`source_id + commit + sha256`. So no copy of these 102 files can be *cryptographically* matched to what P1 read.

## 3. The eight `.pyc` files (B)

- **How they entered:** they are listed in P1's chronology index
  (`brainstorming/20260909-185001_files-to-read-one-by-one.log.md`) and in `00-ROADMAP-VALIDATED.jsonl`, and were
  admitted as `CONTENT`.
- **What P1 did with them:** P1 recognized them as binary. Its summaries read, for example, "compiled CPython 3.13
  bytecode cache … binary, not human-readable text". It extracted **0 contribution rows** from any of them. Their
  provenance labels vary (PRIMARY, SECONDARY-SYNTHESIS, PROVENANCE-UNRESOLVED).
- **What the frozen protocols say about binary content:** **nothing.** Neither v3.5 nor v1.6.4 mentions binary files,
  `.pyc` or bytecode. The roadmap validator has no binary rule.
- **Consequence:** a lexical search over bytecode is not meaningful. They are **not removed** by this proposal. Any
  change to their treatment is a decision (§5, D-3).

## 4. Proposed evidence states (existing vocabulary reused where possible)

The protocol already uses the pattern "may corroborate but cannot alone establish" for SECONDARY-SYNTHESIS and
PROVENANCE-UNRESOLVED sources (§14.2 rule 3), and NEGATIVE-BOUNDED for incomplete searches (§11.4). The new
`census_access` field reuses both patterns:

| `census_access` | Assigned to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `HISTORICALLY-VERIFIED` | 2,665 files (+ V\* if D-1 accepts) | the content at the recorded (or uniquely resolved) commit |
| `RECOVERED-BUT-NOT-VERIFIED` | R1, R2 (and R1 unless D-2 upgrades it) | a copy exists (single committed version); identity to P1's read is **not provable** |
| `HISTORICALLY-UNVERIFIABLE` | none at present | no copy exists |
| `EXCLUDED-BY-AMENDMENT` | none unless D-3 decides so for B | removed from the lexical-search population by a recorded decision; **stays in the registry** |

## 5. What each state permits (the core of the proposal)

| State | Contributes to NEGATIVE-CENSUS? | Can be sole basis of FOUND / ESTABLISHED / SUPPORTED? | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HISTORICALLY-VERIFIED` | **yes** | yes | full evidence |
| `RECOVERED-BUT-NOT-VERIFIED` | **no** (absence in it does not count) | **no**: may corroborate only (the §14.2 rule-3 pattern) | **disconfirmation probe**: a stage-1 hit in it **blocks** a census-absence claim unless stage 2 dispositions it FALSE-HIT. Unverifiable content can only **weaken** a negative, never create a historical positive |
| `HISTORICALLY-UNVERIFIABLE` | no | no | recorded gap |
| `EXCLUDED-BY-AMENDMENT` | not searched | no | recorded exclusion with reason |

**Proposed definition (replaces the A.4 / §11.4 condition for NEGATIVE-CENSUS):** a dimension is `NEGATIVE-CENSUS`
iff (a) the ledger search and the raw search over **every `HISTORICALLY-VERIFIED` file** find nothing, **and** (b) no
`RECOVERED-BUT-NOT-VERIFIED` file has an undispositioned hit. Every NEGATIVE-CENSUS record lists the counts of
unverified and excluded files, so the claim always states its coverage. Otherwise the dimension is NEGATIVE-BOUNDED,
as now.

**Also proposed:** S3 records the **sha256 of every blob it reads**, closing the R11 gap for all future steps. This is
an implementation addition with no semantic effect.

## 6. Can P3b still answer its research question?

**Yes, with an explicitly qualified claim, under any of the options below.** Without a change, the answer is **no**:
under frozen A.4 no dimension can be NEGATIVE-CENSUS, so §13.9a condition (2) and SUPPORTED-IN-CORPUS are unattainable
for every hypothesis.

What a census claim then means, by option:

| Option | Verified census | Unverified (probe only) | Excluded | Claim wording |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| **Strict** (D-1 no, D-2 no, D-3 keep) | 2,665 | 94 (+8 binary as probes) | 0 | "absent from the 2,665 verified files and not contradicted by the 102 recovered copies" |
| **Recommended** (D-1 yes, D-2 no, D-3 exclude from lexical search) | 2,666 | 93 | 8 binary | "absent from the 2,666 verified files; not contradicted by 93 recovered copies; 8 binary files not searchable" |
| **Maximal** (D-1 yes, D-2 yes, D-3 exclude) | 2,745 | 14 | 8 | "absent from 2,745 verified or verified-by-equivalence files; not contradicted by 14 recovered copies" |

**Minimum evidence that would make the 93 fully verified:** P1's read-time content hashes, which do not exist. So
full verification is **impossible** by construction. The decision is about which qualified claim to make, not about
recovering proof.
