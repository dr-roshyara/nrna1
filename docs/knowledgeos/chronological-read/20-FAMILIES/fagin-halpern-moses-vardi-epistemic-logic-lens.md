**Stage 1 — Initial extraction and adoption (S0523, 20 rows)**

- *Foundational principle and possible-worlds semantics* (5 rows): elevates Fagin et al.'s "no single correct model of knowledge" statement to a candidate KnowledgeOS constitutional principle; adopts possible-worlds semantics (an agent knows φ iff φ holds in every world it considers possible); refines "knowledge is continuously changing facts" into "a time-dependent restriction on epistemically possible states of affairs"; formalizes epistemic uncertainty as the size/entropy of the remaining possible-worlds set, with information gain ΔI=H_before-H_after; adopts the Kripke structure M=(S,π,K1..Kn) as a candidate architecture.
- *Boundary, agency, and common knowledge* (5 rows): redefines "knowledge boundary" as the line between still-possible and ruled-out states; broadens "agent" beyond Person (negotiators, robots, computer components, wires, message buffers), proposing PERSON≠AGENT; adds the missing "WHO knows?" axis, formalizing the common-knowledge hierarchy (individual, E(P), E²(P), C(P)); uses the muddy-children puzzle to show a public announcement of an already individually-known fact still changes epistemic state by making it common knowledge; distinguishes externally-ascribed knowledge from algorithmic/computable knowledge.
- *Knowledge-state typology and UNKNOWN decomposition* (5 rows): proposes Semantic/Implicit Knowledge versus Operational/Explicit Knowledge as two fundamentally different knowledge states; decomposes "I don't know" into eight distinct causes (undetermined truth value, insufficient information, indistinguishable states, unrepresented logical consequence, unawareness, computational timeout, and others); illustrates Truth/Agent Knowledge/Agent Awareness/Computability/Evidence as five states that must be kept distinct via a worked example where all five hold simultaneously; defines distributed knowledge (no individual has enough information, but the group collectively does) via an Alice/Bob/Carol/Susan worked example; corrects an assumed monotonic knowledge model, showing knowledge can be gained or lost over time.
- *Kernel candidacy, formal tuple, and convergence verdict* (5 rows): classifies candidates into four tiers of Kernel-suitability (very strong candidates: Identity, State, Proposition, Truth valuation, Agent, Epistemic relation, Context, Time, Event, Transition, Observation, Local state, and more); warns against making possible-worlds semantics the Kernel ontology itself, since the authors disclaim it as uniquely correct; formalizes a candidate Knowledge Space tuple K=(S,P,π,A,~,T,E,C); uses Fagin et al.'s database "when should I-don't-know be returned?" question to argue question-answering is itself epistemic behavior; states the four-book (McGinn/Floridi/Dretske/Fagin et al.) convergent verdict that Knowledge Space is a context- and time-dependent space of states/propositions/agents/observations/epistemic relations/transitions.
