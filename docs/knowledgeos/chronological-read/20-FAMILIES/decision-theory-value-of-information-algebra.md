## Rationale
This object addresses whether "most probable outcome ⇒ best action" is a valid decision rule, concluding it is mathematically wrong in general and establishing the correct chain Knowledge → Prediction → Consequences → Utility/Risk → Decision [S0910]. Its central closing argument (regret analysis) connects the epistemic layer to the business layer: comparing a taken decision (e.g. Upgrade) against what would have been optimal in hindsight (DoNothing) lets KnowledgeOS assess how costly its own epistemic uncertainty actually was, motivating measuring ValueOfKnowledge rather than raw KnowledgeAmount — described as "a profound connection between the epistemic layer and the business layer" [S0910]. A separate, more practical rationale note addresses computational feasibility: the basic decision-theoretic calculations (EU, Risk, VOI, Sensitivity, ScenarioAnalysis) are normal-PC computable, and scaling concerns (large state spaces, many actions, long horizons, stochastic simulation, partial observability) are framed as complexity/scaling problems, not fundamental architecture problems [S0910].

## All rows (source_id order, grouped by theme — 43 rows condensed into 6 themes; full text in 03-CONTRIBUTIONS.jsonl)

**Theme 1 — Rejecting probability-only decision-making; utility, risk, and expected utility (8 rows, all `[S0910]`).** Establishes that "most probable outcome ⇒ best action" is wrong in general and the correct chain is Knowledge→Prediction→Consequences→Utility/Risk→Decision; distinguishes inference from decision (state space S, action space A); defines utility U(a,s), EU(a|E)=sum_s P(s|E)U(a,s), a*=argmax_a EU(a|E); worked example showing a bare 5% outage probability tells almost nothing absent the full consequence structure; rejects reducing Risk to Probability (ExpectedLoss=sum_s P(s|E)L(a,s), six additional real-risk dimensions); worked tail-risk example where identical expected loss masks vastly different catastrophic severity; notes utility functions (e.g. sqrt(x)) are a normative choice KnowledgeOS must not secretly invent; defines the seven-field DecisionContract=(Actions,States,UtilityModel,Constraints,RiskPolicy,Authority,EvidenceRequirements).

**Theme 2 — Authorization boundaries: Recommendation ≠ Decision ≠ Authorization; Lord/Sarathi separation (5 rows, all `[S0910]`).** States optimization occurs only within admissible actions (a*=argmax over A_admissible, not all of A); requires structured blocking-failure reasons (technical risk / governance authorization missing / evidence insufficient) rather than a bare "no"; states Recommendation≠Decision≠Authorization — an AI-computed optimum is only a recommendation; characterizes Sarathi as decision-support/orchestration producing a DecisionCandidate, with governance separately determining Authorize(DecisionCandidate); formalizes Lord's role as choosing between Action and InformationAcquisition across a four-option example (decide now, observe, simulate, ask a human expert) — "the system performs meta-decision-making."

**Theme 3 — Value of Information (VOI) formalized and connected to Zero (7 rows, all `[S0910]`).** Formally defines VOI via two equivalent expressions ("one of the most powerful mathematical ideas for the KnowledgeOS architecture"); worked example (EU(Upgrade)=100 vs EU(DoNothing)=95, VOI(Inspection)=20, Cost=5, NetVOI=15>0) shows AcquireInformation can itself be the rational action, "gives Zero a mathematical foundation"; refines Zero into Zero_epistemic vs Zero_decision (a gap matters only if resolving it could change something important — DecisionRelevantKnowledgeGap); worked VOI≈0 example (irrelevant Linux-version fact) shows Lord should do nothing, preventing pursuit of exhaustive completeness; states the architectural principle optimize for SufficientKnowledge, not CompleteKnowledge; formalizes i*=argmax_i[VOI(i)-Cost(i)] as "a beautiful mathematical formulation of the Lord loop"; worked example where an information-gathering action (production restart) itself carries risk, extending to NetValue(i)=VOI(i)-Cost(i)-RiskCost(i).

**Theme 4 — Exploration/exploitation, uncertainty typing, robustness (7 rows, all `[S0910]`).** States KnowledgeOS=PassiveKnowledgeSystem+ActiveInformationAcquisition (when authorized) as "a significant conceptual transition"; frames Action vs InformationAction as exploitation vs exploration (RL/active-learning analogy, without requiring KnowledgeOS to become an RL system); states VOI(i)>0 only yields Candidate(i), never automatic Execute(i); distinguishes aleatoric uncertainty (inherent randomness) from epistemic uncertainty (unknown model/parameter/missing evidence), with a worked 2-5%-aleatoric-vs-unverified-firewall example; defines Robustness(a) as decision stability across plausible models, argued as often more useful than a single expected-value number; contrasts a worst-case/minimax decision rule with a model-averaged EU rule, concluding "DecisionRule must be explicit."

**Theme 5 — DecisionPolicy, lifecycle, closed-loop verification, regret (7 rows, all `[S0910]`).** Proposes DecisionPolicy as an explicit eight-field DDD object preventing business-critical decision logic from disappearing into an AI prompt; defines a six-stage event-sourced decision lifecycle (Candidate→Evaluated→Recommended→Approved→Executed→Verified, with Failed/Reversed branches); defines closed-loop verification (compare ExpectedOutcome against ObservedOutcome, feeding Decision→Action→Observation→ModelEvaluation, "closes the entire system loop"); defines Regret(a,s)=U(a*(s),s)-U(a,s) as "a useful retrospective metric"; worked regret example (KnowledgeOS recommended Upgrade, DoNothing would have been better in hindsight) motivating ValueOfKnowledge measurement; worked three-item NetVOI prioritization table (E1/E2/E3) concluding Lord should prioritize E2; defines NBA=argmax_x ExpectedDecisionValue(x) over Actions+InformationActions+Escalations as a candidate core formal definition of Lord.
