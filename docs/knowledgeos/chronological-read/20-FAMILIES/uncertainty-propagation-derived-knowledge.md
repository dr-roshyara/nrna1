## Rationale
Warns against assuming a derived proposition inherits its premises' confidence wholesale; its epistemic quality depends on the inference kind, premise dependencies, and the inference rule's own uncertainty [S0865]. Shows via a linear-uncertainty-propagation example (y=2x, σ_y=2σ_x) contrasted with an abductive inference that the propagation function itself depends on the inference method, not a single universal formula [S0865]. Distinguishes rule certainty from premise certainty: a deterministic derivation rule does not itself add uncertainty, but its premises may still be epistemically uncertain — Deterministic rule ⇏ uncertain premises [S0865]. Restates that strongly correlated/copied evidence adds near-zero additional information, so dependency structure must participate in uncertainty/support propagation, not be treated as independent [S0865]. Notes a conservative policy may cap combined support below the independence assumption when dependency is unknown, but this inequality is policy-dependent, not a universal theorem — the kernel only preserves Dependency=Unknown [S0865]. Uses two 0.9-confidence propositions to show joint confidence ranges from 0.81 (independent) to 0.9 (perfectly dependent) — marginal confidence does not determine joint confidence, ruling out naive confidence multiplication [S0865]. Argues KnowledgeOS must store per-pair Dependency status alongside confidence scores rather than bare scores alone, called one of the strongest architectural consequences of the theory [S0865]. Worked backup-recoverability example showing that a missing premise (no restore test) leaves the derived proposition unresolved rather than falsely supported [S0865]. Shows a missing premise generates a Candidate(A3)+MissingPremise(A2) state that becomes a discrepancy Lord can act on, establishing the feedback loop Inference→MissingPremise→Discrepancy→EvidenceAcquisition — inference as another gap-discovery mechanism, i.e. KnowledgeOS can infer what it needs to know in order to infer what it wants to know [S0865]. Shows chained inference (A1→A2→A3→A4) behaves differently under probabilistic, deterministic, and defeasible rule regimes — inference depth affects reliability, but through no single universal formula [S0865]. Concludes uncertainty/derivation computation is tractable under five conditions (finite graph, explicit rules, represented dependencies, computationally-defined calculus, specified uncertainty semantics), while there is no universal algorithm for arbitrary natural-language inference — Computable kernel operations must be distinguished from AI-assisted semantic interpretation [S0865].
