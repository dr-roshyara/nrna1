## Rationale
[S0205] (FORMALIZATION/ANALYSIS): Proposes analogizing DDD bounded contexts to topological spaces using topology's notions of open/closed sets, boundaries, and neighborhoods: a concept can be inside a context, outside it (an unknown external concept), or at the boundary (a translation/anti-corruption layer) -- explicitly framed as an analogy, 'not identical, but useful,' that 'maps surprisingly well.'

[S0205] (FORMALIZATION/ANALYSIS): Sheaf theory is proposed as 'probably the most intellectually aligned topology concept' for KnowledgeOS: it handles local knowledge (e.g. Team A defines 'Payment' as transaction authorization, Team B as customer billing lifecycle -- both locally correct) that must be consistently combined into global knowledge; framed as 'almost exactly a KnowledgeOS problem' and 'extremely DDD-compatible', with the candidate kernel need 'preserve local meaning while enabling controlled composition.'

[S0205] (HYPOTHESIS/ANALYSIS): Strategic hypothesis: the KnowledgeOS kernel may not be 'a database of knowledge objects' but a system that preserves the topology of knowledge evolution -- what can change, what cannot, what remains connected, what becomes separated, what boundaries exist, and what transformations preserve meaning -- judged 'a much closer analogy to an operating system kernel' than a data-model framing.
