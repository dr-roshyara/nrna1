## Rationale
The evidence base attributes the following rationale to this object (as recorded, cited to its source):

- **[PRINCIPLE/ARGUMENT]** [S2528]: States epistemic non-transparency as the strongest single Williamson contribution: an epistemic property (knowledge, evidence, rational requirement) can hold without being accessible to the subject/evaluator, giving a formal reason Sat/Eval_c must not be assumed luminous and must not be confused with truth. Status: [DERIVED - very strong].
- **[DEFINITION/ARGUMENT]** [S2530]: Gives the formal definition of luminosity (a condition C is luminous iff whenever C obtains one is in a position to know it obtains) and the full sorites-style anti-luminosity argument (feeling-cold-at-dawn-to-hot-at-noon, imperceptible per-step changes) proving virtually no mental state is luminous, including love/hope examples generalizing beyond feeling-cold; grounds the epistemic-non-transparency principle with its original formal source material.
- **[ARGUMENT/CORRECTION]** [S2530]: Uses non-luminosity to dissolve philosophical scepticism (if evidence were luminous, evidence would be identical between ordinary and sceptical cases, but mental states are non-luminous so the argument fails) and explicitly rejects the phenomenal conception of evidence (a converging-to-the-empty-set sequence of ever-more-private appearances), translated as: KnowledgeOS's evidence must not be modeled as private/phenomenal appearances.
- **[ARGUMENT/EXTENSION]** [S2530]: Extends non-luminosity specifically to rationality (one cannot always know what rationality requires), translated as: KnowledgeOS cannot assume Determination is always accessible/transparent to the system itself.
