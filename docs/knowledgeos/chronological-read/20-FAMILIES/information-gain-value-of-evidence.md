## Rationale
Illustrates with a migration-safety question that a strongly-evidenced fact (Nexus version) can add little new information, while an unknown backup-status question could dramatically reduce uncertainty with a single answer — Highest Evidence Strength ≠ Highest Information Gain. [S0861] Argues ExpectedDiscrepancyReduction is more fundamental to KnowledgeOS than EntropyReduction, since KnowledgeOS's real objective is moving K_t toward I_t, not merely driving entropy to zero. [S0861] Gives a counterexample where entropy-reducing evidence about an irrelevant proposition A has high IG but near-zero EDR, while evidence about a decision-relevant proposition B would resolve a critical gap — InformationGain ≠ PurposeValue, so purpose must remain in the equation. [S0861] Shows via a cost/benefit contrast (Evidence A: EDR 0.9 at €10,000 vs Evidence B: EDR 0.7 at €10) that maximizing information gain alone is insufficient — Cost, Time, Risk, and Effort must also enter the evaluation. [S0861] Contrasts traditional RAG's Question→Retrieve→Generate pipeline with KnowledgeOS's Purpose→IdealState→Discrepancy→EvidenceValue→AcquisitionPlan→Evidence→Assessment→KnowledgeState pipeline as a fundamentally different architecture.
