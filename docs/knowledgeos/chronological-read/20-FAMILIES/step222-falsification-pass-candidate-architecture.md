## Rationale

Step 222's stated purpose is a deliberate switch from construction to falsification: it defines F(P) as the set of observations that would contradict a candidate principle P, and forbids declaring a principle "Supported" merely because confirming examples exist [S1550]. The gap it closes is the corpus's prior habit of accumulating candidate principles without testing them against counterevidence: each of the 8 named candidates from Steps 220-221 is run through this test, and for principles that fail cleanly (e.g. Identity!=State, Observation!=Interpretation, Knowledge!=Authority, Decision!=Execution) the correct fallback is not outright rejection but a more precise, context-scoped restatement (Architecture_intended != Architecture_implemented; Observation approximately Interpretation within a bounded context; Authority=f(Context,Role,Policy); LocalSemantics!=GovernanceSemantics) [S1550]. Semantic Integrity, judged the strongest candidate central concept, is subjected to the same test by naming rival explanations (Performance, Scalability, Security, OrganizationalPolitics) that would displace it if they better explained the corpus's architectural decisions [S1550], and is then operationalized into a falsifiable Boolean predicate specifically because the anti-vacuity principle ("a good architectural principle must exclude something") demands it — an unfalsifiable "everything is about semantic integrity" claim would be meaningless [S1550]. A later verifier pass (S1554) reconstructs why this operationalization mattered: it tracks (as TV-F-083) a three-stage unprompted self-repair of the corpus's own ill-typed central claim, from Step 215's unfalsifiable "SemanticIntegrity proportional-to DistinctionPreservation" through Step 219's well-formed-but-untested SI(K,T)=Preservation(CriticalSemantics(K)) to Step 222's SS222.12 falsifiable set-theoretic form — noting that none of the three stages cites or supersedes the earlier ones, so all three remain live as unreconciled alternatives [S1554]. S1554 also credits Step 222 with introducing conditional invariants (P|C) as an advance over the corpus's earlier universal-principle framing, since conditional invariants dissolve 4 of 5 documented minimal-conflicting-subsets (MCS) from the JOINT-SATISFIABILITY-REPORT [S1554], and independently reaches the same "contradiction reveals a bounded-context boundary" adjudication this verification programme had already applied elsewhere [S1554]. S1554's overall assessment is double-edged: Step 222 is rated the strongest single file in the last thirty steps for its self-repair, its correct falsifiability statement, and its refusal of three separate overclaim opportunities — but it is also faulted for performing no actual falsification: its falsification matrix and philosophical evidence matrix are left entirely unfilled, and F(P) is computed for zero of the 8 principles [S1554]. An executed Python test of the SI predicate (S1560) then supplies the missing empirical check S1554 says Step 222 itself never ran, and finds the operationalization only partially succeeds: SI is well-typed, decidable, and falsifiable (a genuine repair of TV-F-083), but is vacuously satisfiable when the critical-semantics set is empty, does not detect over-declared loss, is properly a ternary relation (so unqualified "T preserves semantic integrity" claims are ill-formed), and — the decisive defect — does not compose across chained transformations, which matters because the corpus's own motivating multi-stage AI pipeline example is exactly such a composition [S1560]. S1560's re-test of the conditional-invariant repair likewise corrects S1554's "4 of 5 MCS dissolved" claim: only 2 are genuinely dissolved by conditionalization, 1 is dissolved by an unrelated mechanism (relation-splitting, misattributed to context-scoping), 1 is relocated into an undefined condition rather than resolved, and 1 survives unresolved [S1560].
