## Rationale

The report reframes the underlying problem: rather than a fixable bug, the invariant system consists of roughly 500 invariants (of which one, I_Dependency, is genuinely decidable) spread across roughly 25 uncrosswalked registries, and the five detected conflicts are a symptom of that fragmentation, not the disease itself [S1548]. Conditional invariants I_i:C_i=>R_i structurally dissolve 4 of the 5 minimal conflicting subsets (MCS) found in JOINT-SATISFIABILITY-REPORT: MCS-1 (Unknown semantics) dissolved by scoping the requirement to the verification context; MCS-3 (Aggregate ownership, one owner vs three-context lifecycle) dissolved because 'owns' and 'participates' are different conditions; MCS-4 (Epistemic ordering, partial order vs total chain) dissolved because the chain holds within a context where states are comparable; MCS-5 (Entropy, filtration vs entropy growth) dissolved as different conditioning contexts yielding different H. MCS-2 (Retention: gate AND unknowable Q AND required discard) is explicitly NOT dissolved and remains a genuine governance conflict [S1554]. SS222.30 states the adjudication principle verbatim: a contradiction such as Identity!=State in one bounded context vs Identity=State in another may simply mean 'different domain semantics' -- the contradiction then reveals a boundary, giving Contradiction->ContextDiscovery. This is the same analytical move this verification programme independently applied in JOINT-SATISFIABILITY-REPORT-v2-ADJUDICATED (reclassifying MCS-3 as a semantic mismatch and MCS-5 as an ambiguity); two independent analyses converging on the same repair is recorded as meaningful convergence evidence, not mutual corroboration [S1554].
