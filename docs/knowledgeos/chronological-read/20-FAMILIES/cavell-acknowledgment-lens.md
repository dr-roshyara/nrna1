## Rationale
[S0417] (ARGUMENT/RESTATEMENT): Five headline findings applying Stanley Cavell (via Austin/Wittgenstein/aesthetics) to the Kernel: (1) knowledge and acknowledgment are not the same -- acknowledgment is a response to another's claim upon us, not reducible to 'knowing that p'; (2) what we 'must mean' by our words is a function of position in the language game, not intentions or definitions, so ignoring context/role/occasion risks storing signs without sense; (3) the experience of fraudulence (deciding genuine vs counterfeit) is a feature of the aesthetic/interpersonal, not a bug to be eliminated; (4) the problem of other minds is managed through acknowledgment, not solved by proof, so a system that only tracks 'knowing' may be structurally incapable of representing acknowledgment; (5) saying != meaning -- meaning is not in words alone but in the human position from which they are uttered, so propositions cannot be treated as self-contained units of meaning.

[S0417] (ANALYSIS): Ten Cavell observations (Part 1): acknowledgment vs knowledge (a system knowing 'that someone is in pain' may still fail to acknowledge them); words carry a categorial 'must' (implications like confidence/responsibility that are part of an utterance's meaning, not optional pragmatics); the ordinary is in plain view yet unseen, a failure of acknowledgment not discovery; fraudulence (deciding genuineness) is endemic and not a defect; the self is concealed in assertion, revealed in temptation/wish; criteria/justification come to an end at 'this is what I see/hear' as a necessary stopping point, not a failure; taste is partiality, a personal responsibility not lost objectivity; the data of philosophy's 'we say' is an invitation to test against oneself, not a sociological report; tragedy (Lear) is the failure of acknowledgment, not lack of knowledge; the audience is implicated in tragedy by misperceiving characters, refusing to see.

[S0417] (ANALYSIS): Five most-surprising observations: acknowledgment is not a kind of knowing, suggesting a separate Kernel category for claims (the standing of one person upon another) not reducible to propositions; the categorial 'must' of an utterance ('I know' is a claim of authority/responsibility, not just a mental-state report) means treating 'I know' as a mere propositional attitude misses its performative dimension; the ordinary is unseen through avoidance, an existential not epistemic failure, so more information will not fix it; fraudulence/trust/betrayal may need to be modeled as fundamental categories, not failures of verification; the audience's misperception implicates them in the tragedy, showing knowing is never neutral but participatory or avoidant.

[S0417] (EXTENSION/ANALYSIS): Five observations ranked most kernel-relevant: separate data structures for claims (requiring acknowledgment) versus propositions (which can be known); storing only the bare proposition loses the categorial meaning carried by the act of asserting it; the Kernel needs a concept of ultimate grounds -- a recognized boundary where justification ends, not a gap to fill; the Kernel may need to distinguish asserted propositions from positions (what the speaker is doing with/concealing behind the proposition); the Kernel must model avoidance -- the refusal to acknowledge what is already in plain view -- since more information will not address it.

[S2115] (ARGUMENT/EXPERIMENTAL-RESULT): Introduces Cavell's central structural objection ('what we need is not more knowledge but the willingness to forgo knowing') that knowledge and acknowledgment are categorially different (knowledge can be true/false, acknowledgment can be given/withheld), and measures the corpus: 'acknowledg*' never appears in typed form as an epistemic concept -- Acknowledgment is genuinely absent as an epistemic relation from the corpus.
