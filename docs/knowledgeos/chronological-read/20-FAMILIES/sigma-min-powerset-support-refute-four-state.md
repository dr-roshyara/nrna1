## Rationale
Argues Conflict is a mathematically necessary fourth epistemic state: when independently qualified evidence sets simultaneously support p (E+ |= p) and refute p (E- |= not-p), a three-state model {Unknown,Supported,Refuted} would have to arbitrarily choose one, losing information relevant to DetectContradiction and Resolve; hence a distinct Conflict state is required. [S1804] Proves minimality of the four-state structure by exhaustive deletion: removing any one of Unknown/Supported/Refuted/Conflict leaves some (S,R) combination unrepresentable, which breaks a mandatory operation's output (e.g. removing Conflict destroys DetectContradiction's output for the (1,1) case). Concludes |Sigma_0| >= 4 is forced and, since four states are sufficient, |Sigma_0| = 4 exactly. [S1804] Identifies five distinctions that collapse inside Sigma_0 by design rather than by oversight: never-asked vs asked-and-found-nothing (needs a missingness/asked fact), meeting different sufficiency bars (needs a policy bar in Gamma), accepted vs rejected (needs governance in Gamma), current vs superseded (needs lifecycle in Lambda), and currently-valid vs expired (needs a temporal evaluation). Reconciles this with so_exp05 and first-order gap SG-2 by distinguishing two different questions: 'how many distinctions must the SYSTEM preserve?' [S1805] ATTACK 9 (marked 'THE DECISIVE ONE') lands and directly resolves decision D-4 (is Sigma stored or derived?): OR-based merge (sigma1 ⊔ sigma2 = (s1∨s2, r1∨r2)) is monotone, so if Sigma is stored as accumulated state, a Refute-then-Retract sequence (evidence e1=Support, e2=Refute reaching Conflict, then Retract(e2)) leaves the stored value permanently at Conflict since OR can never clear a bit -- yet Step 272B's own operation set includes Retract, and correctly recomputing Sigma from the current evidence set after Retract gives Supported, not Conflict.
