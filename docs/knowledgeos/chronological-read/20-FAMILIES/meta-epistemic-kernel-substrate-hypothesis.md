## Rationale
Doignon and Falmagne's Knowledge Space Theory defines a domain Q of knowledge items and a collection of possible knowledge states K, giving (Q,K) an established, different mathematical meaning of 'Knowledge Space' than KnowledgeOS's broader intended sense (which asks how a computational system preserves, evolves, reconstructs and operates over knowledge-bearing states, evidence, participants, context and derived epistemic results, not merely which knowledge states are possible for an individual in a domain); recommends treating 'Knowledge Space' as a contested/established term requiring explicit disambiguation rather than renaming the KnowledgeOS concept. [S0630] Argues for retaining a full trajectory K_{t0}->K_{t1}->K_{t2}->... rather than only the latest snapshot, illustrated by an architecture-A-to-architecture-B migration example: without History(K_{<=t}), the system cannot answer 'what was known at t1?', a fundamentally different question from 'what is known now?' — supporting the historical-reconstruction hypothesis. [S0654] Distinguishes Knowledge extraction (may be probabilistic, AI-based, logical, statistical, human-assisted) from Knowledge storage: the Kernel may need to preserve the evidence and history from which extraction can be reconstructed, rather than only the extracted conclusion — illustrated by storing only 'Nexus runs version 3.69' losing how that conclusion was reached, versus preserving underlying information/relationships enabling later reconstruction of estimated-K_t; flagged as a strong argument for provenance and temporal history, though which parts are indispensable still needs proof. [S0659] Distinguishes documentation (which can record what happened, what was decided, why, which rule applied, who authorized it, which evidence supported it) as a carrier of justification, not the justification itself; argues the Kernel may need to preserve the justification relationship (Document -> Evidence/Decision/Rule/Provenance), not merely 'a document.'. [S0662] KST shows the same knowledge structure can be represented in multiple formally equivalent ways (family of feasible states K, surmise system sigma, entailment relation P, skill map tau), related by order-reversing Galois connections; the invariant is the knowledge structure itself -- the set of feasible states K, the domain Q, and the closure properties (for knowledge spaces) -- and the representations are equivalent because they all determine the same K. [S0677] Analyzes what KST stores as substrate (domain Q, structure K, response probabilities r(R,K), prior p(K)) versus what it reconstructs (latent state K_0, estimated state K-hat_t, uncertainty P(K_t|O_{<=t})); per KST's Theorem 10.24, reconstruction requires K, r(R,K), initial distribution L_1, the questioning rule V, and the updating rule u, but does NOT require the actual state K_0, the history of how K_0 was acquired, or the justification for K_0. The KST substrate is S_KST=(Q,K,O_{<=t},r,p,u), noted as one specific substrate, not necessarily the minimum. [S0680] Refines the summary's substrate/knowledge-attribution/measurement-outcome distinction into a longer chain Domain event -> Observation -> Recorded observation -> Interpretation -> Epistemic state -> Knowledge attribution -> Measurement/probability/logical evaluation, with RecordedObservation ≠ Knowledge, KnowledgeAttribution ≠ Truth, MeasurementResult ≠ Reality — called 'one of the strongest pieces of evidence... for keeping the Kernel below the semantic/evaluative layer'. [S0824] Argues KnowledgeOS 'should not silently encode a philosophical answer to what ultimately exists' — it must remain neutral between realism (reality=observer-independent state) and anti-realism (reality=operationally accessible observations), preserving enough substrate that philosophical interpretation occurs above it: Kernel ≠ World Ontology. Suggests distinguishing W (world/domain phenomenon) ≠ O (observation) ≠ M (measurement result) ≠ R (representation/model) at least conceptually — 'a very important research correction'. [S0824] Reading an unread-in-this-batch corpus document, reports its chain WorldState_t -> Observation_t^A -> InformationState_t^A -> EpistemicState_t^A -> KnowledgeAttribution (with preserved history H_{<=t}) and its participant/regime-relative K_t^{A,R}; explicitly distinguishes this from the dialogue's own new atomic-K_t hypothesis, treating the latter as a possible FURTHER refinement (one measurable statement per observation dimension) rather than something the attached document already established, and notes the document's own probability treatment P(K_t^A|F_t^A) as extraction-uncertainty, explicitly distinct from Knowledge itself. [S2309]
