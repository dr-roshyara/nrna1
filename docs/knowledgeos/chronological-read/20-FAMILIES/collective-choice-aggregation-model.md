## Rationale
Frames Step 87's central problem: how an organization derives one legitimate decision D* from multiple agents' differing knowledge/preferences/authority, without confusing majority preference with legitimate organizational decision [S0985]. States Arrow's impossibility theorem (no rank-order voting system can satisfy all classical desirable conditions with ≥3 alternatives) and draws the KnowledgeOS lesson that there is no universally perfect aggregation mechanism, so the organization must explicitly choose which properties to prioritize [S0985]. Concludes governance design must explicitly specify an AggregationRule rather than assume a generic 'correct aggregation' exists [S0985]. States that whether human and AI participants' preferences carry equal voting power has no universal mathematical answer — it is an institutional design decision [S0985]. States FormalAuthority ≠ EffectiveInfluence: the agenda-controller may hold substantial effective power without formal voting rights [S0985]. Argues information framing/selection (which evidence set a decision-maker sees) can change the decision outcome, making InformationSelection itself a governance concern [S0985]. Argues KnowledgeOS's provenance/evidence lineage lets it present both supporting and contradicting evidence together, mitigating selection bias [S0985]. Argues probability disagreements P_i(H) among agents should not be resolved by majority vote alone; evidence quality Q_i per agent may be needed [S0985]. Notes that a more principled evidence-weighted aggregation still requires the weight Q_A to have genuine semantics, not be invented arbitrarily (e.g. Q_A=0.9) [S0985]. Argues BestDecision means the authorized mechanism selected the action under the applicable decision model, not necessarily the highest-probability outcome [S0985].
