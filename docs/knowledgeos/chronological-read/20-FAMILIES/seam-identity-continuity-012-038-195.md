## Rationale

Resolves the standing transitivity problem for identity equivalence (cong_I), flagged unsafe by five independent loci (25J.27, 25S.6, 25S.29, 38.16, 38.47), via context-relativization: step-195's invariant I_48 states identity equivalence must be transitive within a defined identity context, justified by 38.6/38.7/38.81's principle that RealWorldIdentity != DomainObjectIdentity and identity is contextual (KnowledgeOS must not globally impose a GlobalEntityIdentity where a domain has intentionally separate identities). Conclusion: cong_I is an equivalence relation within an identity context and is NOT one across contexts, so quotients exist per-context and are undefined globally -- described as the first genuine closure recorded on the equality axis by this research programme. Two provisos: (1) this resolves identity equivalence cong_I, not semantic equality (whether semantic equality is transitive within a semantic context remains open, though 25I.11's context-indexed relation is the same move); (2) 'identity context' itself is nowhere defined, so I_48 is well-formed with an unbound parameter, the same defect shape as other unbound relevance/observation parameters elsewhere in the programme. Step 195.15 also names the violation pattern A cong B and B cong C and A not-cong C as an 'identity consistency violation', proposed as a candidate checkable invariant [S2135]. Additionally, Strengthens the Level-5 verdict on semantic equality (previously marked undecidable only for the weak reason 'no procedure specified'): step-012 section 35 gives the strong reason -- for arbitrary natural language, semantic equivalence P1≡P2 is not generally decidable, so KnowledgeOS cannot promise it will always determine whether two arbitrary sentences mean the same thing. This is framed as a theorem-shaped limit, not a work item: semantic equality can never reach a total Boolean decision procedure. Section 35 draws the corollary that the system must be able to say 'I don't know whether these mean the same thing' as a feature, not a failure -- the same rule independently found at three other loci (Step 224.11, 25S.4, 38.22), now four independent loci agreeing [S2135]. Further, Corrects a prior count of two rival equality-decision codomains to four: 25S.3 {Same,Different,PossibleSame,Unknown}; 38.10 {Unknown,Candidate,Probable,Confirmed,Rejected,Contextual}; 012 section 35 {Determined,Supported,Candidate,Unknown}; 012 section 36/46 {Equivalent,Compatible,Contradictory,Related,Independent,Unknown}. Step 012 section 36 supplies the sorting principle: 'the relation itself should not be confused with its epistemic certainty' -- distinguishing a Relation dimension (what holds) from a Status dimension (how well we know it); 25S.3 is flagged as conflating the two (Same/Different are relations, PossibleSame/Unknown are statuses). Corpus product forms are recovered (012 section 36/47): SC=(P1,P2,Relation,Context,Basis,Status), ComparisonResult=(Relation,Basis,Confidence,Context,Provenance), ResolutionResult=(CandidateEntity,Status,Evidence,Provenance) -- equality decisions return epistemic objects, not verdicts. A non-corpus reconciliation is offered (marked DERIVED, not corpus, not adopted): Decide_equiv: K x K -> (Relation x Status), noting three of the four corpus codomains are flattenings of this product -- the same defect shape independently noted for Sigma's ten-value flattening of five axes, a second (still unpromoted) occurrence of that pattern [S2135]. Relatedly, Restates the four rival result-domain codomains (25S.3, 38.10, 012 §35, 012 §36/46) and the Relation-vs-Status sorting principle (012 §36), applying the mandate's own distinction between 'decidable relation' (a total procedure that returns TRUE/FALSE correctly) and 'procedure that always returns a decision' (a total procedure may legitimately answer Unknown without deciding); boxes the resulting principle that an Unknown-returning procedure is NOT a complete equality decision procedure, and reiterates 012 §35's theorem-shaped result that semantic equality can never reach a total Boolean procedure, agreeing with three other independent loci (224.11, 25S.4, 38.22) that this is a feature, not a failure [S2141].
