## Rationale

Warns against probabilizing every assertion — a constitutional requirement is a matter of NormativeValidity, not probability, and assigning P=0.97 to it would make the representation worse. Not every uncertainty should be probabilized. [S0877] Shows interval propagation (y∈[3,5]) differs fundamentally from probabilistic distribution transformation for the same formula y=2x+1 — propagation semantics depend on uncertainty type, so a bare scalar confidence=0.8 loses critical information. [S0877] Shows uncertainty about the evidence itself (e.g. a possibly misconfigured monitoring system, or Beta-distributed source reliability) propagates into uncertainty about the supported proposition, forming a hierarchical statistical model. [S0877] Reaffirms RequiredEpistemicStrength=f(ActionRisk) — the same 0.95 confidence may suffice for a low-stakes suggestion but not for deleting production data, connecting to Step 19's risk-adjusted requirements. [S0877] Defines information-acquisition actions a_info:K→K' reducing (not necessarily eliminating) uncertainty, and VOI=EU(with info)-EU(without info)-Cost, framing this as 'Active Epistemic Management', a significant distinction from ordinary RAG systems. [S0877] Classifies uncertainty operations into three computability tiers (deterministic, statistically computable, AI-assisted), warning AI extraction never by itself makes an uncertainty claim mathematically valid. [S0877]
