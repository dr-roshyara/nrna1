## Rationale
This label's rationale-typed rows explain why the candidate-multiplicity pipeline needs a partial order and a bias-aware separation of Selection from Validation, rather than a single scalar winner: Argues for a partial order over hypotheses rather than a scalar 'best' score: two hypotheses with incomparable strengths (H1 stronger evidence/weaker explanatory power, H2 vice versa) neither dominates the other under a partial order, so both survive in the admissible set -- directly implicating the still-undefined epistemic-status ordering (>=) as this document's central bottleneck too. [S2460] Introduces the statistical selection-bias/multiple-comparisons/winner's-curse phenomenon into KnowledgeOS: selecting the best-scoring candidate from a large generated set can look impressive even when all candidates are pure noise, establishing 'best observed candidate != best underlying hypothesis' -- directly relevant to AI systems generating thousands of candidate explanations and picking the best apparent fit (best overfit). [S2460] Formalizes the anti-overfitting principle: the hypothesis maximizing training-evidence score need not maximize future/validation-evidence score, giving the architectural distinction Selection Evidence != Validation Evidence. [S2460] Notes that a generator which never emits the truth (four of seven tested) is a genuine epistemic failure mode in its own right, and correctly produces |A_t|=0 as the right outcome (confirming H7), not a measurement defect. ## All rows (source_id order)
This label's 51 rows come from six documents/stages of one continuous research sequence on "candidate multiplicity" (the critique and reworking of a biological "millions of hypotheses -> one knowledge" metaphor). Grouped by document/stage in chronological order; every source_id below is a full row for this label, condensed rather than quoted in full — complete text is in `03-CONTRIBUTIONS.jsonl`.

**Theme 1 — Critique and reformulation (16 rows: S2460 ×16).** Rejects four claims attributed to the "Million->One" framing: that a selected hypothesis constitutes Knowledge (selection gives at most `Accepted`, not `Knows` — the factivity problem is untouched); that "the field selects the best proposal" is meaningful before a comparison ordering exists; that exactly one candidate survives (the admissible set `A_Q` may have cardinality 0, 1, or >1); and that "most hypotheses are false" is a universal law rather than a generator-specific empirical property. In their place it proposes a four/five-stage pipeline (`Hypotheses -> Assessment -> AdmissibleCandidates -> Transformation`, later extended to `Generation->Assessment->Selection->Determination->KnowledgeAttribution`), redefines `Select` as power-set-valued (`H x V -> P(H)`), defines a candidate-reduction rate `rho_t` explicitly distinct from knowledge gain, argues for a partial order over hypotheses rather than a scalar "best" score, establishes `Selection != Determination` as a DDD boundary, introduces the statistical selection-bias/winner's-curse phenomenon into KnowledgeOS, requires `Selection` to be separated from `Validation` (out-of-sample evidence), and closes by proposing the KR-M2O-2026-09-02 experiment together with a five-function formal architecture (`Generation`, `Assessment`, `Selection`, `Determination`, `Knowledge Attribution`, each with an explicit type signature).

**Theme 2 — The KR-M2O experimental protocol (15 rows: S2462 ×15).** Specifies the twelve-hypothesis test matrix H1-H12 (more candidates does not necessarily improve quality; candidate reduction != progress; selection != validation != determination; a unique survivor does not imply Knowledge; multiple or zero survivors can be correct; "best candidate" requires an ordering; more candidates can increase overfitting; the pipeline stages may be separate responsibilities; no new primitive is justified if existing operations reproduce all behavior; the biological ratio has no automatic epistemological status). Mandates: preserving eighteen named distinctions unmerged; implementing `Selection` as strictly set-valued (forbidding argmax-only); investigating partial ordering before any scalar ranking; five-to-six experimental arms varying candidate count (1 to 10,000, optionally 100,000); fifteen controlled synthetic epistemic worlds with ground truth hidden from the agent; seven distinct candidate-generator types with empirically measured (not assumed) false-hypothesis proportions; explicit modeling of candidate dependence versus count; a selection-bias/winner's-curse control test comparing training vs. held-out performance; three explicit selection-vs-determination test cases; fourteen negative controls; and a derivability test against the existing KnowledgeOS operator vocabulary. Frames the decisive three-way kernel outcome (Redundancy / independent-but-derivable DDD responsibility / genuinely irreducible capability) and repeats the standing rule that kernel-minimality claims remain blocked pending a semantic-equivalence definition.

**Theme 3 — Experimental results (13 rows: S2465 ×13).** Reports: apparent training-fit quality of the best-selected candidate rising from 0.00 to 0.61 across candidate counts while held-out performance stays statistically indistinguishable from zero (confirms H1, H9); candidate count and evidence quantity shown to be statistically coupled (a genuine signal can become wrongly excluded as `n` grows unless evidence `m` grows as `2 ln n`); a self-correction narrowing an overclaim about evidence-vs-thresholds; H12 confirmed with measured false-hypothesis proportions (0.20-1.00) refuting any universal "most hypotheses are false" claim, plus confirmation that candidate count != candidate diversity; confirmation that a generator producing zero admissible candidates is a genuine failure mode, not a defect (H7); H8 confirmed by construction (Pareto/admissibility selection preserves genuine incomparability while scalar methods manufacture a unique winner as a scoring artifact); confirmation of H5, H6, H7, and H2 together; a result stronger than H3 — a decoy hypothesis with genuinely higher within-channel evidential signal is picked by both selection and independent validation on the same channel, and both are wrong; a second self-correction narrowing the validation-repair claim to be channel-specific; H11 confirmed (kernel outcome A, Redundancy — Selection is exactly reconstructed from three existing v1.2 capabilities, 200/200 agreement); and a final tally explicitly downgrading all twelve now-supported hypotheses from "established" to "supported," judged a methodologically weak result by the programme's own standard since the truly novel findings (multiplicity-evidence coupling; selection and validation agreeing while both wrong) were not among the twelve pre-registered hypotheses.

**Theme 4 — Follow-up channel-independence experiments (5 rows: S2467 ×4, S2468 ×1).** A follow-up test shows decoy-detection probability rising from 0.75% (same channel as selection) to 99.28% (independent channel), establishing that channel independence — not validation per se — does the epistemic work; proposes a candidate `ValidationContext=(Channel,Evidence,Model,Population,Time,Standard,...)` tuple and a `ChannelRelation` taxonomy (same / independent-under-stated-assumptions / partially-dependent / unknown / causally-coupled) distinguishing channel *declaration* from channel *independence*; and proposes extending `Select`'s signature to take a dependence-structure parameter `Lambda`. All of this is explicitly marked `[PROP]` (proposed, not adopted). One row restates the "Linga's Millions" metaphor's useful abstraction in light of these results as "multiplicity + competition + dependence + selection pressure" rather than "millions produce the best one."

**Theme 5 — Closing status and cross-reference (2 rows: S2469 ×1, S2478 ×1).** Lists the determination layer (`Hypotheses -> EvidenceAssessment -> Determination -> KnowledgeAttribution`, cardinality 0/1/>1) as stable; and restates a prior-batch finding (attributed to the Linga-Yoni experiment) that generation is not progress — a system can change state without that change being an improvement, motivating the need for an explicit progress ordering.

No theory amendment (v1.2) is recorded as accepted across any of these rows: the Selection kernel primitive is rejected as a new primitive (found derivable), a candidate-count parameter is left `[OPEN]` pending the epistemic-status ordering, and every follow-up proposal (`ValidationContext`, `ChannelRelation`, the `Lambda`-extended `Select` signature) is recorded `[PROP]`, not adopted [S2465, S2467, S2468].
