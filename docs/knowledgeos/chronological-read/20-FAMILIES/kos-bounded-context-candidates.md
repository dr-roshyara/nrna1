## Rationale
Analyzes Knowledge as BC candidate 1: strong semantics (knowledge object, claim, source, provenance, validity, version, supersession, authoritative/inferred state), strong invariants (AuthoritativeKnowledge->Provenance, CurrentKnowledge->Validity, Inference≠Authority), a lifecycle Observed->Imported->Proposed->Verified->Approved->Authoritative->Superseded, but an unresolved ownership question — since ArchitectureKnowledge and OperationalKnowledge may have different owners, 'Knowledge' may be a domain concept rather than one BC; verdict: Strong Candidate, ConfirmedBC=NotYetEstablished [S1026]. Analyzes Evidence as BC candidate 2: strong identity (answers 'what supports this claim'), invariants (Evidence->Provenance, Evidence->Producer, Verification->Evidence; historical evidence must not silently disappear), lifecycle Generated->Captured->Validated->Associated->Retained->Superseded/Expired; notes Evidence is consumed by nearly everything (Knowledge/Governance/Assurance/Agents) but high consumption does not itself imply a separate BC — whether it owns an independent consistency boundary remains open; verdict: Strong Candidate, ConfirmedBC=Unknown [S1026]. Analyzes Governance as BC candidate 3: very strong DDD characteristics — distinct language (policy, authority, decision, approval, delegation, exception, escalation, effective date, supersession), strong invariants, a coherent lifecycle Request->Assessment->Review->Decision->Approval->Effective->Superseded, and likely organizational ownership (GovernanceAuthority belongs to defined roles/bodies) — stronger evidence than mere tables; verdict: Very Strong BC Candidate, potentially Confirmed once ownership/implementation boundaries verified [S1026]. Analyzes Assurance as BC candidate 4: own language (rule, check, verifier, test, result, evidence, conformance, violation, verification), purpose DetermineConformance, lifecycle Rule->Check->Execution->Result->Evidence->Finding->Verification (noting Finding could belong to Assurance or Governance depending on ownership); distinguishes Assurance ≠ Governance via example 'dependency violates architecture rule' — Assurance determines Violation=True, Governance determines what to do about it; verdict: Strong BC Candidate [S1026]. Analyzes Engineering State (candidate 5: runtime/deployment/implementation state/infrastructure/observations/actions) as problematic because it belongs to many external systems (Git, Kubernetes, Nexus, CI/CD, Monitoring) — KnowledgeOS likely consumes rather than owns it, potentially owning only ObservationRecord/EvidenceReference; verdict: Supporting Domain, not necessarily BC, explicitly to prevent KnowledgeOS becoming an accidental replacement for Git/Kubernetes/monitoring [S1026]. Analyzes Agent Interaction (candidate 6: agent, session, task, tool, recommendation, execution, delegation) asking whether this is KnowledgeOS domain semantics or AI infrastructure; concludes AgentIdentity may belong to KnowledgeOS-facing integration while PromptManagement belongs to the agent harness; verdict: Supporting Context, not a core KnowledgeOS bounded context [S1026]. Analyzes Policy (candidate 7: rules, constraints, applicability, scope, exceptions, effective dates) distinguishing Policy (what must/should happen) from Decision (what was decided in a particular case), Policy->DecisionContext, leaving open whether Policy deserves its own BC independent of GovernanceDecision [S1026]. Analyzes Authorization (candidate 8: actor, resource, action, scope, delegation, permission, decision) as a classic identity/security domain likely belonging to enterprise IAM rather than KnowledgeOS; states the anti-pattern warning that KnowledgeOS should not recreate EnterpriseIAM, but consume an AuthorizationService instead [S1026]. Applies the domain-verb test to the four core candidates, finding each has strong distinctive verb sets (Governance, Assurance, Evidence, Knowledge as listed), refining the hypothesis that Governance+Knowledge+Evidence+Assurance are the strongest potential core contexts, with other concepts supporting/integrating; tentatively classifies Core (Governance, Knowledge, Evidence, Assurance) vs Supporting (AgentInteraction, EngineeringObservation) vs External (IAM, Git, CI/CD, Kubernetes, Monitoring, Nexus), explicitly a target hypothesis, not current-state fact [S1026].
