## Rationale
- [S1152] (ARGUMENT/DEFINITION) A system whose admission machinery is governed by a policy that nothing governs is the deepest defect the programme found, discovered independently four times; the ratified answer is stratification.
- [S1152] (ARGUMENT) The constitutional layer's Chapter V is the same idea in constitutional form: engineering evolution free below the articles; amendment of the articles only by ratified act; no weakening. Two traditions, one stratification, arrived at independently.
- [S1186] (EXPLANATION/EXAMPLE) Step 121 found the self-governing-policy gap first on the formal side: the machinery of admission was governed by a policy that nothing versioned and no one owned ('critical governance gap', the record says). The era's own freeze decisions -- stopping parts of the model mid-flight with no governing act -- were the same disease in behavioral form. The falsification pass would later catch the synthesized model repeating it, and the conformance pass would find its mildest cousin on the repository side. Four sightings; III.8 tells what the ratified architecture did about it.
- [S1292] (EXAMPLE/ANALYSIS) Traces the AcceptancePolicy (the object whose mis-layering caused the most recorded trouble in the programme) through all five layers as a worked example: as content it is L2 material at rest in K_t; as the governor of admission it is an L2 object with an L1 shadow (Article 3 forbids self-authorization, I-11 forbids ungoverned change); any enforcement would be L4 code that must first consult the governance record; and the failure the layering prevents is the programme's most-sighted defect (the policy that governs while nothing governs it, sighted independently four times, per Chapter III.8).
- [S1294] (ARGUMENT/PRINCIPLE) States the chapter's central answer to why self-modifying admission policy is not an uncontrolled system: four interlocking guards -- (1) mode separation (proposed policy is content only, force requires the approved in-force version; "a rule that is sort of in effect" is unrepresentable), (2) the change is a Decision under the full III.7 Decision-Contract machinery (no-averaging conjunction, Unknown->Block, Auth as an act, per A6), (3) versioning with a transition schema (change rationale, authority, effective date, superseded version, verification; forward-only supersession), and (4) recursive self-subjection (Constitution subset-of AuthoritativeKnowledge -- the governing documents obey their own provenance/authority/temporal-validity/traceability disciplines) -- making self-modification exist exactly once, as a narrow contract-guarded versioned human-authorized transition, with every other path a type error.
- [S1294] (EXPLANATION/ANALYSIS) Resolves the fourth of the four independent sightings of the self-referential-policy defect: the implementation-side constitution's stale in-force banner was found by governance investigation to mask a real (but untracked) ratification act -- the mildest form of the defect, since the act existed but was not carried on the artifact -- offered as a live demonstration of why guard 3 (recorded version-transition tracking) matters, since an untracked-but-real act still reproduces half the original disease.
