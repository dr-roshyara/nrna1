## Rationale
Cross-experiment diagnosis: five apparently separate failures (Sat_c:=value∘Eval_c collapsing 9 evaluation situations into U; Zero<=>Delta=empty naming three disagreeing predicates; Gap=pi_Q(boundary) attribute-lossy; DetectGap derivable from ZeroLens; Gamma cannot be factive because its domain excludes truth) are one repeated structural mistake: defining a projection and asking it to do the work of the structure it projects from, with the needed information sitting in the projection's kernel each time [S2436]. D-4 (invariant custody): a kernel reduction can shrink |K| while increasing the number of operators whose removal breaks an invariant -- eliminating Challenge moved custody of the invariant 'a verdict must be challengeable' from one operator to two; cardinality minimization is blind to this redistribution of invariant custody [S2436]. Kernel membership is a question about operations; operation semantics (delta), equality (=,~,cong_lambda) and the closure predicate are all open (the closure predicate itself shown three-way ambiguous in -B), and THM-10 shows cardinality is representation-relative -- so a kernel selected now would be a selection of a representation disguised as a discovery about knowledge; the kernel question is hard not because the answer is elusive but because it was asked first [S2436]. Gives a claim-by-claim verdict table on the Structure-First document: strongly agrees projection causes information loss and induced equivalence, rates Representation Adequacy and Invariant Custody as strong/promising research hypotheses, keeps the four structures as candidates only, and rejects the 'general mathematical theory' framing as too strong [S2466]. Restates factivity as the most serious unresolved theoretical problem: the desired Knows(a,p,c,t)=>True(p,c,t) cannot be satisfied because an epistemic state cannot determine external truth from internal contents alone, forcing a choice between Model A (KnowledgeOS models factive knowledge itself) and Model B (it models a broader epistemic state with a separate knowledge-attribution layer) -- experiments strongly favor B, but the theory has not yet formalized this properly [S2469]. Summarizes the kernel-minimality research trajectory (original candidate too small; DetectGap derivable; 14 then 12 irreducible-power counts under different representations; two same-cardinality minimal kernels; instability under semantic-granularity changes) into the methodological conclusion that kernel minimality cannot be decided by operator-counting alone, requiring fixed carrier + capabilities + observable behavior + semantic equivalence + invariant preservation together [S2469]. Proposes a structural parallel between KR-PROJ's induced-equivalence framework (x1~_pi x2 iff pi(x1)=pi(x2)) and KR-NEFF's candidate-space question (how many candidates are genuinely distinguishable under a regime), hypothesizing effective multiplicity may ultimately be a consequence of distinguishability/equivalence -- not yet aware of (or not referencing) the immediately-preceding KR-DIST experiment that tests and refutes exactly this construction [S2473]. Argues the Projection/Invariant framework should be moved earlier / treated as a parallel foundational lane rather than placed serially after D-E-F-G, since it is conceptually relevant to evaluating those very questions [S2479].
