## Rationale
Reports Dretske's account that a physical/informational structure can simultaneously carry many logically or nomically nested pieces of information (if F implies G, information(F) also carries information(G)), while a cognitive system must discriminate among those pieces and select one as the content of its higher-order state -- proposed as the content of the previously near-empty semantic map Phi. [S2324] Finds Dretske's relevant-alternatives account of knowledge (knowledge requires eliminating all RELEVANT alternative possibilities, where relevance is purpose/interest/context-dependent) structurally corresponds to KnowledgeOS's Zero(K,G,EC) test, framed as a correspondence hypothesis (not identity) and called potentially one of the most important convergences found; separately uses the same context-dependence of relevance to further justify the Ideal State's parametrization I_t(P,C). [S2324] Uses Dretske's examples of signals carrying a determinate quantity of information without a determinate propositional content to establish Information quantity != Semantic content != Knowledge quantity, concluding any attempt to define K_t as a scalar measure (entropy H(X), a probability P(X), or a bit count) is fundamentally too weak. [S2324] Cleanly divides remaining research labor between the two mathematical strands: Kallenberg/Shum answer what information is available, how uncertainty is represented, and how variables depend; Dretske answers what information is carried, what semantic content can be selected, and which alternatives matter; KnowledgeOS itself answers what knowledge state is constructed, whether it is sufficient, and what should be decided/authorized -- with probability treated as evidential support, never as knowledge itself.
