## Rationale
The document's overall purpose is to test Davidson's account of radical interpretation against gaps already identified in the KnowledgeOS theory, and it repeatedly finds structural correspondences rather than merely decorative analogies. Its foundational move frames Davidson's radical-interpretation problem — constructing an interpretation without already knowing the language's meaning or the speaker's beliefs — as structurally identical to a core KnowledgeOS problem, yielding Observation≠Interpretation, Evidence≠Meaning, Meaning≠Belief, with the key insight that Meaning/Belief/Truth must be reconstructed together rather than derived linearly [S2336]. It independently corroborates a separate (Rescorla) hypothesis that the KnowledgeOS kernel is a generative transformation mechanism, not a stored answer database, since Davidson's interpreter likewise constructs a finite generative theory from which interpretations of infinitely many utterances follow [S2336]. It draws two independent arguments for keeping RepresentationEquality(A,B) separate from SemanticEquivalence(A,B): Davidson's indeterminacy-of-interpretation result (distinct theories can fit all evidence equally well, giving epistemic uncertainty ≠ representational indeterminacy) [S2336], and the Fahrenheit/Celsius temperature-scale example (numerically different representations preserving the same relational/semantic pattern), extending the corpus's existing SNF-based non-collapse principle with a positive converse case [S2336]. It also explicitly threads a middle position on ontological pluralism: Davidson rejects radically incommensurable conceptual schemes (different representations ≠ different worlds) but the document is careful not to over-read this as licensing one neutral universal KnowledgeOS ontology, since Davidson denies any neutral common coordinate system entirely — ontology remains constrained by truth/evidence/language/relations, not unified [S2336]. The document's own stated most-important conclusion combines an unread-in-this-batch "Freedman" source's inference-scrutiny principle with Davidson's interpretation-scrutiny principle to reframe the central KnowledgeOS question from "how do we infer knowledge from evidence" to "how does an epistemic system construct, constrain, validate, and revise the interpretation through which evidence becomes knowledge" [S2336].
