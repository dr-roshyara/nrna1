## Rationale
- [S1664] (ANALYSIS/LIMITATION) Per-object computability audit for Entity, Dimension, Value and Proposition: Entity's semantic definition ('subject of knowledge') is conceptually clear but membership is not universally decidable without an explicit ontology/registration boundary; Dimension's structural validity is computable but semantic adequacy is not; Value membership is computable only where its value space V_D has an effective representation, ranging from trivially decidable (finite sets) through grammar-parseable (formal version strings) to fundamentally undecidable (open-ended conceptual domains); Proposition well-formedness is therefore CONDITIONALLY COMPUTABLE, contingent on all three membership predicates being computable.
- [S1664] (CONSTRAINT/ARGUMENT) Replay is conditionally computable under six named preconditions, and is proven to REQUIRE operation versioning (since changing an operation's semantics between (T1^2026) and (T1^2027) could replay the same history to different states, replayability implies immutable-or-versioned transformation semantics); Replay is also proven distinct from Reversal — if T is many-to-one (K1!=K2 but T(K1)=T(K2)), no inverse can uniquely reconstruct the original state, so Replay only reconstructs the forward computation, it does not recover an original state from an output.
- [S1664] (ARGUMENT/LIMITATION) Policy/authority, evidence, statistics and uncertainty are each shown to contain a non-computable normative core: Authorize(actor,action,policy) is decidable only if policy is fully formalized as rules, otherwise it is a possibly non-computable governance-decision input; evidence handling is computable in layers (retrieval/hashing/linkage computable, but Assess(e,A) may require a human or statistical model); a statistical formula's mere existence (e.g. Bayes' rule) does not make a KnowledgeOS quantity computable — a probability claim is UNDER-SPECIFIED without an explicit probability space (Omega,F,P); and Uncertainty is not computable until its semantics are fixed, since a bare number like U(A)=0.2 could mean probability, confidence, epistemic strength, error bound, entropy, or subjective belief — mathematically different things.
- [S1664] (FORMALIZATION/ANALYSIS) A 23-row 'hidden human judgment map' rates every major KnowledgeOS concept's current computability from green (canonical serialization, structural equality, hash identity, registry lookup, finite status membership) through yellow (typed proposition validation, value-space membership, provenance lookup, deterministic replay, transition validation, semantic equality) to red (entity identity resolution, status assignment, evidence assessment, authority determination, policy interpretation, truth, relevance, correctness, adequacy, minimality proof, uncertainty semantics, statistical quantities without a model) — presented as the step's most valuable single output.
- [S1664] (ANALYSIS/WARNING) DDD interpretation of the three-layer architecture: the domain model must distinguish ValueObject from DomainDecision from ExternalPolicy from Assessment, and must never hide a human governance decision inside an innocuously-named method like isValid() when the result actually depends on unmodeled authority judgment, which would violate the Ubiquitous Language boundary.
- [S1664] (ARGUMENT/CORRECTION) Critical negative result: formal mathematical notation for a predicate (Relevant(A), Minimal(K), Truth(A)) does not by itself imply computability — each remains semantically under-specified or merely meaningful, not decidable/computable, unless an actual decision procedure is supplied; this falsifies the implicit assumption that writing something mathematically is the same as making it computable.
