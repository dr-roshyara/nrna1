## Rationale
Argues a dimension's epistemic state needs several orthogonal axes (Dimension, Assessment, Value, Evidence), not one enum collapsing "investigated-and-absent" and "never-established" into a single NULL, using worked contrasting examples of the same dimension under different Assessment/Value/Evidence combinations [S0721]. Applies Zero to AI evaluation specifically: an AI's "no evidence found" must not be interpreted as "no dependency exists"; argues AI systems must be evaluated on what they failed to discover, not only on what they extracted — a fundamentally different evaluation problem [S0721]. Distinguishes a "known gap" (a specific missing dimension is identified) from an "unknown gap" (the set of missing dimensions is itself partially unknown), generalizing to recursive uncertainty about unknown dimensions, unknown relationships, and unknown interpretations — concluding `Zero(K_t)` may itself contain unresolved structure and therefore should not be an ordinary database field [S0721]. Describes Lord and Zero as near mathematically dual: Lord=Ω (the ideal space), Zero=Ω−K_t (the boundary, itself partially unknown since Ω is not fully enumerable) — Lord defines the horizon, Zero examines the system's inability to see the entire horizon [S0724]. States a major architectural insight from the Gita-derived Chapter 2 material: the Zero Lens and Krishna Lens are interdependent, not independent — Zero's role is to create/identify the crisis (Arjuna's despair is Zero manifesting as a missing framework), and Krishna's role is to fill that identified gap, functioning as a coupled pair in the epistemic lifecycle [S0745].
