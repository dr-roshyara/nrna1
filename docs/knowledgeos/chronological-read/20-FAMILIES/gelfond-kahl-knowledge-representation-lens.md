The earlier flat UNKNOWN category should become an Epistemic Status taxonomy: true/false/unresolved-alternatives/not-represented-unaware/defeasibly-inferable-but-blocked/etc; the exact taxonomy remains a research question but the distinction itself is now strongly grounded." (anchor: "KnowledgeOS should not have one universal "knowledge confidence" number.")

- [S0563] types=[PRINCIPLE, EXAMPLE] scope=THEORY-LEVEL — "Default reasoning (e.g. 'normally car is not broken') shows that a new observation can withdraw a previously derived default conclusion while the historical observations and the default rule itself remain unchanged — only the derived epistemic projection changes. This yields the principle that a knowledge projection must support retraction of derived conclusions without rewriting historical inputs, and implies three things KnowledgeOS must preserve separately: INPUT HISTORY (observations/sources/rules/assumptions), DERIVATION HISTORY (what was inferred, under which regime, from which inputs), and CURRENT PROJECTION (what is currently supported/derived) — proposed as a major Kernel principle." (anchor: "A knowledge projection must support retraction of derived conclusions without rewriting the historical inputs from which those conclusions were previously derived.")
- [S0563] types=[FORMALIZATION, DISTINCTION] scope=THEORY-LEVEL — "A knowledge snapshot is reconstructible from history<=t, domain model D, reasoning regime R, and participant A: K_t^A = Project(H_{<=t}, D, R, A) — a stronger form of the earlier S_t^A equation. Distinguishes World state (W_t), Agent epistemic state (K_t^A), and Knowledge projection/view (V_t^A / P_t^A) as different things: the world can change without the agent knowing, and the agent's knowledge can change without the world changing." (anchor: "K_t^A = Project(H_{\le t},D,R,A)")
- [S0563] types=[EXTENSION, EXAMPLE] scope=OBJECT — "A diagnostic agent stores observations + its own actions + history, detecting new observations incompatible with possible past trajectories and searching for explanations involving previously unobserved exogenous actions (e.g. expected bulb lit, observed not-lit, possible explanations: bulb broke/relay broke/surge occurred). A knowledge gap is therefore not merely UNKNOWN but can be 'observation incompatible with current model', giving rise to diagnostic hypotheses — proposing a richer set of epistemic situations (SUPPORTED, REFUTED, UNRESOLVED, INCOMPLETE, CONFLICTING, UNEXPECTED, DEFAULT-SUPPORTED, PROBABILISTIC, HYPOTHESIZED, OUT-OF-SCOPE) that must remain outputs of different analytical regimes rather than one universal enum." - [S0563] types=[DEFINITION, RESTATEMENT] scope=THEORY-LEVEL — "Refined working definitions: Knowledge is a bounded, time-dependent epistemic state of a participant or collective, oriented toward a part of the unbounded Knowledge Space, represented by commitments, observations, assumptions, rules, available alternatives, evidential support, inferential consequences, and applicable uncertainties under an explicitly declared epistemic regime — the representation is not identical to knowledge itself. Knowledge Space is the unbounded space of entities, states, events, concepts, relations, institutional structures, propositions, explanations and possible connections toward which finite participants orient observation/reasoning. The KnowledgeOS Kernel is the domain-independent preservation substrate making bounded knowledge projections reconstructible (identity, context, boundary, participant, time, inputs/observations, content references, commitments/assessments, provenance, relations, conflicts, transitions, history), leaving semantics/reasoning to explicit external epistemic regimes."
