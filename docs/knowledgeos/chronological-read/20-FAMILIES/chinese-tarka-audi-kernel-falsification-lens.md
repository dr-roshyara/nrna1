## Rationale
Ten Chinese-tradition observations against the Kernel's Western assumptions: (1) names are normative not descriptive (Kongzi's zhengming), (2) identity is relational not intrinsic (Xunzi/Confucianism role-based identity), (3) change is continuous not discrete (Daoist hua vs discrete state transitions), (4) contradiction is not necessarily error (Zhuangzi perspectivalism: some contradictions are legitimate differing perspectives), (5) justification is practical (Mohist non-correspondence truth: a claim is true if it works), (6) context is constitutive not external (Zhuangzi/Xunzi), (7) knowing and acting are inseparable (Xunzi: knowledge as capacity not possession), (8) authority is relational/role-based (Confucianism), (9) continuity can be transformation (Zhuangzi: sameness via continuous process, not essence), (10) absence and withdrawal are different (Daoism) -- each posed as a challenge the Kernel's naming/identity/change/contradiction/justification/context/knowledge/authority/continuity/retraction treatment must answer [S0405]. Ten Tarka-Nyaya observations for testing the Kernel boundary: (11) a defining characteristic must avoid avyapti (under-covers), ativyapti (over-covers), asambhava (cannot apply); (12) reason must establish the conclusion via the paksa-sadhya-hetu discipline (proposition/reason/example/application/conclusion); (13) vyapti (invariable concomitance) must be tested in both directions (anvaya/vyatireka); (14) five defective reasons (savyabhicara/viruddha/satpratipaksa/asiddha/badhita) give precise argument-defect vocabulary; (15) absence claims need a counter-correlate (pratiyogi) and a specified kind of absence; (16) viparyaya (erroneous apprehension) shows representation is not truth -- the Kernel must not silently convert representation into semantic truth; (17) samsaya (doubt: mutually contradictory attributes in the same substratum) must be kept distinct from contradiction and error; (18) nigrahasthana (argument failure points) catalogs precise failure modes (abandoning/changing the proposition or reason, etc.); (19) the conclusion (nigamana) must not exceed what the premises establish; (20) abhava (negation/non-existence) has multiple types (absolute/prior/subsequent/locus-specific absence) [S0405]. Ten Audi (Western epistemology) observations: (21) justified true belief need not be knowledge (Gettier cases) -- Kernel must handle justified false beliefs differently from knowledge; (22) multiple basic sources of knowledge (perception/memory/introspection/reason/testimony) must not be collapsed; (23) direct (non-inferential) vs indirect (inferential) knowledge must both be handled; (24) justification is prima facie/defeasible (undermining and overriding defeaters); (25) knowledge does not require certainty; (26) internalism vs externalism is a real distinction the Kernel must take a position on; (27) memory preserves but does not create knowledge -- preservation vs generation must be distinguished; (28) testimony transmits knowledge via the attester's knowledge, but testimony-based justification can arise independently; (29) coherence alone is not sufficient for justification; (30) skepticism (hallucination/illusion/dreaming) challenges knowledge claims -- the Kernel must handle uncertainty/fallibility without collapsing into skepticism [S0405]. Five most-surprising observations: normative naming (names prescribe, not just classify); contradictions are not necessarily errors (perspectival difference vs logical inconsistency); absence != withdrawal (a claim no longer existing vs no longer assertable); knowing is becoming, not possessing; the smallest boundary may be defined by what it must NOT include (per Tarka's ativyapti/asambhava tests) [S0405]. Five observations ranked most relevant to a minimal Knowledge Kernel: identity is relational not intrinsic; justification is practical (consequence-based, not merely internal-consistency-based); change is continuous (versioning/state management must handle continuous transformation, not just discrete states); context is constitutive (must be internal to knowledge representation, not external metadata); naming is normative (must be handled as part of the knowledge itself) [S0405]. Ten questions conventional DDD analysis may not expose: whether bounded contexts/boundaries are actually stable or shift with context; whether identity is a relation rather than an aggregate property; whether names are normative rather than stable identifiers; whether contradiction can be a legitimate perspective difference rather than an error; whether justification is fundamentally practical/consequence-based rather than internal; whether context is constitutive of knowledge rather than external; whether knowledge is a capacity ('becoming') rather than a possession; whether absence has multiple types rather than being uniform non-existence; whether 'retraction' is a continuous process rather than a deletion event; whether certainty is required for knowledge at all [S0405].

S1-F023's self-labelled inference that S1-F018's Entity/Value-Object dilemma is an asambhava case (a definition describing something that cannot exist) is independently corroborated: S2-R-F018.1 established from law (not from the Tarka taxonomy) that the dilemma's option set omits the route the law actually takes -- EvidenceLinks as a bounded Collection of external-content references under C-3 -- which is exactly an external-but-atomic member the dilemma's options cannot represent; recorded as a possible independent arrival (third in the review series's register) [S1276].
