## Rationale

The S0931 material argues that once participants know how KnowledgeOS evaluates evidence, they may strategically optimize behavior against the evaluation mechanism itself — Goodhart-style epistemic gaming [S0931] — and analogizes intentionally arbitrary or contradictory evidence-producing actors to Byzantine fault tolerance [S0931], while explicitly rejecting wholesale import of Byzantine-consensus quorum formulas and keeping only the non-universal-honesty principle (see row list). Two robust-decision alternatives to a single expected-utility optimum are formalized as a response to this: minimax regret, "useful when worst-case regret matters more than expected performance" [S0931], and distributionally robust optimization over a ball of distributions near an empirical/reference distribution, as "a middle ground" between full minimax and point-estimate optimization [S0931].

The S0993 material reframes the underlying problem one register down, from epistemic evaluation to system security: it argues "Fault tolerance ≠ Security: a reliable system survives crashes, a secure system must also survive intentional manipulation" [S0993], building on the immediately preceding Model→Specification→Implementation and invariant-preservation-under-concurrency/failure steps. It extends the non-independence-of-evidence reasoning already established for epistemic aggregation into a security concern: "Evidence_1 and Evidence_2 may not be independent. If both originate from the same compromised system ... apparent corroboration may be illusory" [S0993].

### Theme 1 — S0931, Step 37, "Adversarial Epistemology, Epistemic Integrity, Trust, Manipulation and Strategic Behavior" (79 rows)

- **Error taxonomy & neutral-source removal** (7 rows, e.g. S0931 first four rows): distinguishes RandomError/SystematicBias/StrategicManipulation as mathematically distinct kinds; removes the neutral-source assumption so evidence-quality assessment must consider source incentives, illustrated with a vendor-conflict-of-interest worked example and a shared-upstream-origin worked example; defines a common-cause predicate extending provenance into a dependency graph for statistical aggregation.
- **Six-dimensional epistemic integrity** (7 rows): defines epistemic integrity as preservation of the reality-to-decision relationship, decomposed into six dimensions (Evidence/Provenance/Semantic/Inference/Validation/Decision Integrity — see `EI=(...)` notation), each illustrated with a worked violation example (misattributed source; meaning-altering transformation; good-evidence-bad-conclusion; gamed test; unauthorized threshold manipulation) and an eight-dimension epistemic attack surface broader than conventional cybersecurity.
- **Strategic-actor model & Goodhart gaming** (6 rows): formalizes a strategic-actor/game-theoretic model; argues participants can optimize against known evaluation mechanisms (Goodhart-style epistemic gaming), worked with a metric-gaming/evidence-volume-inflation example and an ADR-count KPI example; states the epistemic Goodhart problem requires periodic meta-validation of the metrics themselves.
- **Missingness & absence-of-evidence** (7 rows): defines strategic reporting/selective disclosure (missingness itself carries information), connects it to the MCAR/MAR/MNAR taxonomy, formalizes selection bias, requires an explicit missingness-mechanism model, and distinguishes NoEvidence from EvidenceOfAbsence via a detection-capability predicate and a monitoring-alert worked example; extends the observability model from earlier identifiability work.
- **Compositional integrity & weakest-link principle** (4 rows): names the failure mode of correct inference proceeding from adversarially corrupted evidence; states downstream correctness cannot repair an upstream integrity failure; formalizes integrity as compositional (with an explicitly simplified Boolean form) and states the weakest-link principle.
- **Contextual, non-scalar trust** (10 rows): requires trust to be contextual rather than a static per-source scalar; distinguishes trust from truth; ties trust to Step 36's drift model; states reputation is evidence not proof (99%-reliability example); requires independent corroboration for high-criticality assertions via a graded independence-status taxonomy; warns of self-validating circular-trust structures and reputation-gaming via low-stakes-then-high-stakes-false-claim; formalizes trust decay and a trust-update pathway via meta-evidence; formalizes contextual reliability as a function of source/task/context.
- **Byzantine analogy & robust aggregation** (5 rows): analogizes adversarial evidence-producers to Byzantine fault tolerance but rejects wholesale quorum-formula import, keeping only the non-universal-honesty principle; defines robust-aggregation alternatives to the mean; distinguishes a legitimate statistical outlier from adversarial suspicion; distinguishes robustness (sensitivity reduction) from a truth/correctness guarantee; defines strategic model manipulation via chosen adversarial input.
- **Adversarial uncertainty & robust decision-making** (7 rows): introduces adversarial uncertainty as a third kind distinct from aleatory and epistemic uncertainty, illustrated with a worked example; restates minimax robust decision-making; formalizes minimax regret and distributionally-robust optimization as alternatives (warning that overly broad uncertainty sets over-conservatively bias minimax); formalizes the strategic information game between an anticipating actor and the decision system; extends the response into mechanism design for incentive-compatible truthful reporting, framed as a governance rather than purely technical problem, distinguishing what KnowledgeOS should provide from what governance/organization must determine.
- **Authenticity vs. truth vs. reliability** (7 rows): defines accountable knowledge via full claim attribution; distinguishes cryptographic non-repudiation from epistemic truth; states authenticity does not imply truth (authentic falsehood); defines a four-way distinction among authenticity/source reliability/support/validation, illustrated with an executive-email worked example; presents an eight-dimension epistemic integrity matrix where no dimension substitutes for another.
- **Falsification tests & closing principles** (9 rows): runs falsification tests 1–12 in three grouped experiment rows (all PASS — authenticity vs. reliability; non-independence of shared-upstream sources; no-alert ≠ no-failure; authentic-but-incorrect document; Goodhart-style metric degradation; domain-specific reliability; MNAR withholding; gamed validation flagged; compromised-upstream degrades downstream assurance; model-uncertainty surfaced; metric-gaming flagged; undetectable-mechanism absence correctly classified); records the Step 37 self-verdict PASS; defines an extended six-member uncertainty taxonomy (adding semantic, selection, adversarial); states six boxed Step 37 principles (authenticity/trust ⇏ truth; independent identities ⇏ independent evidence; absence of observation ⇏ absence of phenomenon absent observability; a validated transformation is only as trustworthy as its critical dependencies; epistemic metrics can be gamed; no assumption of universal honesty); presents an updated architecture diagram with an explicit AdversarialEnvironment box affecting every layer; states the deeper conclusion that this is a substantial transition from uncertainty-only to uncertainty-plus-strategic-conditions modeling.
