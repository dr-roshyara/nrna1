## Rationale
Challenges the framework's use of the word 'established' for facts feeding logic/rules: a fact can have a graded epistemic state (e.g. P(F1)=0.8) even while the logical rule F1 and F2 => K applying to it is completely valid; therefore certainty of the rule, certainty of its premises, and certainty of the conclusion are three distinct things -- this is the combination problem the framework identifies but has not yet solved. [S0679]

Gives an explicit verdict on the uploaded framework document: five strong points (correctly separates probability from Knowledge; correctly identifies conditional evidence; correctly identifies conditional reasoning; correctly keeps this as research rather than architecture; correctly demands cross-framework comparison) and six points needing correction (P(F|O) too simplistic; 'established facts' should not automatically be treated as deterministic; listed common properties are not yet proven invariants; the common algebraic structure is premature; epistemic state may be multidimensional; determination is currently missing as an explicit mathematical object). Also explicitly agrees with the framework's constraint: 'Do not design the Kernel. Do not change its boundary. Challenge the architecture; do not become its architect.' [S0679]

Problem 1 (Information is not automatically evidence): 'Port 8081 is open' is an observation, not proof that Nexus is available -- it is evidence supporting that possibility only, since alternative explanations exist (another process on the port, network-inaccessible service, outdated information, wrong server); therefore Observation != Evidence != Fact. [S0684]

Problem 3 (the rules for determining something matter): given a five-criterion migration-readiness policy (dependencies/network/backup/certificates/rollback) and mixed evidence strength per criterion (Strong/Strong/Uncertain/Missing/Strong), whether 'migration ready' can be asserted cannot be read off the data alone -- an explicit rule for determining readiness is required. [S0684]
