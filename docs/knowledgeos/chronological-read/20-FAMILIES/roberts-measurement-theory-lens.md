## Rationale

Names Roberts and Aggoun & Elliott as now fitting together well (Roberts: when is a numerical measurement mathematically meaningful and what are its scale/uniqueness properties; Aggoun & Elliott: measurable spaces/filtrations/conditional expectation/change of measure/filtering for a probabilistic state-estimation regime), but identifies six category errors in the same prior synthesis (S0574): (1) Knowledge Space is not automatically a probability space — Ω must be split into Ω_D (domain/world) and Ω_E (epistemic state space), with a probabilistic regime optionally adding (Ω_D,𝓕_D,P); (2) filtration is not knowledge — ℱ_t^A -> K_t^{A,R} via a regime R, not directly; (3) conditional expectation is not the universal projection — it is 'the mathematical heart of the filtering regime, not of KnowledgeOS as a whole'; (4) different participants do not necessarily require different probability measures (P_A=P_B=P with ℱ_t^A≠ℱ_t^B suffices); (5) non-absolute-continuity is not universal epistemic incomparability; (6) martingale≠stable knowledge, submartingale≠learning, supermartingale≠forgetting, quadratic variation≠knowledge noise, and the SDE is a regime choice, not the evolution law of knowledge [S0576].
