## Rationale
- [S1629] (ALTERNATIVE, OPEN-QUESTION): Poses two mutually exclusive identity models for Revise (identity-preserving: same entity changes state; replacement: effectively Remove+Add of distinct entities) with different lineage consequences, concluding revision cannot be formalized without an explicit identity rule choosing between them.
- [S1629] (ARGUMENT, DISTINCTION): Establishes 3 findings for Supersede: it requires stable identity for both operands (Supersede implies Identity, without settling whether identity lives in K); temporal event-ordering (t1<t2) must not be conflated with semantic supersession precedence (x2 succ x1) -- they are logically independent; and provenance propagation under supersession cannot be assumed equal between old and new, requiring an explicit rule.
- [S1629] (ALTERNATIVE, OPEN-QUESTION): Poses 3 mutually exclusive identity semantics for the result of Merge (entirely new identity, one source's identity retained, or a composite function of both sources' identities), none established by the corpus, concluding Merge cannot be fully typed until this is resolved.
- [S1629] (ANALYSIS): Aggregates the per-operation dependency findings, showing Identity is required by 3 of the 4 discriminating operations (Supersede, Revise, Merge) and Provenance potentially by a 4th aspect (Merge), tentatively elevating Identity to a more fundamental kernel candidate than some earlier components -- explicitly flagged as a candidate, not a proven conclusion.
- [S1629] (DISTINCTION, ARGUMENT): Draws a critical architectural distinction between an operation's implementation inspecting history internally (That(H1)!=That(H2)) versus history actually affecting the resulting semantic state (F(That(H1))!=F(That(H2))) -- only the latter disproves state sufficiency; an operation may legitimately look at history while still producing an observably identical state.
- [S1635] (ARGUMENT, OPEN-QUESTION): Proves Composability of Merge over the quotient is a distinct congruence requirement (representative-independence: equivalent inputs must produce equivalent Merge outputs), not automatic from mere quotient existence; if it fails, Merge simply does not descend to a well-defined K* x K* -> K* operation, requiring one of 5 named remedies (a richer K, additional external context, a restricted Merge domain, a partial operation, or a different semantic interpretation).
