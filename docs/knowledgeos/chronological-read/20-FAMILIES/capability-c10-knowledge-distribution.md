## Rationale
Headline finding: of a 20-capability inventory (C-1..C-20) derived from evidence of what the platform actually does, four capabilities (C-5 separation attestation, C-10 knowledge distribution, C-14 policy enforcement, C-19 communication composition) have no declared owner -- proposed as the real substance of ADR-AIP-04, in contrast to the six proposed roles. [S0091] Finding F-5/F-5a: BC-7's HANDOFF tokenRef is performing de-facto knowledge distribution it does not own, evidenced by EKS-01 where a lane was taught a superseded path via its own workflow record's tokenRef -- the strongest evidence for a distinct C-10 (Knowledge Distribution) capability rather than 'Communication' in the ordinary sense. [S0091] 'Communication' as commonly discussed is proposed to be two distinct capabilities: C-10 Knowledge Distribution (a genuine unowned mechanism, evidenced by EKS-01) and C-19 Communication Composition (a stewardship, failing the bounded-context test on language and reason-to-change); a single 'Communication Engineer' role would wrongly bind a mechanism gap and a reporting duty into one actor. [S0091] A pattern of substantive external input (a cited review, a 925-line brainstorming document, external research) repeatedly arriving outside the governed channel is recorded as evidence potentially bearing on C-10, but explicitly not assumed to be owned by C-10 without further decision. [S0096] Structural finding: C-5, C-10, C-14, C-19 are not four independent candidate bounded contexts but four capabilities within one control loop -- BC-1's policy/knowledge is delivered by C-10 to a gated session, C-14 gates the act, C-19 expresses the outcome, and C-5 attests who acted and how separately -- while still having four distinct reasons to change, so the relationship, not a merged boundary, is what explains them. [S0102] C-10's smallest coherent capability boundary is proposed to be applicability determination plus receipt (not the whole created->published->selected->retrieved->delivered->acknowledged->applied->verified path), since creation/publication is BC-1's, retrieval/bootstrap is BC-6's, and only applicability-determination and receipt-evidencing are genuinely unowned. [S0102] C-19 fails a ten-part bounded-context test on six of ten criteria (own language, independent lifecycle, independent authority, authoritative state, distinct reason to change, audience/consent/channel concepts) and is classified a stewardship/cross-cutting expression concern; the one property that could give it authoritative state (delivery-with-acknowledgement) actually belongs to C-10, so realizing C-10 reduces rather than increases the case for a Communication bounded context. [S0102] Governance recommends C-10 existence = YES, arguing that D1's decided receipt-claim boundary presupposes a subject for that claim, supported by a distinct reason to change, a distinct outcome, and two genuinely unowned stages sharing one purpose (applicability + receipt) -- this recommendation is later overturned by the PO/ARB (S0120 D2) as conflating a decided semantic with a performed capability. [S0111] Applying the ten-question existence test to the estate's evidence: of C-10's five constituent activities (applicability, delivery, acknowledgement, claim-scoped receipt, invalidation/reconciliation), only one occurs and it is owned by other contexts (BC-6/BC-7), while four never occur at all and have no owner -- the counter-evidence supporting the later NOT YET ESTABLISHED verdict. [S0111] The sharpest finding in the D3 prep: a C-10 receipt's binding to a specific session execution can never be stronger than what INV-ATTR-1/INV-ATTR-2 permit (self-declared, not attested), so identity binding cannot be placed inside C-10's authoritative state -- it is C-5-shaped, and C-5 owns no attestation record either. [S0112] Recommendation: Option C (SPLIT) for D3 -- C-10 would own only the receipt/attestation lifecycle and state (receipt identity, lifecycle state, invalidation, completeness), explicitly excluding applicability determination, delivery transport, session execution, identity binding, dispute adjudication, execution authority, and compliance/application. [S0112] Selecting an enforcing response (HALT or BLOCK) for any missing-knowledge condition would convert C-10 into a control-plane capability by consequence (per the canonical analysis's own control-plane test: control-plane if it gates START), the same back-door category-deciding risk D3's non-constitutive clause was designed to prevent. [S0113] Decisive practical-arithmetic finding: because D2 established C-10 as NOT YET ESTABLISHED, zero receipts currently exist, so every governed act is currently in the Missing-receipt condition -- meaning any blocking/halting response to Missing would halt the entire estate on the day it is enabled, not selectively. [S0113] A 'compensating control' option is elevated from underrated fallback to preferred transition mechanism, on the observation that the estate's own recorded human START act is already, in practice, a compensating control -- a human authorization substituting for absent machine enforcement, evidenced by seq 25 and seq 27 elsewhere in the batch. [S0113] Option E ('session responsible', i.e. evidence recorded but nothing enforced) is argued to be empirically falsified rather than merely weak: it is the estate's actual current behaviour and is cited as the direct cause of the same-day EKS-01 distribution failure, the missing-START off-record-execution pattern, and the false identity-attestation claim. [S0113]
