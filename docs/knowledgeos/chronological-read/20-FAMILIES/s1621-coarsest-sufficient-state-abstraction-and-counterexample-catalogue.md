## Rationale
- [S1621] (DISTINCTION/ARGUMENT) Distinguishes Replayability (can Replay(H) reconstruct K) from State Sufficiency (does the induced equivalence remain a congruence under every future legal operation), showing a state can be perfectly replayable while still lacking information some future transformation needs -- a stronger requirement than replay alone.
- [S1621] (ARGUMENT/DISTINCTION) Establishes a critical general distinction using the Policy and Time counterexamples: a piece of information being relevant to a transformation's outcome (semantic dependency) does not prove that information must be a member of the state K -- it may instead be supplied as an external parameter to the transformation (T:KxPolicy->K), so 'semantic dependency != state membership' as a general rule.
- [S1629] (DISTINCTION/ARGUMENT) Draws a critical architectural distinction between an operation's implementation inspecting history internally (That(H1)!=That(H2)) versus history actually affecting the resulting semantic state (F(That(H1))!=F(That(H2))) -- only the latter disproves state sufficiency; an operation may legitimately look at history while still producing an observably identical state.
