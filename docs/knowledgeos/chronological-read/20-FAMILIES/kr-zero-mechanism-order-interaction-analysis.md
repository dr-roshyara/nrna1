## Rationale
Derives an explicit architectural requirement from the KR-ZERO-ORDER result: any future Knowledge Algebra must be capable of representing interaction between information units, since independent unary importance values are insufficient for exact eliminability in the general case, sketching a pipeline from Information (value, provenance, context, relation, transformation, preservation contract) through an interaction structure to eliminability, while noting the mathematical structure of that interaction remains one of the most interesting open questions. [S2652] Frames a business-facing consequence of the Zero-order result: because information cannot always be classified as removable by evaluating each piece independently, a future knowledge-extraction system cannot safely rely only on token importance, entity importance, frequency, salience scores, or independent relevance classifications if it wants exact contract-level preservation, and must instead be capable of reasoning about groups and relationships. [S2652] Offers a one-sentence business summary of the whole Zero research phase -- information eliminability is contract- and transformation-dependent and exact elimination cannot in general be determined from independent unit properties, establishing a concrete constraint that any future Knowledge Algebra must represent structural interaction rather than merely independent information relevance -- and characterizes the epistemic progress as moving from 'maybe there is an algebra' to 'if an algebra exists for this problem, we now have experimentally established properties it must satisfy', explicitly short of 'we solved Knowledge Algebra'. [S2652] Argues that because KR-ZERO (can S be eliminated without changing the relevant result) and KR-REP-REDUCTION (how far can D be transformed while preserving Q under Pi) study eliminability and representation-preservation respectively, and because KR-REP-REDUCTION has provided evidence against using Zero as a simple preservation-boundary criterion, keeping the two research programs analytically separate is now even more strongly justified.
