## Rationale
Policy/authority, evidence, statistics and uncertainty are each shown to contain a non-computable normative core: Authorize(actor,action,policy) is decidable only if policy is fully formalized as rules, otherwise it is a possibly non-computable governance-decision input; evidence handling is computable in layers (retrieval/hashing/linkage computable, but Assess(e,A) may require a human or statistical model); a statistical formula's mere existence (e.g. Bayes' rule) does not make a KnowledgeOS quantity computable — a probability claim is UNDER-SPECIFIED without an explicit probability space (Omega,F,P); and Uncertainty is not computable until its semantics are fixed, since a bare number like U(A)=0.2 could mean probability, confidence, epistemic strength, error bound, entropy, or subjective belief — mathematically different things.
