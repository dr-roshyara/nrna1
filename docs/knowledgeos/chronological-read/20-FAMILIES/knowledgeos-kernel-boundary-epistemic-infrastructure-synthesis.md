## Rationale

## All rows (source_id order)
This label's 77 rows trace one continuous research arc — repeatedly reframing "what is the KnowledgeOS Kernel boundary?" — across 14 documents (S0509 through S0535). Grouped by document in chronological order; statements are condensed, not quoted in full — complete text for any row is in `03-CONTRIBUTIONS.jsonl`.

**Theme 1 — First Kernel-boundary conceptualization (10 rows: S0509 ×10).** Surveys ~27 candidate concepts across a lens series, concluding none individually is "knowledge" and reframing the programme as "negative-space discovery." Rejects three candidate KnowledgeOS definitions (content-management-system, KnowledgeGraph, AI memory) for "a system for maintaining the semantic, epistemic and contextual integrity of a changing body of knowledge." States the file's central conclusion: the Kernel must not own the world model, only the rules/structures making a knowledge system trustworthy — an epistemic infrastructure, not a repository. Divides the Kernel into five capabilities (Identity, Meaning/semantic structure, Epistemic integrity, Inquiry, Assessment). Places six categories explicitly outside the Kernel (NLG/LLMs, embeddings, statistical/ML models, domain knowledge, UIs, documents). Distinguishes four previously-conflated senses of "boundary" (semantic, context, epistemic, system). Refines the Kernel definition to "what makes a knowledge system capable of distinguishing, relating, evaluating and evolving claims about reality." Identifies CHANGE as an inadequately-modeled missing dimension. Declines to freeze an ADR, proposing instead a 20-row candidate classification table. Closes with the file's central open question: the smallest thing KnowledgeOS must preserve so knowledge can be expressed/questioned/challenged/evaluated/changed/reconstructed without losing identity, context, provenance, or epistemic meaning.

**Theme 2 — Assertion/Aggregate/Projection distinctions and the falsifiable boundary test (14 rows: S0510 ×14).** Argues against freezing an "AssertionAggregate," distinguishing a preservation unit, a consistency boundary, and a projection view. Reformulates the KnowledgeOS mission as an eight-step capability chain (preserve→identify→contextualize→relate→evaluate→challenge→change→reconstruct). Lists ten candidate kernel invariant-surface principles. Generalizes a temporal principle so that six distinct temporal roles must all remain separately preserved. Corrects a naive `Assertion{truth=TRUE}` model into a richer Assertion/Assessment pair. Resolves the "truth" problem via a Reality→Observation→Representation→Proposition→Assertion→Assessment chain, named "reconstructible epistemology." Moves Topic outside the Kernel (a projection/context construct, illustrated by "Kafka" belonging to seven topical groupings at once). Proposes a falsifiable Kernel-boundary criterion and removal test, then applies it to 24 candidates (splitting them inside/needs-test/outside). Refines the Kernel definition again, explicitly removing "evaluating" from the Kernel's own responsibility. Proposes quantifying ten measurable properties of a Knowledge Projection instead of a single Knowledge Score. Reframes topology (and Dhatu/Karaka/Pramana/ML/statistical-inference/graph-analysis) as external analytical lenses over the Kernel's preserved structure, not competing definitions. Poses the open, to-be-proven question of whether Assertion is really the universal semantic/epistemic center, and proposes an adversarial follow-on "Kernel Boundary & Primitive Discovery" exercise against eighteen further candidate types.

**Theme 3 — Eliminativism test on Assertion (2 rows: S0511 ×2).** Applies an eliminativism test asking whether Assertion is fundamental or merely the relationship/event binding a more primitive Agent-asserts→Proposition-applies-in→Context structure, suggesting KnowledgeOS may need the event-centric formulation. Sharpens the Kernel question using Merricks' seven-step elimination method, declining to freeze the Assertion Aggregate until the non-redundancy question is answered.

**Theme 4 — Reframing to irreducible distinctions (1 row: S0513 ×1).** After incorporating Merricks, replaces "what is the one fundamental object called Knowledge?" with "what are the irreducible distinctions that KnowledgeOS must preserve?" as the guiding question.

**Theme 5 — Converging constraints and an ontological-discovery proposal (1 row: S0515 ×1).** States three converging constraints (from Merricks, O'Connor, and the project's own investigation) concluding the right next step is a 22-candidate "Knowledge Space Ontological Discovery" exercise rather than declaring an ontology outright.

**Theme 6 — Reframing to a decidable engineering question (1 row: S0524 ×1).** After ~130 documents of unanswered "what is knowledge?" inquiry, converts the question into "what is the minimal record such that any epistemic regime could later be run over it?", identifying preservation and inference (e.g., Fagin's model) as different obligations with different invariants.

**Theme 7 — Sharpening the non-negotiable Kernel job (3 rows: S0525 ×3).** Sharpens a prior finding (F-4) that unqualified regime-pluggability protects no invariant and degrades the Kernel to a generic store, posing "what does the Kernel decide that no regime may override?" Answers that the Kernel's non-negotiable job is to keep the record sufficient and un-foreclosing, not to adjudicate truth — possible-worlds/Pramana/statistical/algorithmic regimes all "read the record" rather than being part of the Kernel. Closes with five explicit open questions (OQ-1 through OQ-5) about override authority, inference ownership, unawareness representability, which "Knowledge Space" sense applies, and whether the minimum-preservation-unit already subsumes the K-tuple.

**Theme 8 — The "preservation substrate" synthesis (4 rows: S0526 ×4).** Fully adopts the "minimal record for any epistemic regime" reframe as the single most important sentence of the review. Introduces the "preservation substrate" concept: the Kernel preserves sixteen named items while reasoning over that substrate (Fagin, Bayesian, Pramana, statistics, LLM, topology, causal models, human review) stays entirely outside. States the sharpest Kernel-boundary definition reached in the batch (the minimum preservation boundary required to reconstruct, under more than one epistemic regime, a knowledge-bearing episode's identity/context/provenance/temporal-state/relationships/history). States the final rewritten research position closing the whole McGinn-Floridi-Dretske-Fagin arc, splitting the architecture into Preservation Substrate versus Reasoning Regimes, and explicitly declaring this the point to stop ontology expansion.

**Theme 9 — Reduction to thirteen items and the Zero-Lens residual (2 rows: S0527 ×2).** Reduces the candidate Kernel, after four books plus the critique cycle, to thirteen items, explicitly excluding a Truth engine, possible-world engine, Bayesian engine, Knowledge score, universal epistemology, LLM, and Topic hierarchy. Applies a "Zero Lens" removing every named philosophical/technical source, leaving a ten-statement residual judged closer to the actual Kernel than any philosophical ontology constructed so far.

**Theme 10 — A further Preservation Substrate revision (2 rows: S0528 ×2).** Revises the candidate Preservation Substrate to a different sixteen-item list (Identity, Agent, Context, Time, Epistemic input, Input provenance, Assertion reference, Epistemic attitude, State-transition event, prior/resulting state references, Change type, Rationale, Conflict, Evidence, Entrenchment assessment). States the cleanest Inside/Outside Kernel split reached in the batch (facts needed to reconstruct who/what/when/why/what-changed/what-history-led-here, versus the rules for how to revise/calculate/rank/infer/explain/parse/summarize).

**Theme 11 — Knowledge-Space snapshot and projection formalization (14 rows: S0529 ×14).** States the central architectural problem: an infinite Knowledge Space exists independently of any agent's limited capacity, so the engineering question is capturing a faithful projection-state at time t, not the whole space. Formalizes `S_t != Omega` with a worked example showing two systems can share identical facts yet differ in epistemic state. Proposes a nine-component snapshot state vector `S_t=(B_t,E_t,Q_t,C_t,T_t,R_t,A_t,U_t,X_t)`. Distinguishes six temporal roles that a single timestamp cannot capture. Distinguishes Snapshot from History, arguing KnowledgeOS should preserve both together. States a working definition of Knowledge State as a time-indexed, context-bounded, regime-relative projection — deliberately not "the truth at time t." Formalizes a Projection object and the equation `S_t = P(Omega | B,L,U,R,A,t)`. Distinguishes "missing from the snapshot" from "unknown" via a six-state epistemic-coverage classification. Proposes five measurable ratio dimensions (Coverage, Uncertainty, Conflict, Provenance coverage, Question resolution) replacing a single Knowledge score. Resolves the "infinite time" concern via a finite certificate (immutable events + deterministic projection + snapshot/checkpoint). Names the "State Reconstruction Invariant." States there is no single universal KnowledgeState — distinct per-participant states are all derived from the same substrate via projection. Refines the "knowledge goes back into the Knowledge Space" intuition into new representations becoming future objects of inquiry, not modification of a cosmic database. Illustrates the Knowledge Space's effective unboundedness via a four-step knowledge-about-knowledge regress.

**Theme 12 — Cross-participant divergence and knowledge progress (10 rows: S0530 ×10).** States the central refinement that different participants' knowledge states can legitimately diverge without implying divergent underlying reality. Replaces "projections must never contradict" with "projections must remain semantically reconcilable given context/time/boundary/regime," illustrated by an apparently-contradictory pair resolving once observation time/layer/scope are accounted for. States the candidate "epistemic orientation" principle `O(S_t)=(scope,purpose,LoA,regime,time)`. Refines the projection hierarchy into four explicit levels (Omega, Projection, State, History). Distinguishes four separate questions previously conflated under "truth" (local consistency, cross-projection compatibility, truth orientation, reality adequacy). Reframes knowledge evolution as coarse→refined→expanded→qualified→corrected→recontextualized rather than false→true. Defines Knowledge Progress as a six-dimension delta vector. States the file's deepest formulation: knowledge as local, time-dependent, legitimately-differing but semantically-reconcilable projections, with KnowledgeOS preserving projections/transitions/evidence/boundaries/orientations rather than owning the Knowledge Space. Leaves open whether the Kernel needs an explicit Orientation primitive or whether it is fully derivable from existing parameters.

**Theme 13 — Six-book synthesis and projection kinds (2 rows: S0534 ×2).** Argues not all projections project the same kind of reality (natural facts vs. semantic structures vs. institutional statuses vs. epistemic states), calling this preservation-of-kind a major boundary condition. Synthesizes the whole six-book research arc (McGinn, Floridi, Dretske, Fagin, Gärdenfors, Searle) into one table and a boxed chain (Infinite Knowledge Space → Reality/Semantics/Institutions → Observations → Epistemic States → Projections).

**Theme 14 — Knowledge Capacity as the final formulation (11 rows: S0535 ×11).** Rejects "knowledge = capacity to write information" as too narrow. Formalizes a four-stage chain (Infinite Knowledge Space → Information → Knowledge Capacity → Knowledge Projection), placing Capacity as an intermediate layer. Lists what Knowledge Capacity determines (representability, distinguishability, connectivity, justifiability, communicability, actionability). Ties the Capacity construct back to Floridi/Gärdenfors/Dretske/Searle. Formalizes Knowledge Capacity as the tuple `K=(B,L,R,C,P)`. States that knowledge is not only a projection's content but also the capability/boundary determining what can enter it. Distinguishes three separate capacities (information, epistemic, expressive) with worked profiles (e.g., an LLM: high expressive/representational capacity, uncertain epistemic capacity). Proposes a revised KnowledgeOS definition centered on preserving the structures of projection-construction rather than storing knowledge. Offers a final single-sentence definition: "Knowledge is a capacity to construct and maintain a bounded, meaningful projection of an unbounded Knowledge Space by selecting, relating, evaluating, preserving, and expressing information within a context and purpose."
