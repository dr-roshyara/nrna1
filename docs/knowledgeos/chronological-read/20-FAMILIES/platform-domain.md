## Rationale
This label documents a long, self-critical strategic-DDD discovery effort to determine what "platform-domain" candidates exist for KnowledgeOS and whether any survive an admissible-justification bar. The purpose across its rows is consistently to test candidate domains against Round47-OP's criteria rather than assert them: PD-1 Governance, PD-2 Method, PD-3 EKP, and PD-5b Records score above the ≥3-of-9 bar while PD-6 Runtime is rejected [S0002]; six further candidate domains (Bootstrap/Genesis, Engineering Governance, Knowledge Validation, Runtime Integration, PKS Generation, Operational Learning) are each individually tested and found not to be new stable strategic domains [S0033]. A recurring finding is that DOMAIN itself is "decision-silent" — the frozen Phase-02 bounded-context map existed through the entire discovery programme and was never consulted, and no recorded decision changed because of a PD-1..PD-6 classification, though domains did scaffold the discovery work itself [S0017]; a companion finding concludes DOMAIN is an archetype of classification, not ownership — orthodox strategic DDD "empirically rediscovered," since ownership work is actually done by DP-n (Design Policy), not by Domains [S0026]. Three independent, mechanical (not aesthetic) proofs show the D-1..D-7 domain candidates cannot be expressed in the existing ontology schemas (key collision, vocabulary insufficiency, subject mismatch), concluding they belong to an as-yet-nonexistent ontology layer L-B [S0020]. A separate strand finds PD-3 (the Engineering Knowledge Platform) is in fact the most strongly evidenced domain in the repository (6/9 criteria) yet was entirely missed by the earlier D-1..D-7 model, which had mistakenly treated it as an ontology layer rather than a governed domain with its own constitution and owner — named explicitly as a category error [S0037]. The overall interpretive conclusion reached late in the corpus is that KnowledgeOS "is not yet ONE thing" but three separately-governed, overlapping domains plus a generic runtime, with no single owner and no reconciling authority — offered as an explanation for the programme's own recurring symptoms (repeated rediscoveries, parallel ontologies, colliding verdict vocabularies) [S0037]. A final product-boundary discovery pass finds only one relationship among six candidate platform↔product edges is actually evidenced, framing the sparse context map itself as the finding, not a shortfall of the discovery [S0039].
