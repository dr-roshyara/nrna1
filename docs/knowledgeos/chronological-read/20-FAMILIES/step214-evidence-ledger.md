## Rationale

This label carries two very different voices, and the rationale is legible only if they are kept apart. The first voice is the original Step 214 document itself (`S1523`, "Build the Evidence Ledger"), which frames its own purpose as operationalizing Step 213's F/D/P/U (Fact/Derived/Proposed/Unknown) discipline into a working data structure — a per-step ledger record, later expanded into an Evidence Lineage Graph — explicitly claimed as an extension of Step 213 ("Step 214 turns that distinction into a working ledger"). The second voice is a later verification pass (`S1541`) auditing Step 214 as one file inside an eight-file band (Steps 208–215); everything in `family.rationale_evidence` comes from this second voice, so the "why it was introduced" and "what gap it closed" below are the audit's characterization, not Step 214's own words, except where explicitly marked.

Structurally, the audit observes that each file in the 208–215 band ends with the agenda section for the *next* step, so the band is a chain of overlapping documents rather than eight independent artifacts [S1541]. Read against that chain, Step 214 is the third of four consecutive files (212→213→214→215→216) that each announce that empirical, evidence-gathering work will happen — and each defers it to the next file: "the next stage should be an evidence-driven reconstruction" (212→213), "create the first Evidence Ledger" (213→214), "the next step should be retrospective reconstruction" (214→215) [S1541]. The audit's decisive verdict is that Step 214, titled "Build the Evidence Ledger," performs no empirical act of its own — no shell commands, repository reads, file paths, commit hashes, or tool invocations — extending the corpus's prior 465-file/207-step record of zero empirical acts, unbroken, to 215 steps and 473 files; Step 214 Section 214.1 defines a 10-tuple ledger but records no ledger entry [S1541]. Formally this shows up as an emptiness result: `EL_i` and `EL=∪_i EL_i` are DEFINED, but `EL` is a union over an empty index set (`EL=∅`); `ELG=(V,E)` is DEFINED with 0 nodes and 0 edges; and across its three internal tables (a 15×2 field glossary, an 8×2 self-assessment table, and an 8-column matrix header) Step 214 produces 0 ledger rows [S1541].

That zero-row outcome is sharpened by a prior-art comparison: Step 109 (Section 109.69) had already specified a 6-column Evidence Ledger schema with 4 fabricated example rows, but Step 214 does not cite Step 109 (a grep for "109" in Step 214 finds only a false positive) and instead re-mints the ledger schema from scratch — first as a 10-tuple (214.1), then as a 15-field table (214.6), then as an 8-column matrix header (214, tail) — three incompatible arities for "the ledger" inside one file, with no statement of which is normative [S1541]. Step 214 does cite Step 213 by number, but the audit frames the Step 109 omission as an ES-005.4-style "never a copy" violation transposed into the research corpus, and notes that Step 215 goes on to adopt F/D/P/U and expand the schema to 20 columns without reconciling any of the prior arities [S1541].

Against this execution gap, the audit credits several ideas Step 214 introduces as genuinely strong: NegativeKnowledge is precisely scoped ("demonstrated to be unsuitable under specified conditions"), the Hypothesis-vs-Invariant pipeline with `Observed(H)` not implying `Universal(H)` is well executed, the treatment of apparent contradiction as a missing covariate is called "the most sophisticated statistical move in the entire band," and the "bounded knowledge" / `Translation_{A→B}` framing is a legitimate transposition of DDD bounded-context thinking [S1541]. Yet the file is also charged with violating its own headline principle — "no architectural claim should be stronger than the evidence supporting it" — eight lines later, when its own self-assessment table rates "Risk of overclaiming" as "Explicitly controlled" with zero supporting evidence [S1541]. Finally, the audit places Step 214 inside a band-wide pattern: across Steps 208–215, only 7 individual step citations occur in 11,287 lines (mostly to the immediately preceding step) while 162 new identifiers are minted — evidence, in the audit's framing, that the band mints concepts faster than it cites or reconciles them [S1541].

### Part A — What Step 214 itself defines (`S1523`, 23 rows)

**1. Ledger record formats and structures.** The core formalizations: the per-step 10-field record `EL_i=(Step,Artifact,Observation,Fact,Derivation,Decision,Architecture,Invariant,Evidence,Status)` with `EL=∪_i EL_i`; the two-directional chain (forward: Observation→Fact→Derivation→Architectural Principle→Invariant; feedback: Invariant→Implementation→Test→Evidence); a separate, more practical 15-field schema (Step ID, Artifact, Date/sequence, Observation, Fact, Interpretation, Derived principle, Decision, Rejected alternative, Architectural consequence, Invariant, Verification, Evidence status F/D/P/U, Confidence, Open question); a six-field experimental-lineage record `Experiment=(Hypothesis,Setup,Observation,Result,Interpretation,Consequence)`; and the ledger recast as a graph `ELG=(V,E)` with seven node types and seven edge types, explicitly positioned as richer than a conventional requirements traceability matrix.
- `[S1523]` §"EL_i=(...) ... EL=∪_i EL_i" — the 10-tuple record and its union.
- `[S1523]` §"Observation -> Fact -> Derivation -> Architectural Principle -> Invariant ..." — the two-directional chain.
- `[S1523]` §"| Field | Purpose | ... Rejected alternative | What was not chosen |" — the practical 15-field schema.
- `[S1523]` §"Experiment=(Hypothesis,Setup,Observation,Result,Interpretation,Consequence) ..." — the experimental-lineage record.
- `[S1523]` §"ELG=(V,E) ... substantially richer than a conventional requirements traceability matrix" — the ledger-as-graph formalization.

**2. Chronology, history, and knowledge-continuity.** A cluster distinguishing chronology from content: the ledger must preserve *when* a belief was held (an earlier hypothesis H1 does not stop having existed once a better H2 arrives); three non-identical chronologies (calendar, engineering, epistemic — code can precede the naming of its own concept); the "late naming" phenomenon (a pattern exists implicitly before it is named, and the name must not be projected backward); the formal "new state does not know old state" principle `S_{t+1} ⊉ S_t`, requiring an additional historical mechanism; and a knowledge-continuity triple `K_t=(CurrentKnowledge_t,HistoricalKnowledge,Uncertainty_t)` with `K_{t+1}=Update(K_t,E_{t+1})`, alongside a KnowledgeVersion-vs-KnowledgeState distinction drawn by analogy to a git commit vs. the working tree — an analogy the source itself immediately flags as "useful ... not proof of the architecture."
- `[S1523]` §"Knowledge_t != Knowledge_{t+1} ... The fact that H_2 is better does not mean H_1 never existed" — chronology-preservation principle.
- `[S1523]` §"Calendar chronology ... Engineering chronology ... Epistemic chronology ..." — the three-chronologies distinction.
- `[S1523]` §"implementation -> repeated problem -> experiment -> pattern recognized -> concept named ..." — the late-naming phenomenon.
- `[S1523]` §"S_{t+1} does not contain all information about S_t ... S_{t+1} ⊉ S_t ..." — the new-state-does-not-know-old-state formalization.
- `[S1523]` §"K_t=(CurrentKnowledge_t,HistoricalKnowledge,Uncertainty_t) ... Git commit != current working..." — the knowledge-continuity model and its self-limited git analogy.

**3. Negative knowledge, rejection, and over-generalization discipline.** Architecture is argued to be defined as much by what was rejected as by what was selected (justifying a dedicated "Rejected alternative" ledger field); NegativeKnowledge is introduced as knowledge that an approach/assumption/transition/interpretation has been demonstrated unsuitable *under specified conditions* — distinguished from mere failure, which becomes negative knowledge only once its cause is understood; and a guardrail that evidence for H under tested conditions (`Observed(H)`) must not be generalized to `Universal(H)`, illustrated with a prompt-strategy-specific finding that must not be generalized to all AI.
- `[S1523]` §"What we rejected ... as by: What we selected ... That rejection itself is architectural knowledge" — rejection-as-architectural-knowledge principle.
- `[S1523]` §"NegativeKnowledge ... demonstrated to be unsuitable under specified conditions ... different from failure" — the NegativeKnowledge concept.
- `[S1523]` §"Observed(H) does not imply Universal(H) ... AllAI -> InconsistentCode [is unwarranted...]" — the over-generalization guardrail.

**4. Hypothesis-to-invariant promotion pipeline.** A single row distinguishing hypothesis ("X may be true") from invariant ("X must remain true"), with the explicit pipeline `Hypothesis -[evidence]-> Pattern -[validation]-> Invariant` and the note that not every hypothesis is promoted.
- `[S1523]` §"A hypothesis says: X may be true ... Hypothesis -[evidence]-> Pattern -[validation]-> Invariant. Not every hypothesis becomes an invariant" — the promotion pipeline.

**5. Context-relative claims and cross-context semantic preservation.** Every conclusion is proposed to be represented as `Claim=(Proposition,Context,Conditions)`, so validity is always conditioned (`Valid(C|Context,Conditions)`, never bare `Valid(C)`) — extended to "bounded knowledge" (`Knowledge_A != Knowledge_B` across DDD bounded contexts, requiring an explicit translation when a concept crosses a boundary); and a semantic-preservation condition for cross-context evidence transfer, `p(C_A) => p'(C_B)`, where failure of the implication signals semantic loss and evidence for a claim in one context does not automatically establish it in another.
- `[S1523]` §"Claim=(Proposition,Context,Conditions) ... Valid(C) should really mean: Valid(C | Context,Conditions)" — the context-relative claim model.
- `[S1523]` §"p(C_A) => p'(C_B) ... If that implication does not hold, the contract may introduce semantic loss" — the semantic-preservation condition.

**6. Contradiction as first-class evidence, including AI-output adjudication.** Contradiction is treated as a graph edge to be recorded, not resolved by deletion — an apparent contradiction (e.g. `ModelA` accurate vs. inaccurate) can dissolve into conditional coherence once Context/Population/Time/Purpose are made explicit; the same principle is applied to AI-generated competing interpretations, treating `{AI_1,AI_2}` as an explicit candidate knowledge set requiring adjudication via `Candidates -> Evidence -> Adjudication -> AcceptedKnowledge`, architecturally separating Generation from Adjudication.
- `[S1523]` §"C_1 <-[contradicts]-> C_2 ... We should not immediately delete one ... Sometimes the contradiction disappears once context is made explicit" — contradiction as a first-class edge.
- `[S1523]` §"Generation != Adjudication ... Candidates -> Evidence -> Adjudication -> AcceptedKnowledge" — AI-output adjudication architecture.

**7. KState — the eight-value non-linear epistemic-state enum.** A single row defining `KState in {Observed,Hypothesized,Derived,Corroborated,Contested,Rejected,Accepted,Deprecated}`, explicitly not a single linear lifecycle (states may coexist, e.g. Accepted+Contested), and reiterating that `verified=true` alone is insufficient — use KnowledgeState+Evidence+Context+Time.
- `[S1523]` §"KState in {Observed,Hypothesized,...,Deprecated} ... should not automatically be treated as a single linear lifecycle ... Accepted + Contested" — the KState enum and its non-linearity warning.

**8. Analogy discipline — heuristic, not proof.** A Gita chapter-4 analogy (enduring knowledge vs. changing manifestation, applied to `DomainInvariant != CurrentRuntimeState` and `HistoricalKnowledge != CurrentRepresentation`) is explicitly bounded by the source itself as interpretive only ("helps us ask better questions, does not prove them"). (The git-commit-vs-working-tree analogy in Theme 2 carries the identical self-limiting caveat.)
- `[S1523]` §"UnderlyingPrinciple != CurrentManifestation ... The analogy helps us ask better architecture questions. It does not prove them" — the self-limited analogy discipline.

**9. Book/methodology governance and the Step 215 preview.** A nine-part chapter template is proposed for the eventual book (Problem→Observation→Experiment→Failure/Contradiction→Insight→Abstraction→Architecture→Verification→Remaining uncertainty), argued to let the reader follow Why→How→What→Evidence; a candidate "methodological constitution" dual principle is stated ("no architectural claim should be stronger than the evidence supporting it" and its converse, that no evidence should be interpreted outside its demonstrated context); and Step 215 is previewed as a pivot from adding more theory to retrospectively constructing the ledger for Steps 1–182, explicitly to let evidence — not prior decision — locate the book's real chapter boundaries.
- `[S1523]` §"1. Problem 2. Observation 3. Experiment 4. Failure/Contradiction 5. Insight 6. Abstraction 7. Architecture 8. Verification 9. Remaining uncertainty" — the nine-part chapter template.
- `[S1523]` §"No architectural claim should be stronger than the evidence supporting it ... No evidence should be interpreted outside its demonstrated context without an explicit derivation" — the dual boxed principle.
