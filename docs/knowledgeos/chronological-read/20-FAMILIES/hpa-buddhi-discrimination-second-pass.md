## Rationale
Correction of a prior KnowledgeOS wording: the Gita does not literally say 'the mind is always busy determining what is right and wrong'; rather, in Gita 3.40-43 the philosophical structure is that Buddhi (intelligence) must discriminate and govern the lower mental/sensory tendencies (Senses < Mind < Intelligence/Buddhi < Self, concluding at 3.43 with instruction to use the higher faculty to control the lower forces) [S2217]. From Gita 4.17-18 (the intricacies of action are hard to understand; action-in-inaction/inaction-in-action paradox): observed activity is not equal to the correct interpretation of that activity, so the kernel cannot merely ask 'what happened?' but must also ask 'what does this mean?' and possibly 'was this the right action?' [S2217]. Interprets the idea that 'without governance, the mind/kernel can become its own enemy' as meaning an ungoverned state-transition mechanism can repeatedly transform K_t in the wrong direction, connecting the Buddhi/discrimination hypothesis to a governance-failure warning [S2217]. Reinterprets 'the mind can become its own enemy': under the philosophical lens the danger is not the kernel disappearing but the kernel's discrimination becoming misdirected, corrupted, or dominated by an invalid internal process (bad qualification -> bad discrimination -> bad decision -> K_{t+1} -> future reasoning built on bad state), i.e. the kernel becomes its own enemy through epistemic feedback. Formalized as K_{t+1}=F(K_t,O_t,B_t) where B_t is the Buddhi/discrimination operation; a defective B_t can systematically produce defective future states [S2220]. The kernel/mind 'enemy' danger is not only bad data but can arise from: (1) incomplete observation, (2) overwhelming observation, (3) incorrect interpretation, (4) defective discrimination, (5) incorrect decision, (6) state transition based on that decision -- pipeline O_t -> B_t -> D_t -> K_{t+1}. Hence Buddhi is a critical control point in the kernel architecture. Observation != Understanding and Understanding != Discrimination [S2221]. Explains the 'kernel can become its own enemy' worry: if the kernel transforms its own state without constraints (K_t ->F-> K_{t+1} ->F-> K_{t+2}...), it can create self-reinforcing errors, circular reasoning, confirmation loops, unjustified certainty, loss of provenance, and contradiction propagation. Therefore Buddhi != unrestricted inference; it needs constraints, and this is where Governance becomes structurally distinct from the kernel. Also refines Buddhi's codomain away from TRUE/FALSE toward {supported, contradicted, unknown, insufficient evidence, conflicting}, framed as more compatible with the Zero/uncertainty/qualification theory [S2235]. Formalizes Buddhi as mode-sensitive: B_t:(E_t,K_t,M_t)->{accept,reject,investigate,suspend,transform}. The same evidence E can lead to different actions depending on mode: in Sattva, E->B_S->investigate carefully; in Rajas, E->B_R->act/acquire immediately; in Tamas, E->B_T->ignore/suspend/block. Refines the earlier 'kernel always busy determining right and wrong' into: the kernel continuously evaluates the epistemic condition of its current state and determines which transitions are admissible under its current operational mode; a kernel may legitimately decide DO NOTHING because evidence is insufficient, and that itself is a Buddhi decision [S2247]. From Chapter 18's distinction between understanding that correctly distinguishes proper/improper action and understanding that fails to (per the modes): having an evaluation mechanism != having correct discrimination. Therefore B_t != truth oracle; Buddhi is an epistemic discriminator, not an omniscient truth machine -- assessed as much more compatible with KnowledgeOS than treating Buddhi as infallible [S2249].
