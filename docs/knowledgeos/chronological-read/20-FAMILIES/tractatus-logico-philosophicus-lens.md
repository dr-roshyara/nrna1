## Rationale

Three headline findings applying the Tractatus to the Kernel: (1) it distinguishes what can be said (facts, states of affairs, propositions with sense) from what can only be shown (logical form, the limits of language, the mystical) -- a Kernel treating all content as propositional may attempt to represent what cannot in principle be represented; (2) meaning is not a psychological phenomenon -- a proposition has sense independent of anyone believing/knowing/understanding it, challenging a Kernel that ties knowledge to belief states; (3) names (referring to simple objects) are distinct from propositions (configurations of names) -- knowledge is always propositional (says how things stand), never mere naming [S0409]. Additionally, Ten Tractatus observations (Part 1): (1) TLP 1.1 the world is facts not things -- knowledge requires propositions saying how objects are configured, not a mere list of objects; (2) TLP 4.01 a proposition is a picture of reality sharing logical form/multiplicity with what it depicts; (3) TLP 4.1212 logical form cannot be said, only shown -- it manifests in propositions; (4) TLP 4.022 a proposition has sense (presents a possible situation) independent of truth/falsity, which are relations to reality not properties of the proposition; (5) TLP 4.461 tautologies and contradictions lack sense -- they are not pictures of reality; (6) TLP 5.473 logical syntax is self-contained, not requiring reference to meaning or world; (7) TLP 5.6 the limits of language are the limits of the world -- what cannot be expressed is 'mystical', not part of the world; (8) TLP 3.203 names are simple signs standing for objects, not further analyzable; (9) TLP 5.4 there are no 'logical objects' -- logical constants (and/or/not) are operations, not things represented; (10) TLP 4.53 the general propositional form is a variable, not itself a proposition, defining the scope of what can be said [S0409]. Further, Five most-surprising observations: knowledge is always of facts, never of objects in isolation (a Kernel treating 'knowledge of an object' as its own category may be a fundamental error); sense is prior to truth -- a false proposition still has sense and may be worth storing as a record of belief/hypothesis/refutation; logical form cannot itself be represented, only shown through the propositions' structure -- attempting to represent the 'structure of knowledge' as content may be a category error; tautologies/contradictions say nothing and are scaffolding, not knowledge; the limits of language bound the world, so what cannot be expressed (ethics, aesthetics, the mystical) is not 'knowledge' by this definition yet may be what is most important [S0409]. Relatedly, Five observations ranked most kernel-relevant: the Kernel's fundamental unit should be the proposition/fact, not the entity (entities are constituents of propositions, not knowledge themselves); the Kernel should store both sense (what is said) and truth (correspondence to reality) since false propositions retain value; the Kernel's own structure may need to show logical form without trying to say it; the Kernel should distinguish names (identifiers) from propositions (configurations) since names alone are not knowledge; the Kernel needs a concept of its own representational limits, including what it cannot say but might show [S0409].
