## Rationale
States the business meaning of the research in one sentence: there is evidence that 'information can be removed safely' and 'information can be removed without changing the business outcome' are two different questions, a distinction argued to be useful for KnowledgeOS [S2663]. Translates the bridge experiment's negative result into business terms: whether information is removable ('business decision A') and whether the business result remains correct ('business decision B') must be treated as separate governance questions, since a rule of the form 'IF Zero=true THEN information may safely be discarded' is not supported by the evidence [S2663]. Translates the bridge experiment's confounding discovery into a familiar business-analysis pattern using a CRM/sales-drop example (a correlation between a new CRM and falling sales does not establish causation if a market downturn coincided), arguing this is exactly the kind of discipline the redundancy-controlled experiment demonstrated [S2663]. Produces an eight-row strategic status table (removability demonstrated, removability!=preservation per BRIDGE, redundancy affects both in the tested regime, misleading correlations detectable, no universal reduction/Zero law, carrier unknown, promising research direction exists), concluding this should not be called a failure but a move from 'maybe Zero and preservation are one thing' to experimental evidence that they are separate dimensions whose apparent relationship can be caused by structural factors like redundancy [S2663]. Contrasts a naive AI compression story ('we compress your information') with a stronger commercial KnowledgeOS story ('we transform your information while explicitly defining what business meaning must survive'), reformulating the optimization target from minimizing representation size to minimizing cost subject to an explicit adequacy constraint Adequate(R,Q,Pi), motivated by the experiment's demonstration that the first representation-reduction step can already cross a preservation boundary [S2663]. Argues compression alone should not be the commercial objective since the measured reduction dimensions (bytes, fields, cardinality, entropy) behave differently and cannot be collapsed into one scalar, illustrated with a business request to 'reduce the data by 80%' which is ambiguous across at least five genuinely different objectives (bytes, records, semantic claims, entropy, business-relevant distinctions) [S2663].
