## Rationale
Verdict: R1 and R2 are the genuine repairs and share one intervention (stop calling attribution knowledge); the experiment removes the factivity-based objection to defining Sat but Sat should remain [PROP] for other, independent reasons (Zero's three-way ambiguity, undefined status order, unproven class exhaustiveness). [S2403] Records that R1 (rename) and R2 (externalize) were experimentally behaviorally identical, so the experimental lane alone could not adjudicate between them — the choice of R1 required a separate human/governance decision. [S2479] Explains precisely what R1 does and does not do: it dissolves DEF-1's joint unsatisfiability by making the kernel's object an attribution rather than a knowledge claim, preserving factivity externally; it does not make Contr or evaluation semantics any easier, does not repair the U-collapse (still an unadopted [PROP]), and does not change the 390/3320 false-attribution error rate — it is an improvement in honesty about what may be claimed, never in accuracy. [S2481] Closes by arguing that R1 does not violate the 'strongest statement must never exceed evidence' principle, because R1 was never claimed to be evidence-backed — it was openly made on authority and recorded in a separate governance register precisely to keep that distinction visible. [S2481] Argues that because R1 and R2 are behaviourally identical, the remaining choice between them is not empirical and cannot be settled by any further simulation -- it must be a governance decision. [S2482] Details option R1: rename K_t to A_t (AttributedState), retaining DEF-1 as governing a predicate the kernel never computes. Commits to KnowledgeOS never claiming knowledge, only attribution. Cost: vocabulary change everywhere K_t appears. Gain: removes the contradiction, preserves factivity as a concept; no new measurement added. [S2482] Details option R2: kernel emits ClaimToKnowledge, an external verifier (a separate bounded context) applies factivity; no kernel component may ever assert Knows. Gain over R1: makes a previously invisible quantity measurable -- 88.25% of the kernel's claims survived external verification. Constraint: the verifier must be on an independent channel or it adds nothing. [S2482] Details why R3 (attribute only where verification is available) is refuted rather than merely a weaker option: as specified it does not repair factivity (0.1181 vs baseline 0.1175) and costs 66% coverage; its corrected form reaches 0 violations only by reading the world directly, with 71.2% of attributions bypassing the epistemic pipeline -- it exits the theorem's hypotheses (Gamma is no longer a function of E alone) rather than refuting the impossibility result. [S2482] The research lane's own recommendation is R2 (externalize factivity), not R1 -- diverging from the R1 actually selected by governance. The stated reasoning rests entirely on one asymmetry: R2 makes the 88.25% survival quantity observable where R1 leaves it invisible; R1 is acknowledged as 'the cheapest' and a defensible choice if a verification context's cost is judged too high.
