## Rationale
Overall verdict on the 'Beyond Measure' (quantum-measurement history/philosophy) book summary: it should be used as evidence about difficult epistemological questions, not a KnowledgeOS specification, since the book itself disclaims providing a formal computational model or complete ontology. Its real value is attacking six unconscious assumptions: measurement≠passive observation, observation≠reality, probability≠necessarily ignorance, knowledge≠complete representation, one description≠necessarily the whole phenomenon, knowledge≠understanding [S0824]. Endorses the epistemic-vs-ontic probability distinction from the book (epistemic P(X|F)=uncertainty from lack of information; ontic=a feature of the physical process itself) as strengthening the earlier 'extraction may be probabilistic, knowledge itself is not' finding: a stored probability=0.73 is meaningless without the semantics/regime under which it was produced, so ProbabilityValue + ProbabilitySemantics must be treated as distinct, with probability belonging to a regime/evaluation layer, not the substrate [S0824].
