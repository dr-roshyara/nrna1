## Rationale
Argues even strong, uncontradicted, current, high-provenance evidence does not automatically warrant acceptance, introducing AcceptancePolicy as the missing concept determining what may be admitted as accepted knowledge [S0863]. Defines Commit_ρ(P,Purpose,Authority) as purpose-dependent, e.g. migration commitment requiring Architecture Board authority versus lighter requirements for internal research, echoing the earlier purpose-dependence of the Ideal State [S0863]. Justifies Accepted+Contested coexisting via an architecture-board example (formally accepted decision still disputed by an architect), establishing that epistemic status and governance status must not be collapsed [S0863]. Connects Acceptance to the Ideal State: Accepted(P) ⇏ IdealSatisfied(P) — a proposition can be accepted while the underlying knowledge objective (e.g. independent verification) remains incomplete [S0863]. Corrects the apparent ladder Candidate→Supported→Accepted→Committed: it is not a universal ordering (Accepted⇏Committed; Contested can coexist with Accepted), so AcceptanceStatus must be a state space, not a scalar hierarchy [S0863].
