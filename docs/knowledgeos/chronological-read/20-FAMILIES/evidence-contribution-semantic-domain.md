## Rationale
[S0860] (WARNING/ARGUMENT): Warns that an unlabeled scalar contribution value (e.g. 0.8) is dangerously ambiguous — it could mean probability, evidential support strength, source reliability, LLM confidence, likelihood ratio, degree of belief, plausibility, or source quality — and has no fixed epistemic meaning until its semantics are specified.

[S0860] (ARGUMENT/EXAMPLE): Argues KnowledgeOS should first construct a relational structure Rel(E,P) (supports/derived-from/contradicts/stale-supports edges) rather than a Score(E,P) scalar, calling the relational structure the epistemic foundation.

[S0860] (RESTATEMENT/ARGUMENT): Answers the recurring 'one evidence algebra?' question definitively No, drawing the DDD boundary: the kernel defines what evidence means and how it relates; the reasoning policy determines how it is mathematically assessed.

[S0860] (ARGUMENT/LIMITATION): Notes Bayesian Pr(P|E) requires priors, likelihoods, an evidence model, dependency and sampling assumptions, and model adequacy that cannot be inferred merely from a document's existence — 'Probability requires a model.'

[S0860] (ANALYSIS): Re-examines candidate calculi against the semantic-domain requirement: weighted arithmetic's 0.8 still lacks universal semantics; saturating aggregation (1-∏(1-s_i)) does not itself resolve dependency without the Evidence Graph; Bayesian and Dempster-Shafer each carry their own assumptions; argumentation uniquely preserves why an assertion is supported/attacked.

[S0860] (ARGUMENT/PRINCIPLE): Argues KnowledgeOS needs two complementary calculus families — quantitative (Bayesian/Statistical/Possibility) for numerically meaningful uncertainty, and qualitative/argumentative (for ADRs, architectural principles, competing interpretations) — proposing the principle 'Not every epistemic problem should become a probability problem.'

[S0860] (DISTINCTION/ARGUMENT): Introduces InformationGain as distinct from EvidenceStrength, illustrated by a probability shift from 0.5 to 0.99: a weak piece of evidence can have enormous information value if it resolves a critical uncertainty — EvidenceValue ≠ EvidenceStrength, foreshadowing Lord/Sarathi's evidence-acquisition selection.
