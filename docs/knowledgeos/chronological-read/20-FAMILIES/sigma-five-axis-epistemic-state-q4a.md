## Rationale
- [S2132] (FORMALIZATION/ARGUMENT) Observational equality is formalized as an axis-projection family: Sigma_1 ≈_X Sigma_2 iff π_X(Sigma_1) = π_X(Sigma_2) for X ⊆ {A,S,R,V,C} (the five epistemic-state axes from Q4A, 2240 states). Because ≈_X is the pullback of equality along the projection π_X, it is automatically an equivalence relation for every choice of X, so no individual X needs to be checked; the file corrects an earlier overclaim ('the axes ARE the observations') to 'the five axes provide a bounded candidate space for state-level observations,' with the selection of X remaining a normative (governance) choice, not a derived one.
- [S2133] (ALTERNATIVE/CORRECTION) A rival, corpus-native parameterization of observational equality is found: 25I.35 defines K1 ≈_C K2 as 'epistemically equivalent for context C' where C is a purpose/policy (KnowledgeEquivalence is purpose-relative; the running example: equivalent for MigrationPolicy_X but not for PatchCompliancePolicy_Y). This is explicitly not the same relation as the author's own X ⊆ {A,S,R,V,C} axis-subset parameterization (32 projections); the 32-projection catalogue is a defensible construction but not established as the corpus's ≈. Also recovers 25I.36's purpose statement: 'Zero should not ask are these identical? It should ask is the available knowledge sufficient?' giving Sufficient(K,r,C) and the boxed principle Identity != Equivalence != Sufficiency.
- [S2155] (ANALYSIS/LIMITATION) Directly answers what Sigma and K are and what maps between them: an element of Sigma is a settled 5-tuple of labels (cardinality 2240, independently verified); an element of K is NOT settled -- competing candidate forms exist (Step 285's K_t as ratified anchor; K=(A,R,Sigma,E_L) per Step 273; a canonical construction K=(D_t,A,R,Sigma_c,E_L); and 258.35's requirement that K be defined extensionally through behavioural sufficiency, not as a tuple at all). No mapping between Sigma and K is specified anywhere in the corpus; under the tuple readings, Sigma appears merely as a component field of K (per-assertion), never as an image or projection of K.
- [S2155] (ARGUMENT/CORRECTION) Escalates the Sigma-order-vs-K-order separation from 'unproven' to 'not even type-correct': the candidate mapping relations (representation, abstraction/projection, quotient, observation, interpretation, analogy) are tested and all fail except 'interpretation' (the only defensible reading of approx_X) and 'analogy' (identified as what a Sigma-order-to-K-order inference actually, illegitimately, rests on). Since Sigma orders one field of one assertion while K ranges over assertions, relations, provenance, history, governance, events, and retraction, the inference Sigma_1<=Sigma_2 implies K_1<=K_2 is a category/type error, not merely an unproven implication.
- [S2169] (ANALYSIS/DISTINCTION) Answers the mandate's five audit questions about approx_X definitively: it does NOT instantiate the corpus's actual approx (corpus approx is indexed by the observation set O_K via 261.5's forall-O formula; approx_X is a Sigma-axis projection pi_X, a different index and a different carrier); it IS exactly one formalization of the informal word 'observational' (agreement on observable labels, explicitly distinguished from behaviour per 258.11); no primary corpus artifact ever specifies which X is intended; approx_X is not even the same KIND of relation as equiv_K, since approx_X is Sigma-level and equiv_K is K-level, and no projection pi:K->Sigma exists anywhere in the corpus (Sigma being a component field of K, not an image of it, per Step 289's boundary audit).
- [S2169] (ARGUMENT/WARNING) Determines that the 30 non-degenerate approx_X candidates (carried forward from Step 289's degeneracy audit, excluding the universal approx_empty-set at 1 block and the discrete approx_full at 2240 blocks) demonstrate UNDER-SPECIFICATION of a single parameter X, not a genuine multiplicity of 30 canonical candidate relations -- a bounded candidate family is never the same thing as a selected canonical relation. Explicitly warns that citing '30' is seductive because it looks like progress toward answering 'which approx?' when it actually answers a different question, 'how many ways could one read approx at the Sigma level?' -- and that approx_X was built in Step 287 to bound an undefined mathematical universe down to 30 enumerated options, which is real work, but is not itself evidence about the corpus's actual approx.
