## Rationale
Analyzes Decision's candidate invariants owned by Governance; distinguishes Policy from Decision (a Decision adopts a principle, e.g. 'adopt architecture principle X'; a Policy generalizes it, e.g. 'all production services must satisfy X'; Decision->Policy is a governed relationship); states Exception does not invalidate the underlying policy but combines with it (Policy+Exception->EffectivePolicy), belonging conceptually to Governance [S1036]. States a Fitness Rule represents a formally expressible architectural/governance constraint, not merely a script, listing required attributes; defines Verification = Execution of a Rule against a Subject, formalized V(R,S,E,t)->Verdict; states Finding ≠ Violation — a finding can originate from FAIL or UNKNOWN and may represent non-conformance, insufficient evidence, stale state, suspicious drift, or unresolved ambiguity [S1036]. Works four ownership-test examples: 'architecture finding' belongs to Assurance (evaluates expected vs observed, may reference Knowledge and Governance); 'architecture recommendation' belongs to Agent/Application (a proposal, may reference Finding and Knowledge); 'architecture decision' belongs to Governance even if an agent drafts it (agent produces DraftRecommendation, Governance produces Decision); 'architecture rule' belongs to Assurance for machine-verifiable conformance but its authority derives from Governance (Governance->Rule); yields the six-line hierarchy 'Governance defines / Knowledge represents / Assurance verifies / Engineering executes / Evidence proves-records / Agents reason and act within these boundaries.' [S1036].
