## Rationale

Core challenge: the Kernel identity invariant assumes identity is fixed (immutable Value Object), intrinsic (property of the entity), and assigned/forced (mechanism-imposed); Daoist ziran ('self-so', opposite of wei/forcing) suggests identity is instead transformational, contextual, and natural, drawn from Daodejing ch.25/37/51 and Zhuangzi ch.2/13 plus Ames & Hall, Moeller, and Ziporyn scholarly interpretation [S0455]. Additionally, Connects the Chinese-philosophy research explicitly to the recognition-criterion question, reading it as having surfaced identity, context, authority, naming, contradiction, action, and transformation as falsification questions (not architectural conclusions) about what must remain invariant across K_t -> K_{t+1}, endorsing this as exactly the right use of the philosophical lenses [S0653]. Further, Applies information theory (I(C;E), mutual information between competing claims and evidence) to give the Viveka principle ('which distinctions must not collapse') a precise mathematical operationalization: does the evidence contain enough information to distinguish claim A from claim B, identified as a beautiful convergence between an epistemological lens and a mathematical one [S0670]. Relatedly, Applies nine lenses (Logic, Probability, Information theory, Temporal mathematics, Causal reasoning, Argumentation, Nyaya, Navya-Nyaya, Viveka, DDD) to the same Reason/Fact pair, each posing a distinct question (e.g. does R logically entail p; how strongly does R support p; does R distinguish p from competitors; when was R valid; does R explain or merely correlate with p; what is the valid pramana by which p is known; what precise relation holds between Reason and Proposition; have Observation/Evidence/Reason/Fact been accidentally collapsed; which distinctions must be preserved as domain invariants) [S0672].
