## Rationale
Argues absolute completeness (K_t=I^K_{P_r}) is generally unrealistic and not required, given always-possible unknown information, future change, unmodeled dimensions, and irrelevant details [S0867]. Shows full requirement coverage can still be insufficient (e.g. a contested backup status), establishing Coverage ≠ Sufficiency [S0867]. Shows Sufficient=False can still coexist with Ready=True given explicit risk acceptance at Medium criticality — epistemic insufficiency does not always imply operational prohibition [S0867]. Shows different purposes require different uncertainty thresholds (exploratory tolerates High, architecture decisions Moderate, production None for critical items) — thresholds belong to PurposePolicy [S0867]. Worked full ReadinessCertificate example (18 requirements, 16 satisfied, 2 outstanding/risk-accepted, named authority and policy version) contrasted favorably with an unauditable 'the AI said it was okay' [S0867]. Notes computational viability of readiness evaluation depends on semantic evaluation, which Step 12 already showed is not always decidable — hence requirement evaluations remain three-valued, preserving computability without pretending omniscience [S0867].
