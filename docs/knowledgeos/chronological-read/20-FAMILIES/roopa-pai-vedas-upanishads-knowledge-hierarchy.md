Note: Extracts an implicit (non-Shannon) theory of information from the same Roopa Pai Vedas/Upanishads source's treatment of oral transmission, distinct from that file's knowledge-hierarchy extraction. Central claim: information is a representation transmissible between knowers but not identical with knowledge. Models the Vedic sound-based information carrier as multi-dimensional (words+pronunciation+rhythm+tone+emphasis+sequence, with Sama Veda melody notation as an encoding example), subject to transmission degradation (loss/distortion/addition/reinterpretation), for which the tradition's redundant multi-pattern recitation functioned as an error-detection/integrity mechanism (Original->multiple recitation Patterns A/B/C->cross-check->integrity). Enumerates five information channels (oral, musical, written, demonstrative, dialogical) noting the Upanishadic teacher-student form is INTERACTIVE (question-driven), not one-way broadcast. States the central distinction 'successful information transmission != successful knowledge transmission', splitting Information Transmission (Sender->Signal->Receiver) from Knowledge Transmission (Teacher->Signal->Student->Interpretation->Understanding->Experience->Realization). Adds a syntax/semantics/pragmatics decomposition of a signal, and states information is inherently contextual (Message+Context+Knower=Interpretation). Uses the historical example of ritual practice persisting after its meaning was lost (~700 BCE) as a concrete case of 'procedure preserved != purpose understood' (artifact integrity != semantic integrity), motivating a three-part integrity requirement: representation integrity (was what was said preserved), semantic integrity (was what it meant preserved), and intent integrity (was why it was said preserved). States information quality depends on the receiver, not just the message (World->Senses->Perception->Mind->Interpretation->Understanding, each stage introducing possible distortion), and that information is broader than language (linguistic/visual/auditory/behavioral/emotional/contextual/experiential), reformulated generally as 'information is a detectable difference that can alter the state of a receiver.' Concludes with a formal extracted definition ('information is a structured representation...carrying distinctions, meaning, or experience from a source to a receiver through a transmission channel; information becomes knowledge only when the receiver successfully integrates it into understanding') and a three-layer architecture (Information: representation/encoding/signal/storage/transmission/integrity -> Knowledge: meaning/context/understanding/validation/experience/reasoning -> Wisdom: judgment/action/transformation/capability/realization), plus four preserved principles: preserving information != preserving knowledge; preserving representation != preserving meaning; transmitting meaning != producing understanding; knowledge exists only when information has transformed the knower's capability.

## Sources (how this label entered the ledger)
- **OBJECT-INDEX** (batch B0056, scope OBJECT): A knowledge-theory reconstruction (explicitly not a claim of literal Vedic/Upanishadic doctrine) from Roopa Pai's 'The Vedas and Upanishads for Children', read deliberately AS a knowledge theory rather than a philosophy summary. Central claim: Knowledge != information (true knowledge combines intelligence, instinct, intuition, wisdom, empathy, and understanding of participation in the Supreme Energy). Builds a six-level hierarchy Data -> Information -> Understanding -> Knowledge -> Wisdom -> Realization, and a parallel six-stage epistemic-process hierarchy (Information/memorization -> Understanding -> Inquiry/questioning -> Reflection-and-reasoning -> Experience/testing -> Realization), with Belief != Knowledge (agreement is insufficient; the seeker must test recommendations personally) and Knowledge = transformation produced by understanding reality (not storage in a database). Describes an ancient knowledge-integrity architecture (Guru->Student->Memorization->MultiplePathaRepresentations->CrossChecking->Recitation->NextGeneration) using redundant recitation patterns to detect transmission corruption, and separates three distinct knowledge problems: Integrity (was it preserved correctly), Meaning (do we understand it), Realization (have we verified/transformed ourselves through it) -- with preservation explicitly NOT sufficient for knowledge. States knowledge requires an epistemically prepared/qualified knower (humility, patience, discipline, faith, curiosity, willingness to question) and models faith as shraddha -- willing to question, analyze and demand answers, not blind acceptance; states the Upanishads permit multiple, even contradictory interpretations with 'the debate never over', separating the stable knowledge artifact from its open interpretation. States knowledge's purpose is transformation/liberation, not possession, and that knowledge has an inherent social/sharing dimension (Discovery->Preservation->Transmission->Verification->FurtherDiscovery). Culminates in Tat Tvam Asi / Ayam Atma Brahma / Prajnanam Brahma as the self-referential claim that the deepest knower is itself part of or identical with the reality it seeks to know. Extends the KnowledgeOS notion by proposing a relationship chain Reality->Observation/Experience->Insight->Representation->Transmission->Interpretation->Validation->Practice->Realization->ChangedCapability, and the distinction 'a document is a representation of knowledge; a database is a storage mechanism for representations; a validated model is structured knowledge; a capable engineer is embodied knowledge', concluding knowledge exists only when the knower has understood, verified, internalized, and can act from it -- not when a document or database merely contains it.

## Rationale
Reports the book's central claim that Vedic 'true knowledge' is not information from books or applied skills but a combination of intelligence, instinct, intuition, wisdom, empathy and understanding of universal participation -- stated as the single most important sentence for the reconstruction: Knowledge != information. [S2328] States that the Upanishadic seeker does not merely acquire propositions but undergoes a transformation through an eight-step process (question, withdraw, discipline, concentrate, contemplate, experience insight, test, share), giving Knowledge = transformation produced by understanding reality, contrasted with the modern notion of knowledge as something stored in a database. [S2328] Describes the Vedic oral-transmission system as an ancient knowledge-integrity architecture: multiple recitation patterns (Patha) encode the same content through different sequences functioning as redundancy mechanisms for detecting corruption, explicitly analogized to a KnowledgeOS integrity concern; separates this from meaning, stating Problem A (integrity: was it preserved correctly), Problem B (meaning: do we understand it), and Problem C (realization: have we verified/transformed ourselves through it) as three distinct knowledge problems, with correct preservation alone insufficient for knowledge.
