## Rationale
The evidence base attributes the following rationale to this object (as recorded, cited to its source):

- **[ARGUMENT/VALIDATION]** [S2313]: Finds RevisionType::Evolution (the corpus's 'the system really changed from 3.69 to 3.70', boxed 'Evolution is not an error') is exactly Katsuno-Mendelzon's motivating case for UPDATE (the world changed, the old belief was correct then), while Correction/Refutation match AGM REVISION (new information about a static world); notes AGM is mentioned only 5 times across 564 corpus files and the KM revision/update distinction appears nowhere, so the corpus independently re-derived, from a version-number example, a distinction belief-revision literature has held since 1991 -- and split it six ways where KM splits two.
- **[ARGUMENT/FORMALIZATION]** [S2313]: Finds the corpus's governed admission (Closure-03: LLM proposes, Lord evaluates, Governance authorizes; a proposal may be rejected) is exactly credibility-limited (non-prioritized) revision K⊛phi = K*phi if phi in C, else K, with the credibility set C identified structure-preservingly as Policy x Authority.
- **[ARGUMENT/FORMALIZATION]** [S2313]: Finds the corpus's boxed Revision = EventAddition + StateReDerivation (H'=H union {E_revision}, K'=Derive(H',Omega,EC,M)) is exactly a Doyle-style JTMS: the corpus's own Fagin extraction already states 'knowledge history is monotonic in preservation; knowledge state is not necessarily monotonic in projection', which is the JTMS invariant verbatim (justification store grows monotonically, derived belief set is non-monotone).
- **[ANALYSIS/LIMITATION]** [S2313]: Structural incompatibility: AGM's carrier K=Cn(K) is deductively closed, but the corpus's assertion set A (measured: zero occurrences of a consequence operator Cn) is a finite set of asserted items with identity/provenance/evidence links, i.e. a Hansson belief BASE, not a belief SET; since base contraction and AGM contraction on Cn(B) are provably inequivalent, naively importing AGM theorems would import claims false of A.
- **[ARGUMENT/LIMITATION]** [S2313]: Decisive finding: AGM holds the language L and its interpretation fixed -- every operator maps L-sentences to L-sentences, with no operator for changing what sentences MEAN -- but the corpus's Reinterpretation ('the original observation was valid, but we interpreted it incorrectly', a change in Evidence(O,P,C,R)'s rule R) and ModelRevision ('reasonable under the old model, but the model itself was subsequently changed', a change in M within Derive(H,Omega,EC,M)) are both first-class corpus revision types with no AGM counterpart at all; framed as the corpus formalizing something AGM chose to hold constant, i.e. the gap runs against the external theory, not against KnowledgeOS.
- **[CONTRADICTION/ANALYSIS]** [S2313]: Refutes mapping Sigma onto an AGM entrenchment ordering: AGM entrenchment <= is a total, transitive preorder satisfying dominance and minimality, but the corpus's Sigma (isomorphic to {0,1}^2, the Supported/Refuted product) is a product structure, not an order -- Supported(1,0) and Refuted(0,1) are incomparable, neither more entrenched than the other. Offers, only as a stated-not-adopted hypothesis (BR-H4), a lattice ordering (0,0)<={(1,0),(0,1)}<=(1,1) under set inclusion on the support set, explicitly noting this would order informativeness, not resistance-to-retraction, and the corpus asserts no such order.
- **[ANALYSIS/RESTATEMENT]** [S2313]: Final verdict: belief revision is 'the closest of the five' external theories compared in this series, with four exact correspondences (more than any other), a resolvable carrier mismatch (move from AGM to base revision), and one axis (Reinterpretation/ModelRevision) where KnowledgeOS is ahead of the external framework rather than under-formalized; endorses and sharpens the corpus's own Gardenfors-derived self-description ('epistemic states and their dynamics as the primary modelling problem', Kernel=preservation substrate) into 'the substrate the corpus describes is a justification store, and that is a JTMS.'
- **[ANALYSIS/EXTENSION]** [S2599]: Names two places where KnowledgeOS's own formalization exceeds the compared external theory rather than merely matching or falling short of it: two of the corpus's six revision types (Reinterpretation, ModelRevision) change the interpretation function itself, something AGM/KM hold fixed and have no operator to express; and the corpus's three-way Determination/Decision/Action split adds a cut beyond Wald's two-way decision-theoretic split, specifically the cut that governance needs.
