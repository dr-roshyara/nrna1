## Rationale
Shows why more information does not automatically mean more knowledge: Person A knows 5 dimensions extremely precisely; Person B knows 20 dimensions reasonably well and may have more knowledge overall by having discovered more of the relevant state space despite less precision per dimension; Person C knows 5 dimensions extremely well AND knows that many dimensions may remain undiscovered -- Person C has lower completeness confidence but arguably better epistemic awareness than either A or B. [S0691] Argues KnowledgeOS cannot only store 'what do we know' -- it must also preserve which dimensions are currently believed relevant and how confident the system is that its dimensional model is complete enough; without this, an AI could falsely claim 'I know everything about Nexus' when it has only modeled a handful of dimensions well. KnowledgeOS should instead be able to honestly say it has strong evidence on known dimensions but unresolved uncertainty about whether additional relevant dimensions exist. [S0691] Extends the Team A/B example to a harder case: Team A claims 95% completeness with high confidence but low awareness of unknown dimensions, Team B claims only 70% completeness but has high awareness that unknown dimensions may exist; Team B may possess better epistemic knowledge because it knows what it does not know. Connects this to the known-unknowns/unknown-unknowns concept, but notes the present formulation is more precise since it concerns dimensions of the ideal state specifically, not merely missing facts in general. [S0692] Shows time changes both what is known and what is considered relevant: discovering five new dimensions at t2 does not mean the underlying object changed, only that knowledge of it changed; at t3 a dimension can become irrelevant purely because the purpose changed -- the relevant dimension set is itself time- and context-dependent, important for KnowledgeOS. [S0696] Establishes three results: (1) a dimension can exist even when nobody knows about it -- unknown dimension != non-existent dimension; (2) the observer's incomplete D_t does not mean the underlying state D* is incomplete, only that knowledge of it is incomplete; (3) dimensions are not necessarily independent -- discovering a new dimension can change the interpretation or determined value of already-known dimensions, so D_{t+1}!=D_t can require State_{t+1}=Recalculate(D_{t+1},Information_{t+1}) rather than simply appending one more fact, described as one of the strongest results reached. [S0703] Places the current research maturity on a 12-stage diagram (Research -> Phenomenon -> Dimensions/State -> Observation/Evidence -> Extraction/Determination -> Knowledge -> Time-change/Uncertainty -> Knowledge trajectory -> What must be preserved -> Kernel admission -> Architecture), self-assessed as sitting around the Knowledge->trajectory->preservation boundary, not yet at architecture. Reduces everything to one biggest unresolved question: given the complete state may have potentially infinite dimensions, how can an observer construct, evaluate, and continuously revise a knowledge state without knowing the dimensions that remain undiscovered -- and commits to challenging the dialogue's own model with external research rather than assuming every prior conclusion is correct. [S0703] Establishes that more information does not imply more completeness confidence, since new information can reveal the previous model was incomplete -- requiring three separately-tracked variables: knowledge content, knowledge completeness, and knowledge uncertainty, which can move in different directions (tabulated across six event types: new fact discovered, unknown-dimension-identified-with-known-value, unknown-dimension-identified-with-uncertain-value, existing value confirmed, contradictory evidence discovered, new dimension ruled irrelevant). Even when a newly discovered dimension's value is 100% certain, comparing yesterday's knowledge to today's still yields K_Monday < K_Tuesday in completeness -- the comparison is temporal (K_yesterday vs K_today), not K_yesterday vs Truth. [S0706] Explicitly rejects the source Ashtavakra-Gita document's own architectural conclusion ('KnowledgeOS is not about dimensions -- it is about consciousness'), since the document itself acknowledges it establishes no formal knowledge-state model, dimension theory, measurement theory, probabilistic extraction model, or formal epistemology, and therefore cannot overturn the project's dimension-based model; only the lens (do not mistake the knowledge representation for ultimate reality) is extracted, matching the corpus-wide pattern of 'extract the distinction, refuse the ontology'. [S0715] Reframes the multi-lens research method itself: lenses (engineering, security, dependency, governance, legal, Zero, Sanskrit/semantic, epistemic) are not merely alternative interpretations of the same knowledge but distinct MECHANISMS for discovering dimensions other lenses fail to see, formalized as D_t = ∪_lenses D_t^(lens); called potentially one of the most important roles of the lens architecture. Also notes that new dimension discovery can retroactively change the interpretation of already-known values, not merely add facts: K_{t+1} = Recalculate(K_t, ΔD, ΔV, ΔE). [S0720] Distinguishes a 'known gap' (a specific missing dimension d ∈ D*\D_t is identified) from an 'unknown gap' (D*\D_t is itself partially unknown, i.e. we don't know which dimensions are missing), and generalizes this to recursive uncertainty about unknown dimensions, unknown relationships, and unknown interpretations — concluding Zero(K_t) may itself contain unresolved structure and therefore should not be an ordinary database field. [S0721] Restates that adding a dimension does not automatically constitute improvement in every sense, since a newly discovered dimension can force recalculation of an already-known dimension's value; concludes knowledge improvement means a better model, not merely more records. [S0723] Gives a mathematical justification for multi-lens knowledge discovery: applying lenses L_1..L_n each discovering dimension sets D^(1)..D^(n), the union D_t=∪D^(i) still generally satisfies D_t ⊆ D*; the purpose of multiple lenses is explicitly to increase dimensional coverage, not merely to produce different interpretations. [S0723] Argues, from the text listing several parallel dimensional systems (elements, speech types, temporal states, consciousness states) all describing the same underlying reality, that there is no single canonical dimensional decomposition of an observation — multiple lenses producing D_i ≠ D_j are not competing/wrong but different valid projections, with D* ⊇ ∪_i D_i. **Theme 4 — Dimension existence independent of the observer; the unsolved `D*-D_t` reasoning problem; research maturity staging** (4 rows: S0703 ×4). **Theme 5 — Category-error corrections to Knowledge's definition; partial ordering and `K^obs_t` layering** (4 rows: S0706 ×4). **Theme 6 — Statement/dimension/value/claim/evidence separation (Nexus sentence experiment) and the seven-field `KnowledgeState_t` tuple** (12 rows: S0709 ×6, S0713 ×6). **Theme 7 — Zero-lens applied to dimension discovery: Value-Zero vs Dimension-Zero, the Mithya excursion, kernel requirements** (10 rows: S0715 ×8, S0716 ×2).
