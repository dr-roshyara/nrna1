## Rationale
- [S0677] (FORMALIZATION, EXPLANATION): Knowledge Space Theory's formal model of conditional evidence: a response function r(R,K)=P(observing response pattern R | state K); a Bayesian update P(K|r) prop P(r|K)P(K); and an assessment update L_{n+1}(K)=u(R_n,Q_n,L_n) where u is either convex (L_{n+1}(K)=(1-theta)L_n(K)+theta*conditional(K|R_n,Q_n)) or multiplicative (L_{n+1}(K) prop c_{q,r}*L_n(K)).
- [S0677] (LIMITATION, ANALYSIS): KST's update is state-based (the state K is the variable of interest; evidence updates the probability of each state; the update rule -- convex, multiplicative or Bayesian -- is specified externally), and under convergence conditions the distribution concentrates on the true state; but KST does not model how evidence should be combined in general, what the 'correct' update rule is, whether Bayesian updating is the only valid approach, how to handle conflicting evidence, or how to model evidence structure beyond response patterns -- it is one specific implementation, not a general theory.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Bayesian probability P(H|E)=P(E|H)P(H)/P(E) models probability as degree of belief, evidence as conditioning, update as multiplication by likelihood; strengths: coherent (Kolmogorov axioms), optimal (Dutch book arguments), well-understood, extends to infinite spaces via measure theory; weaknesses: requires priors and likelihoods, assumes a single probability measure, struggles to represent 'no information' (ignorance). Relevance: the Kernel might provide observations; a Bayesian regime would use them to update a probability distribution over epistemic states.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Dempster-Shafer theory assigns mass m:2^Theta->[0,1] with m(empty)=0 and total mass 1, defining Belief Bel(A) and Plausibility Pl(A), and combines evidence via Dempster's rule; models belief as mass over sets of possibilities and handles ignorance explicitly (mass assignable to the whole space), distinguishing belief from plausibility; weaknesses: controversial combination rule, computationally expensive, less well-understood, and Bayesian probability is a special case of it. Relevance: a Dempster-Shafer regime might represent uncertainty about the state as a belief function with different evidence sources providing mass assignments.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Fuzzy/many-valued logic represents degree of membership or truth via mu:Theta->[0,1], combined via min/max, product, Lukasiewicz etc. with no single standard rule; models vague/graded concepts and degrees of truth rather than degrees of belief; strengths: handles vagueness naturally, continuous gradations, well-developed theory; weaknesses: controversial philosophical foundations, no canonical combination rule, can be confused with probability, evidence combination is arbitrary. Relevance: a fuzzy regime might represent an epistemic state as a fuzzy set of possibilities, with evidence updating the membership function.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Non-monotonic logic models phi following defeasibly from Gamma unless there is evidence to the contrary, via default rules with exceptions, preferential entailment, and circumscription; models reasoning with defaults and withdrawable conclusions; strengths: models common-sense reasoning, handles exceptions, has formal semantics (preferential models); weaknesses: multiple competing formalisms, less agreement on semantics, computationally expensive, evidence combination is rule-based not numerical. Relevance: a non-monotonic regime might model an epistemic state as a set of defeasible conclusions, with evidence adding or overriding rules.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Argumentation theory constructs arguments from evidence, defines attack relations between arguments, and uses acceptability semantics (Dung et al.) to determine justified conclusions; models reasoning as debate, conflicting evidence, justification and defeat; strengths: explicit conflict representation, dialectical reasoning, well-developed formal semantics; weaknesses: computational complexity, less quantitative, evidence treated as propositions rather than observations. Relevance: an argumentation regime might model the epistemic state as the set of justified conclusions, with evidence generating arguments that attack or support each other.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Epistemic logic formalizes K_i phi (agent i knows phi) with axioms K, T ('knowledge implies truth'), 4 (positive introspection) and 5 (negative introspection); models knowledge as factive belief, logical closure, and higher-order knowledge; strengths: well-understood possible-worlds semantics, captures factivity and closure, extends to multi-agent scenarios; weaknesses: assumes logical omniscience, knowledge is non-probabilistic, evidence combination is not the focus, and it is static unless extended via dynamic epistemic logic. Relevance: an epistemic logic regime might model an epistemic state as a set of propositions known, with evidence updating the knowledge relation.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Temporal logic formalizes always (Box phi), sometimes (Diamond phi) and until (phi U psi) operators; models time-dependent truth, sequences of states and eventualities; strengths: rigorous temporal semantics, model checking algorithms; weaknesses: evidence combination is not the focus (temporal reasoning is), and it assumes complete information about the model. Relevance: a temporal logic regime might model the epistemic state at different times, with evidence constraining the temporal evolution.
- [S0677] (EXPLANATION): Measurement theory maps f:X->R subject to representation conditions, combining evidence through numerical operations; models numerical representation of attributes and mapping of empirical relations to numerical relations; strengths: rigorous foundation, connects empirical observations to numerical constructs, handles error and uncertainty; weaknesses: assumes numerical representation, evidence combination is numerical (e.g. averaging), less suited for qualitative reasoning. Relevance: a measurement theory regime might model epistemic states as numerical vectors, with evidence updating the measurements through filtering (e.g. Kalman filters).
