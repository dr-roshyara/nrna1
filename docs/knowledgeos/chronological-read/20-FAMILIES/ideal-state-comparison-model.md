## Rationale

The label enters the ledger with S0685's reframing of the whole KnowledgeOS pipeline: instead of building an ideal state up from arbitrary observations, a human first has or conceives an Ideal State and evaluates reality against it. The concrete problem this closed was explaining where graded, sub-1.0 confidence values come from: if the ideal state requires four conditions A, B, C, D but observations only partially resolve them (P(A)=1, P(B)=0.9, P(C)=0.6, P(D)=0.2), the resulting uncertainty belongs to the *extraction* of the current state relative to the ideal (I_t -> hat-I_t) — it is not a property of the ideal state itself [S0685]. The model's next major extension, worked out against a concrete Nexus-version example, generalized this into an argument that being "old" is not an intrinsic property of an observed value at all, but a relational one: `Old(2.69) iff 2.69 < ReferenceVersion_t`, a proposition whose truth value depends entirely on the current reference/ideal state rather than on the observation alone — knowledge is frequently produced by comparing an observed state against a reference state, not by inspecting the observation in isolation [S0688]. That, in turn, exposed a gap the model needed to close: the reference/ideal state is not a given, but is itself Knowledge, established through its own prior chain (a vendor releases 3.85 → this is observed → evidence accumulates → a determination is made → "3.85 is current/required" becomes the new reference state). This gives KnowledgeOS a recursive structure — Knowledge → Ideal/Reference State → Comparison → New Knowledge → Updated Reference State → … — and a chain from Evidence through Justification and Trusted Statement to the reference state itself, carefully keeping trust distinct from truth (a trusted source's claim must preserve *why* it is trusted, not merely who asserted it) [S0688, S0706].

A second load-bearing move addressed how the model behaves over time. Knowledge at different times is understood as different epistemic states, and a determination can become historically obsolete or superseded without the historical record being deleted — the K_Monday → K_Tuesday trajectory must be preserved. This is sharpened by a genuinely counter-intuitive consequence: discovering a new dimension can retroactively reveal that a *past* knowledge state was less complete than previously assessed, even though the value of that new dimension is known today with full certainty — so completeness assessments of the past change as later dimensions are discovered [S0703]. The model resolves what otherwise looks like a reality/knowledge contradiction here: the ideal state I_t exists independently of what is known about it, so an unknown dimension is not a nonexistent one — the observed state is only a projection of the ideal state through available information, and discovering a dimension is an epistemic event, not an ontological one. From this the model draws what it calls one of its most important unresolved distinctions: state change (the ideal state itself changes) versus knowledge change (a previously-existing but unknown dimension is merely discovered) — a Kernel unable to keep these apart will eventually corrupt the history of Knowledge [S0706].

The model then generalizes into an explicit architectural consequence and, later, into the corpus's Gita-derived Sañjaya/Arjuna/Krishna reading of the pipeline. Architecturally, it argues that Truth and Determination must be kept formally separate — a proposition can be true in reality while remaining undetermined simply because nobody has yet found the relevant evidence — flagged as possibly the single most important missing foundational distinction in the whole model [S0730]. From this comes a DDD consequence: the object being observed and the process or actor observing it must not be conflated into one aggregate, so the model proposes treating Observation, KnowledgeState, Evidence, Reasoning, Determination, Decision, and Observer as seven separate bounded concepts rather than one giant "Knowledge" aggregate — this is also the label's CANDIDATE-GOVERNANCE-BIRTH [S0733]. The Gita-derived chapter then supplies the human-agency argument that completes the rationale: from Arjuna's crisis (more information about who he faces reduces, not increases, his certainty about what to do) the model derives that KnowledgeOS must never equate information volume with decision quality [S0737], and from the Sañjaya/Dhṛtarāṣṭra role separation it derives that KnowledgeOS ≠ DecisionOwner — the system may report, explain, challenge, and expose, but the human remains owner of understanding and decision [S0739]. This is worked out in detail through an eight-row responsibility table contrasting Arjuna (the Warrior, who acts and owns the action) with Krishna-as-Sārathi (the Charioteer, who supports and navigates but does not act or decide) [S0747] — though, notably, a companion row in the very same source batch (S0747) simultaneously assigns "Frames the problem: Yes" to the System, a claim the same-batch S0746 explicitly retracts (see Notes for P3). The rationale closes on the formalized non-implications KnowledgeOS ⇏ Decision and KnowledgeOS ⇏ Action, argued to prevent an "Oracle fallacy" — the system is not a dispenser of truth but a Sārathi supporting a journey the Knower must undertake — which the model credits with maintaining human agency and giving the whole architecture a clean DDD boundary [S0747].
