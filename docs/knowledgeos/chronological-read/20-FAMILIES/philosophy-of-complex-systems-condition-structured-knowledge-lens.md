## Rationale
- **[ARGUMENT/EXAMPLE]** [S0429]: Third major principle: explanation is not necessarily causal explanation -- the simple A->B model breaks down under feedback, global constraints, emergence, nested correlations, stochastic interdependence, and multiple interacting processes, so Hooker proposes 'dynamical interdependency' as the more general relation; example: 'API latency increased' may depend on database load, connection pool, network topology, cache state, request distribution, GC behaviour, deployment topology, external dependency, and time-of-day traffic simultaneously -- there is no useful single cause, only a dependency structure.
- **[ARGUMENT/EXTENSION]** [S0429]: Fourth major principle: change is the default, stability requires explanation -- Bickhard's process metaphysics reverses the classical Stability->explain Change default into Change->explain Stability/Persistence, since process identity may be based on recurring organisational patterns rather than fixed material components; consequence for KnowledgeOS: knowledge objects (e.g. ADR-123) should perhaps be treated as KnowledgeIdentity+KnowledgeLifecycle+KnowledgeTransitions where identity persists through transformation, challenging Git-style v1/v2/v3 thinking with the question 'what is the invariant pattern that makes v1, v2 and v3 instances of the same evolving thing?'
- **[ARGUMENT/EXTENSION]** [S0429]: Seventh and eighth major principles: reduction does not mean deletion of higher-level knowledge (emergent constraints make processes well-defined while reduction identifies those processes with realizing dynamical flows, giving a bidirectional Domain-constraint <-> mechanism-realisation relationship, not higher-level=disposable-abstraction); mechanisms are not necessarily fixed components (a mechanism may reuse components in different roles, have multiple realisations, interweave with other mechanisms, or emerge from lower-level mechanisms) -- Mechanism != fixed list of components, but 'organised process under conditions that realises a function.'
- **[ARGUMENT/EXAMPLE]** [S0429]: Eighth/ninth major principle group: decomposition is inherently partial -- mechanism/pathway/module/function decomposition strategies are each useful but partial, so KnowledgeOS should not assume one canonical decomposition of a system (bounded-context/aggregate/service/module/workflow/mechanism/data-flow/runtime-dependency/business-capability/team-ownership are different, equally legitimate projections), giving theoretical support for multiple legitimate architecture views over one system.
- **[DISTINCTION/ARGUMENT]** [S0429]: Twentieth/twenty-first major principle: data-driven (model-free/machine-learning) and model-driven knowledge are complementary rather than opposed, with a mixed strategy depending on data-richness vs domain-knowledge-richness; AI-generated pattern != explained mechanism, since model-free learning can produce internal representations with no obvious physical interpretation -- KnowledgeOS should distinguish Predictive Pattern, Mechanistic Explanation, and Causal/Dynamical Model along an explicit, non-monotonic epistemic ladder (Observation->Pattern->Correlation->Model->Mechanism->Explanation->Validated invariant), where a pattern does not necessarily become a mechanism nor a model become reality.
- **[EXTENSION/ARGUMENT]** [S0429]: Proposes a much richer AI-agent loop than Question->LLM-answer->Fact: Question->Frame problem->Determine system boundary->Determine validity context->Collect observations->Construct candidate models->Generate hypotheses->Test->Assess robustness->Record uncertainty->Adjudicate->Act->Observe feedback->Revise model; reinforces Knowledge != Agent behavior -- the agent operates over KnowledgeOS (supplying observations/hypotheses/actions/tests/interpretations) while KnowledgeOS preserves identity/provenance/validity/context/history/evidence/adjudication, never becoming the owner of the knowledge.
