## 2. The corpus-count discrepancy — resolved, and it is a contamination finding

The audit hypothesized corpus growth. The actual cause is more serious and more useful.

1. **A derived analysis lane is writing its output into the primary corpus.**
   `brainstorming/three_model_convergence/` holds **325 `.md` files, every one created on
   2026-09-01**, generated by a sequential source-analysis pass that is *still running*. It is
   derived material by construction, but it is **not in the protocol's five-directory exclusion
   list**, so the stated inclusion rule sweeps it into "primary".
   **1 452 − 325 ≈ 1 153, against the earlier lane's 1 155.** The discrepancy is essentially entirely
   this one directory.

2. **The corpus was mutating during the experiment.** 1 782 `.md` at 22:28 → **1 808** at 23:02, with
   `per-file/NNNN.md` appearing about every two minutes throughout the run. Any corpus statistic in
   this repository is a **timestamped snapshot**, not a population parameter.

**Effect on the findings: none.** MR-1 and all 13 operator role-counts were recomputed under the
corrected rule: MR-1 returns the **identical six files**, and every role count is **unchanged
(delta 0)**. The denominator moves in the direction that makes MR-1 *stronger* — **6 of 1 153**.

`[INF]` **Standing consequence:** the protocol's exclusion list is a *hard-coded directory list*.
That is the wrong mechanism — a derived lane created after the list was written is invisible to it.
The rule should be a **property of the artifact** (declared `authority: generated` / derived-from
provenance), not a folder name. This is a defect the protocol's own provenance discipline was meant
to catch and did not.

## 3. Audit concerns tested against the implementation

The audit (§42) asked to inspect the simulator to check for *hidden representation, hidden
capabilities, vacuous guards, operator-name substitution, and whether ablation removes a capability
or merely bypasses a function call.* Three of those are decidable by running it.

### 3.1 `Observe` — is the ablation label removal? **NO.** (audit §25 refuted)

```
drop=None       Observation=True   SemanticContent=True   Representation=True   Hypothesis=True   Evidence=True   Claim=True
drop=Observe    Observation=False  SemanticContent=False  Representation=False  Hypothesis=False  Evidence=False  Claim=False
```

Worlds are **not** pre-populated with observations. `World` is ambient; `Observation` is not, and it
is derivable only from `(World, world-contact)`. Removing `Observe` collapses the entire downstream
chain. Two independent confirmations already in the report: C1 is lost, and **P7's guard-activation
rate reads exactly 0.00 for the `Observe` arm and 1.00 for every other arm** — the guard *is*
`Observation ∈ Reach`, so that column is a direct measurement of capability removal.

### 3.2 `Represent` — is representation free elsewhere? **YES, and worse than stated.** (audit §24 confirmed)

```
drop=Represent  Observation=True  SemanticContent=True  Representation=False  Hypothesis=True  Evidence=True  Claim=True
capabilities naming the carrier `Representation`:  ['C3']   ← a singleton
```

Removing `Represent` leaves `Hypothesis`, `Evidence` and `Claim` all reachable — **structured
artifacts survive**. And the carrier `Representation` is named by **exactly one capability, C3**.

> `[NEG]` **`Represent`'s irreducibility is DEFINITIONAL, not structural.** It is irreducible because
> C3 asks for the carrier only it produces, and *nothing else in the model needs that carrier*. This
> is the sharpest form of the audit's concern and it is conceded in full. `Represent` is downgraded
> to **UNSUPPORTED** in §6.

### 3.3 V6 — does it bake in the answer? **Partly; here is the exact scope.** (audit §10)

Conceded: `V6 ⇒ Challenge required` is close to definitional, and V6 is not evidence *for* `Challenge`.

But V6 was never the evidence. **The evidence is V0**: under the *baseline* model, with `Challenge`
removed, a `Verdict` is still reachable — a system that validates without ever being able to attack.
V6 is the *contrast case* showing a model in which that is structurally impossible. The finding is
therefore about a **defect in the baseline capability model**, not about the operator, and that is
how it was reported (`12-randomized-results.md`, final paragraph). The audit's stricter grading of
`Challenge` — functional necessity, not primitiveness — is accepted, and §5 now measures it.

## 4. Audit of the protocol as an experimental instrument

Layer 1, audited by layer 2. Ordered by severity.

### P-1 (CRITICAL) — the supplied capability list is a 1:1 restatement of the operator names

Part III instructs: *"Construct a capability set independently from the operator names. Do NOT derive
capabilities merely by restating operator names."* The list supplied immediately below that
instruction is:

| | C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | C6 | C7 | C8 | C9 | C10 | C11 | C12 | C13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| capability | acquire an observation | semantic meaning | a representation | relate | discriminate | hypotheses | inference | detect insufficiency | challenge | validate | revise | determine resolved | select next action |
| `C0` member, same order | Observe | Interpret | Represent | Relate | Discriminate | Hypothesize | Infer | DetectGap | Challenge | Validate | Revise | Determine | Select |

**A positional bijection, 13 for 13, in the same order.** The protocol violates its own instruction
in the paragraph that states it.

**Consequence.** Had that list been honoured literally — one capability per operator — *every
operator would have been irreducible by construction* and the experiment vacuous: removing operator
`i` uniquely destroys capability `Ci`, for all `i`. The experiment produced results **only because
the execution broke the bijection**, by deriving a *shared* atom vocabulary in which
`difference-decision` has two holders and `norm-comparison` has two holders. Every non-trivial
finding — `DetectGap` derivable, the two minimal kernels, the four synergistic pairs, the
powers-vs-packaging asymmetry — lives in exactly that departure.

This is the deepest answer to the audit's own Q-14: the inherited capability model was not merely
"largely from the protocol", it was **isomorphic to the object under test**.

### P-2 (MAJOR) — no procedure for a failing baseline

Part VIII states *"The baseline must succeed before ablation results are interpreted"* and specifies
no contingency. The baseline failed. The repaired-baseline-plus-V7 treatment was **invented during
execution**, not prescribed. A protocol whose only stopping rule is an unreachable precondition has
no defined behaviour in the case that actually occurred.

### P-3 (MAJOR) — Part XI's simulator spec contradicts Part VII's smuggling test

Part XI prescribes *"Implement abstract transformations such as `observe(...) interpret(...)
represent(...)`"* — one function per operator. Under that design, ablation is the removal of a
function call, which is **precisely the failure mode the audit raises in §24 and §25**. Part VII then
demands a smuggling test that a function-per-operator simulator cannot mechanically support.

The execution **deviated deliberately** — building a typed algebra in which an operator *is* its atom
set — and that deviation is why §3.1 could refute the audit's `Observe` concern by computation rather
than by assertion. **The deviation is recorded as an implementation-fidelity departure**
(`09-simulation-design.md`), and on the evidence it was necessary.

### P-4 (MODERATE) — no stopping rule for reconstruction

Part VIII.9: *"Stop only after reasonable reconstruction attempts are exhausted."* "Reasonable" is
undefined, so a negative reconstruction result is unfalsifiable as specified. The execution replaced
judgment with an exhaustive fixpoint — strictly stronger, but that was a choice, not a requirement.

### P-5 (MODERATE) — closed-world reduction and open-world discovery are mixed

The audit's own point, and correct. Part I audits the supplied operators **downward** (*"if an
operator has no meaningful corpus support, record that explicitly"*) but nowhere **upward** (*"is any
required capability unowned?"*). See §4.1 for what should replace it.

### P-6 (MODERATE) — robustness is sampled, never quantified over

Part XXIII asks that the experiment be *repeated under alternative reasonable models* along seven
listed dimensions. It does not ask for the **space of admissible representations** to be
characterized. Complying exactly therefore yields **syntactic** irreducibility under a sample — the
audit's §14 point. It is a protocol gap, not an execution shortfall; §5 begins to close it.

### P-7 (MINOR) — the statistics section invites a category error

Part XII asks for *"confidence interval where appropriate"* on randomized failure rates. Those rates
are properties of an experimenter-chosen generator; an interval on them measures Monte-Carlo error
about a number nobody needs. The execution reported them **and said so**, but the protocol requested
the artifact.

### P-8 (MINOR) — three parts are hedged into undetectability

Parts XIII, XIV and XV are qualified *"where meaningful" / "where useful"*. Their omission would have
been invisible. They were run; the protocol could not have told.

### 4.1 What should replace "candidate completeness"

The audit boxes: *"The experiment did not begin with the complete candidate universe."* True — but
**no reduction experiment ever can.** Completeness of a set of epistemic primitives is not a decidable
property; there is no procedure that certifies "nothing is missing".

What **is** decidable, and what actually happened:

```
CAPABILITY-CLOSURE (decidable):
    for every required capability c :  ∃ o ∈ C with a power reaching c ?
```

This check **fired**, unprompted, and located `Qualify`. The correct reading is therefore not only
*"the operator layer was incomplete"* but also:

> `[EXP]` **A capability-indexed model has a built-in completeness check that an operator-indexed
> model does not.** The gap was found because the requirement `C10 validate` demanded a `Verdict`,
> `Verdict` demanded `Evidence`, and no operator could produce it. An operator-list experiment would
> have reported 13 irreducible operators and noticed nothing.

That is an argument for capability-first design, and it is the strongest methodological result of
the exchange. The proposed pipeline
`Corpus → Capability Inventory → Semantic Normalization → Candidate Primitive Types → Minimality`
is endorsed, **with capability-closure as the gate between stages 2 and 4** — a decidable substitute
for an undecidable completeness demand.

the granularity, not the count.*

### 5.3 Reduction is not free — it redistributes invariant custody `[EXP]` NEW

The audit lists as candidate invariant **I9**: `Verdict ⇒ Defeater consideration`. Asking which
single removal yields a state where a `Verdict` is reachable but a `Defeater` is not:

| Model | Operators whose removal breaks I9 |
|---|---|
| **V0** (baseline) | `Challenge` — exactly one custodian |
| **V8** (audit's reduction of `Challenge`) | **`Hypothesize` *and* `Infer`** — two custodians |

> Eliminating `Challenge` does not remove the vulnerability. It **relocates** it, from one guardian to
> two, either of which now breaks the invariant when removed. The operator count falls by one and the
> invariant's attack surface doubles.

**`[PROP]` A cost dimension neither the protocol nor the audit carried: *invariant custody*.** For
each candidate invariant `I` and kernel `K`, define
`custody(I, K) = { o ∈ K : I fails in K \ {o} }`. A reduction is only an improvement if it does not
inflate `|custody(I, K)|` for the invariants one intends to keep. Cardinality minimization alone is
blind to this — which is a concrete reason the protocol's own final principle ("optimize for semantic
irreducibility before numerical smallness") is right for a reason it did not state.

## 7. Agreement on what happens next

Endorsed from the audit, and now with the reasons the extended search supplies:

1. **Do not rerun 150 000 random worlds.** Confirmed by §5 — the randomized layer contributed one
   non-vacuous result across the entire experiment (`Challenge`/P10). Representation search yields
   more per unit of effort by a wide margin.
2. **Semantic Kernel Equivalence over operator-name ablation** (audit §33–35). §5 is a first
   instalment: four new representations, a 12-representation derivability ranking. The CEGAR framing
   is the right next form.
3. **A behavioural-equivalence notion** (audit §34) is genuinely missing and is the correct next
   formal object.
4. **The `S^epi` factorial experiment** (audit §36–37) — endorsed without qualification. `E × S × Q × C`
   is the right design, and this experiment could not have addressed it: epistemic standards were
   folded into `Policy` and `warrant-assessment` and never varied.
5. **Capability-closure as a gate** (§4.1) — proposed here as the decidable replacement for the
   undecidable "candidate completeness" demand.
6. **Invariant custody** (§5.3) — proposed here as a cost dimension of reduction that neither
   document carried, and which shows that at least one of the audit's own reductions is not free.

## 8. What neither party has established

* That any operator is a KnowledgeOS primitive. Nothing here licenses that.
* That 8, 13 or 14 is the size of anything. Three numbers from three representations.
* That the capability model is right. It was isomorphic to the object under test (§4.1 P-1), and
  replacing it is the highest-value open task in the programme.
* That governance, authorization or provenance behave as modelled. All three were **assumed by the
  protocol and never tested** (N-7, N-11).
