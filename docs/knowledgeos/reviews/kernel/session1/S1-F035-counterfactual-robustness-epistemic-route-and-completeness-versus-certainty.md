## Finding 1 · Counterfactual robustness as the evidence→conclusion test ⟦TEST⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**How did this evidence produce the conclusion, and how robust is that relationship?**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Would the system still have arrived at the correct conclusion under relevant counterfactual
variations?**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is the first *test* the corpus offers for the evidence→conclusion link**, as opposed to
prohibitions on inference (`S1-F027`) or preconditions for it (`S1-F034`). It asks for **stability under
perturbation**, which is checkable in principle.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ It also gives `S1-F029`'s construction/validation separation a mechanism: counterfactual
variation is precisely how one obtains validation evidence that is *not* the construction evidence.
Recorded as a convergence; neither document links them.

## Finding 2 · The epistemic route must be carried ⟦CLAIM — third arrival, and the sharpest⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**A knowledge claim should carry not only its content, but the epistemic route by which it was
obtained.**"*
⟦C⟧ With a worked non-collapse: *"**Analogy is not observation.**"* (the pramāṇa distinction between
*upamāna* and *pratyakṣa*)

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **Third arrival at route/source retention** — after Audi's *source-type retention* (`S1-F024`)
and `S1-F030`'s constitutive acquisition — and the sharpest, because it names the carrier (*the claim
itself*) and supplies a concrete taxonomy (the pramāṇas) rather than a general requirement.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Taken with F030 (acquisition constitutes evidential status) and `S1-F029` (evidence role), the
corpus now has **three separate things an evidence/claim reference may need to carry: route, acquisition
procedure, and role.** No document lists all three; that composite requirement is my inference and bears on
unruled `W:C-10`.
⟦L⟧ Comparison only: v1.1's Port Contract obligation 2 requires the **justification path** (premises, rules,
assumptions, inference rule) — ⟦INFERENCE⟧ *route* is close to but not identical with that: a justification
path says *how the conclusion follows*; a route says *by what epistemic means it was obtained*. **Adjacent,
not the same.** Recorded, not adjudicated.

## Finding 3 · The trade the corpus states exactly once ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**The knowledge state became more complete, but less certain.**"*
⟦C⟧ Paired with the survival test: *"What can survive systematic attempts to disprove, weaken,
contextualize, or reinterpret its claims?"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is the only place in 93 processed documents where completeness and certainty are said to
trade against each other.** Everywhere else they are treated as independently desirable. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ It has a
direct consequence for the unruled `W:C-11`: if adding knowledge lowers certainty, then Confidence cannot be
monotone in evidence volume — which forecloses the simplest derivations of it. Recorded as a research claim,
**not** an answer to C-11.

## Finding 4 · Meaning is level-relative ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ Hofstadter: *"**Knowledge is not located at one level of representation. It emerges from relationships
between levels.**"* · ⟦C⟧ *"**At which level does a particular knowledge structure become meaningful?**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ A **seventh genus for Knowledge** in the ledger's series — knowledge as an *inter-level*
phenomenon, distinct from relationship, state, product, capacity, ratified commitment and understanding.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Its architectural bite is on **altitude**: if knowledge emerges *between* levels, then any
single-altitude Kernel (v1.1's kernel/mechanism/representation split, or the corpus's boundary
formulations) cannot contain it. That is a challenge of a different shape from all eight formulations —
none of which considered that the object might not sit at one altitude. ⚠ Single source; recorded as a
claim, not a model.

## Finding 5 · catuṣkoṭi, and the one line that resists it ⟦DISTINCTION + CONSTRAINT⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Knowledge ≠ certainty.**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Which invariants must remain valid regardless of epistemic state?**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Zero is not 'nothing.'**"* · ⟦C⟧ *"the **minimum invariant structure that survives changes in
representation**."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ The second question is the most useful thing in the catuṣkoṭi pair, and it **cuts against** the
four-valued proposal those same documents make (census T-2): asking which invariants hold *regardless of
epistemic state* separates the invariant layer from the state layer — which is an argument for **fewer**
states, not more. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ *"Zero is not nothing"* and *"minimum invariant structure that survives changes
in representation"* are the closest the corpus comes to defining what the Zero lens is *for*, and they align
with `S1-F021`'s *boring Kernel*. Recorded as **partial internal resistance** to the state-adding proposal
in the same documents.
