## Finding 1 · Four layers, and only one may decide ⟦MODEL / METHOD⟧

⟦C⟧ *"Reading through all 26 lenses, I notice they naturally separate into **four distinct architectural
layers** when viewed through DDD's separation-of-concerns lens."*

⟦C⟧ **Layer 1 — Observation Instruments** (*"the 'How to See' layer"*): *"These lenses ask questions
**about** the domain but **don't make architectural decisions**"* — Vāṇī (*"is expression being confused
with meaning?"*, DDD role: *ubiquitous language vigilance*) · Pāṇini (*anti-corruption layer design*) ·
Karaka (*domain model discovery*) · Navya-Nyāya (*relation modeling*) · Nyāya (*invariant
identification*) · Escher (*"what survives transformation?"* → *identity vs value object distinction*).

⟦C⟧ **Layer 3 (decision layer):** *"These lenses **make architectural decisions** about what belongs
where"* — with a *Lens → Architectural Decision → DDD Mechanism* mapping.
⟦C⟧ **Layer 4 — Mechanism Candidates** (*"the 'How to Engineer' layer"*).

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is lens *governance*, and it is the corpus's own safeguard against the failure mode
this programme has repeatedly flagged**: a lens that observes must not thereby decide. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ It
also assigns each Layer-1 lens a **DDD role**, which is why lens-derived findings elsewhere in the corpus
(pramāṇa → evidence, Escher → identity/VO) land on DDD questions rather than philosophical ones.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Directly relevant to `S1-F018`: the Entity/Value-Object question it calls decisive is the
**Escher lens's assigned role**. So the corpus's most consequential question was produced by a Layer-1
instrument — an observation lens — not by a decision lens.

## Finding 2 · The *"boring Kernel"* principle ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**The Kernel is boring.** It only protects invariants."*
⟦C⟧ Restated in the conclusions: *"**The Kernel is boring** — it protects only the smallest possible set
of invariants."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ The most compressed statement of the minimality position in the corpus. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ It is
**consistent with `S1-F020`'s derivation rule** (derive from invariants) and with `S1-F016`'s single
retained invariant, and **opposed to `S1-F008`'s** *too-small* warning and `S1-F012`'s explicit *"this is
not minimalism"*. Recorded on the minimality side of a live disagreement.

⟦C⟧ Appearing in the same lifecycle sketch: `Revision · Supersession · Removal (without deletion of
history)`.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ *Removal without deletion of history* is a **fifth formulation** of the retraction question
(after `S1-F010`, `F014`, `F015`, `F018`) and the only one phrased as *removal*. Bears on unruled
`W:C-15`. **Not adjudicated.**

## Finding 3 · Lens admission is scope-controlled ⟦METHOD⟧

From the Chinese-lens assessment (`230117`), which rates ten candidate lenses and rejects on **scope**
grounds rather than merit:
⟦C⟧ *"**Shi must not become a semantic truth engine**."*
⟦C⟧ *"Introducing 'skill knowledge' now would be **scope expansion**."*
⟦C⟧ Verdict rows include *"**Kernel** | Not currently — procedural knowledge outside scope"* and
*"**Adjudication** | Not yet — would expand scope beyond current question"*.

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **The corpus applies an admission test to its own instruments**, and refuses lenses that
would enlarge the question. That is the same discipline it applies to Kernel candidates, turned
reflexively. It is also evidence against reading the philosophical corpus as unconstrained accumulation.

  supersession and retraction as classified in F010/F014/F015/F018.
