## Finding 1 · Three modelling prohibitions ⟦CONSTRAINT ×3⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Do not automatically turn every fundamental concept into an aggregate of lower-level database
constructs.**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Do not derive the ontology from the representation format.**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Do not solve a primitive concept by renaming it.**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **These three are the corpus's own diagnosis of how it went wrong, arrived at on day four.**
Mapped against what this extraction has recorded:
- The **first** is what produced the god-aggregate (`S1-F007`) and the four member lists — `Identity`,
  `Evidence`, `Confidence`, `History` decomposed into aggregate members.
- The **second** is `S1-F027`'s inference-licensing family (*never infer ontology from representation*)
  restated as a **modelling** rule rather than an inference rule — and it is the general form of ⟦L⟧ v1.1's
  ⟨C-1⟩ (*canonical-form equality is not identity*).
- The **third** names a pattern the census registered as a **recurring temptation**: `KnowledgeClaim` →
  `KnowledgeElement` → *"identity-bearing, contextualized assertion record"* → `Assertion` — four names for
  what may be one unresolved primitive (ledger PS-1). ⟦INFERENCE⟧ **The corpus names the failure mode it has
  been performing.** ⚠ That mapping is my inference; the documents state the rules, not the diagnosis.

⟦C⟧ Underlying them, McGinn's claim: *"**Identity is unitary, primitive, and indefinable**"*, and
*"Existence is a first-order property, contra Russell's quantifier view."*
⟦INFERENCE⟧ ⟦L⟧ **CONSISTENT** with INV-KOS-IDENTITY-001's *assigned, never derived* — but note it arrives at
the same place from the opposite direction to `S1-F031`'s ziran challenge (identity too fundamental to be
assigned) and to `S1-F016` (identity merely administrative). **Three positions on identity now stand
unreconciled**: primitive-and-indefinable, self-so-not-assigned, administrative.

## Finding 2 · A Knowledge-Space boundary is a boundary of **abstraction** ⟦DEFINITION⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**A Knowledge Space boundary should normally be a boundary of abstraction, purpose, scope and
observability.**"*
⟦C⟧ Prompted by: *"**Should a topic have a boundary?**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **The corpus's only positive definition of what a Knowledge-Space boundary consists of**, and
its four terms are all *observer-relative* — abstraction level, purpose, scope, observability. ⟦INFERENCE⟧
That makes Knowledge-Space boundaries **categorically different from the Kernel boundary**, which every
formulation treats as invariant-determined and observer-independent. Two boundary concepts, one word.
⚠ Registered as a **VOCABULARY COLLISION** (*boundary*) — the corpus's seventh collision, after *Kernel*
(four senses), *projection* (six), *agent*, *UNKNOWN*, `C-1`, *altitude/boundary*.

## Finding 3 · Participants traverse the space; they are not objects in it ⟦DISTINCTION⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Persons are not merely objects inside Knowledge Space. They are participants that traverse and
transform Knowledge Space.**"*
⟦C⟧ Its own modelling question, applied to every candidate: *"Is this a primitive entity, a relation, a
property, a state, a representation, a perspective, or **merely a useful projection**?"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ The distinction bears directly on `S1-F028`-style scoping: if participants **transform** the
space, then per-participant divergence is not a view over a fixed space but a change *to* it — which is a
stronger claim than the projection thread's *"different projections of one space"*. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Its seven-way
classification question is also the sharpest form of the blocker `S1-F013` named (*state vs relationship vs
event*) — extended from three options to seven, and applicable to any candidate.

## Finding 4 · The corpus asks what its central term means ⟦QUESTION⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Is Knowledge Space fundamentally a space of information, or is information itself only one layer
inside Knowledge Space?**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**What is knowledge itself?**"*
⟦C⟧ Dretske: *"**information is not the same thing as meaning**"* · *"**KnowledgeOS should be
representation-independent.**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ On 2026-08-25, after ~150 documents, the corpus asks *what is knowledge itself* and whether its
own central construct is information-shaped. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Recorded as a **coverage fact, not a criticism**: it
confirms what `S1-F026` declared — the book programme produced distinctions, not a definition — and it is
consistent with the Knowledge-genus series remaining at **seven unreconciled genera**.

⟦C⟧ The information-theory readings contribute Zero-lens probes rather than content: *"What if there is no
source? What if there is no encoder?"* ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Same shape as `S1-F033`'s Zero prerequisite question,
applied to a communication chain; recurrence, no new finding.
