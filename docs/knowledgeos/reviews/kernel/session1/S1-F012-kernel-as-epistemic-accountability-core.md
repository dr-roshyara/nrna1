## Finding 2 · Fifth Kernel formulation ⟦HYPOTHESIS⟧

⟦C⟧ **"Hypothesis D: 'Kernel as Epistemic Accountability Core' (derived)"** — selected as ⟦C⟧ *"best
supported by existing domain law"*.

| Aspect ⟦C⟧ | Content |
|---|---|
| Domain purpose | *"To create and maintain **self-contained units of epistemic accountability**"* |
| Owned concepts | *"Accountable epistemic object (identity + state + confidence + justification + evidence links + history)"* |
| Invariant protected | *"An admitted object must be **fully accountable**: its identity, state, confidence, justification, evidence, and history must be **co-consistent and recoverab[le]**"* |
| Inside | *"The complete act of **epistemic ratification**: from gate-crossing to historical record"* |
| Outside | *"Candidate production, evidence acquisition, reasoning, representation, consumption, **projection**"* |
| Stated weakness | *"'Accountability' is not a standard DDD term; might need to be translated to 'invariant protection'"* |

⟦C⟧ Core domain act, restated: *"**epistemic ratification** — the domain's assertion that a particular
candidate now occupies a specific epistemic state with…"*
⟦C⟧ And the aggregate's purpose: *"to make the epistemic claim **auditable** … to create a
self-contained [unit]"*.

⟦C⟧ Reasons given for preferring D over A/B/C: *"'Admission boundary' (A) captures the gate but **misses
the state**. 'Epistemic decision core' (B) captures the state but **risks inviting reasoning**.
'Consistency boundary' (C) is clos[e]…"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **Fifth formulation.** The corpus now holds: admission boundary (`S1-F009`) · consistency ·
decision · transformation boundary (`S1-F011`) · **epistemic accountability core** (here). ⟦INFERENCE⟧
D's distinctive claim is **co-consistency and recoverability across all six components** — a
*conjunctive* invariant rather than a gate condition. That is a different kind of invariant from
anything in the earlier lists.

## Finding 3 · Explicit rejection of the *Kernel-as-machine* framing ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Speculative Hypothesis:** The Kernel is **NOT a machine** that performs `candidate → justified
admissible knowledge`. That description conflates the *bounded context*…"*
⟦C⟧ *"If we say 'the Kernel is a machine,' we risk **reifying it as a workflow component**. Better:
**the Kernel is the domain space within which the epistemic transition is [made]**."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ This is the corpus's first explicit refusal of the machine metaphor, and it is the same
error-class `S1-F007` names (metaphor mistaken for domain concept) applied to a *different* metaphor.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Note it also declines `S1-F011`'s *transformation* framing on the same grounds: ⟦C⟧
*"Transformation of a proposal into knowledge? Strong — but risks implying the domain performs
transformation. **The domain performs status assignment, not content transform[ation]**."*
⚠ Two Phase 2 documents, 7 minutes apart, where the later explicitly rejects the earlier's selected
formulation. **Recorded, unresolved.**

## Finding 4 · An equilibrium restated, and a warning against minimalism ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**Equilibrium:** The Kernel must be large enough to **perform the complete admission act** (from
intake through history recording) but no larger. **This is not minima[lism]**…"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ Compare `S1-F009`'s equilibrium (*"protect the aggregate invariant during admission, and
nothing more"*). Same shape, different bound: F009 bounds by *invariant protection*, this bounds by
*completeness of the act* — and explicitly disclaims minimalism. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Two equilibria that can
disagree: an act may be complete yet include steps no invariant requires.

only. ⟦PROVENANCE: UNCERTAIN⟧ — recorded as an **unverified claim about the law**, not as a gap.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Q3 is the `W:C-15` question, and Q4 the `W:C-11` question, both stated as research questions
