## Finding 3 · Three further candidate models ⟦MODEL⟧

⟦C⟧ **Model A: "Kernel as Constitutional Distinction-Preservation System"** — purpose: *"Preserve
distinctions, provenance, identity, and epistemic coherence **while knowledge changes over time**"*.
Owned: Identity (*mechanism-independent*) · Evidence (*immutable record*) · Justification (*immutable
record*) · EpistemicState (*immutable state*) · Confidence (*immutable score*) · History (*record of
transitions*). Invariants: identity uniqueness · evidence linkage · justification linkage · epistemic
state coherence · confidence coherence · **history completeness**.
⟦C⟧ Self-stated weaknesses: *"May be too abstract (does not capture the transformation from Candidate to
Knowledge)"* · *"May suggest the Kernel is a 'thing' rather than a boundary."*

⟦C⟧ **Model B: "Epistemic Coherence Boundary"** · **Model C: "Traceable Relationship System"** (named in
the conclusion).

⟦INFERENCE⟧ With these, the corpus now holds **eight distinct Kernel formulations**: admission boundary ·
consistency boundary · decision boundary · transformation boundary · epistemic accountability core ·
constitutional distinction-preservation · epistemic coherence boundary · traceable relationship system.
⚠ **Six of the eight were produced within about 13 minutes on 2026-08-23 by model-generated documents
sharing one prompt lineage.** Recorded as a fact about the corpus, not as eight independent arrivals.

⟦INFERENCE⟧ Model A is notable for one thing no earlier formulation states: **five of its six owned
concepts are declared *immutable*, with change carried entirely by History.** That is a substantive
temporal claim — mutation is modelled as accretion, not update — and it is the first corpus statement
that would answer *"does change create a new identity?"* with *no, change is recorded not applied*.
⚠ Recorded as a candidate reading; the document does not draw that consequence itself.
