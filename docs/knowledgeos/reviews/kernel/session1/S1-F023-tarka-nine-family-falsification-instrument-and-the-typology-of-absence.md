## Finding 1 · Nine attack families — a falsification instrument, not a source of content ⟦METHOD⟧

⟦C⟧ *"The Tarka lens I would derive for KnowledgeOS"*, structured as *"**nine major attack families**,
rather than simply listing philosophical concepts"*:

| # | Family ⟦C⟧ | What it attacks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lakṣaṇa** — definition adequacy | is the definition too narrow / too wide / inapplicable |
| 2 | **Pakṣa–Sādhya–Hetu discipline** | is the claim, the thing to be proved, and the reason kept distinct |
| 3 | **Vyāpti** — *"the hidden invariant test"* | is the claimed universal relation actually universal |
| 4 | **Hetvābhāsa** — *"attack the reason itself"* | fallacy classes in the reason |
| 5 | **Saṃśaya** — *"don't collapse unresolved alternatives"* | premature resolution of doubt |
| 6 | **Viparyaya** — erroneous apprehension | misattribution |
| 7 | **Abhāva** — *"absence needs a counter-correlate"* | unqualified negative claims |
| 8 | **Anvaya–Vyatireka** | one-sided causal inference |
| 9 | **Nigrahasthāna** | *"identify where an argument has actually failed"* |

⟦C⟧ Family 1 operationalised directly against the corpus's own question:
> ⟦C⟧ *"**Does our definition of the KnowledgeOS Kernel have avyāpti, ativyāpti, or asambhava?**"*
with the test table: ⟦C⟧ *avyāpti* — *"is something genuinely required by the Kernel but excluded?"* ·
*ativyāpti* — *"does something enter the Kernel that doesn't actually belong?"* · *asambhava* — *"does our
definition describe something that **cannot actually exist under the Constitution**?"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is the instrument the corpus needed and did not have on 2026-08-23 morning.** The eight
competing formulations (`S1-F009`…`F019`) were each argued *for*; none was tested for over-inclusion,
under-inclusion, or impossibility. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Applied retrospectively: `S1-F016`'s falsification is an
*ativyāpti* result (members that do not belong); `S1-F008`'s *too-small* warning is an *avyāpti* claim;
and `S1-F018`'s Entity/VO dilemma is a candidate *asambhava* — a definition that may describe something
that cannot exist (an external-but-atomic member). **That three-way mapping is my inference, not the
document's.**

⟦INFERENCE⟧ Family 5 (*Saṃśaya* — do not collapse unresolved alternatives) is the methodological
justification for `S1-F013`'s eightfold UNRESOLVED verdict, and for this extraction's own refusal to
reconcile.

## Finding 2 · A typology of **absence** — directly sharpening `W:C-15` / `W:C-17` ⟦MODEL⟧

⟦C⟧ *"This is one of the most interesting findings for us… the book goes into considerable detail about
**abhāva**, negation/non-existence, and the **pratiyogi**, the **counter-correlative** of a negation…
**you cannot simply say 'X does not exist' without the relevant counter-entity being
established/recalled**."*

⟦C⟧ Worked against the corpus's own claim — *"Retraction doesn't exist as a Kernel state"* — Tarka forces:
> ⟦C⟧ *"What exactly is the thing being negated? What is its **pratiyogi**? What kind of absence is being
> asserted? **Absolute absence? Prior absence? Subsequent absence? Absence at a particular locus?**"*
⟦C⟧ *"That is far more precise than saying 'there is no retraction concept.'"*
⟦C⟧ Routed explicitly: *"potentially **very valuable for C-15 and C-17**."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is the most precise treatment of absence anywhere in the corpus, and it is a
*seventh* angle on retraction** (after `S1-F010`, `F014`, `F015`, `F018`, `F021`, `F022`). Its distinctive
contribution: a **four-way absence typology** — absolute / prior / subsequent / locus-relative — where
every other document treats absence as one thing.
⟦L⟧ Comparison target only: v1.1 §9 has `ABSENT` as one of seven states and *unknown ≠ absent ≠ false*;
⟨Z-1⟩ adds *absence of a constitutive prerequisite is not an epistemic state*. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ So v1.1 already
distinguishes **two** kinds of absence (state-absence vs prerequisite-absence); this typology proposes
**four**, and asks what the *counter-correlate* of each is. Recorded as a **research question against
`W:C-15`/`W:C-17`** — not a gap claim, not adjudicated.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ It also connects to `S1-F022`'s new non-collapse `absence ≠ withdrawal`: the pratiyogi
requirement is *why* that non-collapse holds — withdrawal has an agent and a prior assertion as its
counter-correlate; absence need not.

## Finding 3 · The refusal, stated as a sovereignty principle ⟦REJECT / METHOD⟧

⟦C⟧ *"I would **not** tell Claude: 'Use Tarka Śāstra to redesign the Kernel.' Nor: 'Use Indian philosophy
to decide what the Kernel should contain.' **That would contaminate the architecture.**"*
⟦C⟧ *"Instead: **use the Tarka-derived reasoning apparatus only as an independent falsification lens
against the already-defined and adjudicated boundary.** That **preserves the architecture's
sovereignty**."*
⟦C⟧ Registered as an instrument, not a rule: *"**TARKA-KERNEL-FALSIFICATION-001** — not yet as an
architecture rule."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ Two things follow. (i) The instrument is explicitly **post-adjudication**: it is designed to
attack a boundary that has already been ruled, which is exactly the sequence the programme has installed
(research → ruling → falsification). (ii) *"Contaminate"* is the corpus's own word for the failure mode —
the same one `S1-F007` names as metaphor-becoming-domain-concept and `S1-F021` guards with lens layering.
**Third independent statement of the same discipline in one evening** (`S1-F020`, `S1-F022`, this).

## Finding 4 · A meta-principle justifying the corpus's terminology work ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ *"when truth is communicated through words, **vagueness and looseness in terms and argumen[t]**"* is
itself the obstacle — which the document applies to the corpus's own distinction list: ⟦C⟧ *"KnowledgeOS vs
KnowledgeCore vs Kernel · altitude vs boundary · aggregate vs consistency boundary · capability vs
invariant vs anti-capability · ambiguity vs contradiction · protect vs produce · knowledge vs wisdom.
Those aren't merely terminology discussions. **They are logical preconditions for valid reasoning about
the architecture.**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ This is the corpus's own defence of the vocabulary-collision register — and it names seven
collisions, four of which this extraction has independently recorded (`Kernel` four ways in `S1-F020`;
ambiguity/contradiction at `W:F-CM-1`; capability-vs-invariant across `S1-F008`/`F016`;
aggregate-vs-consistency-boundary across `S1-F014`/`F016`). ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Two are new to Session 1:
**altitude vs boundary** and **protect vs produce**.
