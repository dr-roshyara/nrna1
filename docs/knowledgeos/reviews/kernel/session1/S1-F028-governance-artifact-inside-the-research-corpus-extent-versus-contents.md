## Finding 1 · **Extent vs contents** ⟦DISTINCTION — and it reframes the entire Kernel debate⟧

⟦C⟧ *"it turns on a distinction worth making sharp — **extent versus contents**:"*
- ⟦C⟧ *"**Extent** = which aggregates, and which gate. That is the **K-1 structure: KnowledgeAggregate +
  ConflictRecord, Verification Port as sole inbound gate**."*
- ⟦C⟧ *"**Contents** = which members, states, events, and port obligations sit inside that extent."*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This distinction dissolves a large part of the corpus's apparent disagreement.** The eight
competing formulations (`S1-F009`…`F019`) argue about **contents** — which capabilities, which members —
while presenting themselves as rival answers about the **Kernel** as such. And `S1-F016`'s falsification
attacked *contents* co-location, not *extent*. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Read through this lens:
- the four Phase 1 member lists (`S1-F003`, `S1-F005`) are **contents** proposals;
- the eight boundary formulations are mostly **extent** claims with contents smuggled in;
- `S1-F020`'s ADR is the only research document that separates the two (KnowledgeOS ⊃ KnowledgeCore ⊃
  Kernel), and it does so without the extent/contents vocabulary.

⟦INFERENCE⟧ It also identifies where the corpus's *Kernel* vocabulary collision (`S1-F020`, four senses)
does real damage: a document answering *extent* and one answering *contents* look like rivals and are not.
**This is the most clarifying single distinction extracted from the corpus so far** — and it arrives from
the formal track, not from the research corpus's own reasoning.

   an argument bearing directly on it (selection inside ⇒ circularity). ⟦INFERENCE⟧ So the research
