## Finding 1 · The convergent formulation ⟦CLAIM — four independent arrivals⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**The Kernel preserves the structures that enable discrimination; reasoning regimes perform the
discrimination.**"*
⟦C⟧ Gelfond & Kahl: *"**KnowledgeOS Kernel should not itself be 'the reasoning engine.'**"*
⟦C⟧ Brachman & Levesque: *"**Reasoning bridges the gap between what is represented and what is believed**"*
— with ⟦C⟧ *"There is an unbounded/infinite Knowledge Space, but every person or machine can only capture a
**finite projection** of it."*
⟦C⟧ Brandom: knowledge → concepts → language → inference → **commitments** → social assessment → explicit
representation.

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This is the corpus's most convergent architectural claim, and it is late.** Four independent
sources — two KR&R textbooks, an inferentialist philosopher, and a project synthesis — arrive at the same
split: **structures preserved / reasoning performed elsewhere.** ⟦INFERENCE⟧ It is the constructive form of
what the corpus had only ever stated negatively (*"the kernel does not reason"*, `S1-F011`, `S1-F012`,
`S1-F025`) — it says what the Kernel *does* instead: **preserve what makes reasoning possible.**
⟦L⟧ **CONSISTENT** with v1.1 §16 (*"the kernel does not reason… engines at its ports do the reasoning"*),
and it supplies the positive complement to that prohibition.

⟦INFERENCE⟧ Brachman & Levesque add the reason this split is *necessary* rather than merely tidy:
representation is **finite**, belief is **unbounded**, and reasoning spans the gap. If so, a Kernel that
tried to hold the closure would be attempting the infinite. **First technical argument in the corpus for
why the Kernel must be small.**

## Finding 2 · Institutional counting-as ⟦DISTINCTION — new⟧

⟦C⟧ Searle: *"**Institutional context can change what something counts as.**"*
⟦C⟧ *"**Knowledge Space should not be modeled as isolated 'facts.'**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ *Counting-as* is a mode of context-dependence the corpus has not previously recorded. It differs
from `S1-F022`'s *context may constitute knowledge* and from `S1-F031`'s *contextual identity*: those ask
whether context determines **what a thing is**; this asks whether an institution determines **what it
counts as** — a status conferred by collective acceptance, not a property.
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Direct bearing on the authority family (six arrivals so far): if status is institutionally
conferred, then *authority* is not merely relational (`S1-F022`'s Confucian reading) but **constitutive of
the status itself**. ⚠ Recorded as a distinct fourth position on context, unreconciled with the other three.

## Finding 3 · Williamson: stop trying to define Knowledge reductively ⟦REFUSAL⟧

⟦C⟧ *"**KnowledgeOS should not attempt to define knowledge by reducing it to information + truth + belief +
evidence + justification.**"*
⟦C⟧ Yet the same document asks: *"**What is the object called Knowledge?**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ **This closes the Knowledge-definition programme in the negative.** The ledger holds **seven
genera** (understanding · relationship · product · state-evolution · capacity · ratified commitment ·
inter-level) plus the factive and measurable variants — and Williamson's position is that the reductive
project itself is misconceived. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ Read with `S1-F037`'s *"do not solve a primitive concept by
renaming it"* and `S1-F026`'s *"the books do not prove our Kernel architecture"*, the corpus's own verdict
on its central question is: **Knowledge was not defined, and may not be definable by decomposition.**
⚠ That is a summary of positions the corpus states, not my conclusion about knowledge.

⟦C⟧ The capacity formulation persists alongside: *"Knowledge is a **capacity** to write information and
define the scope of information"* · *"Knowledge permits **selection** of information according to context,
purpose, evidence and inferential consequences."*
⟦INFERENCE⟧ Note these are compatible with Finding 1: if knowledge is a capacity to select, the Kernel
preserves what the capacity operates on. Recorded as a **coherent pairing**, not a resolution of the genus
question.

## Finding 4 · Projections may differ in commitment but answer to correctness ⟦CLAIM⟧

⟦C⟧ Brandom: *"**Different Knowledge Projections can differ in commitments and perspectives while remaining
answerable to objective correctness.**"*

⟦INFERENCE⟧ This is a **second, weaker form** of `S1-F038`'s projection-consistency invariant (*projections
must never contain contradictory propositions*). Brandom permits divergence in **commitment** while holding
a common answerability standard; F038 forbids divergence in **content**. ⟦INFERENCE⟧ The two are not
obviously compatible — a differing commitment can entail a differing proposition. ⚠ Recorded as a
**tension within the projection thread**, unresolved, and it also relaxes the tension F038 had with
`S1-F037`'s transforming participants.
