# S2-F020 · One Phase 2 document demonstrably reasoned without v1.1 — and every formulation in the 13-minute burst is now suspect on the same ground

**SOURCE SESSION-1 ARTIFACT** — `S1-F012` Finding 5, Q2 · cross-read with `S1-F008`, `S1-F009`, `S1-F011`, `S1-F013`.

**FINDING CLASS** — PROVENANCE PROBLEM + NEW FINDING (consequence the source did not draw)

---

## OBSERVATION

`S1-F012` records that its source claims: ⟦C⟧ *"The law mentions 'epistemic-state determination' but **does not enumerate states**."*

Session 1 flags the conflict and offers two explanations: *"Either the document reasons from a **pre-v1.1 law snapshot**, or 'existing law' here means the F-1…F-5 act only. ⟦PROVENANCE: UNCERTAIN⟧ — recorded as an unverified claim about the law, not as a gap."*

**The first explanation is unavailable on the dates.** And the consequence for the document's own warrant is not drawn.

## EVIDENCE

Verified against v1.1 directly (`20260822-1402`), since `S1-F012` nominates the law as its comparison target:

```
§9  seven states enumerated as a value object:
    VALIDATED · QUESTIONABLE · REJECTED · CONFLICTED · UNKNOWN · ABSENT · FALSE
§9  "No eighth state is introduced."
OBS-2  "the accepted member set has seven states" — an eighth was
       considered and refused
```

**Dates:**

```
v1.1                    2026-08-22 14:02
F012 source (112855)    2026-08-23 11:28    → v1.1 precedes it by ~21.5 hours
```

So the law **did** enumerate states, explicitly and with a refusal of an eighth, nearly a day before the document said it did not.

## ANALYSIS

### (a) The pre-snapshot explanation fails

A document cannot be reasoning from a pre-v1.1 snapshot when v1.1 predates it by 21.5 hours *and* the document claims to reason from *"existing law."* Two possibilities remain:

1. **The document did not have v1.1** — its "existing law" was the F-1…F-5 material only, second-hand.
2. **It had v1.1 and did not consult §9.**

Session 1's second explanation is possibility 1, and it is the more likely. But naming it as *"'existing law' here means the F-1…F-5 act only"* understates what follows.

### (b) The consequence for `F012`'s own warrant

`S1-F012` records that its source selects Hypothesis D as ⟦C⟧ *"**best supported by existing domain law**."* If that document's "existing law" excludes the reference architecture, then **"best supported by existing law" is a claim about a proper subset of the law** — and the claim's headline strength does not survive the qualification. A formulation selected as best-supported-by-law, chosen without the document that enumerates the states it says are unenumerated, is not thereby wrong; it is **less warranted than it presents itself as being.**

Session 1 flags the Q2 claim as unverified and stops there. The Q2 claim is not just an error in a question list — **it is a probe that reveals the document's reasoning base**, and that base is the ground of its central selection.

### (c) The same question now applies to the whole burst

Session 1 establishes that `S1-F008`, `F009`, `F011`, `F012` and `F013` all reason from F-1…F-5 **second-hand**, and that the F-1…F-5 artifacts are **not in the corpus** (`S1-F008`: *"the F-1…F-5 artifacts themselves are not in `kernel/`"*). All five post-date v1.1. One of the five is now demonstrated to have been unaware of §9.

That raises a question Session 1 never asks: **how many of the eight formulations were produced with access to the governing law?**

⚠ **What I can and cannot establish.** I can establish it for `F012`: one demonstrated case. I **cannot** establish it for the others — absence of a v1.1 citation is not proof of absence of access, and treating it as such would be exactly the *"not mentioned → does not exist"* conversion the second lens forbids. So this is **one proven case and an open question about four more.**

The test is cheap and available to Session 1: for each burst document, check whether it references any v1.1-specific artifact — the seven-state vocabulary, the eleven `INV-KOS-*` identifiers, §16's altitudes, the ⟨C-n⟩/⟨r4-n⟩ markers. A document reasoning from v1.1 would be expected to name at least one.

### (d) Why this changes what the corpus is evidence of

If the burst was produced without v1.1, then the five documents are not *rival theories of a modelled domain* — they are **independent re-derivations of a domain whose model already existed.** Their disagreements would then say little about the domain's difficulty and much about the difficulty of re-deriving a model from a second-hand summary of it.

That reading also supplies a cheaper explanation for two things Session 1 records as puzzling: `S1-F010`'s **rival state set** with four states having no v1.1 counterpart, and `S1-F013`'s **eightfold UNRESOLVED** on classifications that §9 and the eleven invariants substantially settle. Neither is mysterious if the law was not in the room.

⚠ **This is a hypothesis, not a finding.** It is not adopted, and per `S2-F013`'s lesson it should be treated with more suspicion for being tidy — it would explain several things at once, which is a reason to demand the (d) test be run rather than to accept it.

## WHY IT MATTERS

Because it bears on the **evidential status of the entire batch**, not on one claim inside it. Every formulation, exclusion set, state set and negative verdict in `S1-F009` through `S1-F013` inherits its warrant from what its author had access to. One document is now shown to have lacked the governing enumeration while asserting it did not exist.

`S1-F008` was right to mark its law claims *"recorded, not verified"* and Session 1's caution there is exactly correct. This finding says that caution should extend from *"we cannot verify the law says this"* to *"we cannot verify the author had the law."*

**On the extraction** — recommend the (c) test be run across the five burst documents, and that "reasoning base" join provenance class as a recorded field. This is the single cheapest check available that would materially reprice the batch.

**On adjudication** — **none, and deliberately none.** If the burst lacked v1.1 then its formulations are weaker evidence than they appear — which is a conclusion with obvious adjudication consequences, and precisely why it must not travel there from a research artifact. Not routed. No Wave 1 or Wave 2 item touched.
