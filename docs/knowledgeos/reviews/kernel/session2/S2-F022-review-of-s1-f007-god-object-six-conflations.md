## `.2` — "Six conflations" → **TYPE ERROR: the rows are not the distinctions**

The table has six rows. The rows do not contain six distinctions:

| Row | Distinctions actually stated |
|---|---|
| 2 | **two** — *Evidence Evaluation ≠ Evidence Preservation*, **and** the three-way *Mechanism Confidence ≠ Epistemic Confidence ≠ Evidence* |
| 6 | **a three-way** — *History ≠ Provenance ≠ Audit Log*, plus *"different concepts with different **ownership**"* |
| 1, 3, 4, 5 | one binary each |

So the artifact counts **rows** and reports them as **conflations**. A three-way split is not one distinction; it asserts that three concepts are pairwise distinct. And *ownership* in row 6 is a further, separable claim — two concepts can be distinct yet co-owned.

**Independence is also untested.** Rows 1, 3 and 5 all instantiate one principle — *a mechanism/quality-attribute is not a domain responsibility* — which Part A itself supplies as the root cause. Under the convergence test these are **RESTATEMENT** of one distinction at three sites, not three findings.

**Consequence, and it cuts both ways:** the count of *stated* distinctions is **larger** than six; the count of *independent* distinctions is plausibly **smaller** — perhaps three families (mechanism-vs-domain · evidence handling · history/provenance). Either way, *"six"* is a row count wearing the clothes of a result. Same defect family as `S2-F017`, opposite direction.

## `.3` — *"NOT ADDRESSED"* for distinction 4 → **CHALLENGE: may be addressed by inclusion**

`S1-F007` marks row 4 (*Adjudication ≠ Execution*) **NOT ADDRESSED** in law, noting: *"§16 says a kernel member 'can refuse a transition, record a state, assign an identity, retain a history' — which is adjudication-side, but the distinction is not stated."*

Read that list again. *Refuse a transition* is adjudication. *Record a state · assign an identity · retain a history* are **executions**. So §16 places **both sides of the proposed distinction inside the kernel.**

That is not silence. It is closer to the distinction being **addressed by inclusion** — the law declines to split what the critique wants split, because it assigns both to one locus. The Zero lens question here is *exclusion, unknown, unrepresented, or unmentioned?* — and the available reading is **not unmentioned**: the constituents are named, on the same side.

⚠ **What I am not saying.** I am not saying law is right, nor that the distinction is unnecessary — that would be adjudication, and per `X-001` the sufficiency test is not mine to apply on the research side. I am saying **`NOT ADDRESSED` is the wrong classification for a case where both terms appear in one enumeration.** `CANDIDATE DISTINCTION AGAINST AN EXISTING INCLUSION` says what the evidence shows.

Row 6 (*History ≠ Provenance ≠ Audit Log*) is **not** affected by this — `S1-F007`'s reading there stands, since v1.1 carries `INV-KOS-HISTORY-001` and *Revision ≠ Erasure* without any three-way split.

## `.4` — *"The project accepted the diagnosis and refused the prescription"* → **CHALLENGE: half of that is inferred**

The quotations support the **refusal** completely: *"I would **not yet accept** its final Kernel definition"* · *"**not yet accepted**"* · *"resist the temptation to declare it finished."*

What is quoted is *"this is a very **useful** result"* and *"we are much **closer** to the Kernel definition than we were before."* **Useful ≠ accepted.** A critique can be useful for sharpening a question without its diagnosis being adopted — and the project's own next-step sentence (*"The next step is **DDD reconciliation**"*) reads as *the diagnosis needs reconciling*, not *the diagnosis is settled*.

**Why it matters:** `S1-F007` builds a weight claim on the inferred half — *"the six conflations carry **more corpus weight** than any Kernel definition offered alongside them."* That ranking inherits the over-read. On the quotations available, the defensible statement is: **the project explicitly refused the definition and explicitly called the result useful; its stance on the six conflations is not recorded.**

**Defensible restatement:** *the project refused the prescription and did not record a verdict on the diagnosis.*

# IMPLEMENTATION RELEVANCE · `S1-F007` (six conflations)

Assessed per row, because the rows differ sharply in implementation consequence.

## Rows 1, 3, 5 — *mechanism / quality-attribute ≠ domain responsibility*

**1 · Need?** No — this is already law. §16's altitudes place parsing, rule engines and self-audit outside by placement.
**2 · Where?** Already at **Kernel/Governance** as an existing altitude rule.
**3 · Status → DO NOT IMPLEMENT (already protected).**
**4 · Why?** A second statement of an existing rule creates two places to change it. `ES-005.4`.
**5 · Lose?** Nothing. The protection stands without action.
**6 · Consequence?** None.

## Row 2 — *Mechanism Confidence ≠ Epistemic Confidence ≠ Evidence*

**1 · Need?** **Possibly, and this is the sharpest of the six.** A model's self-reported score, a domain confidence assignment, and the evidence itself are three things that will arrive through one port and can be stored in one field. If they are, a mechanism's number becomes an epistemic property — which ⟨R-1⟩ forbids but a schema with one `confidence` column cannot express.
**2 · Where?** **Port/Interface** (keeping mechanism-reported scores on the candidate side) with a **Domain/Core** consequence (only the domain-assigned value is a member).
**3 · Status → NEEDS FURTHER EVIDENCE.** Trigger named: *the first time a mechanism supplies a numeric score that any consumer wants persisted.* Until then there is nothing to separate.
**4 · Why?** Not because a three-way split is elegant. Because ⟨R-1⟩ is currently protected by *placement* only, and placement fails silently the moment one field carries two meanings.
**5 · Lose?** The ability to answer *"who assigned this number?"* after the fact — an authority question, and unrecoverable once merged.
**6 · Consequence?** Smallest thing: mechanism-supplied scores are **named differently** from the domain's `Confidence` at the port. A naming decision, not a subsystem.

## Row 4 — *Adjudication ≠ Execution*

**1 · Need?** Unclear, and `S2-F023.1` shows this is the only genuine `F007`↔`F008` disagreement. §16 currently places **both** inside the kernel.
**3 · Status → NEEDS FURTHER EVIDENCE.** This is an **ownership** question, not a capability; it produces no implementable unit until someone must be told who executes.
**5 · Lose?** If law already includes both, nothing is lost by leaving it. If it does not, a transition could be adjudicated by one party and executed by another with no invariant covering the gap — but that gap is **hypothetical on present evidence.**

## Row 6 — *History ≠ Provenance ≠ Audit Log*

**1 · Need?** **The most likely genuine implementation consequence in the artifact.** The three carry different retention, immutability and access requirements. Conflated into one store, the store gets built to whichever requirement was noticed first, and the other two fail silently — an audit log is routinely purged; `INV-KOS-HISTORY-001` history must not be.
**2 · Where?** The **distinction** is **Domain/Core** (history is an invariant); the audit log is **Mechanism/Infrastructure**; provenance is candidate-side at the **Port**.
**3 · Status → NEEDS FURTHER EVIDENCE**, with a concrete trigger: *the first retention, deletion or access-control requirement that lands on any of the three.* At that moment the distinction becomes load-bearing and is expensive to introduce retroactively.
**4 · Why?** Not because three concepts are conceptually tidier than one. Because **retention policy is where the conflation becomes a data-loss bug**, and forward-only history cannot be reconstructed after a purge.
**5 · Lose?** **Reconstructibility** — the property that makes the whole model auditable. This is the one row where the loss is concrete and irreversible.
**6 · Consequence?** Smallest thing: the three are **named separately** wherever they are persisted, so a retention rule can attach to one without attaching to all. Naming, not architecture.
