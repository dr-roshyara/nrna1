# S2-F018 · `S1-F010` calls itself a reversal of `S1-F009` — but the two answer different questions, and `F010`'s own framing sentence says so

**SOURCE SESSION-1 ARTIFACTS** — `S1-F010` Finding 1 · `S1-F009` Finding 1.

**FINDING CLASS** — CHALLENGE + ALTITUDE DIFFERENCE

---

## OBSERVATION

`S1-F010` Finding 1 states: *"**This reverses `S1-F009`, written seven minutes earlier**, which selected Admission as the core domain act against six alternatives. Two documents, same day, same register, **opposite conclusions on the same question**."*

They are not conclusions on the same question, and `S1-F010` quotes the sentence that establishes this two paragraphs earlier.

## EVIDENCE

Both quotations are Session 1's own, from the same artifact:

| | The question the document asks |
|---|---|
| `F009` source (`110950`) | what is the **Kernel's** core domain act? — *"The core domain act is the constitutionally-gated transition from candidate to admissible knowledge state"*, chosen against six alternatives |
| `F010` source (`111647`) | ⟦C⟧ *"What is the complete KnowledgeOS **epistemic lifecycle**, **independently of the Kernel**?"* — and Session 1 glosses this itself: *"deliberately setting the Kernel aside"* |

## ANALYSIS

The two propositions are:

1. *Admission is the core act **of the Kernel**.*
2. *Admission is the first transition **of the epistemic lifecycle**.*

These are **jointly satisfiable, and naturally so.** If the Kernel's whole responsibility is the gate, then admission is both the only act the Kernel performs *and* the first of many transitions the lifecycle contains. Everything after admission — contestation, supersession, withdrawal — would then belong to the lifecycle but not to the Kernel. Far from contradicting `F009`, that is close to what `F009`'s equilibrium criterion predicts, since the criterion is explicitly bounded *"during admission."*

Under lens 7, proposition 1 is about the **extent of a boundary** and proposition 2 is about the **structure of a lifecycle**. Different subjects; no shared altitude on which to collide. The standing rule applies: *nothing is a contradiction until both propositions are shown to sit at the same altitude.* That demonstration is not attempted.

### What survives, and it is not small

`S1-F010`'s **second** observation is sound and independent of the reversal claim:

> *"F009's equilibrium criterion is scoped `during admission`. If admission is only the first of many transitions, a criterion bounded by admission cannot govern the whole lifecycle."*

That is a genuine **coverage gap**, correctly identified: F009's criterion, whatever its merits, decides nothing about post-admission transitions. But a criterion that governs less than the whole lifecycle is *incomplete*, not *reversed*. The finding is stronger stated as coverage than as contradiction, because coverage is what the evidence shows.

### Why the mislabel is more than pedantry

`S1-F010` also notes *"Neither cites the other."* Combined with `S1-F011`'s finding that these documents share a prompt lineage, the more economical account is: **one prompt, two documents, two different sub-questions, no disagreement to reconcile.** Reading it as a seven-minute reversal implies instability in the project's position — which is the `S1-F002`/`S1-F003` narrative — where the evidence shows division of labour.

## WHY IT MATTERS

This is the third time in the register that a Session-1 contradiction dissolves under the same-altitude test (`S2-F008`, `S2-F011` Result 2, here). The tally in `S2-00` still reads **GENUINE CONTRADICTION: 0**, and this batch — despite containing the corpus's most dramatic apparent clashes — does not change it.

That is now a substantive result about the corpus rather than about any one artifact: **the Kernel material may contain far less genuine disagreement than its surface suggests**, with the apparent conflict generated by unstated question-scoping (here), untested synonymy (`S2-F017` Part 2), and terminological difference (`S2-F017` Part 3).
