### `.2` *"Boring Kernel"* vs `S1-F008`/`S1-F012` is **not opposition** — ninth dissolution
Session 1 records: *"opposed to `S1-F008`'s *too-small* warning and `S1-F012`'s explicit *'this is not minimalism'*. Recorded on the minimality side of a live disagreement."*

Test the propositions:

| | Claim |
|---|---|
| `S1-F021` | *"The Kernel is boring. It only protects **invariants**"* — a claim about **purpose**: one job |
| `S1-F008` | *"The Kernel **cannot simply be a generic rule evaluator**"* — a claim about **extent**: one job may take several capabilities |
| `S1-F012` | *"large enough to perform the complete admission act… **This is not minimalism**"* — also **extent** |

**Protecting `INV-KOS-IDENTITY-001` requires assigning identity.** So a Kernel with exactly one purpose may still require identity assignment, evidence-link preservation, history recording and state determination. *Only protects invariants* constrains **why** it does things, not **how many** things it does.

**Classification: DISSOLVES — PURPOSE-COUNT vs CAPABILITY-COUNT.** Minimality of purpose is not minimality of extent, and the corpus's "minimalism debate" conflates them. Ninth dissolution, ninth distinct mechanism.

⚠ And it connects to `S2-F024.1`: *"protect the aggregate invariant and nothing more"* (F009) and *"only protects invariants"* (F021) are **the same statement**, and both leave the capability count undetermined **for the same reason** — neither names the invariants. The minimality position has never actually been given an extent.
