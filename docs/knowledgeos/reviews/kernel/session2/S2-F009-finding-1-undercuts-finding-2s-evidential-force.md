## FINDING

The two headline findings of `S1-F004` pull against each other, and the artifact does not notice.

**Finding 1:** ⟦C⟧ *"No mature research field appears to define a universally accepted `knowledge kernel` · `epistemic kernel` · `organizational memory kernel`"* · *"KnowledgeOS is **not entering an established category**."* Session 1 draws the correct consequence: *"every Kernel property must be justified internally."*

**Finding 2** then uses external evidence — *"knowledge systems lack deterministic execution"* — to raise a **material contradiction** against a project definition of the Kernel.

> **If no established category of knowledge kernel exists, then external observations about *knowledge systems* are evidence about the category's neighbours, not about the category.** The same reasoning that makes external evidence unable to *validate* a Kernel property makes it weak evidence to *contradict* one.

## EVIDENCE

- Finding 1 states the category is *"metaphor, prototype vocabulary, or isolated conceptual work."*
- Finding 2's B-side is drawn from an assessment of **knowledge systems** and of the **Unix analogy** — neither of which is a knowledge kernel by Finding 1's own account.
- Session 1 applies the internal-justification consequence in one direction only: it concludes properties cannot be *validated* externally, and does not ask whether they can be *falsified* externally.

## WHY IT MATTERS

**Evidential asymmetry is the thing to avoid here.** A rule that external evidence cannot confirm a Kernel property, but can contradict one, would let the corpus accumulate external objections it can never externally answer. That biases the register in one direction — and the direction is *against* whatever the project proposes, which is precisely where an extraction register should be most careful.

It also matters for how Finding 2's classification is read. Combined with `S2-F008` (the clash may be **modal**), Finding 2 has two independent reasons to sit below *TRUE CONTRADICTION*: the statements are in different moods, **and** the contradicting evidence is about a different category. **Neither reason touches whether determinism is a good idea.**

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH

It does **not** dismiss the external review. Finding 3's material — *"epistemic accountability over time"* as the candidate core, and the rejection set — is unaffected, because that material is offered as **evidence about what organisational knowledge systems are for**, a question squarely within the category the review does cover.

The challenge is confined to using neighbour-category evidence as a **contradiction** of a definition in a category that Finding 1 says does not exist.

**No adjudication.** `W:C-2` is untouched and this finding must not be cited there.
