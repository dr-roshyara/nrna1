## ANALYSIS

### (a) Disagreement among non-independent outputs is weak evidence of anything

Session 1's stated ground for privileging the negative result is *"S1-F009/F011/F012 … disagree with each other."* But if those documents share a prompt lineage — Session 1's own finding — then their disagreement is weak evidence, exactly as their agreement would be. **Non-independence cuts both ways.** `S1-F011` states the principle for convergence: *"Convergence between them is therefore weak evidence, and I do not count it as independent corroboration."* Divergence between them is weak for the same reason, and the principle is not carried across.

### (b) "More defensible" is true, but for a different reason

The conclusion is probably right. The *reason* given is not the reason available. A negative verdict is more defensible because **it claims less** — the standing rule that the strongest statement must not exceed the evidence favours it automatically. That is a property of the claim's *modesty*, not of the disagreement among its rivals.

The distinction is load-bearing. Grounded in modesty, the verdict says *"nobody here has shown enough to decide."* Grounded in rival disagreement, it acquires an unearned suggestion that **the question itself resists resolution** — which is a much stronger claim about the domain.

### (c) UNRESOLVED is a statement about the knower

Eight UNRESOLVED verdicts from a document with zero external sources, reasoning second-hand from artifacts not present in its corpus, establish that **that document could not resolve them.** They do not establish that the classifications are unresolvable, or even that they are unresolved elsewhere.

This is the second lens's question asked of a verdict rather than a data field: is the absence *exclusion*, *unknown*, *unrepresented*, or *unmentioned*? Here it is **inability** — and inability yields UNKNOWN, which is a fact about the assessor's position, not about the object assessed. Session 1 correctly guards this elsewhere (`S1-F008`: *"recorded, not verified"*); here the verdict is elevated instead.

### (d) The project's own hedge points the other way, and is quoted

`S1-F013`'s provenance block quotes the response part beginning: *"Yes. This Perplexity round is useful, but I would **not** treat its conclusion as…"* — truncated, but unmistakably a withholding. The project declined to accept the conclusion of the very document whose conclusion Session 1 elevates to *"the more defensible position."*

⚠ I cannot read the completion of that sentence — the corpus is sealed to me and Session 1 quoted only the fragment. So I do not know what the project withheld. **That is itself the point:** an artifact should not be elevated on the strength of a sentence whose end the extraction did not capture.

### What this does not say

`S1-F013`'s **Finding 2 is excellent and I confirm it.** The *classification blocker* — until it is known whether `SUPERSEDED` / `CONTESTED` / `RECONCILED` / `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` are states, relationships, events or assessments, no member list can be evaluated — is a genuine prior question, is well argued, and retrospectively explains `S1-F010`'s `WITHDRAWN` being state, event and history-remainder at once. Nothing here weakens it. **The blocker stands on its own reasoning, not on the eightfold verdict's authority** — which is why separating them matters.

## WHY IT MATTERS

This is the **third occurrence** of the evidence-class family in the register (`S2-F001`: a report about persistence is not persistence-derived evidence · `S2-F015`: persistence reported as convergence · here: a report of irresolvability treated as evidence of irresolvability). The shape recurs: **a document's description of a state of affairs is promoted to evidence for that state of affairs.**

Negative results are the most dangerous case, because they feel epistemically safe. A confident formulation invites scrutiny; *"UNRESOLVED"* invites agreement. It escaped the audit that `S1-F008`'s far weaker claim received.

## LENS

Lens 6 (evidence/justification — a report treated as the thing reported) · Lens 3 (provenance — non-independence applied asymmetrically) · Lens 2 (Zero — inability read as a property of the question) · Lens 5 (authority — who is entitled to declare a question unresolved?).

That preserves everything valuable and drops only the unearned authority.

**On adjudication** — **none, and this one needs saying.** An extraction finding that the domain's core classifications are unresolvable would, if accepted, bear on whether adjudication can proceed at all. It must not acquire that force from this provenance. Not routed.
