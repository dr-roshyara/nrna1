## 11 · What is challenged

### `.1` *"Largest reasoned exclusion set"* is a granularity artifact — **CHALLENGE**
Session 1 compares 16 against KCON-016 (5), the external review (6) and v1.1 §17 (11 rows). Two problems:

- **Ill-typed.** v1.1 §17's rows reject *characterisations of the whole system* (*Language engine · Database · Chatbot · LLM wrapper · Ontology repository · Truth machine*); F011's 16 are *mechanisms*. Counting them against each other compares different objects.
- **No granularity normalisation.** `Parsing · Normalization · Canonicalization` are one family (representation processing) split three ways; `Retrieval · Search · Ranking` likewise. **A longer list at finer granularity is not a larger exclusion.** Same defect as `S2-F014(b)`: a count with no measure, rewarding decomposition.

### `.2` The asymmetry has counterexamples — and correcting them makes it better
The stated pattern gives the domain the receptive verb and the mechanism the productive one: *admits* but does not *acquire* · *determines* but does not *reason* · *preserves* but does not *retrieve*.

But the companion documents give the domain **active** verbs: *assigns* identity, *records* history, *assigns* confidence. So the asymmetry as stated is not general.

**Corrected, it is sharper:** the domain produces **status, identity and record** — never **content**. That formulation covers the counterexamples and is a genuine exclusion test.

⚠ **And it is precisely `S1-F012`'s formulation** — *"the domain performs **status assignment, not content transformation**."* So once the asymmetry is stated correctly, F011 and F012 say the same thing. This **strengthens `S2-F017` Part 3**: their apparent disagreement over the word *"transformation"* is terminological, and F011's own governing principle is the evidence.
