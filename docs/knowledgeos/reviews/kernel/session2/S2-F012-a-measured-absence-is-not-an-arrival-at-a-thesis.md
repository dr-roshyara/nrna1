## FINDING

`S1-F004` Finding 3 counts three arrivals at *over time* as the load-bearing dimension:

> ⟦I⟧ *"this is the **third independent arrival** at *over time* … with `S1-F001` (0/20 grants carry validity information, so historical authority validity is unanswerable) and the Phase 2 temporal thread."*

**The `S1-F001` leg is not an arrival at the thesis. It is a measured deficiency, and deficiencies are neutral on importance.**

`S1-F001` establishes: *the authority record cannot express temporal validity.* To move from that to *temporal validity is load-bearing* requires the further premise *the system needs temporal validity* — **which is the thesis under test.** Without it, the inference is *X is absent, therefore X matters*, and absence supports that conclusion no better than it supports *X is absent because X is unnecessary*.

## EVIDENCE

- `S1-F001`'s content is entirely negative: `0/20` validity · `0/20` delegation · `0/20` ownership. It reports what the record **cannot** express.
- `S1-F001`'s own status line is careful about exactly this: *"**Not a defect claim** against the formal architecture."* Session 1 declined to read the absence as a deficiency-in-need — and then, in `S1-F004`, counts it as support for the thesis that the absent thing is central.
- Per `S2-F002`, the leg is additionally **one schema fact observed twenty times**, not twenty observations — so even as evidence it is a single structural datum.

## THE COUNT, RECONSIDERED

| Leg | Status as support for *"time is load-bearing"* |
|---|---|
| External research (*"epistemic accountability over time"*) | **counts** — a direct positive claim, though see `S2-F005` Ext 2 on framing inheritance |
| Phase 2 temporal thread | **uncharacterised** — later, and possibly descendant rather than independent |
| `S1-F001` measured gap | **does not count** — a deficiency observation, neutral on importance |

**So the defensible count is one clear arrival, possibly two — not three.** And the one clear leg is the one whose independence `S2-F005` already questions on framing grounds.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM

**It does not claim time is unimportant, and nothing here should be read that way.** The temporal thread may well be the most important direction in the corpus — `S2-F003` (the model time-indexes the claim but not the authority) and `S2-F004` (three distinct temporal questions) are both this reviewer's own findings pointing at its richness.

The challenge is confined to **arithmetic of convergence**: a thesis supported by one framing-inherited claim plus one uncharacterised thread is at an earlier stage of support than *"third independent arrival"* implies. **Under-supported is not false**, and the appropriate response is more evidence, not less attention.

## WHY IT MATTERS

Convergence counts are how this programme decides what deserves first-class investigation. If a **deficiency** can be counted as an **arrival**, then every gap the extraction finds becomes self-justifying: *we noticed X is missing, therefore X is important, therefore look harder at X.* That is a loop, and it will preferentially inflate whatever the extraction happens to measure.

## POSSIBLE IMPACT

Extraction methodology, and it generalises: **distinguish positive claims about what matters from negative observations about what is absent, and do not sum them.** Both belong in the register; only the first supports a thesis about importance.

## PROVENANCE

Derived solely from `session1/S1-F001` and `session1/S1-F004`. No corpus document read.

## STATUS

**OPEN — CHALLENGE to the convergence count, not to the temporal direction.** Session-1 artifacts not modified.

# RECURRENCE · 2026-08-25 — `S1-F005` repeats the counting pattern

**SOURCE** — `S1-F005`, "Possible relevance": *"`Time` appears as a primitive here; **fourth arrival** at the temporal dimension after `S1-F001` (measured), `S1-F004` (external), and the Phase 2 thread."*

**Why this is the same finding, not a new one.** The defect is identical to the one recorded above: heterogeneous items counted as repeated arrivals at one thesis. The four "arrivals" are four different *kinds* of statement about time:

| # | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| `S1-F001` | a **measurement of the model** — 0/20 grants carry temporal properties |
| `S1-F004` | an **external** party's observation |
| Phase 2 thread | the project's own **later** work |
| `S1-F005` C-list | the project's own **earlier** work naming `Time` a primitive |

Items 3 and 4 are the same author, and `S2-F015` shows the Phase 1→Phase 2 relationship there is more consistent with **persistence than convergence**. Item 1 is not an assertion about time at all — it is a count of time's absence. Summing them to "four" treats a measurement, an outside opinion, and one author's position stated twice as four independent votes.

**What is defensible from `S1-F005`:** the corpus contains an *early, dated* instance of `Time` being treated as a kernel primitive (2026-08-22 00:21). That is a useful provenance fact and it stands. It is not a fourth arrival.

**Recurrence matters.** This is now the pattern's **second appearance in a separate Session-1 artifact** (first: `S1-F004`, above). One occurrence is a slip in a document; two in independent documents make it a property of the **extraction method** — arrival-counting without an independence test. Recorded as a method observation about Session 1's analytical structure, not as a further criticism of either finding.

⚠ Unchanged from the original entry: **this says nothing against the temporal thesis itself.** Time may well be a genuine gap in the model. The objection is to the arithmetic offered in its support.
