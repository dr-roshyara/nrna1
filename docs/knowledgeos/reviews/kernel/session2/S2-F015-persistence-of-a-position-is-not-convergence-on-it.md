# S2-F015 · "MAJOR CONVERGENCE" — one position held consistently is not two arrivals at it

**SOURCE SESSION-1 ARTIFACT** — `S1-F005`, Finding 1 and its finding class.

**FINDING CLASS** — PROVENANCE PROBLEM

---

## OBSERVATION

`S1-F005` is classed **MAJOR CONVERGENCE** on the ground that Phase 1 (2026-08-22 00:21) stated *"the kernel protects the rules; the mathematics describes the behavior"* and the Phase 2 measure-theory thread reached the same verdict on 2026-08-25. **Independence between the two statements is never tested.** The available provenance points away from convergence and toward a single position restated.

## EVIDENCE

Three items, all from `S1-F005` itself:

1. **Same origin.** Provenance is recorded as `P1 ORIGINAL_PROJECT`. Phase 2 is the same project's continuation. Nothing in the artifact identifies a second, independent author.
2. **Same stimulus shape.** C *"responds to the complex-numbers proposal of `20260821-2325`"*. The Phase 2 thread responds to the measure-theory proposal of `181038`. Both are the same move: *a mathematical formalism is proposed as the kernel; the project rejects it as the kernel and re-files it as a description or regime.*
3. **The artifact concedes it.** Finding 1's own wording — *"arrived back at a position the project already held"* — states that the project **held** the position continuously across the interval. A position that was held throughout was not arrived at twice.

## ANALYSIS

Convergence means separate paths meeting. Its evidential force comes from the *separateness*: two investigators reaching one conclusion by different routes is informative precisely because neither could have copied the other.

Here the null hypothesis is **persistence** — one author holding one methodological commitment (*don't start from the mathematics*) and applying it to whichever formalism is proposed next. Persistence predicts the observation exactly: same verdict, same shape, same author, four days apart. Nothing in `S1-F005` distinguishes persistence from convergence, and item 3 is the artifact's own testimony for persistence.

This is **mechanism 2 of `S2-F005`** (undeclared descent) at the level of a *stance* rather than a proposition: Phase 2's reasoning did not need to inherit C's text to inherit C's framing.

⚠ **Note what this does not say.** `S1-F005` is careful — *"Recorded as a convergence to accumulate — ⚠ not as evidence that the Phase 2 work was unnecessary, and not as a ruling."* I confirm that caution, and I am not claiming the Phase 2 work was redundant. Re-testing a held position under five independent refutations is real work and produces real assurance; the five K4 refutations may well be independent of each other. **The independence that fails is between Phase 1's statement and Phase 2's conclusion — not among Phase 2's internal refutations,** which this session has no evidence about either way.

### The framing inverts the direction provenance supports

Finding 1's headline is *"Phase 1 reaches the Phase 2 verdict four days early."* That casts Phase 1 as prescient and Phase 2 as the later arrival. But if the position was held continuously by the same project, the supportable direction is the reverse: **Phase 2's verdict may descend from Phase 1's commitment**, in which case its agreement corroborates nothing. Reported as prescience, it reads as two votes for the conclusion. It may be one.

## WHY IT MATTERS

Because `MAJOR CONVERGENCE` is the strongest finding class in the register, and it is carrying an untested independence assumption. Under the standing rule — *the strongest statement made must never exceed the strength of the available evidence* — the defensible class is **PERSISTENT POSITION** or **RESTATEMENT**, with convergence available only if a second independent author can be identified.

This is now the **second Session-1 artifact** to count non-independent instances as arrivals (`S2-F012` recorded the first, in `S1-F004`). Two occurrences in separate artifacts make it a property of the extraction method rather than a slip in one document — see the recurrence note appended to `S2-F012`.
