# S2-F017 · "The corpus now holds eight distinct Kernel formulations" — the count is inflated by four separate mechanisms

**SOURCE SESSION-1 ARTIFACTS** — `S1-F013` Finding 3 (the count) · `S1-F011` Finding 1 · `S1-F012` Finding 3 · `S1-F009` / `S1-F011` / `S1-F012` (Projection).

**FINDING CLASS** — TYPE ERROR + VOCABULARY COLLISION

---

## OBSERVATION

`S1-F013` reports: *"the corpus now holds **eight distinct Kernel formulations**"* — admission · consistency · decision · transformation · epistemic accountability core · constitutional distinction-preservation · epistemic coherence boundary · traceable relationship system. **Four separate mechanisms inflate this number**, and Session 1's own artifacts contain the evidence for each.

## ANALYSIS

### Part 1 · Two of the eight were rejected candidates, not held positions

`S1-F011` Finding 1 records that its source document *"tests **four** boundary candidates before selecting"* — consistency, decision, admission, transformation — and selects **transformation**. So `consistency boundary` and `decision boundary` are entries in one document's *comparison table*, considered and set aside.

A formulation that was examined and declined is **not a formulation the corpus holds.** Counting it as one conflates *the set of options enumerated* with *the set of positions taken*. On the evidence Session 1 supplies, positions actually taken are: admission (`F009`), transformation (`F011`), accountability core (`F012`), and F013's three models — **six, not eight.**

### Part 2 · "Consistency" and "coherence" are never tested for synonymy

Three terms circulate: `consistency boundary` (F011 §6, rejected) · *"smallest **consistency** boundary"* (F013's own research question) · `Epistemic **Coherence** Boundary` (F013 Model B). And `S1-F011`'s selected formulation is characterised as protecting *"epistemic **coherence** invariants"*.

So Model B's name is close to a paraphrase of what F011's transformation-boundary claims to protect. **Nothing in either artifact tests whether coherence and consistency denote one concept or two.** Under lens 1 this is the first question to ask, and under lens 8 the terms' similarity is not licence to merge them either — the point is that the count assumes distinctness without testing it.

⚠ This matters more here than it would elsewhere, because `S1-F002` is *itself* a finding about ubiquitous-language instability in this corpus. **A count of distinct formulations is exactly the quantity that instability corrupts**, and the two findings are never brought into contact.

### Part 3 · The `F011` ↔ `F012` "rejection" may be a dispute about a word

`S1-F012` Finding 3 records that F012 declines F011's framing: *"The domain performs **status assignment, not content transformation**."* Session 1 records this as *"the later explicitly rejects the earlier's selected formulation … Recorded, unresolved."*

But read what each means by the term, using only Session 1's own quotations:

| | What "transformation" refers to |
|---|---|
| `F011` | the span **candidate → knowledge** — a change of *status*, with all content mechanisms explicitly in its 16-item exclusion set |
| `F012` | **content** transformation — the thing it says the domain must not be read as performing |

F011's exclusion set already excludes `Normalization · Canonicalization · Reasoning · Model Inference` — i.e. F011 already denies that the domain transforms content. **On this evidence the two agree on substance and differ on the connotation of one word.** That is a vocabulary collision, not two rival formulations, and it removes one more item from any count of substantive disagreements.

⚠ I do **not** assert they agree. I assert the artifacts as extracted do not establish that they disagree, and that the available reading is terminological.

### Part 4 · The three Projection exclusions are one lineage, not three arrivals

Session 1 records Projection excluded in `F009` (*"first exclusion of projection found in the corpus"*), `F011` (*"excluded again — second corpus exclusion"*), `F012` (*"third exclusion of projection, again unargued"*).

But Session 1 also establishes, in `S1-F011`'s own provenance note, that these documents share a prompt: *"Structurally near-identical to `110950` … **two models answering one shared prompt, not two independent arrivals.** Convergence between them is therefore weak evidence, and I do not count it as independent corroboration."*

**That discipline is applied to the formulations and not to the exclusions.** Three unargued exclusions from one prompt lineage are one exclusion restated three times. The `S1-F011` note and the Projection tally cannot both stand as written.

⚠ Note what is *not* affected: the observation that **all three exclude Projection without argument** remains interesting regardless of independence — an unargued exclusion repeated is still an unargued exclusion. What fails is the accumulation, not the observation.

## WHY IT MATTERS

`S1-F013` is, correctly, the batch's headline: eight systematic UNRESOLVED verdicts. Its force rests partly on *breadth* — many formulations, none settled. If the true count of substantive positions is six or fewer, with at least one pair possibly terminological, then the picture is less "a field of eight rival theories" and more "a handful of positions, some of which may be the same position in different words."

That changes what the corpus is evidence *of*. Eight irreconcilable formulations suggest a genuinely hard question. A smaller set with untested synonymy suggests a **vocabulary problem** — which is `S1-F002`'s finding, and a different kind of blocker with a different remedy.
