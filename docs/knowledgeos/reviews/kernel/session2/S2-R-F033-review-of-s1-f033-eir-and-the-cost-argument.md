## 4 · Confirmed

**`.1` The justification-type tension is the artifact's real finding — and it is excellent**
Session 1: *"the only argument in the entire corpus that justifies a Kernel by what it **saves** rather than by what it **protects**"*, and the consequence: *"cost arguments reward **more** Kernel content, invariant arguments reward **less**."*

✅ Confirmed, and it names a pressure this register has felt without labelling. Every `DO NOT IMPLEMENT (already protected)` verdict I have issued rests on the invariant framing: *law already protects it, so build nothing*. Under a **cost** framing the same finding could read: *law protects it but the Kernel could do it more cheaply, so build it*. **The two framings can produce opposite verdicts from identical evidence.** That is worth carrying into the synthesis.

⚠ And Session 1 is right to mark it **RESEARCH ONLY**: the economic claim carries **no measurements**. An empirically testable justification with no measurement is a hypothesis about economics, not evidence of it — and it is the one claim in the corpus that could in principle be settled by a benchmark.

**`.2` Three converging admission conditions.** *Standing* (`S1-F032`: was this input allowed to participate?) · *prerequisite presence* (Zero: what happens when a prerequisite is missing?) · *context sufficiency* (Leonardo: have we understood the whole relevant context?). ✅ Genuinely three different questions, and Session 1's observation that the corpus is converging on them is sound.

⟦L⟧ The Zero question is exactly what ⟨Z-1⟩ answers. Session 1 says the Leonardo question has no counterpart — *"v1.1 requires context to be **present** (⟨Z-1⟩) but not **sufficient**."* ⚠ `ContextTuple` (l. 208) is *"the delimiting conditions… **the frame beyond which the claim is incomplete**"*, which does model incompleteness. Whether *delimiting* amounts to *sufficiency* is a **semantic** question about that phrase — the same kind I declined to settle by grep at `S2-R-F029`. Recorded as a question, **not** a gap.

**`.3` The retroactive-state consequence.** Session 1 composes `S1-F033`'s *"knowledge changes when evidence changes"* with `S1-F030`'s constitutive acquisition and derives: **re-assessing an acquisition procedure could change knowledge state retroactively** — noting *"no document draws that."*

✅ Sound composition, and I can close it: ⟦L⟧ retroactivity is **already handled** — l. 219, *"no member is modified in place without creating a new forward-only state."* A re-assessment produces a **new** state; the prior state is retained. So the consequence is real and law absorbs it. **DO NOT IMPLEMENT.**
