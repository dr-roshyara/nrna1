## FINDING

S1-F002's table records, as its **first row**:

> **`Knowledge`** — *"Broad; current representation boundaries unclear"*

S1-F002 then builds its argument around `Rule` (seven senses) and the method rule *preserve, do not normalize*. **The `Knowledge` entry is left as a table row.**

**A project whose central term is recorded by its own baseline as having unclear representation boundaries has stated something more consequential than that `Rule` is overloaded.** `Rule`'s overload is a naming problem with a known remedy. `Knowledge`'s unclear boundary is the question the entire Kernel programme exists to answer.

## EVIDENCE

- The row appears first in S1-F002's own table, which suggests the source ordered it first.
- Two adjacent rows compound it: **`Observation`** — *"relationship to evidence and knowledge not fully formalized"*; **`Artifact`** — *"broad; not equivalent to knowledge"*. So the baseline records that it cannot cleanly bound `Knowledge` **against evidence, against observation, or against artifact**.
- S1-F002 draws out the `Artifact ≠ Knowledge` row (correctly, as CONSISTENT with §15) but does not connect it back to the `Knowledge` row it qualifies.

## WHY IT MATTERS

This is an **extension**, not a challenge: S1-F002's finding stands, and this is material it already surfaced but did not elevate. The elevation matters for two reasons.

First, it changes what the table *is*. S1-F002 presents it as evidence of *vocabulary instability* — a method finding. Read with the `Knowledge` row foregrounded, it is also evidence about **the subject matter**: the baseline could not say where knowledge ends and evidence, observation or artifact begin.

Second, it supplies the strongest available support for S1-F002's own method rule. *Preserve, do not prematurely normalize* is far more compelling when the term at stake is `Knowledge` than when it is `Rule`.
