That is worse than I recorded, and it is Session 1 that established it.

## 3 · The absence-claim warning, and how far it reaches into my findings

⚠ Session 1: *"**Absence-based findings are the weak class.** Statements of the form 'no document asks X', 'seven genera and no eighth', 'the corpus never resolves Y' rest on search coverage, not on reading… **Treat every absence claim in these artifacts as unconfirmed unless the artifact says otherwise.**"*

✅ Correct, and it is the right warning. **But the reach must be stated carefully, because two different kinds of absence claim are in play:**

| Claim of the form | Warrant | Status |
|---|---|---|
| *"no document in the **corpus** says X"* — Session 1's | search over 160 documents, **3–5% read** | **UNCONFIRMED**, as Session 1 says |
| *"no **Session-1 artifact** says X"* — mine | I read all 40 artifacts in full | **sound within its scope** |
| *"**law** does not carry X"* — mine, e.g. evidence roles at `S2-R-F029` | verified by direct inspection of v1.1 | **sound**, and independent of Session 1 |

**So my `DO NOT IMPLEMENT (already protected)` verdicts are unaffected** — every one rests on my own verification of law, not on a Session-1 absence claim. What *is* affected: any Session-2 finding whose force depends on a corpus-scope absence I inherited. `S2-R-F035.5` (*"the only place in 93 documents where completeness and certainty trade"*) is the clearest case, and it is now **downgraded to unconfirmed**.
