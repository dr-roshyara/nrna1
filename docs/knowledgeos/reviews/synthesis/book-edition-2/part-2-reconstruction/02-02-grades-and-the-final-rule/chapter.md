# II.2 · Grades and the Final Rule

> **Edition 1 abstract** *(frozen baseline, verbatim opening)*: Every load-bearing statement in
> this programme carries a grade, and the grading system is the method's spine. — *Full Edition-1
> text: `../../../book/part-2-reconstruction/02-02-grades-and-the-final-rule/chapter.md`.*

## 1 · Why grades — one voice is not enough — *status: method [M]*

[M] A technical book ordinarily speaks in one voice: assertion. This book cannot, because its
subject matter spans certainties that differ by orders of magnitude — a computationally tested
law, a read-verified corpus claim, a coherence-forced repair, an interpretive reading, and an
open question can all occur in one paragraph, and a voice that levels them commits the exact
collapse the architecture forbids (one score replacing epistemic structure — the Dimension
article's failure signature, applied to prose). The grading system is the method's answer: every
load-bearing statement carries a mark that says *how* it is known, and the reader can always
distinguish the claim from the confidence.

## 2 · The element grades — *status: currently ratified [FA/M], with per-grade meaning*

[FA/M] Five grades attach to model elements, each with an operational meaning — what a reader may
expect to exist behind the mark:

- **TESTED** — a recorded test artifact exists (behind it: inputs, procedure, outcomes). In the
  model's concept and invariant tables exactly one concept (Evidence) and two invariants (I-5,
  I-6) carry it, plus one within-scope variant (K_t, whose parenthesis is load-bearing); the
  object inventory adds its own TESTED marks (the EXP-01 objects; M₄₉ within scope).
- **READ** — read-verified against corpus sources: the source *asserts and argues* it; no
  experiment confirms it. The model's most common grade — and the book always says which source.
- **COMPOSITION** — assembled by the synthesis from multiple sources (Authorization: four
  sources, no single home); the assembly is the synthesis's act and is marked as such.
- **SPECIFIED** — designed but unexercised (the reference machine's elaborations; the EG-05
  suites). A specification is a promise, not a property.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — proposed, awaiting evidence; the corpus's native mode, from which everything
  above was promoted or not.

[M] The grades are a *provenance* scale, not a truth scale: a READ claim may be true and a TESTED
claim narrow. What the scale guarantees is only — and exactly — that the mark never overstates
what stands behind it.

## 3 · The claim types — and how the two families compose — *status: currently ratified [R]*

[R] Orthogonal to element grades, every substantive sentence carries a claim type: **[FA]** the
ratified architecture speaking (always traceable to the authorized model or a ratified
determination) · **[H]** history (dated source or archaeology finding) · **[M]** method and
governance record · **[P]** prospect, binding no one — refined where needed by **[E]** evidence,
**[IN]** marked interpretation, **[RC]** required-by-coherence, **[R]** explicit ruling, **[U]**
open. The families compose: "I-5 [FA]←TESTED" says *the ratified architecture asserts it, and a
test artifact stands behind the assertion*; "I-11 [FA]/[RC]" says *ratified, and argued into the
model under ruling rather than read from one source* — a difference this book shows on the row,
because hiding it is precisely the failure the system names.

## 4 · Where the discipline came from — *status: explicitly recorded [E], dated*

[E] The grading system was not designed at the book's desk; it is the corpus's own oldest habit,
formalized. The first file in the record (2026-08-01) already refuses to imply a target
architecture. The formal era's Step 109 states the governing principle in a box — **"No
architectural assertion without evidence"** — and immediately separates five standings: Observed,
Inferred, Specified, Claimed, and "Not yet evidenced" — the direct ancestor of §2's scale.
[E] The repository tradition, independently, gated even *grammar* on epistemic status: its
research register held that a Candidate speaks with "should," and the final "SHALL is earned" by
the P5 domain-independence test — modal verbs assigned by grade. [Reconstructed] Two traditions, one
instinct, arrived at separately — the same convergence pattern Part III found in the
architecture's content, here in its manners.

## 5 · The honesty rules — each with its origin story — *status: currently ratified [R], origins explicitly recorded [E]*

[R/E] Over the grades sit rules that keep them honest, and each is a monument to a recorded
incident (the §8-of-II.1 pattern, in detail):

- **The final rule** — *the strongest statement made never exceeds the strength of the available
  evidence* — the programme's constitution-level law, inherited from its engineering environment
  and applied to every artifact including this book.
- **No silent upgrade** — no sentence may raise the epistemic status of the evidence beneath it.
  Origin: standing rule of the book plan; enforced twice by independent review against this very
  book (a grade census that dropped a TESTED mark; two phrasings a notch above support — both
  corrected under authorization, both preserved in the record).
- **The echo rule** — the programme's own outputs, found saved back into the corpus, carry zero
  evidential weight. Origin: a saved status report discovered inside the "read-only" corpus,
  which an ungoverned reconstruction would have cited as historical confirmation of itself.
- **Dated statistics** — every corpus count carries its as-of date. Origin: the corpus grew
  while being studied; an undated census is wrong the day after it is right.
- **The verbatim rule** — ratified wording is reproduced exactly, never paraphrased. Origin: a
  grade table whose column said "as ratified" while two annotations had been silently altered —
  caught by independent review, corrected under ruling, and taught in III.10 as content.
- **Claim-strength inheritance** — a summary, table, diagram or abstract may never carry a
  stronger status than the passage it compresses. Origin: this Part's own quality gate, which
  found that every post-correction defect lived in synoptic material; the rule is the newest
  control and binds this chapter.

## 6 · Grading in practice — one claim, end to end — *status: worked method [M/E]*

[M] Follow one real claim through the machinery. **Corpus line** (dated 2026-08-27): the
experiment verdict's §2 headline — *"dependency must come first"* — argued from the case of one API
observation read by two LLMs. **Registry row:** the experiment registry sets down design, method,
result, and — noted there as recorded in the registry, not in the source — the limitations
("single normalized universe; no external replication"); it grades the result TESTED because a recorded artifact stands behind it. **Ratification:** the
synthesis carries it as invariant I-6 at that grade; a later ruling preserves the wording
verbatim. **Verification:** an independent audit re-implements the operators, reproduces the
result, and sharpens it — the property is a *pipeline* property, impossible for any scalar
operator alone. **Book sentence:** Part III teaches I-6 as TESTED *with the pipeline
qualification stated* — the grade unchanged, the scope more precise. [M] At no step did the claim
gain strength it had not earned; at one step it gained *precision* — and the difference between
those two is the entire discipline.

## 7 · The weak class — grading absences — *status: explicitly recorded [E]*

[E] The hardest grading problem is the claim that something is NOT in the record. The programme's
rule: absence-derived claims are a *weak class*, downgraded twice in the record — a claimed
0.6% sampling miss-rate was superseded when one genuine full read recovered ten missed findings,
and an "only appears at" first-occurrence claim fell to a wider search. Exactly one absence claim
was promoted to a load-bearing finding — *the corpus contains no externally ratified decision* —
and its promotion rested on the search being systematic rather than sampled, a distinction the
finding itself records. [M] The operational rule a reader can reuse: an absence claim is worth
exactly as much as the description of the search that produced it.

## 8 · What grading cannot do — *status: limits [U/E]*

[E/U] Grades are assigned judgments: auditable (every mark cites its basis), not provable. They
drift under editing — the record shows three grade-discipline failures inside this very book, all
in synoptic material, all caught by review and corrected under authorization; the newest controls
(post-edit re-verification, negative-assertion greps, claim-strength inheritance) exist because
assignment discipline decays precisely where prose is smoothest. And grades do not compose
automatically: a chain of READ claims is not itself READ — composition is the synthesis's act and
takes the COMPOSITION mark. **Relation-status ledger (BA-ED2-11):**

## 9 · Conclusion — *status: method [M], summary*

[M] The grading system is the book's contract with its reader: five marks for how a thing is
known, a type for who is speaking, rules that forbid quiet strengthening, a weak class for
absences, and a record of its own failures kept in plain sight. The next chapter shows the
discipline under maximum load — the day the synthesis was attacked with its own methods, mis-
stepped once at the gate, and came out with two new invariants and its authority intact.
