## D · Provenance and temporal integrity

The book already operates the four-level separation (GN-55): SOURCE RECORD → HISTORICAL
RECONSTRUCTION → LATER INTERPRETATION → CURRENT RATIFIED UNDERSTANDING, with per-claim classes.
Two live demonstrations were verified in this audit: II.3 §3 keeps the 3B report's own recognition
separate from the later AF-008 census (*"The wider census came later"*), and II.4 §3's status line
declares that correspondence readings are the register's ⟦INT⟧, never repository self-description.

**Required provenance model for any future theory insertion** (five layers, none collapsible):
1. what the original research record actually said (dated, quoted only if the string exists);
2. what the synthesis reconstructed later (marked [Reconstructed]);
3. what independent verification discovered (marked as *later*, with its own artifact identity);
4. what was subsequently corrected (both layers preserved, never overwritten);
5. what remains unresolved (register entry, resolution prerequisite named).

**Rule (already precedent, restated for the handoff):** a later discovery may *explain* an earlier
artifact; it may never rewrite what that artifact historically contained. The GN-19 breach
narrative is the book's own worked example of the failure mode and its repair.

**Finding D-1 (no defect):** the machinery needed to receive a later verification result without
back-projection exists and is exercised. **Finding D-2 (dependency):** the incoming report will
need a *stable artifact identity* (md5 + counts) to be citable under BA-ED2-13/14 — see the gate.
