# III.3 · Knowledge and the World

[FA] The model's most consequential structural humility is one inequality: **`X_t ≠ Observed(X_t)`**
— invariant I-7. The world state is never fully observed; the model is never the reality. This is
not a caveat added for safety; it is load-bearing architecture. Because the inequality is
structural, nothing downstream may treat the knowledge state as the world, and every mechanism
that consumes observations must carry the difference.

[H] The inequality has a lineage worth one sentence here (Part I.4 tells it fully): it is the
survivor of the historical Ω — the "whole space" the early mathematical drafts assumed an observer
could stand over. The discovery process decomposed Ω three ways: its ideal-reference role was
absorbed into the IdealState-and-contract machinery, its unboundedness was deliberately bounded
away, and its whole-space-observation role was *inverted* into the complement principle that
became I-7. What began as an assumption of total view ended as a prohibition of assuming one.

**The knowledge state `K_t`.** [FA] The formal state over eight primitives — Entity, State, Event,
Observation, Proposition, Relation, Policy, Action — the model's carrier of "what is currently
held." Its consistency under attack was computationally exercised (fifty adversarial checks in the
corpus's Step-050 audit; the record itself is careful to say *not a proof*, and so is this book).

[H] A naming caution the reader will need: `K_t` in this book always means this formal state. The
discovery corpus also contains an earlier, unrelated `K(t) = M(t) + iA(t)` — a complex-number
model of knowledge from five days before the formal era — and the engineering documents use
"knowledge state" for an event-flow record. Three senses, one notation family; the ratified
terminology register keeps them apart, and so does every page that follows.

**Two observations, not one.** [FA] The model distinguishes SourceObservation from
SemanticObservation — *what was observed* from *what was understood*. The distinction is
evidence-derived but untested; it earns its place because collapsing it would violate the
constitutional prohibition (Article 6.5) that observation must not silently become inference.
