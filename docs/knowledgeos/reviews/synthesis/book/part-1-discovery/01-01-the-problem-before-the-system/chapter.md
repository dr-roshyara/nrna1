# I.1 · The Problem Before the System

[H] The oldest document in the discovery corpus is dated 2026-08-01, and it is not about
KnowledgeOS at all — it is a reconstruction baseline for something called EKS, the Engineering
Knowledge System, and it opens with a stance that would quietly become the whole programme's
method: *"Evidence-first / architecture archaeology … Reconstruction baseline — not a target
architecture … no future architecture is implied."*

Two things deserve attention in that sentence, and one thing must not be read into it. First, the
discipline — describe what exists, imply nothing about what should — predates every architecture
this book describes. Second, the word "KnowledgeOS" already appears in that document, as a
*neighbor*: something EKS has a relationship to, in a working engineering platform full of roles,
governance experiments, and event-driven designs. [H] Through the first three weeks of the corpus
(the "root era," 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-22), KnowledgeOS is that: a strategic object among others —
meta-models, ontologies, character definitions — being circled, positioned, compared, but not yet
formalized.

What must not be read into it: none of the later machinery is "already there." The archaeology was
explicit on this point (its finding AF-002 stops exactly at the stance, not the system), and this
book follows it. The root era contributed something subtler than concepts: **vocabulary that later
became architecture without knowing it would**. "Proposal," "acceptance," "authorization" — the
words of the later decision boundary — appear here first as *governance practice*, in
role-separation research and lifecycle documents, weeks before anything formalized them. When Part
III's decision machinery reads like a formalization of good governance habits, that is because,
historically, it is one: the archaeology calls it a double ancestry — practice first, formal
model later — and found no conflict between the two.

[H] One relationship the root era opened and never closed: what exactly EKS *is* to KnowledgeOS.
The corpus asserts the relationship repeatedly and settles it nowhere (OQ-11, still open today).
This book inherits the question, not an answer.
