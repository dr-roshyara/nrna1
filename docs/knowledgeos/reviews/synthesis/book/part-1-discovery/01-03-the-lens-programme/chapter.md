# I.3 · The Lens Programme

[H] The kernel-era corpus (2026-08-22 to 2026-08-25, 170 documents) is a reading programme: take
the still-soft idea of a knowledge kernel and pass it through as many disciplined lenses as three
days allow. Navya-Nyāya relation logic. The Bhagavad Gītā's knower of the field. Statistics — the
seven pillars, uncertainty and belief revision. Bayesian models of mind. Typed mathematical
modelling. Each lens was mined for *properties a kernel must have*, not for doctrine — the
repository side's closure ruling would later call this out as the era's achievement: completely
different traditions converged on the same structural problems and *did not produce new kernel
laws*.

[H] Two arrivals in this era matter enormously later, and neither looked important at the time.
**Ω** enters on 2026-08-24 — not in the measure-theory crisis where a reader of Part III might
assume it was born, but in the typed-model and Bayesian-mind readings, as the ambient space a
mathematical epistemic model quietly presupposes. The crisis, when it came a day later, *inherited*
Ω and then had to dismantle it. And the **aggregation question** — how pieces of evidence combine,
what duplicates should do, what corroboration should do — arrives here as reading-derived
intuition from the statistics lenses, three days before the experiment that would make two of
those intuitions the only TESTED invariants in the architecture.

[H] The era's native product was **eight competing kernel formulations** — eight different answers
to "what is the irreducible core?" None was selected; the corpus simply moved on, and the
selection that did happen (the eleven articles, I.2) happened on the repository side from
different material. The eight stand in the record as the shape of the search space. (Coverage
honesty, carried from the archaeology: the kernel-era corpus was processed at extraction depth —
about one document in twenty was read in full — so this book inventories the eight and tells the
era's story at the resolution the record supports, with the depth caveat attached rather than
hidden.)

[H] The era also planted two seeds of later collisions: "Committed" appears here in an unrelated
sense ("what is being committed to" — the kernel's commitment object), and the Gītā reading
introduced the cast — the Knower of the Field, the charioteer, the Lord — whose naming adventures
Part I.5 follows.
