# II.1 · Archaeology Before Architecture

[M] Between the corpus and the architecture stands a method, and this part describes it — because
the architecture's credibility rests on the method's discipline, and the method is reusable where
the architecture may not be.

The reconstruction did not read the corpus as a quarry for the best ideas. It read it
*historically*: 688 documents sorted into three sequential eras (root → kernel → formal), five
methodological regimes identified inside the formal era (measure-theory crisis → question series →
Gītā/Sārathi → computational closures → step series), each regime's vocabulary, evidence standard,
and turnover measured before any concept was extracted. The governing question was never "what is
true in here?" but "what happened in here?" — reconstruction of a knowledge journey, in the
programme's own ruling language, *without pretending the destination was known at the beginning*.

[M] Three structural rules made it work. **The corpus is the unit of completion** — a discovery is
something to record, not a reason to stop reading. **The filesystem artifact is the record** — a
finding exists when its file exists, and chat is only an execution report. **Coverage is stated,
never implied** — where reading was extraction-deep (the kernel era: roughly one document in
twenty read fully), the depth is recorded on every claim that rests on it, and this book carries
those caveats forward visibly.

[M] The same pass built the instruments the later phases ran on: a claim registry, a decision
registry, a contradiction registry (the programme mapped its tensions before resolving any), an
experiment registry, and a semantic lineage map that tracked contested terms — Zero, Ω, Sārathi
and the other lens-era names — across regime boundaries, because in this corpus *the same word rarely keeps the same meaning*.
The archaeology proper (run later, under its own gate) then classified everything it touched:
historical fact, hypothesis, experiment, decision, alternative, abandoned, superseded — and,
crucially, PROCESS/PROMPT artifact: a saved prompt inside the historical tree is not historical
evidence, and a copy is never corroboration.

[M] The pre-governance finding frames it all (AF-009): the corpus contains not one externally
ratified decision. Everything in it is author-level exploration. Authority entered the programme
only downstream, through the recorded rulings Part II.3 describes — which is precisely why the
method insists the corpus is evidence of discovery and can never be authority for architecture.
