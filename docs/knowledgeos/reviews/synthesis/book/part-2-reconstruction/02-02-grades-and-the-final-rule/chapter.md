# II.2 · Grades and the Final Rule

[M] Every load-bearing statement in this programme carries a grade, and the grading system is the
method's spine. Two families:

**Element grades** (what kind of support a concept has): `TESTED` — a recorded test artifact
exists; `READ` — read-verified from the corpus; `COMPOSITION` — assembled from graded parts;
`SPECIFIED` — designed but unexercised; `HYPOTHESIS`. **Claim types** (who is speaking): [FA]
ratified architecture · [H] history · [M] method · [P] prospect · plus [E]/[IN]/[RC]/[R]/[U] for
evidence, inference, coherence-requirement, ruling, and unresolved.

[M] Over the grades sit the rules that keep them honest. **The final rule:** the strongest
statement made never exceeds the strength of the available evidence — the corpus's own day-one
discipline, adopted as law for every synthesis artifact and for this book. **GN-14:** no sentence
may silently upgrade the epistemic status of the underlying evidence — an inference presented with
the cadence of a fact is a defect, not a style choice. **GN-01:** a synthesis echo — the
programme's own output saved back into the corpus — has zero evidential weight; the programme
found one and disarmed it. **GN-03:** every corpus statistic carries its as-of date, because the
corpus grew while being studied. **The hindsight ban:** "the architecture was always…" is sayable
only where the corpus explicitly establishes it, which in practice was never.

[M] The programme also graded *absences*, and learned to distrust them: "no document says X" is
the weak evidence class, downgraded twice in the programme's history when fuller reading recovered
what sampling had missed. The one absence promoted to a finding — the corpus's lack of any
ratified decision — earned it by systematic search, not by sampling.

[M] Why so much apparatus? Because the alternative was demonstrated, repeatedly, in the material
itself: the corpus's most confident documents are its least reliable; its best results (EXP-01,
the Step-050 audit) are the ones that state their own limits. The grading system is the corpus's
hardest-won lesson, institutionalized.
