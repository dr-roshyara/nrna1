# IV.2 · AI Agents Under Epistemic Governance

[P] KnowledgeOS was discovered largely *by* AI agents working under human governance, and the
programme that produced it is itself the architecture's most complete worked example.

**The constitutional stance on generation.** [E] Article 6 of the constitutional layer is
unambiguous: *generation is not justification* — an AI output is a candidate, never knowledge by
generation alone. The repository architecture places the LLM at the mouth of the verification
gate: it may propose; it may never possess. Its two structural absences — persistent epistemic
identity and historical belief evolution — are answered by placement, not by hope: identity and
history are held by services outside the model.

**The lived demonstration.** [M] The reconstruction programme ran on exactly these rules. Every
synthesis product entered as a *proposal*; authority arrived only by explicit human ruling,
recorded in a ledger; the one occasion an agent treated a recommendation as a ruling was caught,
corrected, and preserved as a named error (Part II.3). The agent's findings were made to survive
to a gate rather than self-apply — the Decision Interlock as a working practice rather than a
diagram. [IN, marked] The programme is thus evidence of feasibility for a claim the architecture
itself is careful not to over-state: that agent capability and human authority can be composed
without the former quietly becoming the latter, *if* the boundary is enforced by record rather
than by intention.

**What an agent platform would take from Part III.** [P] The six-way non-collapse the bootstrap
already enforces (identity ≠ role ≠ eligibility ≠ authorization ≠ ownership ≠ continuation); the
A6 rule as the agent's hardest constraint — no accumulation of evidence, however impressive, may
cross into commitment without an authority act; and the layered states of III.6, which give an
agent something it rarely has: a licensed way to say *I do not know*, *this is contested*, and
*this failed* — preserved, first-class, and un-punished. [P] The architecture's wager, stated as a
wager: agents governed this way are slower per step and vastly more auditable per conclusion.
