# IV.3 · Parallel Lanes and Future Intake

[M] Not everything the programme touched is inside the architecture, and the way things are *kept
outside* is itself part of the design.

**The v1.1 lane.** [E] On an unmerged branch of the repository lives a Reference Architecture
v1.1 — a DDD refinement of the constitutional tradition, produced under its own explicit
authorization, revised through several governed rounds, and never conformance-tested against the
formal model. The ratified disposition (D-FA-2) is deliberate on both edges: **no silent intake**
(incorporating 705 lines of untested refinement would be merging by neglect) and **no silent
discard** (it is authorized lane work, not scratch). It waits, as a parallel lane, for the one
thing that could bring it in: a dedicated intake conformance pass (OQ-9). This book cites exactly
two facts from it — its own status line, and two corroborations read *as lane evidence, marked as
such* — and otherwise leaves its content untold.

**The EKS relationship.** [H/U] The oldest unresolved question in the whole record (OQ-11): the
corpus's first document already relates KnowledgeOS to an Engineering Knowledge System, and no era
since has settled what that relationship is. A scoping act would close it; none has been
commissioned.

**The general rule.** [FA] The architecture's boundary discipline generalizes past its own case:
material enters by explicit act — intake, ratification, adjudication — or it does not enter. The
programme's gates enforced this for historical corpora (archaeology before use), for parallel
lanes (v1.1), for prompt residue (classified, excluded), and for the book itself (this volume
required three rulings before its first sentence). [P] Any organization adopting KnowledgeOS
inherits the pattern: the cheapest moment to govern a source is before it becomes load-bearing.
