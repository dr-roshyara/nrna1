## 1 · Constitution ratification

The HPA's assessment ratifies Constitution v1.0. Its verdict (recorded): the constitutional boundary is clear; the **Kernel vs Engine separation** is the most important architectural decision — *it prevents confusing the mechanism that manages knowledge with the meaning of knowledge itself* (graph database ≠ knowledge · vector embedding ≠ identity · LLM reasoning ≠ justification · workflow state ≠ truth · ontology ≠ reality); the eleven laws are **anti-corruption constraints**, not features; the LLM boundary is precise (KnowledgeOS is not "better AI" — it is *fundamentally different*: an LLM optimizes generation, KnowledgeOS preserves epistemic continuity); the Navya-Nyāya contribution found its correct place (mathematics describes, never defines); the negative boundary's five refusals are **constitutional failure modes**, each tied to violated articles; amendment discipline is the right final piece.

## 4 · Questions the Reference Architecture must answer

1. How do constitutional laws map to architectural boundaries?
2. What are the kernel services?
3. What are engine responsibilities?
4. What are representation models?
5. Where do LLMs fit as governed mechanisms?
6. Where are validation, reasoning, contradiction management, and history engines placed?

## 5 · The architecture's nature

The Constitution is **not** architecture — it is the **constraint system architecture must obey**. The Reference Architecture is the first artifact that IS architecture: it maps the constraint system (the eleven articles + the kernel boundary + the negative boundary + amendment discipline) to architectural boundaries, services, responsibilities, and placement. It stays at the **reference** level — the sequence after it is Logical Architecture → Implementation Architecture → Systems.

## 6 · Standing constraints

- **No new laws, no new concepts, no new rows** — every engine, mechanism, and representation named by the architecture SHALL be drawn from the Constitution's own Chapter III lists (engine layer: validation engines · fallacy detection · debate pipeline · intent classification · verdict vocabulary · mechanisms: epistemic-inquiry lifecycle · reasoning pipelines · representations: evidence records · context tuples · epistemic state model · absence taxonomy · epistemic graph · typed encodings) or from the study's established positions (LLM = governed generation mechanism).
- **The kernel does not do the reasoning** — engines do; the kernel gates.
- **No engine, mechanism, or representation may violate an article.**
- **Implementation is out of scope** — no storage, database, API, or class choices; those belong to Implementation Architecture.
- **The final rule** — the strongest statement never exceeds the evidence.
