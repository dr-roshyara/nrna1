$$\boxed{\textbf{6 of 6 candidate SPLITS were produced by analysis of definitions.}}$$

## ⭐ The principle, and why it is `[EMP]` in **both** directions

The prohibition is honoured: *distinction being derived does **not** make existence derived.* And the
independence is not assumed — **it has two witnesses, pointing opposite ways:**

| direction | witness |
|---|---|
| **existence WITHOUT distinction** | ⭐⭐ **`ℐ`** — established by naming; **no distinction analysis is possible**, because `0 of 7` of its definitions is established |
| **distinction WITHOUT prior candidate-level naming** | ⭐ **`𝒦`** — its *distinctness from `K`* was produced by **containment analysis**, not by anyone declaring `𝒦` a separate candidate |

$$\boxed{\begin{array}{c}\textbf{P-EX} \quad \textbf{Candidate EXISTENCE is established by an ACT (naming · stipulation · measurement).}\\ \textbf{Candidate DISTINCTION is discovered by ANALYSIS of definitions.}\\ \textbf{Neither reduces to the other, and both are independently witnessed.}\end{array}}$$

`[DERIVED]` **Corollary:** one candidate may carry **both** — `𝒦` exists by naming *and* is
distinguished by derivation. **The mechanisms are orthogonal, not alternative.**

# 4. `ℐ` as the critical witness — and three further tests

## `ℐ`

**The state to represent:** a candidate treated as existing, carrying status, grounding, dependencies
and a relation, while **`0` of its definitions is established.**

| the four prohibitions | how the derived ontology satisfies it |
|---|---|
| **no invented definition** | ✅ its **four real** definitions are recorded, each `unestablished`. **No fifth is created** |
| **no unsupported candidate identity** | ✅ identity rests on the **establishment act** (naming, `G-67`) — a real, citable act. **Existence does not borrow from any definition** |
| **the glyph is not the candidate** | ✅ `ℐ` and `𝓘` are **two glyphs**; whether they index **one candidate or two** is `Q3`, **left open**. The candidate is not identified *with* either glyph |
| **the candidate does not disappear** | ✅ because **existence ≠ definition-establishment** (`P-EX`, `I-2`) |

⭐ **`ℐ` is representable only because existence and distinction are separated.** Under any model that
derives existence from definitions, **`ℐ` cannot exist** — and it is the estate's most-evidenced
blocker.

## `Qualify`

Exists by **naming** *(explicitly: "named, typed, NO BODY")* · **three** definitions with **three
different codomains** · one definition realized **twice**, both `stipulated`.
✅ Representable: existence by act; three definitions independent (`I-5`); `D-04`'s `Φ` reading held at
**disposition 3, ambiguous** (`I-8`); two artifacts realizing **one** definition (item 7).
⚠️ **`G1` (*no derived body*) and the two implementations coexist** — via item 9 + `I-6`.

## `Π` R1

Exists by **naming** · carries **`Decision 3` (`Π ∈ ≡?`)**, a property of the **candidate**, not of any
definition · is **one of ≥5 candidates under one glyph**.
✅ Representable: glyph and candidate separated (`I-1`); candidate-has-property (§3); the other four
disposition-2 readings are **separate candidates**, R5/R6 **unresolved** (`I-8`).

## `𝒦` — a **derived** candidate

⭐ **The instructive case: it carries BOTH mechanisms.** It exists by **naming** *(the corpus writes
`𝒦`)* **and** its **distinctness from `K`** was produced by **containment analysis**.
✅ Representable **only if** the two mechanisms are recorded separately — otherwise `𝒦`'s existence and
its distinctness are conflated, and the containment finding becomes the *reason it exists* rather than
the *reason it differs*.

## `Zero` — a **multi-definition** case

**Six** definitions; **two `[RF]`** *(state, missingness-representation)* beside **four live**;
`D-03` is **four definitions recorded as one** (`F-5`); `0_i` is a **separate candidate** by shape;
four rival definitions **implemented side by side**.
✅ Representable: definitions independent with **per-definition status** (item 10 + `I-5`); `0_i`
separate (`I-3`); four artifacts each realizing **one** sibling definition (item 7).
⚠️ **`F-5`'s repair is required for this — and it is `[EMP]` independent of `P-06`.**

## The four specific questions asked

### A — can candidate-keying coexist with unestablished identity?

🔴 **No, natively.** `I-2`/`I-8` require a home for a reading whose candidate is not established.
**A therefore needs a second record class** — a *holding* record.

⭐ **A then becomes a DUAL model.** ⚠️ **But not necessarily `C`:** the second class could be
**glyph-keyed** *(→ structurally `C`)* **or definition-keyed** *(→ a distinct dual model not among
A–D)*. **Which it is, is undetermined and is a new sub-question.**

### B — can a candidate live under multiple glyphs without glyph becoming the parent ontology?

🔴 **Not natively** — B's candidates are children of a glyph **by construction**. A cross-glyph link
would make the candidate **not purely nested**, i.e. **partially dual too**.
⛔ **And whether this is even required is `[OPEN]`** — the sole witness is inadmissible. **B is not
precluded by evidence; only by a design commitment.**

### C — can two classes stay synchronized without an unexamined governance assumption?

⚠️ **Only if the mechanism is stated.** `[EMP]` **this estate demonstrably drifts** — *"findings
propagate faster than corrections do"*, witnessed on me twice and on a third party by 3MC's own `32`.
**Two record classes are two places for a finding to go stale.** **C's admissibility rests on a
declared synchronization rule, not on its semantics.**

### D — can a candidate carry properties without promotion?

🔴 **No.** `[EMP]` `ℐ` and `Π` R1. **Promotion is required** — and a promoted candidate is an entity
cross-linked to a glyph record, which is **structurally `C`**.

## ⭐ The structural result

$$\boxed{\begin{array}{c}\textbf{ALL FOUR require additional machinery, and in three of four the machinery makes the model DUAL.}\\ \textbf{Only } C \textbf{ is dual BY DESIGN — and its cost (synchronization) is the one thing no evidence settles.}\end{array}}$$

⚠️ **This is not a selection, and it is not an argument for `C`.** It says that **A and D are unstable
under the invariants**, that their repairs happen to have `C`'s shape, and that **`C`'s own admissibility
turns on a governance judgment the records cannot make.** **`B` remains admissible on evidence.**

# 7. Semantic architecture vs persistence architecture — kept separate

## Semantic conclusion

**Ten items must be semantically distinguishable** — glyph · occurrence · definition · candidate ·
establishment mechanism *(with warrant)* · definition→candidate association *(status-bearing)* ·
implementation artifact · realization-vs-model-of · definition source · assertion/status.

**Twelve relations**, of which **eleven are `[EMP]`** and **one — candidate↔candidate — is `[OPEN]`.**

**Eleven invariants:** `I-1`…`I-8` plus `I-9`, `I-10`, `I-11`. **`I-12` considered and refused.**

## Representation conclusion

$$\boxed{\textbf{The semantic model does NOT select a record architecture.}}$$

`[ARCH]` **Several physical forms could carry it** — among them a single typed-row store where glyph,
occurrence, definition, candidate and association are **row types**; document-per-glyph with nested
structures; document-per-candidate plus a glyph index; two or more cross-linked classes; and each of
A/B/C/D **with the machinery §6 identifies.**

⛔ **What the semantic model DOES do is constrain:** any architecture must satisfy `I-1`…`I-11`, and
**an architecture that cannot represent `ℐ` (`I-2`) or an unresolved association (`I-10`) is
inadmissible regardless of its convenience.**

⚠️ **The representation conclusion is not permitted to redefine the semantic one.** In particular, **a
model is not made adequate by being cheap to migrate.**
