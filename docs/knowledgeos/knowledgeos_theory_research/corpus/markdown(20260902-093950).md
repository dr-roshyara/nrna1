## Part 3: The Evaluation of Arguments

### 3.1 The Weighted Sum

Each argument has a **weight** (strength) and a **direction**:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Weighted Sum} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \text{sign}_i } $$

Where:
- $w_i$ = weight of argument (0 to 1)
- $\text{sign}_i$ = direction (+1 for support, -1 for opposition)

### 3.2 The Net Argument Strength

$$ \boxed{ \text{Net} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \text{sign}_i } $$

| Net Value | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Net > 0** | Arguments in favor dominate | Proposition supported |
| **Net = 0** | Arguments balanced | Undetermined |
| **Net < 0** | Arguments against dominate | Proposition refuted |

### 3.3 The Confidence Interval

The confidence in a proposition is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Confidence} = f(\text{Net}, \text{Variance}) } $$

Where variance represents the spread of argument strengths.

## Part 4: Facts as the Reconciliation of Arguments

### 4.1 The Reconciliation Condition

In the ideal state, arguments are reconciled:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{i=1}^{n} +a_i + \sum_{j=1}^{m} -b_j = 0 } $$

This means:
- All positive evidence has been considered
- All negative evidence has been considered
- They have been weighed and resolved

### 4.2 The Fact as the Limit

A **fact** is the limit of the reconciliation process:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Fact} = \lim_{t \to \infty} \text{Knowledge}_t } $$

Where:
- $\text{Knowledge}_t$ = the state of knowledge at time t
- $t$ = time (inquiry)

### 4.3 The Argument-Fact Spectrum

| State | Formalization | Interpretation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Hypothesis** | $\sum +a_i > 0$, $\sum -b_j = 0$ | Only supporting evidence |
| **Contested** | $\sum +a_i > 0$, $\sum -b_j > 0$ | Both sides present |
| **Determined** | $\sum +a_i > \sum -b_j$ | Arguments in favor dominate |
| **Fact** | $\sum +a_i + \sum -b_j = 0$ | All arguments reconciled |
