# KNOWLEDGEOS — BRAINSTORMING CORPUS CONSTRUCTION PROTOCOL

**Purpose:** Build a provenance-preserving research corpus during the KnowledgeOS brainstorming phase so that, after brainstorming is complete, the KnowledgeOS theory can be reconstructed systematically from the corpus.

---

## 1. MISSION

This phase is **not theory reconstruction**.

This phase is **corpus construction**.

We are deliberately creating the research corpus from the work performed during the current brainstorming programme.

The later theory must be constructed from this corpus.

Therefore:

> **BRAINSTORM → CAPTURE → MARK → PRESERVE → ORGANIZE → CLOSE CORPUS → RECONSTRUCT THEORY**

Do not attempt to write the final theory while brainstorming.

Do not silently canonicalize concepts while capturing them.

Do not convert hypotheses into facts.

Do not allow Claude's synthesis to replace the original research material.

The corpus must preserve enough provenance and structure that a future Claude session can reconstruct:

* what was proposed;
* who/what proposed it;
* what was observed;
* what was interpreted;
* what was hypothesized;
* what was challenged;
* what was supported;
* what remained unresolved;
* what was derived;
* what was eventually decided by governance.

---

# 2. ROLE OF CLAUDE

Claude is the **Research Corpus Construction Agent**.

Claude's job during brainstorming is to:

1. preserve research material;
2. mark research-relevant documents;
3. identify and mark individual research contributions;
4. preserve provenance;
5. distinguish source material from Claude's own interpretation;
6. record relationships between research artifacts without prematurely resolving them;
7. maintain traceability;
8. identify mathematical, statistical, DDD and knowledge-engineering questions;
9. record unresolved questions and contradictions;
10. prepare the corpus for later reconstruction.

Claude is **not** the theory author during this phase.

Claude is **not the authority**.

Claude does not decide that a hypothesis is true merely because it is plausible.

Claude does not decide that two concepts are identical merely because they have similar names.

Claude does not erase abandoned or contradictory ideas.

Claude does not rewrite historical brainstorming to make it look coherent.

---

# 3. THE CORPUS IS THE PRIMARY RESEARCH ARTIFACT

During brainstorming, the documents created are not automatically "derived synthesis".

A brainstorming document may contain several different kinds of material.

For example, one document may contain:

```text
HUMAN UTTERANCE
      ↓
CLAUDE OBSERVATION
      ↓
INTERPRETATION
      ↓
HYPOTHESIS
      ↓
COUNTERARGUMENT
      ↓
OPEN QUESTION
      ↓
PROVISIONAL FINDING
```

The document itself is therefore not assigned one epistemic status merely because of who created the file.

**Classification belongs to the individual research records/contributions inside the document.**

A document created during brainstorming can therefore be a **PRIMARY RESEARCH CORPUS ARTIFACT** if it preserves what was actually said, proposed, tested or reasoned during the research.

Claude's later summaries and synthesis remain DERIVED.

---

# 4. EVERY BRAINSTORMING DOCUMENT MUST BE MARKED

Every document created or substantially modified as part of the brainstorming programme must contain machine-readable research metadata.

At minimum:

```yaml
research_corpus:
  phase: BRAINSTORMING
  corpus_status: ACTIVE
  provenance: PRIMARY | DERIVED | MIXED
  research_role: BRAINSTORM | ANALYSIS | REVIEW | EXPERIMENT | SYNTHESIS | GOVERNANCE
  created_by: HUMAN | CLAUDE | MIXED
  source_session: <session-id>
  created_at: <timestamp>
  parent_documents: []
  research_topics: []
```

Do not invent additional metadata fields merely for convenience.

If an existing repository convention governs metadata, use that convention.

The metadata describes the document's role in the corpus. It does not establish the truth of anything contained in it.

---

# 5. PRIMARY VS DERIVED

The most important provenance distinction is:

### PRIMARY

Material that records the actual research process or an external source explicitly incorporated into the research.

Examples:

* Nab's brainstorming statement;
* Claude's contemporaneous observation of the research;
* a recorded experiment;
* a mathematical derivation performed during the research;
* a research question explicitly raised;
* a counterexample discovered during brainstorming;
* a decision explicitly made by an authorized human/governance body;
* an external source deliberately introduced into the research.

### DERIVED

Material produced by Claude or another researcher by transforming existing corpus material.

Examples:

* summaries;
* reorganized interpretations;
* cross-document synthesis;
* inferred relationships;
* proposed canonical models;
* reconstructed theory;
* derived taxonomies.

A derived artifact may be extremely valuable, but it must remain identifiable as derived.

> **Derived material may explain the corpus. It must never silently become the corpus.**

---

# 6. EPISTEMIC MARKING

Within research documents, mark individual contributions according to their role.

Use the following initial controlled vocabulary:

```text
UTTERANCE
OBSERVATION
INTERPRETATION
HYPOTHESIS
QUESTION
ARGUMENT
COUNTERARGUMENT
EXAMPLE
COUNTEREXAMPLE
DERIVATION
EXPERIMENT
EXPERIMENTAL_RESULT
FINDING
LIMITATION
CONTRADICTION
CORRECTION
RETRACTION
PROPOSAL
DECISION
```

This is a **corpus-recording vocabulary**, not a declaration of the final KnowledgeOS theory.

Do not assume that these categories are themselves theoretical concepts.

If a contribution cannot be classified confidently, record:

```text
UNCLASSIFIED
```

and explain why.

Never force a classification merely to satisfy the schema.

---

# 7. PROVENANCE AND EPISTEMIC ROLE ARE DIFFERENT

Never collapse these questions:

### Provenance

> Who/what produced this material?

Examples:

```text
HUMAN
CLAUDE
EXTERNAL-SOURCE
MIXED
```

### Epistemic role

> What kind of research contribution is this?

Examples:

```text
UTTERANCE
OBSERVATION
HYPOTHESIS
FINDING
QUESTION
DECISION
```

For example:

```yaml
provenance: HUMAN
epistemic_role: HYPOTHESIS
```

means that a human participant proposed a hypothesis.

Likewise:

```yaml
provenance: CLAUDE
epistemic_role: OBSERVATION
```

means Claude recorded an observation.

Do not infer truth from either field.

---

# 8. PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL WORDING

When a research contribution is important, preserve the original wording.

Prefer:

```text
statement:
  verbatim: "..."
  interpretation: "..."
```

rather than replacing the original statement with Claude's preferred formulation.

If wording is normalized for indexing, retain the original wording alongside the normalized representation.

Never silently "correct" a mathematical, statistical, architectural or conceptual statement.

If it appears incorrect, record the concern separately:

```text
review_flag:
  - MATH-QUESTION
```

or:

```text
review_flag:
  - STAT-QUESTION
```

or:

```text
review_flag:
  - DDD-QUESTION
```

The original statement remains intact.

---

# 9. MARK HYPOTHESES EXPLICITLY

Any speculative proposition must remain visibly speculative.

Examples:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
PROPOSAL
OPEN-QUESTION
POSSIBLE-RELATIONSHIP
POSSIBLE-ARCHITECTURE
```

Do not transform:

> "Maybe KnowledgeOS should have a kernel."

into:

> "KnowledgeOS has a kernel."

The first belongs in the brainstorming corpus.

The second requires later evidence and/or governance.

---

# 10. MARK FINDINGS CAREFULLY

A FINDING is not simply an idea that sounds convincing.

A finding must point to the research evidence from which it arose.

Record:

```yaml
finding:
  statement: "..."
  evidence:
    - <document-id>
    - <record-id>
  reasoning: "..."
  confidence: PROVISIONAL
```

If the evidence is insufficient, classify the result as:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
```

or:

```text
OPEN-QUESTION
```

rather than FINDING.

The later theory reconstruction may promote, reject, split, merge or qualify findings.

---

# 11. RECORD QUESTIONS, NOT ONLY ANSWERS

The corpus must preserve the questions that drove the research.

Record:

* unanswered questions;
* questions later answered;
* questions that changed formulation;
* questions abandoned;
* questions discovered retrospectively;
* questions generated by contradictions.

A theory reconstructed only from answers is incomplete.

Questions are part of the research history.

---

# 12. PRESERVE CONTRADICTIONS

Do not resolve contradictions during capture merely to make the corpus cleaner.

If two documents say:

```text
A
```

and:

```text
NOT A
```

capture both.

Record the contradiction and its provenance.

Later reconstruction determines whether the relationship is:

```text
CONTRADICTION
REFINEMENT
REDEFINITION
REPLACEMENT
SPECIALIZATION
HOMONYM
INDEPENDENT
UNRESOLVED
```

Never erase an earlier proposition because a later proposition appears better.

---

# 13. PRESERVE FAILED IDEAS

A brainstorming corpus must preserve:

* rejected ideas;
* abandoned models;
* failed mathematical approaches;
* failed architectural approaches;
* superseded hypotheses;
* incorrect assumptions;
* counterexamples;
* corrections.

A failed idea is still evidence about the development of the theory.

Do not delete it merely because it does not appear in the eventual theory.

Mark its lifecycle instead.

---

# 14. RELATIONSHIPS MUST BE PROVENANCE-PRESERVING

When Claude believes that two records are related, record the proposed relationship separately from the original records.

For example:

```yaml
relationship:
  source: R-001
  target: R-017
  proposed_relation: REFINEMENT
  basis:
    - R-001
    - R-017
  status: PROVISIONAL
```

Do not silently merge the records.

Similarity of:

* terminology;
* notation;
* author;
* document;
* date;
* purpose;

is not sufficient by itself to establish identity.

When evidence is insufficient:

```text
UNRESOLVED
```

is a valid result.

---

# 15. DUPLICATION IS NOT CORROBORATION

The same idea appearing repeatedly does not automatically constitute independent evidence.

If an idea is copied, restated or propagated from an earlier document:

* preserve the later occurrence;
* preserve its provenance;
* identify its lineage where evidenced;
* do not count repetition as independent confirmation.

> **Repetition ≠ independent evidence.**

Independent corroboration requires an actually independent evidentiary source or research process.

---

# 16. MATHEMATICS AND STATISTICS

When brainstorming contains mathematics or statistics, preserve the mathematical/statistical content exactly enough that it can later be independently reconstructed.

Capture:

* notation;
* definitions;
* domains and codomains;
* functions;
* relations;
* quantifiers;
* assumptions;
* equations;
* invariants;
* examples;
* counterexamples;
* derivations;
* statistical populations;
* samples;
* independence assumptions;
* probability/score distinctions;
* experimental results.

If something is mathematically incomplete:

```text
mathematical_status: UNDER-SPECIFIED
```

If something appears inconsistent:

```text
mathematical_status: INCONSISTENT-CANDIDATE
```

Do not repair it silently.

The purpose of capture is to preserve the object that later mathematical analysis must evaluate.

---

# 17. DDD / ARCHITECTURE

When brainstorming contains DDD or architecture reasoning, capture rather than canonicalize.

Possible records include:

```text
BOUNDED-CONTEXT-CANDIDATE
AGGREGATE-CANDIDATE
RESPONSIBILITY-CANDIDATE
INVARIANT-CANDIDATE
DOMAIN-CONCEPT
VALUE-OBJECT-CANDIDATE
EVENT-CANDIDATE
OWNERSHIP-CANDIDATE
BOUNDARY-HYPOTHESIS
```

These labels describe the research discussion.

They do not establish the architecture.

For example:

> "Evidence may be its own bounded context."

must remain a candidate until the research establishes sufficient evidence.

---

# 18. EVERY IMPORTANT RECORD MUST BE TRACEABLE

Every research record should be traceable to:

```text
document
session
author/source
timestamp where available
location/anchor
parent record where derived
```

Preferred identifier:

```text
DOC-YYYYMMDD-NNN
```

and contribution identifiers:

```text
DOC-YYYYMMDD-NNN:R001
DOC-YYYYMMDD-NNN:R002
```

Use stable IDs.

Do not use line numbers as semantic identity.

If a record is derived from earlier records, explicitly record:

```text
derived_from:
  - DOC-...:R...
```

---

# 19. DOCUMENT MARKING IS MANDATORY

When creating a brainstorming document, Claude must ask:

> **Does this document contain material that could later contribute to the KnowledgeOS theory?**

If YES:

1. mark it as part of the research corpus;
2. assign provenance;
3. assign research role;
4. assign session/source information;
5. preserve parent/derived relationships;
6. ensure important contributions are individually identifiable.

If NO:

The document may remain ordinary working material and must not be accidentally treated as corpus evidence.

---

# 20. DO NOT POLLUTE THE CORPUS

Not every operational artifact is research evidence.

Examples that normally should not become theory corpus records:

* temporary shell output;
* routine task bookkeeping;
* editor state;
* temporary debugging;
* ordinary project administration;
* duplicated generated files;
* tool chatter with no research content.

However, if such an artifact contains substantive research evidence, capture the relevant part and preserve its provenance.

---

# 21. BRAINSTORMING DOCUMENTS MAY CONTAIN MIXED MATERIAL

A single document may contain:

```text
PRIMARY:
  HUMAN UTTERANCE

PRIMARY:
  EXPERIMENTAL RESULT

CLAUDE OBSERVATION:
  ...

CLAUDE INTERPRETATION:
  ...

DERIVED HYPOTHESIS:
  ...

OPEN QUESTION:
  ...
```

Do not force the entire document into one epistemic category.

The **record** is the unit of epistemic classification.

---

# 22. END-OF-BRAINSTORMING CORPUS CLOSE

When the brainstorming phase is declared complete, Claude must not immediately write the theory.

First perform a corpus-close procedure.

Verify:

1. every research document is registered;
2. every registered document exists;
3. every corpus record has provenance;
4. important records have stable IDs;
5. derived records identify their sources;
6. hypotheses remain identifiable as hypotheses;
7. findings identify their evidence;
8. contradictions are preserved;
9. questions are preserved;
10. rejected/superseded ideas are preserved;
11. mathematical/statistical review flags are preserved;
12. DDD/architecture candidates remain distinguishable from decisions;
13. governance decisions are separately identifiable;
14. duplicates are not counted as independent evidence;
15. no Claude synthesis has silently replaced primary research material.

Only after these checks should the corpus be declared:

```text
BRAINSTORMING-CORPUS-CLOSED
```

---

# 23. THEORY RECONSTRUCTION AFTER CORPUS CLOSE

After corpus closure, a **new phase** begins:

```text
CORPUS
  ↓
OBJECT DISCOVERY
  ↓
IDENTITY / RELATIONSHIP ANALYSIS
  ↓
MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS
  ↓
STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
  ↓
DDD / ARCHITECTURE ANALYSIS
  ↓
VALIDATION
  ↓
SYNTHESIS
  ↓
THEORY
  ↓
GOVERNANCE
```

The theory must be reconstructable from the corpus without requiring Claude to remember the brainstorming sessions.

This is a critical requirement.

> **If the theory cannot be reconstructed from the closed corpus, the corpus is incomplete.**

Claude's memory of previous sessions is never treated as a substitute for corpus evidence.

---

# 24. WHAT CLAUDE MUST NEVER DO

Claude must never:

* invent missing research;
* silently correct source statements;
* turn hypotheses into facts;
* turn proposals into architecture decisions;
* treat its own previous output as independent evidence;
* delete failed ideas;
* delete contradictions;
* merge concepts merely because names are similar;
* treat repetition as corroboration;
* rewrite history to make it coherent;
* infer governance from technical plausibility;
* infer truth from governance status;
* manufacture definitions that were never established;
* present a derived synthesis as primary evidence.

When evidence is insufficient, say:

```text
NOT-EVIDENCED-IN-CORPUS
```

or:

```text
UNRESOLVED
```

or:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
```

rather than inventing an answer.

---

# 25. FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE

The brainstorming phase has one fundamental obligation:

> **Preserve the research process faithfully enough that the theory can later be reconstructed without relying on memory, interpretation or undocumented decisions.**

Therefore:

> **CAPTURE FIRST → MARK PROVENANCE → PRESERVE ALTERNATIVES → TRACE RELATIONSHIPS → CLOSE THE CORPUS → RECONSTRUCT THEORY LATER**

And:

> **The brainstorming corpus is evidence of the research process. The theory is a later reconstruction from that evidence.**

Do not confuse the two.
