## 2. ⛔ The structural obstacle — it is the ES-005 problem ONE LEVEL UP

> ### **Adopting the index wholesale would adopt a Decision Authority Matrix that CLASSIFIES CANDIDATES — including ES-005.4, which the Authority DEFERRED this morning precisely to avoid making it binding by bundling.**

***The same defect, one level higher: at ES-005 the bundle was a document containing a candidate rule; here the bundle is an index containing candidate classifications. Adoption would grant governance status to entries whose subjects are undecided.***

**And a second structural fact compounds it:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The index points to SIX PROPOSED documents** | ***Adopting it would make the NAVIGATION binding while the DESTINATIONS remain unadopted*** |
| **Only three rules in the entire set are adopted** | ES-005.1 · .2 · .3, adopted hours ago |

---

## 3. What adoption would actually CHANGE — and the answer is the assessment's core

**Applying the governed distinction *adoption changes binding status, not content*:**

| If the index were adopted, what becomes binding? | |
|---|---|
| **Its rule→home POINTERS** | ⚠️ *But ES-005 already declares itself canonical for its own rules, and the index's own convention says registered rules "already have a governed home"* |
| **Its Decision Authority Matrix** | ⛔ *Which contains candidate entries (§2)* |
| **Its stopping rule** | *A constraint on creating new ES standards* |

> ### ***So adoption would make binding: a set of pointers that already point to self-declaring canonical homes, plus a matrix containing undecided entries. It is difficult to identify a currently disputed question that adoption would settle.***

---

## 4. ⚠️ A FOURTH OPTION the commission did not contemplate

**The Authority's framing offered adopt / not-adopt. The evidence suggests a third and fourth:**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **A — ADOPT WHOLESALE** | ⛔ **Blocked by §2** — imports candidate classifications by bundling |
| **B — ADOPT PER-ELEMENT** *(table · hierarchy · matrix-minus-candidates · stopping rule)* | **Available, and it mirrors ES-005's per-element pattern** |
| **C — DEFER until its targets are adopted** | **Available** — *a binding index over proposed content has unclear meaning* |
| **D — ⭐ NO ADOPTION ACT IS REQUIRED** | ***The index registers pointers to self-declaring canonical homes; its PROPOSED status has already been determined to gate nothing; and no currently disputed question would be settled by adopting it*** |

> ### ***Option D is the repository-scope pattern recurring: the commissioned act may not be the required one. Recorded as available, NOT recommended — the assessment classifies evidence and does not choose.***

---

## 5. Answers to the Authority's two questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Is the index mature enough to adopt?** | ⚠️ **Maturity is not the obstacle. The index is coherent, self-consistent, and observes its own no-duplication convention.** ***The obstacle is STRUCTURAL: it contains candidate classifications and points to unadopted documents*** |
| **Does its PROPOSED status still matter?** | ⛔ **NO — for the rules.** *Already determined: ES-005 is canonical and the index registers pointers.* ⚠️ **But it matters for the INDEX's own normative content** — the matrix and the stopping rule are index-hosted, not registered elsewhere, so their status IS the index's status |

***That asymmetry is the finding worth carrying to the decision: the index's status is irrelevant to what it POINTS AT and decisive for what it HOSTS.***
