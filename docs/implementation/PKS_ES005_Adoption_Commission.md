## 1. ⛔ THE DECISIVE STRUCTURAL FACT — ES-005's four hosted rules carry THREE DIFFERENT STANDINGS

| Rule | Standing as the document itself records it |
|---|---|
| **ES-005.1 — The Three-Concern Separation** | *(EM-001, **ARB 2026-07-10**)* |
| **ES-005.2 — The Folder Rule** | *(**ARB 2026-07-10**)* |

| **ES-005.4 — Never a Copy** | ⛔ ***"candidate, **ARB to confirm scope**"*** |

> ### **Therefore ADOPTION MUST BE PER-RULE, NOT WHOLESALE. Adopting the document as a unit would adopt ES-005.4 — which the document itself marks as a CANDIDATE AWAITING ARB CONFIRMATION — by bundling.**

***This is the PMR-7 lesson at CDR-R1, recurring: bundling distinct propositions under one identifier distorts the decision. There it distorted an evidence count; here it would convert an unconfirmed candidate into a governed rule without any act deciding it.***

---

## 2. The three-layer separation, applied PER RULE

**The repository-scope determination established the layers. The adoption decision must apply them to each rule individually:**

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| **DOCUMENT** | Is ES-005 adopted? *(Currently: **PROPOSED — awaiting ARB review**, per its own status line)* |
| **RULE** | Was this specific rule ruled by the ARB? *(Three cite 2026-07-10; one does not)* |
| **BINDING EFFECT** | Does it currently govern? *(ES-005.1: recorded ✅ · referenced ✅ · **at least one implemented use**, extent not established · **binding NOT demonstrated**)* |

***A rule may be ARB-ruled while its host document is unadopted. Adoption of the document does not retroactively create rulings for rules that never had them, and does not add anything to rules that already do.***

---

## 3. What the decision must establish

1. **Per-rule adoption** for ES-005.1 · ES-005.2 · ES-005.3 · ES-005.4 — **four decisions, not one.**
2. **What adoption CHANGES.** *ES-005.1 is already **recorded and referenced, with evidence of at least one implementation use where separately demonstrated**. Adoption would change its **binding status**, not its content — and therefore its **enforceability**, not current practice.* **The decision should say so, so adoption is not mistaken for a behavioural change.**
   **⚠️ Phrased at Authority direction to avoid attributing implementation status UNIFORMLY across ES-005.1/.2/.3** — *implementation evidence was demonstrated for the three-concern classification only, and the decision must not carry it across to the folder rule or the placement litmus without its own evidence.*
3. **The EIGHT unclassified roots.** *`bin` · `bootstrap` · `business_analysis` · `claude` · `config` · `database` · `developer_guide` · `developer_issues` are not classified by ES-005.1 as written.*
   > ⚠️ **Stated precisely (Authority refinement): *adopting a repository-classification rule without explicitly stating the status of the remaining top-level roots risks unintentionally implying that the governed classification is EXHAUSTIVE.***

   **Superseded, retained:** ~~*"adoption would FREEZE that gap into a governed rule"*~~ — ***the narrower claim is the stronger one: the risk is not that the roots become frozen but that READERS MAY REASONABLY INFER COMPLETENESS where none was decided.*** *The repository-scope determination expressly declined to classify them, so no prior act has.*
4. **STANDARDS_INDEX's own status.** *It is likewise **PROPOSED**.* **Can an adopted standard be hosted by a proposed index — and does adopting ES-005 require or imply anything about the index?**
5. **Relationship to R-37.** *R-37 (**adopted**, ARB 2026-07-10) freezes structure: no new top-level folders.* **Adoption must not disturb it, and the decision should state whether ES-005.2's folder rule and R-37 are the same constraint, complementary, or independent.**
6. **ES-005's supersession claim.** *It declares it "supersedes README/MEMORY as the rule homes for the folder rule and placement litmus."* **Does adoption effect that supersession, and what becomes of the superseded homes?**
