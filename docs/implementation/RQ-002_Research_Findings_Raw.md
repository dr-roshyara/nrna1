**Discipline findings:** F1.1 Formal semantics fail at the point of human incentive, not logic (Semantic Web: no author incentive, hand-markup cost, inter-ontology mapping intractable, semantic spam destroyed trust; survived only as narrowed descendants). F1.3 Taxonomy/folksonomy is a false binary — hybrids outperform either; multi-system agreement predicts relevance (Morrison 2008). F1.4 Comprehensive ontologies scale in cost faster than value (Cyc: person-century, ~10^6 axioms, "catastrophic failure" — Domingos; >6-month ontology phases lose sponsors; survivors are minimal extensible cores: PROV, schema.org). F2.1 Discovery is foraging — humans follow information scent, a property of LABELS not content (Pirolli & Card; empirically validated); knowledge without scent is functionally nonexistent. F2.2 KOS trade hospitality against authority; digital systems decisively favor faceted classification. F2.3 Collections rot structurally (ROT framework: Redundant/Obsolete/Trivial); archiving = distinct state (out of search/navigation, preserved); forgetting is an engineered staged transition. F3.1 Provenance is the only quality dimension with a converged standard (W3C PROV) — and even it stays minimal (small core + domain extensions). F3.2 Mature systems let contradictory facts COEXIST under status metadata (Wikidata ranks: preferred/normal/deprecated + reason; deprecation ≠ deletion) rather than forcing resolution; humans curate coexisting truths, machines resolve at consumption. F3.3 Knowledge has measurable half-life and much is BORN STALE (Stack Overflow study: 31.7% of answers became obsolete; 58.4% of those likely obsolete when posted; only 20.5% ever updated; ~47% extra maintenance effort from documentation debt; staleness→distrust→disuse→no-maintenance decay spiral). F3.4 "Single source of truth" is an authority-assignment POLICY, not a fact about truth — scoped canonicity ("canonical within a scope", reference-based reuse) is the observed working practice; global SSOT produces stale mirrors.
