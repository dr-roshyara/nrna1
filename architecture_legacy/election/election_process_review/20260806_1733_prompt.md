# Why this is better than starting with routes

Starting from routes assumes that the routes correctly represent the business.

Sometimes they don't.

By starting with the business process first, every route, controller, and service has to justify its existence against a business capability.

That makes it easier to identify:

* missing business functionality,
* unnecessary endpoints,
* duplicated flows,
* inconsistencies between documentation and implementation.

## The commission I would give Claude

I'd keep it very explicit and read-only at first:

```text
Election Process Review Commission

Role:
Senior Product Architect, Principal DDD Architect, QA Lead.

Objective:
Understand the implemented election process and compare it with the intended business process.

Rules:
- Do not modify code.
- Do not propose architecture changes until the review is complete.
- Gather evidence only from the repository.
- Build a complete process map from business flow to implementation.
- Trace every business step to routes, controllers, application services, domain logic, events, and tests.
- Identify implemented, missing, inconsistent, and weakly tested capabilities.
- Classify findings into MVP, important, and future improvements.
- Stop after producing the review report and wait for approval before recommending or implementing changes.
```

That workflow scales well. After the election review, you can apply the same review methodology to membership management, contestation, adjudication, and any other product capability without changing the overall process.

Your original approach was:

```
Routes
↓

Controllers
↓

Code
↓

Improve
```

This is how most Laravel developers review software.

I think an event-driven approach is stronger:

```
Business Process
↓

Business Events
↓

Routes
↓

Controllers
↓

Application

↓

Domain

↓

Infrastructure

↓

Tests
```

because it reviews the software from the user's perspective instead of the framework's perspective.

After this review, you'll have a **business-event map** of the election system. That map becomes the reference for every future enhancement, bug fix, or refactoring. It also gives Claude a much deeper understanding of the product than simply reading the routes first.
